# Item 2.3: trust in operations (Alec, 2026-11-05; Claude's notes, to be specified after 2.5 closes)

**Status.** Placed by Alec on 2026-11-05, while item 2.5 was closing ([thinking spec §52](2026-10-27-item2-5-thinking.md)). In the countdown it follows 2.5 and precedes 2.0. These notes record his proposal, what the code already has, and the questions a specification must settle. Nothing here is specified or built yet.

## 1. The record (Alec, 2026-11-05)

On a maintained run in which a structural relation over a word not yet learned became that word's definition (item 2.5's item 107):

> It seems like we are learning relations over words, the words are not fixed, and the relations come to encode false statements.
> My guess is that we need a trust value in words, and a trust value in the relations over them ( using trust here is a misnomer, but we already have that value for relations learned from external sources). So first of all, our words will have a low trust until they are learned, which prevents a relation over them from having a high composite degree of trust (which requires that both operands and operator have a high degree of trust, so maybe multiply).
> That would prevent this situation, and perhaps also ensure the learning order that we proposed making a part of the data, so that we could lean words, then operations one by one (we just need to specify the order of the gates and the criteria for trusting in an operation).
> This will make testing difficult, since it requires a trained model, so probably we need a run_trained flag in edition to the run-slow flag

On scope: "Trust in 2.3, but it will move many of the grammar tests to the gate I mentioned."

Then, refining the proposal:

> Actually, wait. Conceptual trust is already measure by their magnitude, so only operators need a certainty or trust value.
>
> Unless those operators exist somewhere as vectors also, in which case we can use their magnitude.

## 2. As Claude reads it

- **A concept's trust is its magnitude.** Words and concepts need no new value. This is the recorded rule that a concept's length is its certainty ([FutureWork, "Concepts as regions, certainty from evidence, no sphere"](../FutureWork.md#concepts-as-regions-certainty-from-evidence-no-sphere-alec-2026-10-24)).
- **Operators need their own value**, unless they exist as vectors, in which case their magnitude serves.
- **A relation's trust** composes its operands' trust and its operator's trust, perhaps by product. A relation over an unlearned word therefore cannot reach high trust, whatever its evidence.
- **Gates** open in an order the data specifies: words first, then operations one at a time. This is [FutureWork's "Stages of learning as gates"](../FutureWork.md#stages-of-learning-as-gates-proposed-2026-09-28), with Alec's order recorded there: "Words must be stable. Then they can represent something. Then they can represent the same thing."
- **A trained-model test tier**, `RUN_TRAINED` beside `RUN_SLOW`. Many grammar tests move behind it.

*Alec corrects this reading if it was meant otherwise.*

## 3. What the code has (live tree, 2026-11-05)

- **Lexical operators are vectors.** A word in a clause's relation slot is a concept, and `ClauseRow` gives such a row the kind `operator`. Architecture records that each word is a projection operator, and that nouns, adjectives, verbs and adverbs are one kind of operator. Their magnitude can serve.
- **The fixed relations are constant codes.** `part` (with `whole`, `equal` and `generic` folded into it) and `implies` exist only as hashed identity codes (`ClauseRow.predicate_identity`, `predicate_point`). These are vectors, but their length is fixed by construction and records nothing learned.
- **The grammar operations are weights, not vectors.** `not`, `non`, `intersection`, `union`, `lift`, `lower`, the `part` layer and the rest are `GrammarLayer` modules with their own parameters (Architecture, the list of grammar ops; the live inventory is in [Language](../Language.md)). No concept vector stands for any of them.
- So, by Alec's rule, the operations in his gate order need their own trust value, or a learned vector whose magnitude would serve.
- **Relations from external sources already carry trust.** A configured or runtime truth has a source `trust` (`<truthSet>` row trust times `architecture.trust`, [Params](../Params.md)). `ClauseRow.write_clause` writes it into the row's evidence pair (`truth_plus`, `truth_minus`), where forgetting reads it. Sentence content never sets its own trust.
- **Item 2.5 computes trust along a derivation**: by minimum, maximum and lane exchange along the path, learned only at the leaves (todo.md, item 2.5). Alec's product must be reconciled with it.
- **A trained-model tier exists in part.** A phase certificate (`@pytest.mark.phase('<lesson phase>')`, `test/phase_artifacts.py`) skips until its phase's artifact is registered in `test/fixtures/phase-artifacts.json` with its hash and producing receipt: "Lesson artifacts are explicit prerequisites, never invitations to train." In Claude's suite of 2026-11-05 all 16 certificates, in 8 phases, skipped because no producing lesson is registered.

## 4. Questions for the specification

1. Whether a concept's magnitude in fact stays low until the concept is learned. Codebooks seed their rows at unit or $[-1, 1]$ scale unless `<initScale>` is set (`Codebook.create` in `Spaces.py`), and no configuration in `data/` sets it. A percept form's certainty is its activation, not its length (Architecture, "full-presence codes"). Whether an allocated concept's length starts low and grows only with evidence must be checked before magnitude can stand for "learned". Also whether trust falls again when a word moves.
2. Each operation's trust and gate: what raises it (a criterion per operation), the order of the gates, and whether a gate once open stays open. FutureWork's open questions for the gates apply.
3. How trust acts on a relation: as a gate (below some level a relation is held structure without authority), as a weight on its evidence, or both. Whether it composes by product (Alec) or by minimum (item 2.5's path rule). How it meets the evidence pair and the epoch rule ("evidence belongs to its source").
4. Whether the external source trust becomes the operators' trust, as Alec's "we already have that value" suggests, or stays separate.
5. `RUN_TRAINED`: whether it is the phase-certificate tier made to run, which lessons produce which phases, how their artifacts are versioned, and which grammar tests move behind it.
6. Two consequences recorded in item 2.5 (thinking spec §52):
   - the aligned serial reader admits whitespace and punctuation as lexical words (finding 5), which bears on what a learned word is;
   - in a chart without a meaning band, MTM associates by identity only (finding 4).
7. Item 2.5 closes with the recorded single-writer contract for definitions (finding 1). Trust generalizes it: a relation over an untrusted word carries no authority.
