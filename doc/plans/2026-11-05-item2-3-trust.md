## 1. The record (Alec, 2026-11-05)

> It seems like we are learning relations over words, the words are not fixed, and the relations come to encode false statements.
> My guess is that we need a trust value in words, and a trust value in the relations over them ( using trust here is a misnomer, but we already have that value for relations learned from external sources). So first of all, our words will have a low trust until they are learned, which prevents a relation over them from having a high composite degree of trust (which requires that both operands and operator have a high degree of trust, so maybe multiply).
> That would prevent this situation, and perhaps also ensure the learning order that we proposed making a part of the data, so that we could lean words, then operations one by one (we just need to specify the order of the gates and the criteria for trusting in an operation).

On scope: "Trust in 2.3, but it will move many of the grammar tests to the gate I mentioned."

Then, refining the proposal:

> Actually, wait. Conceptual trust is already measure by their magnitude, so only operators need a certainty or trust value.

## 2. As Claude reads it

- **A concept's trust is its magnitude.** Words and concepts need no new value. This is the recorded rule that a concept's length is its certainty ([FutureWork, "Concepts as regions, certainty from evidence, no sphere"](../FutureWork.md#concepts-as-regions-certainty-from-evidence-no-sphere-alec-2026-10-24)).
- **Operators need their own value**, unless they exist as vectors, in which case their magnitude serves.
- **A relation's trust** composes its operands' trust and its operator's trust, perhaps by product. A relation over an unlearned word therefore cannot reach high trust, whatever its evidence.
- **Gates** open in an order the data specifies: words first, then operations one at a time. This is [FutureWork's "Stages of learning as gates"](../FutureWork.md#stages-of-learning-as-gates-proposed-2026-09-28), with Alec's order recorded there: "Words must be stable. Then they can represent something. Then they can represent the same thing."
- **A trained-model test tier**, `RUN_TRAINED` beside `RUN_SLOW`. Many grammar tests move behind it.

*Alec corrects this reading if it was meant otherwise.*

## 3. What the code has (live tree, 2026-11-05)

- **Lexical operators are vectors.** A word in a clause's relation slot is a concept, and `ClauseRow` gives such a row the kind `operator`. Architecture records that each word is a projection operator, and that nouns, adjectives, verbs and adverbs are one kind of operator. Their magnitude can serve.
- **The fixed relations are constant codes.** `part` (with `whole`, `equal` and `generic` folded into it) and `implies` exist only as hashed identity codes (`ClauseRow.predicate_identity`, `predicate_point`). These are vectors, but their length is fixed by construction and records nothing learned.
- **The grammar operations are weights, not vectors.** `not`, `non`, `intersection`, `union`, `lift`, `lower`, the `part` layer and the rest are `GrammarLayer` modules with their own parameters (Architecture, the list of grammar ops; the live inventory is in [Language](../Language.md)). No concept vector stands for any of them.
- So, by Alec's rule, the operations in his gate order need their own trust value, or a learned vector whose magnitude would serve.
- **Relations from external sources already carry trust.** A configured or runtime truth has a source `trust` (`<truthSet>` row trust times `architecture.trust`, [Params](../Params.md)). `ClauseRow.write_clause` writes it into the row's evidence pair (`truth_plus`, `truth_minus`), where forgetting reads it. Sentence content never sets its own trust.
- **Item 2.5 computes trust along a derivation**: by minimum, maximum and lane exchange along the path, learned only at the leaves (todo.md, item 2.5). Alec's product must be reconciled with it.
- **A trained-model tier exists in part.** A phase certificate (`@pytest.mark.phase('<lesson phase>')`, `test/phase_artifacts.py`) skips until its phase's artifact is registered in `test/fixtures/phase-artifacts.json` with its hash and producing receipt: "Lesson artifacts are explicit prerequisites, never invitations to train." In Claude's suite of 2026-11-05 all 16 certificates, in 8 phases, skipped because no producing lesson is registered.

## 4. Questions for the specification

1. Whether a concept's magnitude in fact stays low until the concept is learned. Codebooks seed their rows at unit or $[-1, 1]$ scale unless `<initScale>` is set (`Codebook.create` in `Spaces.py`), and no configuration in `data/` sets it. A percept form's certainty is its activation, not its length (Architecture, "full-presence codes"). Whether an allocated concept's length starts low and grows only with evidence must be checked before magnitude can stand for "learned". Also whether trust falls again when a word moves.
2. Each operation's trust and gate: what raises it (a criterion per operation), the order of the gates, and whether a gate once open stays open. FutureWork's open questions for the gates apply.
3. How trust acts on a relation: as a gate (below some level a relation is held structure without authority), as a weight on its evidence, or both. Whether it composes by product (Alec) or by minimum (item 2.5's path rule). How it meets the evidence pair and the epoch rule ("evidence belongs to its source").
4. Whether the external source trust becomes the operators' trust, as Alec's "we already have that value" suggests, or stays separate.
5. `RUN_TRAINED`: whether it is the phase-certificate tier made to run, which lessons produce which phases, how their artifacts are versioned, and which grammar tests move behind it.
6. Two consequences recorded in item 2.5 (thinking spec §52):
   - the aligned serial reader admits whitespace and punctuation as lexical words (finding 5), which bears on what a learned word is;
   - in a chart without a meaning band, MTM associates by identity only (finding 4).
7. Item 2.5 closes with the recorded single-writer contract for definitions (finding 1). Trust generalizes it: a relation over an untrusted word carries no authority.

## 5. Decisions (Alec, 2026-11-06), provisional until the review before the push

Claude put §4's seven questions to Alec with a recommendation each; his answers, and the recommendations he accepted, are the spec. He added: "Let's review these again before pushing the code." So every decision here is provisional: it is built to as written and reviewed again, with the code in hand, before item 2.3 is pushed. The status line of §1 stands corrected: this file is the spec.

1. **Length means learned, by design, from views and stability.** Checked first (§4.1): today length does not mean learned. `IdentityRows.mint` copies the first occurrence's content at full length, codebook rows start at unit scale, and no model file sets `<initScale>`. Alec: "The length of a vector should reflect both number of views and stability (the same concept may have slightly different parts and wholes)." So a concept's meaning-lane length is a function of two things: the number of its occurrences, and the agreement among their contents, each occurrence having arrived with slightly different parts and wholes. Training moves a concept's direction only; its length is written by the close. Because stability is a factor, length can fall when a concept's occurrences disagree; a relation keeps the pair it was written with (LTM is fixed). This is the certainty work that [FutureWork](../FutureWork.md#concepts-as-regions-certainty-from-evidence-no-sphere-alec-2026-10-24) scheduled for item 3 and item 3.0 left without code ("Certainty gets no code", step 2): the radius is the certainty, so the projection loses nothing.
2. **An operation's trust is the definiteness of its formal arguments, else its count.** Alec: "The operation trust can probably be gauged by the definitiveness of the formal arguments. If they are well-defined classes, then the operation has probably been learned reliably. Other than that, we probably have to rely on number of times seen." The formal arguments are the `argin` faces of `PartsOfSpeech.formal_arguments` (`<op>_argin<i>`), whose occupants `PartsOfSpeech.add` counts per word and order. A formal argument is a class; it is definite when its occupants agree. Fixed relations (`part`, `whole`, `equal`, `generic`, `implies`) have trust 1; a word in the relation slot is a concept and uses its length (decision 1). **Gates are not one at a time** (Alec: "Gate order is not one at a time; they need to be motivated by psychological principles"): operations open in groups, each group motivated by a principle of development, the correspondence [FutureWork](../FutureWork.md#gradual-training-an-age-ordered-corpus-read-in-stages-proposed-2026-09-29) records with Alec's three steps inside it. The proposal is §6.3, for his review.
3. **Trust acts as a weight, with one threshold; it composes by minimum.** At the close a relation's evidence pair is scaled by its combined trust and LTM keeps it fixed. The combination is the minimum over operands, operator and source, the rule item 2.5 already uses along a derivation path ([thinking spec §3.7](2026-10-27-item2-5-thinking.md)); a product was Alec's first thought and he accepted the minimum, so a three-operand relation is not weakened for having three operands. The threshold sits at the definition writer: a relation below it cannot become a definition (item 107's case).
4. **External source trust stays separate.** It measures the source; an operation's trust measures the model's skill with the operation. At the close it enters the same minimum.
5. **`RUN_TRAINED` splits the tests.** Tests of mechanism keep running as now, with gates opened in their test data (the gate order is data). Tests that assert learned behaviour move behind `RUN_TRAINED`, built on the phase-certificate tier ([Testing](../Testing.md#tiers-for-a-model-that-has-not-learned-alec-2026-10-16)), which skips until a lesson's artifact is registered. Item 2.3 builds the tier and moves the tests; the lessons that feed it are long runs and wait for the later phase.
6. **The two consequences carried from 2.5.** The aligned serial reader's admission of whitespace and punctuation as words (2.5 §52 finding 5) is repaired in 2.3: a word is a non-whitespace, non-punctuation form unless `whitespaceUnits` is on (corrected 2026-11-06, §9 D5: under either setting; the knob only presents whitespace runs as null units). MTM's association by identity alone in a chart without a meaning band (finding 4) stays with item 0, as recorded.
7. **The single-writer contract generalizes** through decision 3's threshold: a relation over an untrusted word carries no authority to define.

## 6. Design, in code names (Claude, 2026-11-06; provisional with §5)

### 6.1 Concept trust: lane length from views and stability

*Amended 2026-11-06 by §9 D1 to D3: views are counters, not Part rows; trust lives in the meaning lanes only; the operand's trust is the stored radius.* *Amended 2026-11-07 by §11 D6 and D7: the radius is the for lane's length, the against lane keeps its written evidence; a view of empty content counts with agreement 1.* *Amended 2026-11-07 (evening) by §13 D8 and finding 1: operand trust exists only for a seated concept on a tracked chart; a seatless symbol or any operand on an untracked chart contributes one.*

For a concept with identity $i$: $n$ is the number of its witnesses (`IdentityRows.witnesses`, the Part rows referencing the symbol) and $s$ is its stability, the mean over those witnesses of the request coverage returned by `IdentityRows.meet_ratios` between the witness's content and the concept's content, where a witness's content is the fused parts-and-wholes content the occurrence had when it was written, the same content `mint` receives. Each meaning lane's length is

$$\ell = s\,\bigl(1 - e^{-n/\tau}\bigr),$$

with $\tau$ the model-file knob `<trustViews>` (default 3, beside `<raiseCount>`), written at `mint` and at every kept close that adds a witness; the form band is untouched. The lengths live in a preallocated per-row, per-lane tensor beside the codebook, checkpointed and never resized. Training moves direction only: after each optimizer step every concept row's meaning lanes are rescaled to their stored lengths. `Queries.extent` and `ThoughtFaces.gain` keep reading the norm, so a thought's support is the concept's trust with no further change. A frozen concept (`mint_frozen_concept`) and the null-concept keep their lengths as built. An expected consequence, to be read at the freeze: `meet_ratios` reads mass, so a short (unlearned) concept covers a full-length request less, and identity matching prefers learned concepts; ports are expected where tests assumed unit length.

### 6.2 Relation trust at the close

*Amended 2026-11-06 by §9 D4 and Q1: a non-concept operand contributes 1; "the pair is fixed" meant not rescaled when the operands' lengths change later, not sealed against later evidence; where operand trust acts is Alec's Q1.*

In `ClauseRow.write_clause` the relation's pair (`parts.truth_plus`, `parts.truth_minus`) is scaled by

$$t = \min(\text{operands' positive-lane lengths},\ \text{operator trust},\ \text{source trust}),$$

where a word in the relation slot contributes its lane length, a fixed relation contributes 1, a grammar operation contributes its §6.3 value (1 until round 2 gives it one), and the source trust is the configured `<truthSet>` row trust times `<architecture><trust>` as today. Written once, the pair is fixed. The definition writer (`Interpret.define`, `DefinitionIndex.add`, the single-writer contract of 2.5's item 109) refuses a relation whose $t$ is below `<definitionTrust>` (default 0.5). *Superseded 2026-11-06 by §9.3 (the index admits a denotation; `define` writes every named row) and amended 2026-11-07 (night) by §15 D9 (the threshold gates the binding, never the word's identity).*

### 6.3 Operation trust and the gates (round 2; for Alec's review)

*Learning-order clarification, Alec, 2026-11-07: §6.3.1 governs the curriculum/architecture distinction in this proposal and the earlier gate-order wording in §2 and §5.2. The stage table below remains the record of the proposal, not authorization to impose its prerequisite hierarchy in the architecture.*

**Operation trust.** For an operation $o$ with formal arguments `o_argin1..k`: the definiteness $d_j$ of argument $j$ is the stability of its occupant class, read as §6.1 reads a concept's: the count-weighted mean coverage of each occupant's content against the occupants' centroid. With $m_o$ the operation's kept uses,

$$\mathrm{trust}(o) = \min_j d_j \cdot \bigl(1 - e^{-m_o/\tau}\bigr).$$

A well-defined class gives the first factor; before the classes form, only the count speaks, which is Alec's "other than that".

*Amended 2026-11-07 by §20 (Claude, for Alec to overrule): $d_j$ is distributional, not content coverage. A class is definite when its occupants occupy it alone, so $d_j$ is the count-weighted mean, over the occupants of argument $j$, of the categorizer's probability of that argument for the occupant (`PartsOfSpeech.distribution`); content agreement among the occupants is the concepts' own trust (§6.1) and would double-count it here. `interpret`, `sign` and `bind` contribute 1, as the fixed relations do: their arguments have no class by design (§6.3.2 item 2, the one-class state). $\tau$ is `<trustViews>`; no new knob. Round 2 is built in two freezes, §6.5 as amended.*

*Amended 2026-11-07 by §22 (Claude, for Alec's §5 review): D10, a relation row's learned operation is the operation of the composition that first wrote it, and a later write of the same pair through another operation merges its poles as before and leaves the tag; D11, the naming write of `Interpret.define` clears the tag on the pair it names, so a named row is never capped by an operation.*

**Gates, by stage, not one at a time.** Each gate is a group of operations, opens on a measurement, never on an age or a count of sentences, and once open stays open. The groups and their motivation, from Piaget's stages as [FutureWork](../FutureWork.md#gradual-training-an-age-ordered-corpus-read-in-stages-proposed-2026-09-29) tabulates them, with Alec's three steps ("Words must be stable. Then they can represent something. Then they can represent the same thing") as the first three:

| gate | principle | what opens | opens when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a form that recurs is kept (the sensorimotor achievement, read in text) | word admission; a word's length may grow | always open |
| 2 | the symbolic function: a stable word can stand for something | `interpret` to an object; definitions of words | a set fraction of admitted words have $\ell \ge$ `<definitionTrust>` |
| 3 | object permanence: the same thing again | MTM re-identification, `bind`, `sign` | a set fraction of objects have been re-identified across sentences with agreeing content |
| 4 | concrete operations: reversibility and class inclusion | `part`, `whole`, `lift`, `lower`, `intersection`, `union`, `not`, `non`, `equal`, `conjunction`, `disjunction` | gate 3 open and the operands' classes definite (mean $d_j$ over the group above a threshold) |
| 5 | formal operations: reasoning from hypotheses | `implies`, the thought grammar (`infer`, `ask`, `query`, `isTrue`), modality | the group-4 operations' trust above a threshold |

The order and the thresholds are data in `data/model.xml` (`<gates>`), not code. **A correction to Claude's recommendation of 2026-11-06:** a closed gate does not remove an operation from the chooser's menu (as `intuit` was removed in 2.5). If it did, the operation's arguments would never be occupied and the measurement that opens the gate could never be made. A closed gate caps authority instead: a relation written through a closed operation has its pair scaled by the gate's closed trust (a small value in the model file), structure without authority, so the operation keeps being used and learned by reconstruction while it waits. Open for Alec: the fractions and thresholds, whether gate 5 waits for all of gate 4 or for `part` and `implies` only, and where modality's gate sits.

*Round 2b implementation note (2026-11-08): §27 governs the two model-data authority gates and their open defaults; §28 records the code, closed lesson configuration, layout-5 checkpoint step and literal empty-class measurement behavior. The earlier stage table is the historical proposal.*

*Amended 2026-11-08 (Claude, §29, D12): for the clause gate an unoccupied lift argument class measures zero, so the gate waits for both classes to be occupied; the literal reading of §27 (empty class contributes one) opened the gate before lift was ever used. Operation trust's own empty-argument rule from round 2a is unchanged.*

#### 6.3.1 Learning order belongs to the corpus (Alec, 2026-11-07)

**Nouns and verbs must be learned before the curriculum teaches the other parts of speech.** The learning corpus supplies that order through its examples and progression. This is a prerequisite for curriculum progression, not an architectural rule that disables learning or use of another part of speech until a noun/verb mastery flag is set. It does not require suppressing incidental learning from an earlier example. Parts-of-speech membership continues to be learned from participation in formal arguments, as specified in the [parts-of-speech spec](../specs/2026-10-18-parts-of-speech.md); the curriculum order supplies no hard-coded lexical classifications.

Keep this separate from **trust governing use**: the certainty of a particular word or operation can govern how much authority a relation using it carries. Alec regards certainty as a reasonable measure for that purpose, while allowing that other measures of readiness may be possible. Trust is not a declaration that an entire grammatical class or curriculum stage has been mastered. Putting a fixed developmental prerequisite hierarchy in model-file configuration would still make it an architectural constraint; ordering the corpus is a different mechanism.

Round 2 must specify the corpus progression separately from any evidence-based authority rule. The earlier stage table and its Piaget correspondence are retained as a proposal, not as an established psychological law or a requirement to enforce those stages in the learner. This clarification changes the specification only: it authorizes no round-2 implementation, leaves round 1's trust mechanisms unchanged, and leaves Q2 pending. It was added after the §14 sweep; that receipt describes the source and documents at its recorded freeze. *Amended 2026-11-07 (night) by Alec in §6.3.2: what is ordered is the operations learned, not the parts of speech; the architecture may enforce the coarse order to some degree (words before operations, NP + VP before other operations), and the finer order is the corpus presentation's.*

#### 6.3.2 The progression, operation by operation (Alec, 2026-11-07; Claude's filling-out of §6.3.1, confirmed in part the same night)

**Decided (Alec, 2026-11-07).** After word-concepts are well learned, object-concepts are learned, through `interpret`. From then on every word may be assumed to have the same part of speech; the verb operator's parts of speech then differentiate the words into noun and verb classes, or at least into separate classes. So: `interpret`, then the verb operator, then the modifiers (adjective, adverb, and so on), each introducing new parts of speech. **Later the same night, on Claude's three opens:** what is ordered is the operations we learn, not the parts of speech; the parts of speech follow from the operations. Questions go in early: synthetic sentences such as "bill ran." and "who ran?" test comprehension, and testing comprehension is a way of teaching syntactic category. Simple before complex, to whatever degree possible: enforced to some degree in the architecture (words before operations, NP + VP before other operations) and taught to some degree in the corpus presentation, which models child development and the Piagetian stages. The verb lesson's sentences are as Claude read them (names and bare plurals; no untaught operator needed). Intransitive before transitive. The order after the verb stands as written.

*Claude's filling-out (to confirm where marked).* The [parts-of-speech spec](../specs/2026-10-18-parts-of-speech.md) already fixes the form: a part of speech is the occupant class of one formal argument of one operation, learned from kept readings only, and a lesson introduces one operation. Alec's decision adds the order of the operations and its reason: an operation's arguments presuppose what earlier operations produce (an object before a predicate over objects, a clause before a question about it, a predicate before its modifiers), and the parts of speech are read off afterwards from what each operation's arguments make of the words. In code names the operations are `interpret` (the leaf; `witness` its sibling), `lift` (the [verb spec](2026-10-23-verb-and-modal.md#3-the-verb-line-4): the subject NP acted on by the VP, a clause), `ask` with the question words, `verb` (a verb head subtyped by its object NP, the transitive VP), `intersection` and `product` (noun modification, the NP stage's "description"), `lower` and `definite` (determiners), `adverb`, `preposition`, `surface`, `compound`, and last the relation and thought operations (`equal`, `part`, `whole`, `implies`, `generic`, `query`, `infer`, `isTrue`). The lesson table is [parts-of-speech §5b](../specs/2026-10-18-parts-of-speech.md#5b-order-of-operations-alec-2026-11-07-claudes-filling-out-confirmed-in-part), which this section governs; it supersedes that spec's "the order is arbitrary" and its NP-first curriculum.

Four consequences for this item.

1. *"Well learned" is a measurement, read by the curriculum.* A set fraction of admitted words with $\ell \ge$ `<definitionTrust>` ends lesson 0 (the words are stable); a set fraction of defined words whose named row is a denotation ends lesson 1 (they represent something); for every later lesson, the definiteness $d_j$ of the new argument's occupant class, the measure §6.3 gives operation trust, above a threshold, and for a question lesson the answers themselves (§5a of the parts-of-speech spec: gates are behaviours). Alec regards certainty as a reasonable readiness measure and allows others. The fractions and thresholds are data beside the corpus (the lesson manifest). Incidental learning from an early example is not suppressed (§6.3.1).
2. *The one-class state.* After lesson 1 every word occupies `interpret`'s single input, so the categorizer has one prototype in use and the chooser reads no class. The clause lesson's new words are classified by occupation of `lift`'s VP argument (through the words a VP carries) and, where the categorizer is silent, by elimination (parts-of-speech §3); the nouns are the words that reach `lift`'s subject, and later `verb`'s object, through `interpret`. Whether subject and object collapse into one noun class is read from the collapse table (parts-of-speech §2b), never declared. A question lesson adds the interrogatives as the occupants of `ask`'s open role, and its answers test the classes the clause lesson formed: the answer to "who ran?" is a subject noun.
3. *Coarse order in the architecture, fine order in the corpus (Alec, 2026-11-07, night; Claude's reading of the split, to confirm).* The architecture may enforce, to some degree, words before operations and NP + VP before other operations; everything finer (questions, the transitive verb, the modifiers, the relations) is the corpus's order. For round 2 this bounds the §6.3 gate table: at most three architectural gates, words (gate 1), `interpret` (gate 2) and the clause (`lift`, with re-identification and Q2's pending sign tokens beside it, gate 3), each opening on a measurement and each capping authority while closed rather than withholding the operation; the table's gates 4 and 5 become corpus order, not `<gates>`. §6.3's opens for Alec are to be re-read against this split when round 2's text is written.
4. *What the curriculum is modelling.* Alec: the corpus presentation models child development and the Piagetian stages; the stage table of §6.3 is kept as that model's record, with the architecture holding only the coarse order above.

Resolved (Alec, 2026-11-07, night), as recorded in the first paragraph: (i) the order after the verb stands, with questions moved early; (ii) the verb lesson's sentences carry no untaught operator (names and bare plurals), the rest being simple before complex as far as possible; (iii) intransitive (`lift` alone) before transitive (`verb`).

**Alec's general form.** Three values, stored uncoupled: $x_1$, knowing what a wug is (the concept's trust, its radius); $x_2$, knowing what a bird is; $x_3$, the relation "a wug is a bird" (its own value, what its closes wrote). A sentence that applies an operator, "the wug flew over the barn", has the same composite: the spec requires knowledge of the words before an operator is applied to them, and some trust in having learned the operator, which comes from learning the parts of speech of its formal input and output arguments (§5.2, §6.3). The sealing flag goes; the relations are maintained by an order on the pairs (§9.2).

**The example, as mereology.** Two rows, $\mathrm{part}(\text{wug}, \text{bird})$ and $\mathrm{part}(\text{bird}, \text{things with wings})$: wugs $<$ birds $<$ things with wings. Each row's trust is $\min$ of its endpoints' trust times its own value; the chain wugs $<$ things with wings is bounded by both rows, so by the least of wug, bird and things-with-wings and by both rows' values. When wug is first read ($x_1 = 0.28$) the first row's trust is $0.28\,x_3$ and nothing it enters into exceeds that; as wug is learned ($0.8$) the row and the chain rise, with no second reading of the sentence; if wug's stability later falls, the row and every row above it fall with it, in order. "cats are animals", configured at $0.9$ over unread words, is stored at $(0.9, 0)$ and reads $0$ until cat and animal are read, rising with them.

### 29.2 Alec's representation correction: learned formal-argument vectors (2026-11-08)

**Decided in conversation; recorded by Codex.** Alec confirmed that formal-argument classes should be concept vectors with the same learned-magnitude rule as other concepts, starting at zero. He then specified:

> Yes, we don’t want occupation counts and class probabilities: we want each formal argument to be a vector in a hypersphere, and some pressure for the different arguments to cluster (which they will do when words take on multiple roles). Dimensionality is arbitrary, but enough to cover the parts of speech, and this may even dovetail with categories of words (in which case, we can leverage the existing concept hierarchy to create parts of speech, which is a parsimony win).

The representation decision is one vector per formal argument, including input and output arguments. Its direction is learned from use; its magnitude starts at zero and gains confidence through views and stability. Shared uses of words provide pressure for argument vectors to cluster. There is no predetermined one-hot part-of-speech inventory, fixed unit-length prototype, or occupation-count probability table serving as the category representation. The space has sufficient capacity for the learned distinctions; its dimensionality is not a declaration of which parts of speech exist. With variable magnitudes including zero, the engineering reading of “hypersphere” is a bounded vector space including its interior, not a constraint that every vector lie on a unit surface.

Reusing the existing concept hierarchy is Alec's proposed simplification. [Orders §5–§6](../Orders.md#5-two-ladders-one-fold) already places word classes on the forms ladder and describes the same fold with participation evidence. This is a place to investigate reuse; it does not establish that semantic resemblance alone determines grammatical function or that both kinds of class must coincide.

**Scope and current implementation.** This supersedes the fixed-prototype and occupation-count representation in the [parts-of-speech spec](../specs/2026-10-18-parts-of-speech.md), and requires replacing the probability-based definiteness used in §20 and the gate measurement built in §28. At the time of this documentation amendment, `PartsOfSpeech` still implemented counts and probabilities; §30 records the subsequent implementation. §29.1's empty-class gate repair alone is insufficient. The separate empty-`except` issue remains independent. No vector implementation or new freeze is claimed by this documentation amendment; prior receipts describe their offered source.

**Implementation questions, not additional decisions.** The next design must specify the content of a formal argument's view, how the first observation places a zero-radius vector, and how kept use trains direction while confidence follows the concept rule. It must select the shared chart or hierarchy mapping and a similarity read for the chooser, replace the old operation/gate definiteness formula, and define checkpoint migration. The separate scalar operation-use count `m_o` is not the rejected per-word occupation table; whether it remains a separate trust factor must be explicit. The fixed-operation and identity-mechanics authority exceptions also need reconciliation with the exhaustive argument-vector inventory, rather than silent removal.

Clustering pressure needs a learning objective that preserves distinctions required for reconstruction or prediction. Pairwise attraction alone admits every argument vector coinciding; a word used in two functions supplies evidence of overlap, not proof that the functions are interchangeable. These are design constraints to test, not a request for a predefined number of clusters. Evidence should include transfer to unseen fillers or contexts, preservation of useful distinctions despite shared words, and zero confidence before observation. No lesson is run to settle them here.

**Psychological positioning (Codex's reflection, not Alec's decisions).**

- **Connectionist category emergence.** Elman's prediction-trained network developed distributed representations with grammatical and semantic organization. This supports the feasibility of learning category geometry from use, although it does not establish this model's argument-vector, radius or hierarchy design. [Elman, 1990, *Finding Structure in Time*](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15516709COG1402_1).
- **Usage-based constructions.** Tomasello's account derives abstractions by generalizing across fillers of recurring utterance slots. Formal-argument vectors are a possible computational analogue. Predeclared operations and slots remain assumptions supplied by this architecture; learning their vectors is not learning the entire grammar from scratch. [Tomasello, *First steps toward a usage-based theory of language acquisition*](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1457788).
- **Shared contexts, with exceptions.** Adult artificial-language experiments found category generalization from overlapping contexts, modulated by systematic gaps and the amount of evidence. This supports testing both clustering and restrained generalization; it does not identify whether the participants stored vectors or counts. [Reeder, Newport and Aslin, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3621024/).
- **Meaning and grammatical evidence can interact.** Infants exposed to reliable category cues subsequently learned and generalized word meanings more effectively. This supports interaction between linguistic categories and meaning, without demonstrating that they use one hierarchy. [Lany and Saffran, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20424058/).
- **Graded categories need contrast as well as resemblance.** Rosch and Mervis found that prototypicality reflected similarities within a category and differences from contrasting categories. Applied here, that motivates evaluating preserved distinctions as well as attractive clustering; their experiments did not test grammatical argument vectors. [Rosch and Mervis, 1975](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0010028575900249).
- **Learning through errors is a relevant candidate.** A model of syntax acquisition and structural priming used prediction error and meaning–form mappings to develop syntax. It gives a precedent for making useful prediction or reconstruction drive the geometry, rather than choosing an attraction loss solely because it makes clusters. [Chang, Dell and Bock, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16637761/).

The evidence supports experience-dependent, context-sensitive generalization. None of these sources establishes zero initial vector norms, norm as confidence, a specific dimension, or the proposed gate thresholds as human mechanisms. Those are explicit model hypotheses. Vector learning still uses distributional evidence and can remain sensitive to frequency without storing a word-by-argument count table. A hierarchy shared with other concepts would be a parsimony gain if it supports the distinctions and generalizations, to be demonstrated rather than assumed.

**What the built representation does (for Alec to accept knowingly).** A word's fit to an argument is $\max(0, \cos(\text{word meaning}, \text{class direction})) \cdot \ell_{\text{class}}$, the class direction being the unweighted mean of its members' meaning directions. Participation enters only as *which* word's meaning joins the centroid, and as the view count. So grammatical function is read from meaning: a word whose meaning resembles the subjects seen so far scores as a subject whether or not it has been one, and a word with a subject's participation history but an outlying meaning scores low until conditioning has moved its meaning. **Finding 9 (defect): a class radius is capped by its members' semantic spread, so the clause gate cannot open on a diverse class and operation trust is permanently discounted.** `observe_member` hands a member's unit meaning direction to `ConceptTrust.observe`, whose agreement is the meet ratio of the view in the stored content; `refresh` then sets that content to the unit centroid. |---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 24 | 0.690 | 0.690 | 1.000 |
| 3 | 36 | 0.557 | 0.557 | 1.000 |

The same cap applies to operation trust (the minimum input radius), so every `lift` relation's effective pair is discounted for the life of the model.
