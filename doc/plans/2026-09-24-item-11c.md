## 2. Activation is the pair; the signed scalar is its collapse (decided)

A concept's activation is `(c⁺, c⁻) ∈ [0,1]²`, two positive symbols per
concept (Alec, 2026-09-23). The signed value in `[−1, 1]` that Alec uses
in prose is `c⁺ − c⁻`, the collapse that folds *neither* `(0,0)` and
*both* `(1,1)` onto 0. It is the right scalar for trust (`t − f`) and for
the truth chart at the catuṣkoṭi boundary, never for the field or the

## 3. Evidence for and against, sorted, unioned, written separately (decided, Alec 2026-09-24)

Alec, reconsidering the earlier "never witnessed": negative evidence (a
present percept carrying a negative conceptual weight) and positive
evidence (a present percept carrying a positive weight) are **sorted into
the negative and positive activations**. A negative conceptual weight
swaps the two poles. A present percept supplies positive membership;
its absence supplies neither pole. There is no inferred counterevidence
from the numerical complement of an absent percept.

**3a. Evidence is reduced independently on each pole (corrected by Alec,
2026-09-24).** For required parts, take min over the nonzero positive
contributions and min over the nonzero negative contributions, returning
zero on a pole with no contributions. Zero means no evidence on that
pole, including when the other pole is nonzero. Thus A = `(1,0)` and
B = `(0,1)` give `(1,1)` for a concept requiring A and B. Requiring A
and not B swaps B to `(1,0)`, giving `(1,0)`. With graded inputs
`(.8,0)` and `(0,.6)`, the corresponding results are `(.8,.6)` and
`(.6,0)`. This supersedes the De Morgan min/max interpretation above the
perceptual read. Alternatives use max on each pole; negation only swaps
the poles and never changes the reducer. Complete uncertainty `(0,0)`
contributes nothing. WholeSpace pervasion is a separate membership
calculation: min across every observed position of a run, where an
observed zero membership does rule out pervasion.

**The membership operators (Alec, 2026-09-24).** Alternatives use
**max** and requirements use **min**, including WholeSpace pervasion and
conceptual composition. The shared occurrence readout is also max. These
operators are bounded and idempotent: repeating .8 leaves .8, regardless
of the number of positions or inputs. Empty observed support asserts
neither pole. WholeSpace reads the allowed primitive set at each position
before taking min across the positions of a run; it retains that field for
cuts. A digit property can therefore allow 1, 2 or 3 at every position,
without rewarding a larger allowed set or penalizing a longer run.

Existing nonnegative exponents qualify each literal separately: a required
membership is `q**v`, an alternative is `1-(1-q)**v`, and min/max reduces
these qualified memberships. The source pole carries the sign; zero is an
unwritten weight. There is no product across the inputs, probabilistic or
L2 union, evidence floor, or added regularizer. The existing definition
sparsity penalty remains. Item 10's normalized-means measurement is a
separate evaluation.

**3b. Writing (decided).** Alec: concepts have evidence for and evidence
against, and both are written separately. Recorded as: witnessing is
per pole. A present percept co-active with the concept's **positive
symbol** writes a positive weight; a present percept co-active with the
concept's **negative symbol** writes a negative weight. Nothing is ever
written from an absent percept, which keeps the 2026-09-23 reason (a
percept can be absent but has no opposite) while superseding its
consequence "evidence writes positive parts only". The negative symbol's
activation comes from the read (a present contraindication), the seal (an
asserted negation) or the pyramid; whichever pole is active is witnessed.
Existing edges keep their nonnegative exponents and source pole.

## 8. Higher-order percepts: perception is not sigma/pi (decided, Alec 2026-09-24)

**Alec.** It does not make sense to speak of PartSpace and WholeSpace
having sigma and pi. They are perceptual and different from the sigma/pi
layers used in conceptual space: **parts combine with AND, wholes divide
with OR.** Sigma and pi are both **grammatical functions** that can be
called to transform conceptual space. Percepts are reduced to analysis
and synthesis of what is there, not computation in a significantly new
(dimensional) space.

**The towers, in those terms.**

1. **PartSpace combines with AND.** A fused part is present when each
   constituent holds its designated position; rank k is a unit of rank
   k−1 units, admitted by recurrence at the boundary flush. Its code is
   the ladder's rung-0 max over atoms, in the same `[0,1]^D` cube.
2. **WholeSpace divides with OR.** A type is a learned disjunction over
   byte primitives pervading a run (min over positions); it divides into
   subtypes when the LBG split finds its units in two places, and each
   refinement re-cuts the field into more runs.
3. A higher-order percept is therefore the same kind of code over a
   different extent — larger in PartSpace, finer-cut in WholeSpace —
   never a code in a new space. Rank is mereological level. Sequences
   are not percept ranks; they remain LTM chains.
4. **The concept→percept direction is one path**, the reverse of the
   order-0 read (attribution through memberships), shared with
   expectation and attention (entry 9).

**The loops stay (Alec).** `subsymbolicOrder` is a limit on subsymbolic
processing. Three loops remain: the **subsymbolic loop** over the
perceptual spaces and conceptual space; the **symbolic loop** over
conceptual space and perceptual space; and the **loop over words**.
Nothing in this plan removes a loop or its bound.

## 9. Sigma up over order, pi within order 0 (decided, Alec 2026-09-24)

**Alec.** Sigma and pi correspond to moving up and down over conceptual
order. N-to-1 sigma was never invertible, and the representation to work
with is symbolic. No divide operator is needed: order-zero concepts can
**combine (sigma)** or **divide (pi)**, both in conceptual space. Once
concepts have been **symbolized they can only be combined**, increasing
the order, because **symbols do not split**. Pi is allowed but does not
change the order: it is a **syntactic operator in conceptual space** that
does not operate on symbols, though its result may be symbolically
encoded. This is the original synthesis/analysis distinction —
conceptualization and imagination — theorized between percepts and
concepts.

**The store this implies.**

- **Order 0 is the conceptual field.** Its rows are read from percepts
  inside the attentive field (entry 4). Pi is the field's operator:
  it narrows — an intersection of order-0 rows, read over a sub-field —
  and its result is another order-0 row. Dividing a concept
  that reads *both* (entry 6), and forming *black cat* from *cat* and
  *black*, are the same move: merge literal sets, restrict the field,
  and the existing composition and boundary-time promotion of provisional
  rows mint the row. The located witnesses must survive until that match. **Corrected (Alec,
  2026-09-24):** `W_π` edges run **from order-0 rows to order-0 rows**:
  a pi edge creates an **intersection in conceptual space**, and the
  intersection is itself an order-0 row, which may be symbolized — as the
  XOR cases must be. A row's percept literals (PartSpace parts,
  WholeSpace properties, either pole) are its 11b definition at the
  seam, the membership read, not pi edges.
- **Symbolization seals a row.** A row gets its two symbols `c⁺`/`c⁻`
  and thereafter cannot be split.
- **Symbolic encoding raises the order; sigma and pi do not (corrected,
  Alec 2026-09-24).** Sigma, like pi, maps CS → CS: in the field it
  unions readings (at order 0, the 11b *alternatives* of one row,
  `c⁺ = max_alt p⁺_alt`). The field at order k holds readings of
  order-(k−1) symbols; sigma and pi combine and intersect those readings
  within CS; **symbolizing the result seals it as an order-k row.** So
  order counts symbolizations between percepts and the row. A kind's
  negative pole is the maximum of its cases' negative evidence (entry 3a). A higher-order concept is therefore a
  possibly discontinuous **union of lower regions**, and order is
  **taxonomic depth** (event ⊂ individual ⊂ kind, entry 11), the
  symbolic, inferential subsumption already kept out of `.where`. `W_σ`
  edges run from order-k rows to order-(k−1) symbols. **Pi is not an option in the
  symbolic loop (Alec, decided):** pi and sigma both operate on
  conceptual space, since concepts have meaningful intersections; symbols
  do not, so there is no pi over symbols. Why, and what it does to the
  earlier "sigma over pi at every order", is the next subsection. The
  order hierarchy (event → individual → kind, entry 11) contains no pi
  layer; pi lives in the field, where it may make a definition easier.
- **Reverse.** Reverse pi is attribution through percept memberships,
  exact, and is the one concept→percept path shared with expectation
  and attention (entry 8, rule 4). Reverse sigma is a
  choice of case, the chooser. Imagination = reverse sigma to a case,
  then reverse pi to percepts.

**XOR (working-tree review correction, Alec, September 24).** The gate
asserts a learned row's positive pole. P's pooled both corner is diagnostic,
not an intersection and not an acceptable output. Pi must read retained

The two located cases can be united there or symbolized and united by sigma

The implementation uses the DNF path: the observer receives all four

`(A ∨ B) ∧ ¬(A ∧ B)` remains a field composition check only.

**Both at higher order.** A symbol cannot be split, so a higher-order row
reading *both* is not divided there; the remedy is at order 0, by pi in

entry 5.

**Entry 6's deferred route, answered.** The older refinement survives

**The symbolic loop (decided, Alec 2026-09-24).** No pi *edges* in the
symbolic loop: just a `forward()` and later a `reverse()`. Alec's
correction: `forward()` may call sigmas and pis **as dictated by the
grammar** — sigma and pi are grammatical functions (entry 8), so "sigma
is forward and pi is backward" was the pre-grammatical architecture and
is not the rule. What is fixed: **an increase in order is `forward()`**
in the symbolic loop, and what increases order is **symbolic encoding**,
not sigma (Alec's correction); sigma and pi called by the grammar act
within CS at the field's current order, and their result is what
`forward()` encodes. Reversing an order increase can be an
inverse on the sigma layer or a forward on the pi layer, Claude's call:
**a forward on the pi layer.** Descending from a higher concept is
intersecting it with the present field's evidence, which selects the

the chooser only when there is no field (imagination, generation). The
sigma layer keeps no inverse: N-to-1 is not invertible, as Alec said,
and pretending otherwise would put a choice inside a layer that should
have none. So `reverse()` = pi-forward against the field, else the
chooser, then attribution to percepts.

### Why a symbol can be combined but not cut in half (decided, Alec 2026-09-24; argument by Claude)

This is the reason the earlier design — higher conceptual orders
produced by **sigma over pi**, a conjunction row at every order over the
symbols below it — is not possible, and it must be understood by anyone
reading or implementing this plan.

**What a concept is, in the field.** A concept in the conceptual field
is a *region*: a graded set of located readings over a common ground —
percepts at their positions, or symbol occurrences at theirs. Two
regions over the same ground have a meaningful intersection: the
sub-region where both hold, itself a region, itself a concept (*black
cat*). Division is possible for the same reason: a region is made of its
points, so it has sub-regions. Pi is meaningful here because its
operands are *extended* things.

**What a symbol is.** A symbol is not a region. It is an identity: a row
index, an activation times a row-aligned identity (the shared symbol/
concept index). It is the *name* of a concept, not its content, and it
has no internal structure. Its parts, if it has any, are the parts of
what it represents, and those live back in the field. What can be done
with a name? It can be listed with other names: a set of names is a
meaningful thing, and it names the union of what they name. It cannot be
cut: half a name refers to nothing, and there is no sub-symbol. The
"intersection" of two names is the discrete test of whether they are the
same name, which carries no meaning about their contents.

**The exact reason, in one line.** Symbolization is an *existential
pooling* over the field: a symbol is active when its region is present
*somewhere* in the extent, and the *where* is discarded (entry 4: no
concept identity owns a `.where`; a symbol occurrence is a located percept;
entry 5: both can arise at this pooling). Union commutes with that pooling; intersection does not:

    ∃x A(x)  ∨  ∃x B(x)   =   ∃x (A(x) ∨ B(x))      — sigma over symbols is faithful
    ∃x A(x)  ∧  ∃x B(x)   ≠   ∃x (A(x) ∧ B(x))      — pi over symbols is not

A union of symbols is present exactly when the union of their regions is
present, so combining names loses nothing. A product of two symbol
activations says only that each region is present *somewhere*; it says
nothing about whether they *overlap*, because the `x` that would decide
it is the very thing the symbol threw away. A field with a cat on the
left and a dog on the right activates both symbols; their product is 1;
the intersection of the regions is empty. So pi over symbols does not
compute an intersection at all. It computes co-presence, and mistakes it
for overlap. That is the same conflation as the snap-background defect
(inactive parts pooling to near-certainty), and it is why the earlier
pyramid's pi rows had nothing to learn from: the quantity they were
asked to fit had no referent.

**What survives, and where.** The DNF survives, relocated. Every pi is
performed in the field, over located readings that share an `x`. That
covers the order-0 read over percepts, and it covers symbols too: a
symbol *occurrence* is a percept (entry 7) — it is located, it appears in
the attentive field as a code at a position — so when the grammar needs
an intersection involving symbols (*black* and *cat* on the same
occurrence), the symbols are read as located occurrences in the field
and intersected *there*, by the order-0 mechanism. Above the field there
is only combination and encoding: sigma over symbols, then
symbolization, which is what raises order. Reversing an order is decoding
a name back into a region (entry 9: pi-forward against the field, else
the chooser) — i.e. going back to where intersection is meaningful.

**Consequences for the store and the loops.** (i) The symbolic loop is
`forward()` (encode) and `reverse()` (decode); it holds no pi edges,
because there is nothing for them to mean. (ii) The pyramid's `W_π`
rows above order 0 are **retired**: per the no-legacy rule, deleted, not
gated; the pyramid above order 0 is sigma rows and symbolization. (iii)
*Both* is now explained rather than tolerated: `(∃x P⁺(x)) ∧ (∃x P⁻(x))`
is exactly a product of two symbol activations — meaningless as an
intersection (no single `x` has both poles), meaningful as a diagnosis
that the field is heterogeneous, which is why it prompts descent to the
field (entry 6). (iv) A learned XOR row must match located mixed cases before pooling,
then union their symbols. Reading the pooled both corner cannot satisfy the
gate.

**Deletions.** The perceptual fold layers and all higher-order pi-edge
paths are deleted. The XOR gate is rewritten to train and assert the row.

## 11. Words correspond to objects of arbitrary order (decided, Alec 2026-09-24)

conceptual hierarchy.**

**What exists.** Rows carry an order stamp (ramsification allocation,
live sigma/pi stamping); a word's symbol is its concept row's identity,
so a symbol already sits at its row's order. What is missing is the
rule that one word form addresses rows at several orders and that the
grammar selects among them.

**The assignment (decided, Alec 2026-09-24).**

- **Order 0 — events.** A number of *Felix events*: observations of
  Felix-parts in the attentive field.
- **Order 1 — the individual.** From those events the mind forms the
  concept of a **unique individual**: *Felix*, a proper noun, whose
  formation **coincides with object permanence**; and equally ***the
  cat***, a particular *unnamed* cat. Named or not, a particular is
  order 1.
- **Order 2 — the kind.** ***cat*** generalizes over particulars: the
  English **count noun**. Higher kinds above it.

This hierarchy does not necessarily contain any pi layer, though one
may make a definition easier (entry 9). Claude's earlier placement of
*the cat* at order 0 and of the determiner as a pi against the field is
withdrawn: *the* selects a particular, order 1.

**Consequences.** (i) The lexicon maps a word form to a *set* of rows
across orders, not one row; resolution is by the grammar (determiner,
name, generic), never by a fixed order per form. (ii) Symbolization at
each order is separate: *the cat* (order 1) symbolized does not
symbolize *cat* (order 2). (iii) The reference chain of two truths §3.5
runs between orders: a pronoun ties to an order-1 particular, never to
the kind; object permanence is the order-1 symbolization itself. (iv)
Forgetting (item 5) drops order-0 occurrences before the individual and
the individual before the kind, which is the detail-before-rows rule
already decided.
