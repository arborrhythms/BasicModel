## 0. What thinking is

A sentence closes to one row. If every reference in it is bound, the row is
an assertion and the closing is complete. If a reference is open — the
binder found nothing existing to bind it to — the row is a question, and
thinking is what fills the reference: by lookup, by inference over other
rows, or by waiting for later text. The answer is the binding. Nested
questions are references opened on the way; they fill in dependency order,
which is all the stack ever was. Thinking runs at every closing that
leaves a reference open, declarative or interrogative, within a work
budget; it is credited by what follows, exactly as composition is.

## 1. The open reference

1.1 A reference is open when `interpret`'s binding choice (6.5 §3.2; bind
can be implicit, Alec 2026-10-07) binds it to nothing: no existing
occurrence, column or row matches. The row keeps the open slot (null
reference, open role). Wh-words, `?` and the interrogative mode are surface
evidence for an open reference, not its definition; a row with every
reference bound is not a question whatever its surface. *How the evidence
opens one (Alec, 2026-10-08, replacing a declared class — words cannot be
created from birth; they are learned):* `bind()` is a method `interpret()`
calls: it looks backward for a referent among the situation's candidates
(the recency buffer, cued frames, earlier constituents; 6.5's enumeration).
Found, the reference is bound. Not found, **the reference is forward** —
open — and the chooser chooses, credited by what follows, between minting
a new individual and leaving it open to be filled; a determiner forces the
choice (`a` mints, `the` binds), a bare word leaves it to the chooser. A
wh-word is a word whose backward referent is never found: minting for it
never earns credit, leaving it open and having `the answer is X` fill it
does, so the chooser learns that `what` is a question word — by the same
mechanism that leaves `x` open in a premise until `x is three` arrives. `?`
is a learned whole in whole-space, evidence the chooser may read. The
chooser starts near uniform between mint and open, so questions open about
half the time from the first epoch. *Precision (Alec, 2026-10-08, §13.2:
"open references at your leisure, bind them when possible"):* "found" is
the chooser's judgement — the backward candidates, mint and open are
always alternatives of the one softmax; a same-identity column is a
candidate, never a binding by itself. Binding is two-stage, as in the
reading literature (§13.2): a bond at the word when a candidate is there,
and resolution at the closing or when the referent arrives (§1.3's upsert,
§4's episode). No separate binding pass is added.

1.2 Kinds of open slot: an open *referent* (who, what, which — a role's
reference), an open *evidence pair* (is it so? — the row's `(c⁺, c⁻)`
unknown: `(0, 0)` on a well-defined row), an open *relation* (how are they
related — the operator reference). All three are references in the row's
terms.

1.3 A forward reference is an expectation of a referent (Philosophy,
"identity is an expectation"). Later text may fill it (cataphora); thought
may fill it; at document end an unfilled reference stays stored as a
question row (record kind `question`). *Amended 2026-10-08 (§12):* a
relational row with an unresolved reference is not rejected at commit; it
is stored pending with the reference open, and the address upsert fills it
when the referent's occurrence arrives.

## 2. The operations

Two faces for every relation, by Alec's rule (`isX : X :: conjunction :
intersection`): the symbolic face reads LTM and the taxonomy by reference
and leaves the evidence pair with its witnessing rows; the conceptual face
computes over the concepts' codes and leaves conceptual content with a
pair. Every operation leaves a pair or content, never a scalar (catalogue
rule 6).

| operation | symbolic face | conceptual face |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lookup | `query(pattern)`: the best-matching row for a pattern with open slots, by content key and references (Alec: the best match) | — |
| asking | `ask(row)`: the attempt to fill a row's open references — enumerate fillings (query, the chains below, the situation's candidates), choose one; nested questions are new open references | — |
| existence / truth | `isTrue(P)`: P's row by reference; its pair and trust | `exist(P)`: P's presence in conceptual space — the extent's pair at its magnitude |
| parthood | `isPart(a, b)`: part-of rows and taxonomy by reference, with transitive chains | `part(a, b)`: containment and residual of `a` in `b` over codes |
| equality | `isEqual(a, b)`: DEF rows and bound references; with open variables, substitution of equals — an equation applied as a rewrite rule | `equal(a, b)`: identity of codes |
| implication | `isImplied(P, Q)`: implies rows with chains (modus ponens; temporal chains by `.when` order) | `implies(P, Q)`: containment of P's region in Q's |
| negation | `not`: exchanges the lanes of a pair or of a meaning (also of the expectation image: concluding `not X` from an uncancelled image, §2.6.5) | the same |
| the gain act | sets the expectation gain `g` for the next sentence (§2.6.6) | — |

## 3. Chaining

3.1 Every result enters the serial stream as a slot the next operation can
bind — working memory — with its evidence pair, its witnessing rows and the
operation that produced it. Chains are therefore ordinary: `isPart(a, c)`
with no row proceeds through `isPart(a, x)` and `isPart(x, c)`; `isImplied`
through its antecedents; `ask` through nested open references.

A bound meaning carries its constituent graph. Filling imports its children
into the goal's local table and rebases the transferred local references;
each nested child retains its own local table. Validate local references at
fill, including nested meanings. A dangling local reference is a construction
error raised there, never deferred to indexing or writing (§9 repair).

3.2 A conclusion drawn by thought is written to LTM as a row of kind
`inference`, addressed by the occurrence rule (the document/turn it was
drawn in, its ordinal among that turn's thought rows, its content), with
its evidence pair and references to the rows it was drawn from, so that
forgetting can judge deducibility (item 5). Thought's write permission is
exactly this kind.

## 4. The episode, its end, and its budget

4.1 At a closing with an open reference, one episode opens on that row,
with the configured work budget (`attentionBudget`; `selectedThoughtBudget`
and `thinkingBudget` are retired names, rejected at load). Every operation
charges it; nested questions share it.

4.2 **Concluding is not a choice while a reference is open and work
remains.** The chooser chooses among the operations that could fill the
reference; `conclude` becomes legal when the row's references are all bound
or the budget is exhausted. (The toy: with conclude free the chooser learns
to stop at once in a third of seeds and no departure can rescue it; with
conclude gated by the open reference it learns the chain in 20/20 seeds by
the answer and 17/20 by expectation alone.)

4.3 At exhaustion the row is stored with its references still open, as a
question row; later text or a later episode may fill it.

4.4 **Where the episode sits, and what it may leave behind (Alec,
2026-10-08).** `ask()` happens only between `compose()` and `generate()`:
once per sentence, at the closing, after the sentence's two compose trials
have chosen and before the readers and generation. (In the code:
`_commit_sentence → ThoughtClosing.close → run_selected_thought →
thought_pair`.) It is a longer computation of the same form — the thought
steps are chooser steps and their credit is a longer trail through the
same chooser — not a different one. It leaves the state identical except
for: (i) the row's open reference filled, or still open (4.3); (ii) new
LTM rows — the inference rows with their provenance (§3) and the pending
rows of §1.3; (iii) the credit trail (the episode's choices and the
`descend`/`return` records, §2.1). Nothing else: no row-keyed state held
beside the LTM, no result of a form the closing cannot store. A result
that is not a complete row or a point does not fill the slot and is not
scored as if it did (§13.3). *Certificate:* a state diff across one
episode on the real driver shows exactly (i)–(iii).

## 5. Credit

5.1 The compose rule, unchanged: the greedy episode against one departure
(uniform over the episode's rounds, uniform over the eligible alternatives
at that round), both completed, credited by `K·R·p(a_dep)·ΔC` with `C` the
owner-step cost of what follows: the answer where one is supplied; the
expectation's error on the next sentence everywhere; the budget spent. A
conclusion that makes the next sentence more predictable is credited —
thinking trained by expectation and surprise. The REINFORCE/EMA path and
`selectedThoughtPolicyWeight` retire into this.

5.2 One chooser: the thought operations are candidates in the same softmax
as the grammar's (6.5 §3.2 made identity a candidate there; thought's
operations join it), so one policy credit trains all choices.

5.3 Readers: the presented answer is the binding; the comparison reader's
role from 2e carries over for the answer term.

## 6. Entry on declaratives, and the image

6.1 A declarative closing whose references are all bound opens no episode
unless the expectation's image is uncancelled: an expected row that did
not arrive leaves `−g·κ·ê` at the closing (§2.6.1); `not` over the image
concludes an absence (`not X`), written as an inference row with the
image's confidence as its against-pole evidence. This is the one thought
act a fully bound declarative can prompt.

6.2 Heterogeneity (both) that persists at the narrowest extent is the
other prompt — refinement of the concept (plan §43) — and stays FutureWork's
"four corners as prompts" for its policy.

### 13.1 Why all thirty failed: the compose menu's layout is no longer a stable identifier

Twenty-seven attempts raised `compose has no legal alternative at the
selected exploration round`, two raised an out-of-range gather (`index 54
… size 47`; `index 36 … size 35`), all on the same path: `sentence_pair →
compose(cache, exploit) → choose_operation → operation_layer.forward →
select_logits`. The explore trial replays the exploit trial's **flat action
indices** over the compose prefix (`_exploration_prefix_slots`;
`replay_action` in `select_logits`), departing at one round and running
free after it. A flat index identifies a candidate only while the menu —
`(N−1)·r_reduce` binary variants, `N·r_apply` unary variants, STOP, with
`r_reduce = len(binary_ops)` from `prepare_operands` — has the same layout
in both trials.

The new `Interpret.bind` lays the menu out from **batch-wide, data-dependent
conditions**: it keeps the columns `available.any(dim=(0, 1))` selects
across every row and position of the batch, and appends the mint, open and
unchanged alternatives only when `forward.any()` or `(active & ~eligible).any()`
holds somewhere in the batch (`Interpret.py`, `bind`; the compiled branch
enumerates every column and appends all three unconditionally, so eager
and compiled already differ). In a batch whose rows depart at different
rounds, a row past its departure has a different state, so the batch's
column set and the appended alternatives change, `len(options)` changes
for every operation, and the still-replaying rows' indices land on a
different candidate (illegal → the assertion) or beyond the menu (the
gather error). The old `propose` path kept the layout stable: a global
`count` of columns and a per-row recency `topk`, with one alternative
always appended under a validity mask. The repair returned to the
interpreter *before* that retention and replaced masks with presence.
Codex's static audit names this cause; the two gather errors are its
direct signature. This is a defect the repair introduced, not a learning
observation.

*Rule.* The menu's layout — the number of variants per operation and
which candidate each slot denotes — is a function of the row's own state
and of global sizes only; validity is a mask, never presence. *Repair.*
`prepare_operands` applies its per-row recency retention (`count`) before
delegating to the interpreter; `bind` enumerates every column it is given
and always appends mint, open and unchanged, each under its validity mask;
the eager and compiled branches become one. *Certificate.* (a) A unit test:
`bind`'s option count and slot order are unchanged when another row's
`available` changes and when `forward` is false everywhere. (b) The
ordinary training path: one `present()` training step on a batch of eight
corpus sentences whose rows depart at different compose rounds completes,
with `has_choice` true on every active row of the explore trial. The
zero-budget attempts that failed within three seconds are this test.

### 13.2 The open alternative is withheld once a backward candidate exists

Of the 59 (answer) and 61 (expectation-only) questions observed in the
partial epochs, 6 and 18 opened an episode; the evaluation-only
construction of eight corpus documents opened none (`forward: []` on all
eight `what is y ?`). Per start the outcome is all-or-nothing (0 of 8, 0 of
18, or 6 of 8), which is the zero-temperature argmax of a near-uniform
chooser: a start's initial weights decide, once, between mint and open for
the first `what`. After that the mechanism closes the door: `bind` sets
`forward = eligible & ~found`, and appends the mint and open alternatives
only where `forward` holds — so as soon as an earlier `what` (an
occurrence, or the individual a mint created, reached through the
word-form `associations`) is a candidate, `found` is true and the chooser
can only choose *which* earlier `what` this one is. §1.1's own forecast
("questions open about half the time from the first epoch") fails from
the second question on, and the same rule binds `x` in one document to
`x` in another — the opposite of stage 2 (batch differentiation).

*Precision of §1.1 (Alec, 2026-10-08: "Sure, open references at your
leisure, bind them when possible").* "Identified" is the
chooser's judgement, not the existence of a same-identity column.
`bind()` offers, for every eligible operand, the retained backward
candidates **and** mint **and** open as alternatives of the one softmax;
credit decides which. A determiner still forces its choice. Nothing in
the mechanism binds a word to an earlier occurrence by surface identity
alone; "found" informs the chooser's features (`reference_choice`), not
the option set. *Certificate.* The eight-document construction at
initialization opens questions at the near-uniform rate — about
`1/(k+2)` for `k` retained candidates, not 0 of 8 — and the ordinary-path
`the answer is five.` certificate fills an opened reference on the real
driver (§13.4).

Alec raised an alternative — a separate binding pass that looks for
identity across unbound pronouns and possible references — and deferred
to the psychological literature. The literature has both stages, and so
does this spec. Reading is incremental: referents are resolved as words
arrive when a candidate is available (Tanenhaus et al. 1995; Altmann &
Kamide 1999), with recency, first mention and subjecthood as the cues
(Gernsbacher 1989; Gordon, Grosz & Gilliom 1993) — the chooser's features.
And it is two-stage: Garrod & Sanford's (1994) *bonding* (an immediate,
shallow link from the anaphor to a salient candidate) followed by
*resolution* (integration at the clause end, which can undo the bond);
Kintsch's (1988) construction–integration says the same of the whole text.
Forward references are not held until a pass runs over them: a cataphoric
pronoun starts an active search and binds the first licensed noun phrase
that arrives (Kazanina et al. 2007), and antecedents are reactivated at
the gap (Nicol & Swinney 1989). In this spec the bond is `bind()`'s choice
at the word, the resolution is the closing — the episode's `ask` and the
address upsert that fills a pending reference when its referent arrives
(§1.3) — so the second stage already exists and needs no pass of its own.
What the literature adds is a measurement, not a mechanism: a bond that
resolution later undoes should be rare and should cost (the garden-path
reading), which the credit already charges.

### 13.8 Explore per rule: fork at the departure, no replay (Alec, 2026-10-08; the form of the 4.5 draft's §5.3, taken here)

Alec, on §13.1: "compose runs every sentence twice — this is exactly what
we were talking about; it can explore on a per-rule or per-level basis."
The twin derivation with a replayed prefix is the fragile part, and it is
not needed. Today (`compose(cached, exploit)`, `_exploration_prefix_slots`,
`replay_action`; the same in `thought_pair`, "two complete ordinary
episodes") the explore trial recomputes the whole prefix from scratch,
reproducing the greedy's choices by their flat menu index so that it has a
graph of its own; the departure is drawn after the greedy trial from its
trace. Per-rule exploration is: the departure is one rule application; the
explore trial **forks the greedy trial's state at that round** — the STM,
language carries and scope as they stood — takes the alternative there,
and completes greedily; nothing before the fork is recomputed or replayed.

What changes and what does not:

* *Draw.* Uniform over eligible rounds, walk first, as now (§5.1, operators
  round 2d). Drawn during the greedy walk by reservoir sampling — at the
  k-th eligible round of a walk, with probability 1/k take this round and
  snapshot the state (one snapshot per walk, replaced) — so the fork point
  is known without a second pass; the walk is chosen uniformly at the end.
  The estimator's correction `K·R_walk·W` is unchanged.
* *Credit.* Unchanged in form: the two trials costed at identical
  parameters by the owner-step total, keep by reconstruction, SCG at the
  departure round from the cost difference, the departure's probability
  from the chooser's own distribution at the fork. The explore trial's
  graph begins at the fork (detached there): its restricted backward
  trains the suffix — the departure round and the rules after it; the
  prefix is trained once, by the greedy trial, instead of twice on the
  same actions. Per-*level* credit — the departure judged by that level's
  surprise rather than the row's cost — is item 4.5's and is not taken
  here; row-level cost stays.
* *Cost.* The explore trial computes only its suffix; a thought episode's
  explore likewise runs only from its departure. This is the largest
  single reduction available for §13.6.
* *What it retires.* `_exploration_prefix_slots`, `_compose_prefix_slots`,
  `replay_action` for compose and the `exploit`/`actions` replay in
  `thought_pair`; the menu's layout no longer has to be trial-invariant
  for correctness (13.1's rule becomes hygiene: a candidate's identity is
  still the row's own business).
* *Regression.* With an exact replay the forked derivation is the same
  derivation, so the standing thirty are the regression check; the RNG
  stream changes (one draw during the walk instead of after it), so a
  miss on a path that differs only in RNG consumption falls under the
  operators plan §20 rule, shown by bisection as before.

§13.8 replaces §13.1's patch as the repair for this failure class, with
§13.1's certificate (b) kept as the regression test that failed in three
seconds. It is the implementation form of the 4.5 draft's §5.1–§5.3 (one
departure at one level, greedy completion, the cost read at the end), for
compose and for the thought walk alike; per-level *credit* stays with 4.5.

### 13.9 Runtime under the Viterbi principle, and the order of the problems (Alec, 2026-10-08)

#### 13.9.2 Alec's reading of the form, and the conditions under which it is safe (2026-10-08)

Alec: "one explore on each level, trained by the output, but there is no
full (deep derivational) tree for the explore: the exploit branch is what
carries the signal forward. Is this correct, and safe given Viterbi and a
good training corpus?"

Correct, with three precisions. (1) *One die per walk, not per level:* one
departure per sentence over the narrowing and compose walks (walk first),
and one per thought episode; at the departure the explore takes one
alternative and completes greedily — no second tree, a fork and a suffix
(§13.8). (2) *Trained by the output:* the departure is credited by the
cost difference read at the end — reconstruction and the answer. Today's
trial cost is still the owner-step total `R + E + A` (operators round 2);
the 4.5 draft's §5.2 ruling (judge trials by `R + A`, expectation trains
only the predictors — the dark-room guard) supersedes it for the
departure's judgement and is applied at the re-declaration (§13.10 step
8), since nothing has been measured under the old rule. (3) *What carries
forward:* the kept trial, by reconstruction (round 2b) — the exploit
unless the explore reconstructs strictly better. That is Viterbi's own
rule, keep the best partial path; the chooser is trained toward the kept
choice either way, so the greedy path converges on what is carried.

Safe under four conditions, which are the price of one die:

* **The completion must be near-optimal after the departure** (Bellman:
  one departure plus a greedy finish measures `Q(s, a)` exactly when the
  finish is optimal). The graded corpus supplies this one round at a time
  — the last step first — and the toy says it is not optional: optimal
  derivations reached in 0.96 of instances graded, 0.32–0.43 unordered.
  "A good training corpus" is the condition, not a convenience; the
  per-level stall diagnostic (explore-win rate at zero with surprise
  high) says when a step is too steep.
* **First-order credit only.** A better derivation that differs from the
  greedy one at two rounds at once shows no gain to a single departure, so
  it is never found by exploration; grading keeps each stage to one new
  decision, which is what makes this harmless. No softmax temperature or
  uniform floor repairs a two-step dependency; only the corpus does.
* **Coverage is 1/rounds.** The die lands uniformly over eligible rounds,
  so only about one departure in `d` (the derivation's depth) lands on the
  round still to be learned; the rest are credited at no gain. Bounded,
  not exponential; it is the cost of not rolling for all sixes.
* **The signal is the outcome, not predictability** — `R + A`, with `E`
  kept out of the trial's judgement; and the departure's alternative can
  be any eligible one (the uniform floor `φ > 0`), so no alternative is
  unreachable whatever the policy has learned.

What the form gives up, knowingly: no distribution over derivations is
carried (one path, no beam, no superposition — the forward stays a tree,
invertible and writable), and the chooser's distribution is trained by
the score-function term rather than by a mixed forward. The thought walk
is the same case one level up: one departure per episode, the exploit
chain carried unless the explore's row is kept, the corpus graded by chain
length.

### 14.3 Rules (Claude; the third is for Alec to confirm)

1. **There is no evidence slot (Alec, 2026-10-08; replaces the rule first
   written here and §1.2's third kind).** `(0, 0)` is ignorance, never an
   open slot; `open_slots` reports free variables only (a referent, a
   relation), and `question()` no longer manufactures an `evidence`
   entry. How a question is answered — by direct evidence or by reasoning
   — is §14.5. The answer cost scores the referent binding whatever the
   row's evidence pair: unfilled costs 1, filled costs the code distance
   (`.5 + .5·d/(1+d)` as implemented; exact identity 0).
2. **An open slot is a candidate (§1.3 mechanism).** The open referent
   slot of a stored row is a backward candidate for a later bind, offered
   like a column (its id is the row's address with the role; it is no
   longer excluded as id 0). Binding to it co-refers; a copula whose
   subject co-refers with the open slot fills the slot with its object's
   referent — `the answer is five.` fills `what`; `Ref(1) is two.` fills
   the pending sum the same way. This is the cataphoric fill §1.3 promised
   and the corpus's second form relies on.
3. **The cost of thinking about everything — two options (Alec asked what
   `W` is; Claude recommends the second).** The sentence's two trials are
   judged by `R + A`; the episode a closing opens is charged to nobody at
   the sentence level (inside the episode the thought walk already pays
   `WHAT_STEP_COST` = .01 per step between *its* two trials). So on a
   declarative, *leave `x` open* (an episode runs, finds nothing, spends
   the budget) and *mint `x`* (no episode) cost the same, and three
   quarters of declaratives ran an episode in the dry run.
   *Option W:* add the episode's spent steps at `WHAT_STEP_COST` to the
   trial whose closing ran it (32 steps = .32): mint beats open on
   declaratives at once; on questions, open wins only once the episode
   earns more than .32 in `A`. Its risk is a cold start: before the
   episode can answer, open loses on questions too, and the chooser may
   learn to mint `what` before thinking has learned anything — the
   parsimony term penalising the behaviour it is meant to shape.
   *Option S — decided (Alec, 2026-10-08: "if you can't find the identity,
   after a certain point, create a new identity"; `W` is not used).* Keep
   opening free, as "at your leisure" says, and make the wasted episode
   impossible instead: `conclude` is legal, with the reference still
   open, **when the region's search is exhausted** — `query` over the
   region returns no candidate (the absence inference of §6.1). What the
   closing then does depends on the row: a **declarative's** forward
   reference (the row carries its source's evidence, so the name denotes
   something) is **minted** — a new individual; a **question's** free
   variable (the row has no source evidence) **stays open** and the row is
   stored as a question row (§4.3): Alec, "there is power in knowing that
   we do not know". A declarative's episode is then one `query` and a
   `conclude` once the chooser has learned to query first, which
   questions' `A` teaches; the thought walk's own step cost does the
   rest. The gate otherwise stands: with free variables unbound, or a
   region without evidence, and work remaining, `conclude` is illegal.
   The point at which a search counts as exhausted ("after a certain
   point") is a runtime setting, not a learned quantity, for now.

### 14.5 How reasoning establishes truth (Alec, 2026-10-08; Claude's mapping onto the machinery)

Alec, replacing the "evidence slot": a question, like any sentence, is a
**specification of a region in conceptual space**. Truth for it comes two
ways. (1) **Absolute truth:** a search over LTM that finds a content match
returns a row with a trust rating, and that conveys truth. (2) **Reasoning
to truth:** with no direct evidence, a premise that is true (direct
evidence) and a valid reasoning step — an if/then implication — applied
to the premise transforms it into the enquired region. Premises and
conditionals each have trust values: the premises' generally from the
user-contributed truth set, the conditionals' learned.

Mapped onto what exists (two-truths §1.1; thought-loop plan §3; this spec
§2–§3; the forgetting spec):

* **The region.** A row in interrogative mode is a region: the roles it
  fixes, and its free variables where a role's reference is open (`what`
  — a referent; `how related` — the relation). A declarative is the same
  region with the source's evidence attached: the source's trust `t`
  enters as `(|t|, 0)` or `(0, |t|)` (decided 2026-10-07). A question's
  source supplies none, so the row stands at `(0, 0)` — ignorance — and
  the episode's goal is to leave that state: bind the free variables and
  find or derive evidence. `conclude` is legal when both are done or the
  budget is spent (§4.2 amended accordingly). A yes/no question is the
  case with no free variable; nothing is "open" in it but its evidence,
  and that is not a slot.
* **Direct truth.** `query` returns the best content match in the region
  with the row's own pair `(c⁺, c⁻)` and trust; a free variable is bound
  by the match (the row that lies in the region supplies the referent —
  unification). `isTrue(P)` is the same read for a fully specified
  region.
* **Derived truth.** A premise row `A` with pair `(a⁺, a⁻)` and a
  conditional `A → B` — an `implies` row (LTM kind, decided 2026-10-07)
  with its own pair `(i⁺, i⁻)`, or a structural step whose validity is
  the operator's (equality substitution, `part`) — yield an **inference
  row** `B` with provenance `(A, A → B)` (§3). Its for-lane is the chain's
  weakest link, `b⁺ = min(a⁺, i⁺)`; its against-lane comes only from
  chains that end in `¬B` (a premise with `A → ¬B`, or a direct `¬B`
  match). The two lanes are never netted (tetralemma): a region reached
  both ways is heterogeneous and prompts refinement, not averaging.
* **Learning the conditionals.** A conditional's pair is updated by the
  fate of its conclusions: when an inference it licensed is later
  witnessed directly, the conditional gains for-evidence; when the
  witness contradicts it, against-evidence. This is the same two-lane
  witnessing every row receives, applied to `implies` rows through their
  provenance links — nothing new is added for it. Premises from the truth
  set keep the trust the user gave; premises read from text carry the
  source's.
* **Forgetting** already reads this: an inference row whose premise and
  conditional are both retained is deducible, so its utility is low and
  it may go; the premise and the conditional are what is kept.
* **The math chain in these terms.** `what is y ?` is the region
  `y is _`. Direct truth: the premise `y is x plus two` lies in it and
  binds the free variable to a description, not yet a numeral. Reasoning:
  the trusted premise `x is three` and the structural step of equality
  substitution give `y is three plus two`; the counting facts
  (`three plus one is four`, …) are further premises; the successor
  step applied twice reaches `y is five`, an inference row whose
  provenance is the whole chain and whose for-lane is the minimum over
  it. The worked steps in the corpus are such premises, written out so
  the chain can be found step by step; the learned part is the chooser's
  choice of which premise to take next.

What this retracts: §1.2's "open evidence pair" as a third kind of open
slot, and `question()`'s manufactured `evidence` entry; the interrogative
mode is the mark that the source supplied no evidence, not an open slot.

### 14.6 Identities are revisable, and the revision is credit (Alec, 2026-10-08)

Alec, with the ruling above: minting on exhaustion is provisional. "We
might have to break identities or associate identities after the fact,
and that alteration should condition the probability with which we
formed the identity in the first place." And: "I do not have the metric
for that: we will have to rely on ICA on a longer sequence to decide how
many identities (or things) there are."

Recorded as decided in direction, not built here:

* **Split and merge after the fact.** A minted identity may later prove
  to be two things (break it) or the same thing as another (associate
  them). Both are LTM operations over rows and references — the bank's
  merge rule of FutureWork, and its converse — and both leave the
  formation record in place as provenance.
* **The revision conditions the formation.** The choice that formed the
  identity — bind, mint or open, with the probability the chooser gave it
  — is the thing to credit when the identity is later split or merged:
  a delayed cost (or reward) delivered to that recorded choice through
  its provenance, exactly as a conditional's trust is learned from the
  fate of its conclusions (§14.5). The formation record therefore keeps
  the choice and its probability, as the inference row keeps its
  premises.
* **The metric is ICA over a longer sequence.** How many things there
  are — whether two identities are one, or one is two — is what item
  6.5's independent components decide over enough occurrences; there is
  no local metric at the time of minting, and none is to be invented.
  Item 6.5's learning gates (the million-sentence checkpoint) are where
  this is measured; item 5's merge rule and forgetting's pruning are
  where splits and merges are executed.

### 14.12 Exploration without completion: Alec's theoretical question (2026-10-09), Claude's answer

Alec: "my demand about Viterbi was more theoretical, about an explore
which diverged in one way at every layer possibly being sufficient even
without a greedy completion, since the back-prop of the exploit path may
have a gradient that required movement toward the explore branch (at
least at the last level). We aren't doing this now, so you think it
could work?"

What it is. One backward pass over the exploit derivation gives, at every
layer `k`, the gradient `g_k = ∂L/∂x_k` of the row's cost with respect to
that layer's output. For an alternative candidate `x'_k` at that layer the
first-order change in cost is `ΔL ≈ g_k · (x'_k − x_k)`: a dense credit
for every alternative at every layer from one backward and one dot
product each, no second forward, no completion, no die. The chooser is
trained to move toward alternatives with `ΔL < 0`. It is the
straight-through estimator for the hard choice, applied per layer.

Where it is exact and where it is not. At the **last level** it is as good
as exact: the cost is a direct function of the row, and `L(x'_D)` can
even be evaluated outright. At earlier levels it linearizes the
downstream computation around the exploit path *with the downstream
choices held fixed*: it assumes the same later operations would have
been applied to the alternative operand. That is right when the
alternative is close to the choice or the later choices are insensitive
to it; it is wrong exactly when the alternative would have changed the
derivation's shape — which is what exploration is for. The bias grows
with the alternative's distance and with the depth below it; it is
deterministic (no variance), cheap, and it was taken out of 6.8 (§16.2)
for the blend collapse (since fixed by detaching codes) and for this
bias, with the note that REBAR/RELAX use it as a **control variate**:
the first-order estimate subtracted from and added back to the
score-function term keeps the estimator unbiased and cuts its variance
(FutureWork, "a dense chooser signal, if wanted").

So: yes, it could work, in two forms. Alone, as a biased dense signal
that is exact at the last level and degrades upward — likely sufficient
on a graded corpus, where the unlearned decision is always the last one
(the last level is exact, and the levels above it are already optimal,
so their linearization is accurate), and unreliable on an unordered
one. Combined, as the control variate of the one-die departure, it is
unbiased everywhere and dense: the fork and completion stay as the
occasional exact measurement, the gradient supplies the per-layer signal
on every sentence. The second is the form to specify in 4.5. What to
verify before specifying: the Viterbi toy with continuous states and a
differentiable terminal cost — (i) one departure with completion, (ii)
first-order credit alone, (iii) departure plus control variate — graded
and unordered; the fraction reaching the optimal derivation, and the
bias of (ii) by level.
