## 1. Principle

Operators have no return values. An operator of any of the three grammars
(`<compose>` understanding, `<thought>` thinking, `<generate>` speech
production) acts only by reading and writing the **accessible mind**. Its
signature is therefore three things: its grammatical roles
(`I1 … In → O1`), the faces it has, and its **read/write set over the
subsystems below**. There is no other channel: no side tables, no hidden
return objects, no model or reasoner handle. `ThoughtResult` and the typed
result kinds are an implementation of an effect, not the interface.

Every access is **bounded**: a fixed number of frames, items or steps,
declared and charged. "Access to X" never means "all of X".

## 2. The ten subsystems

### 2.0 Fields, codes and ideas

Three forms must not be confused (Alec, 2026-09-20). Let `W ∈ ℝ^{N×D}` be the
codebook, one row per concept.

* A **field** `a ∈ ℝ^N` is an activation per concept over the whole codebook
  at once. It is *subsymbolic* — each activation is graded — and *parallel*.
  This is parallel knowing (2.2, 2.3).
* A **code** (a concept) is the **serial, nearest-neighbour projection of a
  field**: `π(a) = argmaxᵢ aᵢ`, a row of `W`. It is *symbolic*, one discrete
  index, produced one at a time — which is what makes thinking serial. `π` is
  idempotent, which is the sense in which it is a projection. **The serial
  processing of words delivers codes: a word arrives already projected**, as
  an existing row of the codebook.
* An **idea** is what composition makes of codes: a **single vector that is
  in no codebook**. Adjectives *move* a code; lifting operations *extend its
  temporal dimension* (a thing becomes a process); the result is one point
  in conceptual space, held in a slot (2.4). Its tie to the codebook is not
  nearest-row lookup but **generation**: the idea must be able to unfold,
  through the tied inverse and `<generate>` operators, into a *sequence of
  operations and codes that are in codebooks*, each step ending in a
  projection.

That last requirement is an optimistic one and the spec names it:
**generativity**. The operators must map codes into a space meaningful enough
that a single off-codebook vector regenerates its derivation. It is what the
reconstruction objective trains (compose folds, the tied inverse unfolds), it
is what LTM relies on (2.7), and it is measured, not assumed (§6). A code does
not go stale as `W` learns; an idea stays meaningful only while the current
operators can still unfold it.

**Order and extension.** A code or an idea has an order. At order 0 it is a
point among the zeroth-order concepts. At a higher order it is still **one
vector in its own space**, but what it denotes in zero-order space is a
**region**: the zeroth-order concepts that are its members through the
pyramid's membership edges. The region is fixed by membership, not by
geometry, so it may be **discontinuous**. A single vector is therefore not
limited to a concrete prototype; it can be as abstract as the orders allow,
and so can anything LTM stores (2.7). XOR is the textbook case: two disjoint
corners of zero-order space that become one point only once order is raised.

**Sign.** Order 0 is presence in `[0, 1]` and carries no sign; signed
activation begins at the first rung. Negation therefore exists only at
order ≥ 1, and the negation of a zeroth-order point is a region, so it
raises order (2.6.2).

"A vector in knowing" is a category error: an operator on ideas writes 2.4;
an operator on activation writes 2.2 / 2.3. `quantize` takes an idea back to
the field.

"PCA" is the right family resemblance and the wrong mechanism, so the spec
uses it only in quotes. Projection to a code is nearest-row
quantization: rows are local and non-orthogonal — and above order 0 the
region a row denotes need not even be connected — whereas principal axes are
global and orthogonal. The two are nonetheless closely related — Hebbian
learning extracts principal components (Oja 1982); principal components are
the continuous relaxation of k-means cluster indicators (Ding & He 2004);
semantic categories emerge in deep linear networks as the singular modes of
the input–output correlations, coarse distinctions first (Saxe, McClelland &
Ganguli 2019); and word meaning can be recovered as a low-rank decomposition
of a word-by-episode matrix (Landauer & Dumais 1997). Higher-order symbols
(2.3) are where such a coarse-to-fine ordering lives in this architecture.

### 2.1 Perceptual knowing
The activation of the two meronymic towers: PartSpace (synthesis,
bottom-up) and WholeSpace (analysis, top-down), including the word stream
they present. This is perception and visualisation. Located with
`.where`/`.when`; concepts are not.

### 2.2 Parallel conceptual knowing, zeroth order
A **field**: the activation of the zeroth-order symbols, one value per
concept over the whole continuous conceptual codebook. Parallel: all concepts
are active to some degree at once. Words enter it as (concept row,
activation); priming acts on it. Expectation does not: this field is
presence, which carries no sign, so no negative image can be left in it
(2.6.2).

### 2.3 Parallel conceptual knowing, higher order
A **field** over the higher-order symbols: symbols composed of symbols (the
discretized symbolic codebook; abstraction order ≥ 1). Shares row indices
with 2.2 (one symbol per concept). A higher-order symbol is a single point at
its own order and a **region of order 0**: the sigma-pyramid computes its
activation from its members, and `synthesize_higher_order` mints it from a
recurrent member set, so the region is defined by membership and may be
discontinuous (2.0). *Direction (Alec, 2026-09-21):* each rung becomes
sigma over pi — terms of members together, then classes of any of them
([Architecture](../Architecture.md#decided-in-direction-a-concept-is-sigma-over-pi-alec-2026-09-21)).

### 2.4 Serial thinking
**Codes and ideas in sequence**: the symbolic state held in STM's slots
(`[NP1, VP, NP2]`, depth). Words enter as codes, already projected; the
structural operators take codes and ideas and leave an idea — one
off-codebook vector (2.0). **Reducible to 2.2 and 2.3 in content**, under the
assumption that thoughts are processed serially — every idea was built from
codes and must regenerate them — but distinct in form: operators address it
by position (push, fold, the completed idea) and by value, not by symbol.

### 2.5 Priming
Symbols carry a priming: a spreading activation over the concept store's
edges, an attention formed by connectionist principles, more than
connection weights (it dissipates energy and decays). It lives with the
codebook. Automatic and short-lived; distinct from expectation (2.6), which
is context-driven. Composition reads it as a snapshot; retrieval uses it as a
cue (2.7); nothing reads it to recover a route into the taxonomy. The same
surface has a top-down intent channel (desired / hated: priority lowered
with a floor of zero, never a veto). It is unsigned, so what it can express
is selection — the `non` kind of negation — and it is where an object of
observation is set at the readout (2.6.7).

### 2.6 Expectation

The expected next idea, held in conceptual space as a **negative image**
(Alec, 2026-09-20):

> Prediction is the negation (the affirming negation, or sign-reversal) over
> the conceptual activation, which is left in conceptual space and added to
> the incoming conceptual activation. For a perfect prediction we then
> conceive of nothing.

It is an **idea** in 2.4's form — `[NP1, VP, NP2]` with a presence per role,
what `arma` (or whatever method produces the expectation) emits —
sign-reversed. So it is **reducible to 2.4's form, at order ≥ 1** (2.6.2),
after the point at which `arma` has run; it is never a field in 2.2. It is
how the model builds a **world-model**: the predictor is trained on every
sentence that has a prior context.

This replaces the earlier draft of this section, in which the estimate was
unfolded through `<generate>` and its codes were *raised* in the field. That
was the opposite sign. Raising what is expected makes comprehension lean
toward its own estimate; subtracting it leaves comprehension alone and
changes only what is left over to be conceived (2.6.3).

#### 2.6.1 The rule

Let `ê` be the estimate formed from the recency buffer before the sentence
arrives; `κ ∈ [0, 1]³` the predictor's per-role presence (how strongly each
role is expected to be filled); `g ∈ [0, 1]` the gain (2.6.6); `m ∈ [0, 1]³`
the attention on each role (2.6.7; zero unless something is the object of
observation); and `o` the idea composed from the sentence.

```text
n = −g · (1 − m) ⊙ κ ⊙ ê      the negative image, left before the sentence arrives
c = o + n                      what is conceived, per role, at the seal
o = c − n                      exactly, for as long as the estimate is retained
```

| the role in the sentence | the estimate | conceived `c` | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| filled, as expected | `κ ≈ 1` | `≈ 0` | confirmed: nothing new is conceived |
| filled, otherwise | `κ ≈ 1` | `o − ê` | the departure: what is new, less what it displaced |
| filled | `κ ≈ 0` | `o` | novel: conceived in full |
| empty | `κ ≈ 1` | `−ê` | a **conceived absence**, `not X` (2.6.5) |
| empty | `κ ≈ 0` | `0` | nothing expected, nothing came |
| the object of observation (`m = 1`) | any | `o` | spared: conceived in full, expected or not (2.6.7) |

The subtraction is a change of origin, not a distortion: the input is met
from the expected point instead of from zero, and nothing of it is lost,
because `c − n` restores `o` exactly. **Zero is not absence.** A conceptual
activation of `0` is presence `½` — neutral, nothing new — so confirmed
content is not denied; it is simply not conceived again. Denial is a negative
value, and it arises only where an expectation went unmet.

#### 2.6.2 Negation exists only above order 0

Perceptual space does not carry negation (Alec, 2026-09-20). A percept is a
presence in `[0, 1]`: "a percept is one-sided; its opposite is the complement
`1 − x`, not the signed negation `−x`"
([Spaces](../Spaces.md#percept-geometry-positive-unit-hypercube)). The cube
has a complement but **no additive inverse**, and cancelling by addition needs
one. Zeroth-order concepts are assemblages of percepts, and the order-0 field
(2.2) is the pyramid's snap *presence*; sign first enters at the first rung
(`tanh(W[a|1])`). So a negative image cannot be left at order 0: there is
nowhere to put it. Consequences:

1. **Sensation is never subtracted from.** Perceptual knowing (2.1), the
   order-0 field (2.2), the word stream and the codes of arriving words are
   the same whatever is expected. Expectation has no write access to them
   (§4).
2. **The negative image is an idea, and an idea of order ≥ 1.** The negation
   of a point among the zeroth-order concepts is not another such point: what
   `not cat` denotes in zero-order space is a *region* — everything the
   exclusion leaves — and by 2.0 a vector that denotes a region is
   higher-order. Here the region is fixed by exclusion rather than by
   membership edges. So forming `−ê` raises order even where `ê` is a bare
   zeroth-order concept, and it is added where signed serial form exists: to
   the sealed idea. The predictor's whole-sentence estimate is already that
   kind of object.
3. **Only what is conceptualised can be cancelled.** Content that reaches no
   idea — an unparsed span, a percept with no concept — has no negative image
   and is met in full every time. One habituates only to what one has a model
   of.
4. **The two negations are two faculties**, by the carrier each needs:

   | | unsigned carrier | signed carrier |
   |---|---|---|
   | where | presence `[0, 1]` (2.1, 2.2); the priority surface of 2.5 (floor 0); the `.where` reading scope | conceptual activation `[−1, 1]` at order ≥ 1; ideas in slots |
   | negation available | `non`: withdrawal, the complement, "not taken up" | `not`: sign reversal, "the opposite is affirmed" |
   | faculty | **attention**, as selection | **expectation**, as the negative image |
   | failing to register | the unattended is not conceived at all: no trace and no absence | the expected is cancelled: nothing new is conceived, and the estimate stands for it |
   | omission | nothing — one does not miss what one was not looking for | an uncancelled negative image: the absence is conceived |

#### 2.6.3 Purity

"If we do not predict, we process exactly what is there" (Alec). Under this
rule the same holds when we do predict:

**For a fixed input, a fixed object of observation and fixed identities,
everything composed from the input — order-0 presence, the codes, the
derivation, the raw idea `o` — is independent of `ê` and of `g`.** Expectation
enters once, after composition, as an addition to the sealed idea.

*Identity is the exception, and it has to be* (Alec, 2026-09-21). Which
earlier individual a word is tied to is not in the input and is not a fact
anywhere else: it is an imputation, and the predictor's situation carries it
([two truths §3.5](2026-09-16-two-truths-ideas-and-relations.md#35-object-permanence-a-word-may-translate-to-an-earlier-occurrence-decided-2026-09-21)).
Expectation may say *which* lion; it never alters what is said of it.

* **The predictor's target is honest at every gain.** `o` cannot lean toward
  `ê`, so the residual cannot shrink without the world-model improving. The
  earlier draft's rule — train the predictor only on sentences composed at
  `g = 0`, and suspend applying on a random fraction of sentences — is
  withdrawn; it guarded against a contamination this design does not have.
* **Reconstruction is untouched.** The tied inverse unfolds `o = c − n`, the
  understanding, which is the idea it unfolds today.
* **The one place the code adds an estimate *into* composition** — the
  comprehension-time `_c_prior` staging, scaled by `sentencePrimingScale` —
  has the opposite sign and breaks the invariant. It is deleted, not kept
  behind its flag (§5).

**The same estimate has opposite signs in the two directions.** In speech
production the predicted idea is *what is said*: it seeds `<generate>` with
positive sign, as `arma`'s answer seed does. In comprehension it is *what
need not be conceived*: it enters with negative sign. This is the reafference
principle — the copy of the command is subtracted from what comes back — and
it is one more way in which generation is the dual of comprehension.

#### 2.6.4 Surprise is what is learned

The surprise is the residual

```text
r = o − ê          per role, so r is [NP1, VP, NP2] like the ideas it compares
```

the target minus the estimate — the delta rule. It does not depend on `g` or
`m`: it is a fact about the world-model, not about the stance taken toward
the sentence (`c = r` exactly when `g = 1`, `κ = 1` and `m = 0`). `o` is a
detached target; the gradient runs through the predictor and its live source
ideas (plan §8.4); presence is trained beside it. Being an effect and not a
return value, the surprise goes three places: it trains the predictor; its
normalised magnitude is the row's `surprise`, which the
[forgetting spec](2026-09-16-forgetting.md) uses as retention value; and the
per-role `c` is evidence the thought chooser may read — *where* the sentence
departed from expectation, and in which direction.

#### 2.6.5 Absence

An expected role that stays empty leaves its negative image uncancelled:
`c = −g·κ·ê` there. That is a **conceived absence**, the affirming negation
`not X`, and it is the only way an absence is ever conceived. Nothing in the
input says that the dog did not bark; the sentence is silent, and the silence
is informative only against the expectation of a bark.

* **Compose writes no negation it did not read.** The uncancelled image is
  evidence at the seal, not a row.
* **An absence is concluded, not perceived.** A model that is to hold
  absences declares `not` in `<thought>` (today it is structural-only); the
  chooser may then conclude `not X` from an uncancelled image, and the result
  is written as any thought result is — an inference, never an observation.
* **Only what would have been observed can be missed.** The inference is
  licensed by `κ` (the role was strongly expected) and by scope (the role was
  within what was being read). A weak expectation leaves a weak image and
  licenses nothing. This is also what separates "confirmed" from "never in
  play": both conceive `≈ 0`, and only the retained estimate tells them apart
  — so the estimate is kept (2.6.8).

#### 2.6.6 Gain, and beginner's mind

`g = 0` **is beginner's mind**: nothing is subtracted, every sentence is
conceived in full, and no absence can be conceived. It costs load — nothing
is cancelled, so nothing stands out — and it gives up the absences.
Estimating continues: `ê` and `r` are still computed and the world-model
still learns.

* **The model.** `<sentenceExpectation>false</sentenceExpectation>` turns
  estimating off, and with it the world-model's learning; a gain element sets
  `g` (default `1`); a model whose `<thought>` list omits `arma` cannot form
  an expectation in thought.
* **Every document begins in beginner's mind.** There is no prior context, so
  no estimate, so `n = 0`.
* **The network.** Setting the gain for the next sentence is an act the
  thought chooser may take. It comes after the fixed-gain measurements (§6,
  test 32), and it is never trained on the residual (2.6.9).
* **Learning has a separate knob.** `g` governs what is conceived, and
  composition is pure at any `g`. Whether expectation also shapes what is
  *learned* — where codes settle — is a different variable,
  `conceptualContextExpectationWeight`
  ([plan §8.4](../plans/2026-09-15-next-sentence-as-the-production-objective.md#84-gradient-boundaries-and-learning-evidence)):
  zero learns only from what occurred.

#### 2.6.7 Attention: the exclusion of the non-object

Expectation silences; it is attention that facilitates, and the two are
different faculties (Alec, 2026-09-20):

> The object appears to the mind as the negation of the non-object. So we
> apply expectation to everything but the object of observation, which allows
> it to (for example) be recognized more quickly.

> Opposite of non-pot is a non-affirming negation. So I guess attention does
> not need full prediction in order to focus (which makes sense).

That is *apoha*, exclusion, and the kind of negation decides what it needs.
The exclusion by which an object appears is **non-affirming**: it removes the
non-object and puts nothing in its place. So it needs no estimate of what the
non-object *is* — only which thing is the object.

* **Attention focuses without prediction.** It is the unsigned machinery the
  architecture already has: the reading scope over perceptual knowing (2.1)
  and the intent channel of the priority surface at the competitive readout
  (2.5). Neither reads the estimate. Both work in a wholly novel scene, at
  `g = 0`, and at a document start. What they exclude is *not taken up*: not
  conceived, no trace, and no absence (2.6.2).
* **Where there is also an estimate, it is applied to everything but the
  object.** That is the factor `(1 − m)` in 2.6.1, and it is attention's one
  effect at the seal: the context is cancelled to the extent it was expected,
  and the object of observation is conceived in full, expected or not. With
  no estimate the factor does nothing, and attention has lost nothing.
* **Where `m` comes from.** The open roles of the active question or thought.
  A question is an expectation with a hole in it: its bound roles are the
  context and its open role is the object of observation. Laid over a frame
  that `what` has brought into STM, the bound roles cancel and what remains
  conceived is the filler of the open role. *Answering is subtracting the
  question.* This is what the open-role mechanism of 2.7.3 already computes;
  no second mechanism is added.
* **Why it is faster.** Facilitation is *reduced competition*, not a boosted
  signal. Serial thinking takes one thing at a time; with the non-object
  excluded, and cancelled where it was expected, the object is the only
  content with magnitude and wins without a contest. The 2 × 2 follows:
  unattended and expected is silenced; attended and expected is conceived in
  full; unattended and unexpected survives only as far as the exclusion is
  soft, which is the difference between being captured by a surprise and
  being blind to it; attended and unexpected stands out most of all.
* **Exclusion and selection are the same act at a competitive readout.** A
  readout that depends only on relative score — top-K, arg-max, softmax — is
  unchanged by a shift common to all candidates, so lowering every non-object
  by `δ` selects exactly what raising the object by `δ` selects:
  `softmax(z − δ·1_¬X) = softmax(z + δ·1_X)`. A concept can therefore work by
  exclusion alone, with no positive universal standing behind it.
* **In selection, not in content.** "Selection changes, activations never
  distort"
  ([Architecture](../Architecture.md#symbolic-weights-reconstruction-parse-time-attention-2026-06-30)).
  An exclusion written into activations as a signed value would be an
  affirming negation, and would fill the mind with conceived absences of
  everything unattended.
* **Learn before excluding.** `r` is computed over every role, attended or
  not (2.6.4). A model that measured surprise only where it looked could
  never learn about what it ignores.
* **Attention is an act**, bounded and charged (2.8). The negative image is
  not: it is there whenever there is an estimate.

What is left for later is the row-level form of the second point: letting
the estimate lower the *priority* of expected non-object rows at the
pyramid's top-K, so that an expected object is recognised faster within a
sentence ([FutureWork §9](../FutureWork.md#9-exclusion-at-the-readout)).
Selection at the readout can change what is composed, so that form lies
outside the purity invariant of 2.6.3 and waits on item 2's measurements.

#### 2.6.8 Memory: a pointer and a tag

The store already holds what this design needs
([ExpectationRetention](../ExpectationRetention.md)): for a warm stream the
`estimate` row is written before its `observation` row, the two are linked in
both directions, and `expectation_pair()` derives the residual from the
retained pair with no third, mutable record. That is a **script pointer plus
tag**: the estimate points to what was typical, and the residual tags what
was not.

* **Rows keep `o`.** The observation row holds the understanding, so
  retrieval, ranking and generativity (2.0, 2.7.3) are unchanged; `c` is
  derived while the pair lives.
* **Retention by surprise.** A sentence that was perfectly predicted
  conceives nothing new and carries almost no retention value. It is the
  first to be forgotten, and nothing is lost when it goes: the world-model
  regenerates it. Admission stays broad and selectivity stays in retention
  (2.7.6) — this is admission by surprise, without a gate.
* **What was expected is known through the world-model.** Once such a row is
  gone, a question about it is answered by `arma` from the same context — as
  a prediction, never a fact.
* **Recall as estimate plus residual** (direction: the `human` profile,
  [FutureWork §8](../FutureWork.md#8-recall-as-estimate-plus-residual)). A
  row coarsened to its tag would be recalled by adding the *current* estimate
  back, and would drift toward the schema as the world-model changes:
  schema-consistent false recall.

#### 2.6.9 Desire, lack, and the dark room

A desire is a standing prediction. Held as a negative image it makes the
*absence* of the desired thing perceptible: what is wanted and not there is
conceived as `−ê`, a lack. Valence becomes reference-dependent —
disappointment is an uncancelled image, relief a cancelled threat — and at
`g = 0` no lack can be conceived. The reading is developed in
[Philosophy](../Philosophy.md#expectation-as-a-negative-image-attention-as-exclusion-2026-09-20).

The same sign makes a degenerate optimum: a mind rewarded for conceiving
little would seek inputs it can predict, or stop looking. So:

**Residual credit may train how the estimate is *formed* — the predictor, and
the anticipatory queries of plan §8.10 — because their target `o` is fixed
and pure. It never trains what is *observed or conceived*: not the gain, not
the object of observation, not reading attention.** No objective decreases
with `‖c‖`.

### 2.7 LTM: semantic and episodic memory

The store of knowledge: the three-slot rows specified elsewhere, which also
hold the truths read in from the user. It is **declarative** memory, written
in one shot. (What is learned slowly — the codebook, the operators, the
taxonomy — is not LTM in this sense; see 2.7.5.)

#### 2.7.1 What a row holds, and how several things are held at once

**LTM holds serial form only.** A row holds, per role, **one idea** — the
single off-codebook vector composition produced (`slots[capacity, 3, nDim]`)
— together with the derivation that produced it. Because words arrive as
codes, the row's leaf codes are known when it is written: the row is *already
indexed over serial concepts*, and by generativity the idea can regenerate
them. What is missing today is only that those leaf codes sit in a metadata
sidecar rather than as first-class index terms.

An idea may be of **any order**, so a row is not limited to concrete
prototypes: a single stored vector can denote a discontinuous region of
zero-order space (2.0). This is *semantic* memory, and it is what LTM mostly
stores.

There are two different ways of holding several zero-order concepts at once,
and only the first comes free:

* **As a class.** One higher-order idea denotes many zero-order concepts —
  but as a *type*: any or all of its members, generically.
* **As a conjunction.** These particular things, present together, then.
  This is what an episode is, and LTM has **one way** to store it today:
  **chaining NP / VP into a compound sentence** — `conjunction` folds,
  `NP → S` and `NP → REF(S)` nesting, and rows linked by `refs`, stream and
  timestamp. Chaining **approximates episodic memory**. It has two limits: it
  serializes what was simultaneous, and a longer chain strains generativity
  (2.0). And it stores only what was *said*: concepts that were active but
  never composed into the sentence leave no trace.

**Decided in direction, future work — not in the code** (Alec, 2026-09-20;
[FutureWork §7](../FutureWork.md#7-episodic-memory-a-few-active-indices-beside-each-row)).
That missing trace is exactly the extension episodic memory needs: **room,
next to each three-slot row, for a few indices of the most highly active
percepts or concepts** when the row was written, kept as (row, activation)
pairs — a sparse field, over the perceptual tables as well as the conceptual
ones. Composition commits to one reading, so the alternatives that were
active — a word's other senses, the neighbours that nearly won, the things
present but unsaid — are gone unless they are kept, and interpretation-time
discrimination needs them (the "has" of parthood, possession and tense; the
n-ary META of [FutureWork §5](../FutureWork.md#5-n-ary-meta-and-interpretation-time-discrimination)).
Being indices and not vectors, they are re-read through the current tables
and cost a few numbers beside three idea vectors. They are detail, so under
forgetting they go first.

#### 2.7.2 The second axis: context binding

In the psychological literature an episode is defined by *what–where–when*
and by its source, not by grain alone (§7). A row already carries that axis:
`timestamp`, `origin`, stream and document, and `.where` / `.when`
addresses. The two axes are independent:

| | context-free | bound to where / when / source |
|---|---|---|
| **one idea** (any order) | semantic fact — the bulk of LTM | event gist: "this was read, there, then" |
| **several at once** — a chain today; a few active indices beside the row in future | a compound generic statement | an episodic trace: approximated by the chain, proper with the indices |

An episodic trace proper has both: several things held at once *and* their
binding. Most rows written today are event gists.

#### 2.7.3 Two ways in: what is still active, and what is cued

Thought needs **both** (Alec, 2026-09-20):

* **The recency buffer** — what is still active. It is not an LTM view. It is
  STM itself: the live constituents of the current sentence
  (`<stmCapacity>`, 8) and the discourse chain of the last few completed
  ideas (the window `arma` reads, 8). This is the span of immediate memory.
  It is also where an individual persists: a word may be tied, by reference,
  to an earlier noun or sentence held here
  ([two truths §3.5](2026-09-16-two-truths-ideas-and-relations.md#35-object-permanence-a-word-may-translate-to-an-earlier-occurrence-decided-2026-09-21)).
  *Direction (Alec, 2026-09-21):* the predictor may hold the buffer as a
  **situation** — a small set of anchors into LTM, references to the rows
  and individuals in play, rather than copies of their vectors. An anchored
  individual then stays in play for as long as the situation holds it, not
  only for the last few sentences.
* **Cued retrieval** from LTM: access to all of LTM is prohibitive, and
  retrieval must not be a shot in the dark, so **access to LTM means access
  to the several frames that a `what()` has brought into STM**, by cue, index
  and rank as follows.

There is **no "most recent LTM rows" view**. Recency is the buffer's job; LTM
is reached by cue only. (The thought chooser today reads the last N rows of
the store; that is what this replaces.)

* **Cues** the mind already holds: the active idea's bound roles; explicit
  `where` / `when` scope arguments on the `what`; the references the idea
  already carries; recency; **contiguity** (rows adjacent, in the same stream
  or document, to a row already in STM); and **priming** (2.5).
* **Indices**: the inverted index *code → LTM rows whose derivation contains
  it, per role*. The terms are the leaf codes recorded at write time; a row
  with no derivation (an estimate, a thought result) gets its terms by
  unfolding its idea. The index is exact for the codes it holds and is
  maintained by the one writer at the seal and rebuilt on compaction. A row
  that is near in meaning but shares no code with the cue is reached only
  because priming's spread adds related codes to the cue — which is the job
  priming does in retrieval. Beside it: the `.where` / `.when` endpoint-sum
  brackets (containment) and the `refs` column.
* **Rank**: masked similarity on the roles the question binds — selective
  attention to the dimensions the question specifies. No separate
  principal-component basis: the codebook is already the interpretable
  quantization of that space.
* **The wh-words are open roles over LTM rows**, the same open-role mechanism
  `part` already has: *who* / *what* open NP1 / NP2 (or the VP) and walk the
  postings of the bound roles; *where* / *when* open the address and walk the
  brackets; *why* opens the antecedent and walks `implies` rows backward over
  `refs`; *how* opens the operator; *whether* opens nothing and runs `exist`
  / `true` over the same candidates. `what(Q, where?, when?)` with Q's open
  role is the wh-question; `lookup`, `parts` / `wholes` and `what` are one
  retrieval mechanism.
* **Bounds and gradient**: K candidates, top-k ranked, each charged to the
  budget (2.8). Retrieval is hard and detached; what is learned is which
  `what` to issue, by policy credit.

`arma` reads the recency buffer, not LTM: for predicting the next idea the
drifting recent context is the right cue.

#### 2.7.4 Ways of coming back

* **Semantic retrieval** places a frame — one idea — in STM (2.4).
* **A chained episode comes back the way it went in**: as a sequence of
  frames, unfolded one at a time.
* **Once rows carry their active indices** (FutureWork §7), episodic
  retrieval may also *reinstate* them: write the stored activations back into parallel knowing
  (2.2 / 2.3), bounded and decayed, so that the remembered state is partly
  re-entered rather than merely described.
* **Familiarity without retrieval**: the summed match of a cue against the
  postings is a graded, parallel signal available without bringing any row
  into STM. It is the natural reading of `exist`'s support.

#### 2.7.5 Becoming semantic

Two slow processes act on the fast store:

* **Semanticization** (decided, Alec 2026-09-20). Dropping detail under
  forgetting pressure is the precursor to deleting a row, and it is gradual:
  wording first, then subordinate clauses and chain links, the main idea
  last. It is specified in the
  [forgetting spec §4a](2026-09-16-forgetting.md#4a-detail-before-rows-alec-2026-09-20).
  It rests on generativity (2.0): once a row's derivation is gone, its fused
  point alone must regenerate what the memory can still say, and the row is
  re-indexed by exactly those codes.
* **Consolidation.** The slowly learned structure — codebook, operators,
  taxonomy — learns from replay of LTM rows. The forgetting spec's utility
  term, one minus deducibility, is exactly the consolidation criterion: once
  a row is deducible from the slow structure and other rows, its fast trace
  may go. Surprising, schema-inconsistent rows stay longest.

#### 2.7.6 STM and LTM

STM is not folded into LTM's storage, and it need not be. They differ in
form and in life: STM is position-addressed, live to the gradient, displaced
by what arrives next; LTM is content-addressed, detached, and forgotten by
value. But they are one chain in use: a row retrieved by `what` lives in STM
while it is active — working memory as the activated part of long-term
memory — and **the seal is the one writer** from STM to LTM.

**Entering LTM has its own criterion, and it is not time spent in the
buffer.** A composed idea enters when it seals: what was processed to a
meaning is stored; what was merely held is not. Whether it *stays* is
decided by value, and the surprise it carried is part of that value. So there
is broad encoding and selective retention, no separate admission gate, and
no need for a recent-LTM view.

### 2.8 Budget
The remaining work and the closure pressure. The thinking system must be
able to **read** it, in order to know it is about to be cut off and make a
final answer; every thought operation **charges** it. It is a resource, not
content: it is never a learned semantic feature of an idea.

### 2.9 Meronymic access
Access to the **residual of the parthood operator**, as `part` is already
defined in the operators: the vector form `part(x, y) = x · (y / ‖y‖)`
(with `whole(x, y) = (1 − x) · (y / ‖y‖)` and `copart(x, y) = y − x`). The
operator **leaves a residual in conceptual space** — a graded idea **vector**,
hence a serial item in 2.4, not a change to the parallel field —
rather than a symbolic or binary 1/0. Any truth reading is derived from that
residual (the scalar form, clipped cosine in [0, 1]); the effect itself is the
vector. Order 0; computed in activation space; consults no store.

### 2.10 Taxonomic access
Needed for symbols composed of symbols. A higher-order relation cannot be
settled by a definition in conceptual space alone: `cat ⊑ animal` is not a
fact about activation geometry. It is settled by the taxonomy, and what it
emits is a **symbolic value, not a meronymic one**: a graded true or false in
2.3, possible for every question in virtue of the fact that all symbols
participate in the taxonomy. Bounded traversal; charged.

The contrast is the point: **meronymic access leaves a conceptual residual;
taxonomic access emits a symbolic value.**

## 5. Consequences for the code

1. The executor descriptors' `read_scope` strings become members of this
   list; a descriptor may name only subsystems its grammar's row permits.
2. `result_kind` becomes a write target: truth → support on the completed
   idea (2.4); set → members seeded into knowing (2.2/2.3); code → 2.3;
   prediction → 2.6, the staged estimate (an idea, 2.6.1); subgoal → frames
   scheduled into 2.4.
3. `arma`'s estimate, already staged at the preceding boundary, becomes the
   negative image for the next seal (item 2 of the todo): sign-reversed,
   scaled by gain, presence and the object mask, added to the completed
   idea per role (2.6.1). It is never added to compose's inputs, never
   unfolded into codes, never parked in a slot. The comprehension-time
   additive prior — `_c_prior` staged from `_consume_intersentence_seed`,
   scaled by `sentencePrimingScale` — has the opposite sign and
   contaminates `o`; it is deleted, not kept behind `<prediction>`. If
   `generate_sentence` is still reachable, its prior moves to the
   `<generate>` seed, where the positive sign is right (2.6.3).
4. `what` is the single operator that brings LTM frames into STM; LTM reads
   by other operators go through those frames or a bounded reader.
5. `part`'s effect is its vector-form residual in conceptual space (2.9);
   the scalar truth reading is derived. Taxonomic questions emit a symbolic
   value (2.10).
6. Gradient: reads of 2.1–2.6 inside compose/generate are live; every effect
   written by a thought operator, every LTM frame and every reinstated field
   is detached (plan §8.4, superseding decision).
7. The chooser's context is the recency buffer (live STM slots and the
   discourse chain) plus cued LTM frames (2.7.3). Its present "last N rows of
   the store" source is deleted, not kept beside the new one; the code
   postings index is added to the LTM writer and rebuilt on compaction.
8. LTM rows keep their idea vectors (`slots[capacity, 3, nDim]`). What they
   gain is first-class index terms: the leaf codes of each role's derivation
   as tensor columns rather than sidecar metadata, remapped on codebook
   compaction exactly as `refs` are on store compaction. The few active
   indices beside each row are future work (FutureWork §7), not part of this
   item.
9. A chained episode is retrieved as its sequence of frames. Reinstatement
   into 2.2 / 2.3 arrives with the active indices of FutureWork §7.
10. Forgetting drops detail before rows, gradually
    ([forgetting spec §4a](2026-09-16-forgetting.md#4a-detail-before-rows-alec-2026-09-20)).
11. Surprise is first-class: the per-role residual `r = o − ê` is computed
    wherever a prior context exists, over every role, independent of gain
    and of attention; its magnitude is the row's `surprise`.
12. The gain is a `model.xml` element, default `1`, with `g = 0` as
    beginner's mind. No suspension schedule and no zero-gain training
    subset: composition is pure at every gain, so the predictor trains on
    every sentence that has a prior context.
13. The seal computes the conceived remainder `c` per role and exposes it to
    the chooser. Compose writes no negation it did not read; an absence is
    concluded only by a thought operator, and only in a model that declares
    `not` in `<thought>` (2.6.5).
14. The object mask `m` is the open roles of the active question or
    thought, the same open-role mechanism `what` and `part` use (2.6.7).
    Attention itself never reads the estimate: the reading scope and the
    priority surface stay as they are. Letting the estimate lower row
    priority at the readout is not part of this item.
15. The retained estimate / observation pair is the pointer and tag
    (2.6.8). No third record, and the observation row keeps `o`.
16. No gradient and no policy credit reaches the gain, the object mask or
    reading attention from `‖r‖` or `‖c‖` (2.6.9).

Ownership, so that each work item has a definite exit: tests 1, 2, 4–13,

1. Each operator's declared R/W set is a subset of its grammar's row in §4;
   configuration fails otherwise.
2. A structural face given a context with LTM or taxonomy capability refuses
   to run (capability views are absent, not merely unused).
3. **Purity.** For a fixed input and fixed identity choices, the order-0
   field, the codes, the derivation and the composed idea `o` are
   bit-identical with and without a staged estimate, and at `g = 0` and
   `g = 1`. No code path adds an estimate to compose's inputs: the `_c_prior`
   staging is gone.
4. `part(a, b)` leaves its vector-form residual in conceptual space, equal to
   `Ops.part`, with no store read and no budget charge on the compose face;
   the scalar reading is derived from it.
5. A higher-order parthood question with no direct edge emits a graded
   symbolic value from bounded taxonomy traversal, never a lookup miss and
   never a conceptual residual.
6. With the budget one unit from cutoff, the chooser's input differs from the
   same state with ample budget, and the episode concludes.
7. LTM rows not brought into STM by a `what` are unreadable by `equal`,
   `quantize` and the structural faces.
8. A `what` with one open role retrieves, from a store where the answer is
   NOT among the most recent N rows, the row whose bound roles match — via
   the symbol postings, within K candidates and the budget.
9. A primed symbol surfaces a row that the question's bound roles alone do
   not select; with priming cleared the same `what` does not retrieve it.
10. `where` / `when` scope on a `what` excludes content-matching rows outside
    the bracket.
11. **Codes and ideas.** A word's code is a row of the codebook and `π` is
    idempotent on it; the idea composed from an adjective and a noun, or by
    a lift, is not a row of any codebook.
12. **Generativity.** A stored idea unfolds, through the current operators,
    into its recorded derivation — the same codes and the same operations.
    Report the rate at which this holds as a function of composition depth
    and of further training since the row was written; a falling rate is the
    early warning that the space has stopped being meaningful.
13. **Index.** Every row whose derivation contains a cue code is returned by
    the postings; a row sharing no code with the cue is returned only when
    priming's spread supplies a related code.
14. **Remember versus know** (with FutureWork §7, not before).
    Retrieving a row with a field raises, in parallel knowing, at least two
    of its stored concepts; after semanticization it reinstates nothing.
    Both place the same idea in STM.
15. **Familiarity.** `exist`'s support rises with the number of matching
    rows even when no row is brought into STM; a `what` brings exactly the
    best-matching row.
16. **Fan.** Candidates examined, and budget charged, grow with the number of
    rows sharing the cue symbol.
17. **Contiguity.** After a row is retrieved, its neighbours in the same
    stream rank above equally similar rows from other streams.
18. **Semanticization** is tested in the forgetting spec (its tests 11–14).
19. **Identity.** `c − n = o` exactly, per role, recomputed from the retained
    estimate / observation pair at any gain; no slot is written by the
    estimate, no byte or word form is predicted, and there is no third,
    mutable delta record.
20. **Abstraction.** A higher-order idea stored in LTM and retrieved raises,
    through the pyramid, zero-order members that are not neighbours of one
    another — a discontinuous region — and nothing between them.
21. **Chained episode.** Several simultaneous concepts stored as a compound
    sentence are recovered as its constituents by unfolding; the recovery
    rate is reported against chain length (with test 12).

22. **Recency and cue.** The chooser's context holds the live STM slots, the
    discourse chain, and LTM rows only where a `what` retrieved them; no row
    enters it merely for having been written recently.
23. **Surprise.** `r = o − ê` per role; the scalar written to the row's
    `surprise` equals the normalised residual; a sentence departing from its
    estimate in one role has its residual concentrated in that role; none of
    it changes with `g` or with the object mask.
24. **Beginner's mind.** At `g = 0`, `c = o` for every role while `ê` and `r`
    are still computed and the predictor still trains; with
    `<sentenceExpectation>` false neither is computed. At a document start
    `c = o` whatever the gain.
25. **Perfect and partial prediction.** With `ê = o` and `κ = 1`, `c = 0`. A
    sentence departing in one role has `c` concentrated in that role and
    `≈ 0` elsewhere. A role the estimate did not expect (`κ ≈ 0`) is
    conceived in full.
26. **Conceived absence.** A role predicted with high `κ` and left empty
    yields `c = −g·κ·ê` there; with low `κ` it yields `≈ 0`. Compose writes
    no negation. With `not` declared in `<thought>` the chooser can conclude
    `not X` from it, and the result is written as a thought result, never as
    an observation; without the declaration the image is evidence only.
27. **Attention spares the object.** The 2 × 2 at the role level: an expected
    role conceives `≈ 0` unattended and `o` when it is the open role of the
    active question; an unexpected role conceives `o` either way. Laid over a
    retrieved frame, a question's bound roles cancel and the filler of its
    open role is what remains.
28. **Order 0 is untouched, and attention does not predict.** Staging the
    estimate and sealing write nothing to 2.1 or 2.2; no presence carrier
    ever holds a value outside `[0, 1]`; the reading scope and the priority
    surface are identical with and without a staged estimate.
29. **Honest at every gain.** For one batch, the predictor's loss and its
    gradients are identical at `g = 0` and `g = 1`.
30. **No dark room.** Neither the gain, nor the object mask, nor reading
    attention receives gradient or policy credit from `‖r‖` or `‖c‖`.
31. **Retention by surprise.** In a forgetting pass, among observation rows
    equal in trust and luminosity the least surprising goes first; after it
    is gone, `arma` from the same context regenerates it within tolerance.
    Report the rate.
32. **Does the negative image help?** On held-out text, with `c` against `o`
    as the chooser's evidence: thought steps and work to the same answer
    error. And the semantic check: `‖c‖` for a related but unexpected
    continuation is below `‖c‖` for an unrelated one.

### 7.1 Where the literature complicates the claim

1. **Two senses of "semantic".** For Tulving it is a kind of *content*
   (context-free general knowledge). For complementary-learning-systems
   theory it is a kind of *learning system*: slowly acquired structure in
   neocortical weights, as against fast one-shot storage in the hippocampus.
   By the second criterion the whole row store — semantic rows included — is
   the fast system, and the slow semantic system is the codebook, operators
   and taxonomy. This spec uses "semantic" in the content sense for LTM rows
   and names the slow structure separately (2.7.5); the two must not be
   conflated when reading the literature against the code.
2. **One store or two.** Whether episodic and semantic memory are separate
   systems is contested (McKoon, Ratcliff & Dell 1986). Instance and
   multiple-trace theories need no semantic store at all. This architecture
   is one store at two grains, closer to those theories and to tensor-memory
   models than to a strict two-store account, while keeping an explicit
   semantic index for economy (Collins & Quillian 1969).
3. **Grain is not the whole of "episodic".** The defining properties in the
   literature are what–where–when binding and re-experiencing; stored detail
   is characteristic but not defining. Hence 2.7.2: an episodic trace proper
   has several things held at once *and* their binding.
4. **"PCA".** Nearest-prototype quantization is not principal component
   analysis (2.0). The citations establish kinship, not identity.
5. **Parthood is several relations.** At least six kinds of meronymy are
   distinguished, and transitivity holds only within a kind (Winston, Chaffin
   & Herrmann 1987). With parthood the sole primitive
   ([two truths](2026-09-16-two-truths-ideas-and-relations.md)), the kinds
   must separate at interpretation time, not in the operator.
6. **Graded membership does not compose by min.** Fuzzy-set conjunction fails
   for combined concepts such as "pet fish" (Osherson & Smith 1981).
   `conjunction` and `intersection` over graded activations should not be
   assumed to model concept combination.
7. **What is predicted.** Prediction of an expected word's *semantic
   features* is robust; pre-activation of its *form* failed a large
   pre-registered replication (DeLong, Urbach & Kutas 2005; Nieuwland et al.
   2018). That is the pattern 2.6.2 requires: the estimate is an idea, it
   touches nothing at order 0, and it claims nothing about word form.
8. **Commitment is lossy in a specific way.** A single reading — a prototype,
   a projection, or one composed idea — is a centre of tendency: it conflates
   the senses of a word and loses the infrequent ones, which instance stores
   retain (Jamieson et al. 2018). An LTM holding only ideas erases the
   evidence needed to discriminate senses at interpretation time; this is
   the functional argument for the active indices of FutureWork §7.
9. **Convexity.** Conceptual-spaces theory requires natural concepts to be
   *convex* regions (Gärdenfors 2000). This architecture does not: a
   higher-order vector's region is fixed by membership and may be
   disconnected. The human evidence is on the architecture's side —
   categories that are not linearly separable are learned without a reliable
   cost — so the citation of conceptual spaces in the table above is for its
   three levels, not for convexity.
10. **One vector has a capacity.** A fixed-width vector that must regenerate
   a structure decodes less reliably as the structure deepens or widens
   (Plate 1995; the same limit besets recursive auto-association, Pollack
   1990). "Optimistically" is the right word for generativity. The
   architecture already hedges: a relation stays three slots rather than
   fusing to one point
   ([two truths](2026-09-16-two-truths-ideas-and-relations.md)), every row
   keeps its derivation, and test 12 watches the decay.
11. **"Echoic" and the span are different things.** Echoic memory is the
    auditory sensory trace, large and gone in a few seconds (Darwin, Turvey &
    Crowder 1972). The 7 ± 2 is the span of verbal short-term memory (Miller
    1956), nearer four chunks when rehearsal and chunking are prevented
    (Cowan 2001). In this architecture the sensory-trace analogue is the word
    stream in perceptual knowing; the span is STM and the discourse chain,
    both 8 today. Since each item in the discourse chain is a whole idea, 4 is
    the value to try if 8 shows interference.
12. **Weak priors are not free.** Turning expectation down means nothing is
    cancelled, so nothing stands out; perception with under-weighted priors
    is more veridical and more overwhelming (Pellicano & Burr 2012).
    Beginner's mind is a setting to be able to reach, not a default to
    prefer.
13. **Two processes.** The parallel/serial, associative/rule-based division is
   a useful description and a contested theory (Keren & Schul 2009). Nothing
   here depends on its being two *systems* rather than two forms of one
   state, which is in fact what 2.0 asserts.
14. **Silencing and sharpening.** Expectation both reduces sensory responses
    and sharpens the representation of what was expected (Kok, Jehee & de
    Lange 2012; de Lange, Heilbron & Kok 2018; Press, Kok & Yon 2020). This
    design gives the silencing to expectation and the facilitation to
    attention. It has no sharpening of an expected representation itself,
    and the literature has not settled whether one is needed.
15. **Subtraction and division.** Cortical gain control is largely divisive
    normalisation (Carandini & Heeger 2012); the clean subtractive negative
    image is a cerebellum-like circuit (Bell, Han & Sawtell 2008). Both
    forms are present here: a subtractive image in content, a multiplicative
    priority at the readout.
16. **Beginner's mind and habituation.** The report that Zen practitioners
    do not habituate to a repeated click (Kasamatsu & Hirai 1966) was not
    replicated (Becker & Shapiro 1981); later work finds habituation of the
    startle reflex reduced with intensive practice (Antonova, Chadwick &
    Kumari 2015). `g = 0` is a formal reading of the idea, not a claim
    about what meditators do.
17. **Exclusion.** The reading of *apoha*, of the two negations and of
    non-observation in 2.6 is the project's working usage, recorded with its
    scholarship hedge in
    [Philosophy](../Philosophy.md#expectation-as-a-negative-image-attention-as-exclusion-2026-09-20).
