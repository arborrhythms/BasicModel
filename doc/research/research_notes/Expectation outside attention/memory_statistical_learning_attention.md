# Attention and expectation in memory: statistical learning of unattended items, memory-guided attention, internal vs external attention, and the focus of attention in working memory

Verification note. Every citation below was checked against Crossref and/or Europe PMC metadata (authors, year, journal, volume, pages, DOI) on 2026-10-09. Quantitative details come from abstracts or open-access full texts (PMC/Europe PMC/bioRxiv) where I could reach them; where I could only reach the abstract, the entry says so. Two items are preprints (not peer reviewed) and are labelled as such. Nothing below is cited from memory alone except where explicitly flagged "content not re-read here".

Reading key for the design questions: Q(1) = are regularities/predictions formed for content outside attention; Q(2) = does memory supply located (where/when) or unlocated (what) expectation to attention; Q(3) = does internal attention to memory use the same selection mechanism as external attention.

## Key question 1 — Does visual statistical learning (VSL) require attention? Are unattended regularities learned?

### Takeaway
The field's current position (2024–2025) is that *some* attention to the carriers of a regularity is necessary for acquisition, but it need not be top-down or task-relevant — exogenous/bottom-up selection suffices; when both endogenous and exogenous attention to the regular items are prevented, learning is at chance with Bayesian support for the null. Expression (use) of a learned regularity is also attention-gated: expectation suppression in visual cortex and contextual-cueing benefits vanish for unattended content, and the once-popular "latent learning of ignored context" result has failed high-powered replication.

### Cited Findings
- Classic gating result (primary, JEP:General 134(4):552–564, 2005). Two interleaved streams of shapes in different colours, each with its own triplet structure; participants performed a cover task on one colour. Abstract: "(a) the input to visual statistical learning is gated by selective attention, (b) visual statistical learning is nevertheless an implicit process because it operates during a cover task and without awareness of the underlying statistical patterns, and (c) visual statistical learning constructs abstracted representations that are then invariant to changes in extraneous surface features ... visual statistical learning both is and is not automatic: It requires attention to select the relevant population of stimuli, but the resulting learning then occurs without intent or awareness." — [Turk-Browne, Jungé & Scholl 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.134.4.552). Later papers describe the pattern as "learning of temporal relationships that occurred in the attended stream, but not those present in the unattended stream" — [Musz, Weber & Thompson-Schill 2015](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-014-0757-5).
- Grouping extends attention's gate (primary, Psychological Science 15(7):460–466). When observers attended both shape locations, learning was unaffected by whether the two shapes were connected; when attending only one location, "statistical learning was observed only when the shapes were connected." Authors: VSL "is not just a passive process. It can be modulated by both attention and connectedness." — [Baker, Olson & Behrmann 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2004.00702.x).
- Contextual cueing is gated by attended colour (primary, QJEP-A 54(4):1105–1124). Search through attended-colour and ignored-colour items: repeating the attended-colour configuration paired with the target produced cueing (Exps 1, 3, 4); "repeating and pairing the configuration of the ignored items with the target location resulted in no contextual cueing (Experiments 2 and 4). We conclude that implicit learning is robust only when relevant, predictive information is selectively attended." — [Jiang & Chun 2001](https://doi.org/10.1080/713756001).
- The "latent learning" claim (primary, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 12(1):100–106). White target among black and white distractors: repeating the ignored (black) set did not speed search, but "during the transfer test, when the previously ignored set now was attended, it immediately facilitated performance ... the expression of visual implicit learning depends on attention but that latent learning of repeated information does not." — [Jiang & Leung 2005](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196353).
- Failed replication of latent learning (primary, PB&R 27(3):512–519). Same design with a larger sample: no latent learning without selective attention; selective attention equally necessary for acquisition and for expression — [Vadillo, Giménez-Fernández, Aivar & Cubillas 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-020-01722-x). A 2024 PsyArXiv preprint (NOT peer reviewed) by the same group reports "four high-powered preregistered experiments. None of them supported the hypothesis that latent learning can occur without selective attention" — [Vadillo, Aivar, Giménez-Fernández & Cubillas 2024, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/apnux).
- Counter-evidence: unattended stream learned (primary, Attention, Perception & Psychophysics 77(1):78–96). Four experiments attempting to replicate Turk-Browne et al. 2005: "In line with prior work, we observed learning for the attended stimulus set. However, unlike in previous reports, we also observed learning for the unattended stimulus set ... observers could extract temporal regularities for both sets. Our efforts to experimentally decrease this effect by changing the cover task (Exp. 1) or the complexity of the statistical regularities (Exp. 3) were unsuccessful." — [Musz, Weber & Thompson-Schill 2015](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-014-0757-5).
- Resolution with Bayesian nulls (primary, PB&R 32(3):1240–1253, online 2024). Exp 1 (N = 121): a nontarget shape appeared 50% of the time at one location during serial feature search; participants were faster when it was there (t(120) = 3.15, p = .002, d = 0.287) and, when it later became the target, slower at that location (t(120) = 2.72, p = .008, d = 0.247) — learning despite the item being task-irrelevant. Exp 2 (N = 121): colour-subset search such that the regular nontarget never shared the target colour → no learning, BF01 = 9.9. Exp 3 (N = 85): 100%-valid exogenous cue away from the regularity → no learning, BF01 = 6.05. Conclusion: "Some degree of attention to a regularity is needed for visual statistical learning to occur ... When an experiment properly controls for the various forms of attention (exogenous and endogenous), ensuring no attention is directed to visual stimuli with a regularity, then learning does not occur." Exp 1's learning is attributed to transient attention via shared colour — [Duncan, van Moorselaar & Theeuwes 2025](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-024-02605-1).
- Learning without top-down attention (primary, Cortex 131:54–65). A colour singleton appeared mostly at one location during an unrelated task "in which there are no targets nor distractors"; on switching to search, participants showed suppression of that location. "We conclude that visual statistical learning can occur in the absence of top-down attention." — [Duncan & Theeuwes 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2020.07.006).
- Learned distractor-location suppression (primary, JEP:HPP 44(1):13–17). Additional-singleton task with the colour distractor more often at one location: "For the high-probability location, both the amount of attentional capture by distractors and the efficiency of selecting the target were reduced. There was a spatial gradient of suppression ... spatially statistical regularities that are unknown to the observer can influence attention such that locations that have a high probability of containing a distractor are suppressed." — [Wang & Theeuwes 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000472).
- Both target- and distractor-location statistics alter the priority map (primary, Cortex 102:67–95; title verified, full text not read): "Altering spatial priority maps via statistical learning of target selection and distractor filtering" — [Ferrante, Patacca, Di Caro, Della Libera, Santandrea & Chelazzi 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2017.09.027).
- Review position (TiCS 26(10):860–872): "through VSL, attentional priority settings are optimally adjusted to regularities in the environment, without intention and without conscious awareness." — [Theeuwes, Bogaerts & van Moorselaar 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.06.001). Related framework: selection history is integrated with goals and salience in one priority map — [Awh, Belopolsky & Theeuwes 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2012.06.010).
- VSL and top-down attention are additive and independent (primary, AP&P 82(8):3895–3906). Search while holding a location in spatial WM: faster when the target matched the WM location (top-down), and "Independent of this top-down effect, we found a clear effect of VSL as responses were even faster when target (Experiment 1) or the distractor (Experiment 2) was presented at a more likely location." — [Gao & Theeuwes 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-020-02115-x).
- Learned suppression is resilient to WM load: a preprint (NOT peer reviewed) reports suppression "highly resilient even to strong manipulations of working memory load" — [Vicente-Conesa, Castillejo & Vadillo 2023, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/gbwd6).
- Attention is drawn to regularities (primary, Psychological Science 24(5):667–677). Four shape streams, one structured (triplets), irrelevant to the search task: targets were found faster at the structured location, t(24) = 2.31, p = .03, d = 0.46; only 1 of 7 participants who noticed regularities identified the location (chance 25%). Exps 2–3: capture by singletons in the structured colour/dimension was larger (singleton type × colour interaction F(1,19) = 8.34, p = .009, ηp² = .30); abstract: "Attentional capture doubled in magnitude." — [Zhao, Al-Aidroos & Turk-Browne 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612460407); full text at [PMC3734864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3734864/).
- Task goals strongly modulate VSL even when stimuli are attended (bioRxiv preprint, NOT peer reviewed; N = 774, 120 per condition, pre-registered). 2AFC familiarity (chance 50%): Attend Jiggle 50.2% (95% CI 48.1–52.2, n.s.), 1-back 54.0%, Attend Stimuli 57.1%, Attend Relations 62.3%, 2-back 66.9%, Attend Triplets 66.9%, Feedback Training 81.3%; conditions differ (Kruskal–Wallis H = 112.4, p < .001, η² = 0.12) with a monotone trend (Mann–Kendall τ = 1.0, p = .027). Participants who could not re-create any triplet scored 49–55% on 2AFC. "selective attention to the stimuli is necessary, but not sufficient for robust learning." — [Himberger, Finn & Honey 2022, preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.07.04.498716). Crossref shows no journal version as of 2026-10-09.
- Expression of learned expectation in sensory cortex requires attention (primary, eLife 8:e47869). Object pairs learned, then fMRI: expectation suppression present when objects attended (V1 t(33) = 3.573, p = .001, dz = 0.613; LOC dz = 0.662; TOFC dz = 0.880) and absent when attention was on characters at fixation (V1 dz = −0.037; LOC dz = −0.143; TOFC dz = 0.012); expectation × attention interaction V1 F(1,33) = 7.706, p = .009; LOC F = 12.580, p = .001; TOFC F = 16.955, p = 2.4e-4. — [Richter & de Lange 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.47869); full text [PMC6731093](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731093/).
- Caveats on expectation suppression (reviews). Four confounds (surprise, attention, repetition/adaptation, novelty); genuine expectation suppression found "within a specific subset of statistical learning designs that involved weeks of sequence learning", inconsistent otherwise — [Feuerriegel, Vogels & Kovács 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.04.002). Expectation suppression "could be explained by a reduced attention effect ... reduced saliency-driven allocation of attention" — [Alink & Blank 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117824). Attention (relevance) and expectation (likelihood) are conceptually distinct influences — [Summerfield & Egner 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2009.06.003).
- Awareness controversy. Meta-analysis of 73 contextual-cueing studies (181 awareness analyses): awareness-test effect dz = 0.31 (95% CI 0.24–0.37), "showing that participants' learning in these experiments was conscious"; awareness tests "typically insensitive and underpowered" — [Vadillo, Konstantinidis & Shanks 2016](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0892-6). Three high-powered experiments show "clear evidence of explicit knowledge about which area of the display was more likely to contain the singleton distractor" — [Vicente-Conesa, Giménez-Fernández, Luque & Vadillo 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-022-02608-x).
- Implicit anticipation after VSL (primary fMRI, J Neurosci 30(33):11177–11187). Sixteen observers, unrelated cover task, unaware; "the right anterior hippocampus showed greater hemodynamic responses to predictive stimuli"; hippocampal anticipation correlated with processing of predicted stimuli in occipital/parietal cortex; "predictive potentiation of category-selective ventral visual cortex." — [Turk-Browne, Scholl, Johnson & Chun 2010](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0858-10.2010).
- Attention gates what the hippocampus encodes. Attention "induces representational stability in the human hippocampus" (greater pattern similarity within attentional state in all subfields; CA2/CA3/DG stability correlated with behaviour) — [Aly & Turk-Browne 2016, Cerebral Cortex 26(2):783–796](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhv041); and later memory for task-relevant information was better when "the hippocampus was more likely to have been in the correct attentional state during encoding", an effect "specific to the hippocampus" — [Aly & Turk-Browne 2016, PNAS 113(4):E420–E429](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1518931113).

### Inferences
- For design question Q(1): on current evidence, a regularity is acquired only for content that has been *selected* by some form of attention — but "selected" includes transient bottom-up capture and incidental selection by shared features, not only task-set attention. Content that no mask ever admits (Duncan 2025 Exps 2–3; Jiang & Chun 2001; Vadillo 2020) shows no measurable learning. Translated to the architecture: regularities should be learned over whatever the seven regions admit (including brief, exogenous admissions), not over the full unmasked field; an "expectation outside attention" would have no empirical warrant as a *learning* signal.
- Expression is also gated: a learned prediction manifests as surprise/attenuation only inside attended regions (Richter & de Lange 2019; Jiang & Leung 2005 as replicated by Vadillo 2020). So "predicted image subtracted from what arrives" is empirically supported only within the mask.
- The exception is the mask itself: learned location statistics (target-probability facilitation, distractor-location suppression) act on the priority map *before* content arrives and independently of the current task set (Wang & Theeuwes 2018; Duncan & Theeuwes 2020; Gao & Theeuwes 2020; Theeuwes 2022). That is a located prior feeding the mask — close to the design's "priming feeds the mask", except that it is located (where) rather than diffuse.
- Zhao 2013 adds the converse: regularities attract attention, so a system that learns only inside its mask will, over time, move its mask toward structure — a self-reinforcing loop the authors note explicitly ("prioritization may in turn promote further statistical learning").
- The "implicit/unaware" label is contested (Vadillo 2016; Vicente-Conesa 2023); the gating-by-attention conclusion is independent of that dispute.

### Gaps
- Exact percent-correct values for attended vs unattended triplets in Turk-Browne et al. 2005 could not be retrieved (Yale PDF servers return redirect loops); only the qualitative pattern is verified.
- Full text of Ferrante et al. 2018 and of Theeuwes et al. 2022 were not accessible; details beyond title/abstract are not reported.
- No study found that measures prediction/surprise signals for *never-attended* content with a sensitive neural measure other than Richter & de Lange 2019 (fMRI, object pairs); auditory/linguistic analogues were outside this assignment's scope.

## Key question 2 — Contextual cueing and memory-guided attention: how memory delivers located (where/when) expectations

### Takeaway
Memory of many kinds — single-trial priming, within-task regularities, episodic scene memory, semantic knowledge, reward history — supplies attention with expectations that are typically located (a target position in a configuration, a likely region, a point in time) and are implemented as preparatory, spatiotopic biases in visual cortex organized by hippocampal and frontoparietal circuits; reviews now frame memory as operating over a hierarchy of timescales and via "direct" and "indirect" routes into the attention system.

### Cited Findings
- Contextual cueing (primary, Cognitive Psychology 36(1):28–71). Half the search configurations repeated across blocks with consistent target locations; "Targets appearing in learned configurations were detected more quickly ... This benefit was obtained despite chance performance for recognizing the configurations ... implicit learning and memory of visual context can guide spatial attention towards task-relevant aspects of a scene." — [Chun & Jiang 1998](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1998.0681).
- Locus of the effect (tutorial review, AP&P 81(8):2571–2589): "Existing studies support an early locus of contextual cueing, consistent with attentional guidance accounts. Evidence for a late locus exists, though it is less conclusive"; the literature "highlights a distinction between habit-guided attention learned through experience and changes in spatial priority driven by task goals and stimulus salience." — [Sisk, Remington & Jiang 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-019-01832-2).
- Probability cuing of location (primary, Psychological Science 13(6):520–525): faster responses in the more probable region in young and elderly participants and in neglect patients "even in the neglected field", "without explicit instructions to do so or awareness" — [Geng & Behrmann 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00491). Probability cuing "is implicit and persists long after the probability cue is no longer valid ... is viewer-centered and is insensitive to secondary working memory load and aging"; goals set *where* priority goes, habits set *how* attention moves — [Jiang 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2017.06.018).
- Temporal contextual cuing (primary, JEP:LMC 27(5):1299–1313): "sequentially structured event durations, event identities, and spatiotemporal event sequences can guide attention to a point in time as well as to a target event's identity and location. Cuing was found to rely heavily on the element immediately preceding the target, although cuing from earlier items also was evident. Learning was implicit in all cases." — [Olson & Chun 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.5.1299).
- Memory-guided orienting from scenes (primary, Neuron 49(6):905–916): memories of a target's location in a learned scene guide orienting; "Memory-guided attention operated within surprisingly short intervals and conferred reliable and sizeable advantages for detection of objects embedded in scenes"; fMRI: interaction of memory-retrieval areas with the parietal-frontal orienting network; "Activity in the hippocampus was specifically engaged in memory-guided spatial attention and correlated with the ensuing behavioral advantage." — [Summerfield, Lepsien, Gitelman, Mesulam & Nobre 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.01.021).
- LTM prepares visual cortex spatiotopically (primary, PNAS 109(6):E360–E367): "the contents of LTM sharpen perceptual sensitivity for targets presented at memory-predicted spatial locations"; EEG: "spatially specific desynchronization of alpha-band activity over visual cortex"; fMRI: "target-predictive spatial information stored in LTM triggers spatiotopic modulation of preparatory activity in extrastriate visual cortex"; network includes hippocampus and a dorsal frontoparietal circuit — [Stokes, Atherton, Patai & Nobre 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1108555108).
- Multiple memory systems guide attention (review, TiCS 16(12):576–579): catalogued by multiple-memory-systems theory (priming, working memory, episodic, semantic, procedural); "forms of memory-guided attention vary in the extent to which the influence of past experience on attention is automatic"; semantic memory can "bias attention to the typical location of target objects in scenes"; and an "Indirect Route" exists in which "mnemonic processing is largely performed in dedicated memory systems ... To influence attention, these systems must generate a signal that informs the perceptual attention apparatus." — [Hutchinson & Turk-Browne 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2012.10.003); full text [PMC3728770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3728770/).
- Dissociable memory systems in one task (primary fMRI, Neuron 89(2):317–324): "trial by trial, the hippocampus (not the striatum) predicted attention benefits from context memory, while the striatum (not the hippocampus) predicted facilitation from rewarded stimulus-response associations." — [Goldfarb, Chun & Phelps 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.12.014).
- Hierarchy of timescales (review, Neuron 104(1):132–146): "memory, over a broad hierarchy of timescales, supplies the essential elements for selective attention to guide perception and performance"; short-term traces (preceding stimuli), intermediate traces ("regularities learned and utilized within a task setting"; with repeating templates "attentional control is transferred to intermediate memory (i.e., reduced CDA)"), and long-term traces; "Across episodes, long-term memories can also guide temporally structured anticipatory attention"; "Under most conditions, benefits in LTM-based orienting correlate with measures of explicit memory"; open question "whether or when activated LTM traces necessarily operate through functionally active WM states to guide attention." — [Nobre & Stokes 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.08.030); full text [PMC6873797](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6873797/).
- Earlier synthesis (review, Current Opinion in Neurobiology 17(2):177–184): "memory from past experience guides what should be attended. Brain areas that are important for memory, such as the hippocampus ... are recruited in attention tasks, and memory directly affects frontal-parietal networks involved in spatial orienting." — [Chun & Turk-Browne 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2007.03.005).
- Feature and location distractor regularities both reduce distractor costs and produce an earlier Pd component (primary EEG, Cortex 137:232–250) — [van Moorselaar, Daneshtalab & Slagter 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2021.01.010).

### Inferences
- For design question Q(2): memory delivers expectation to attention in both forms, and the form tracks the memory system. Located (where): contextual cueing, probability cuing, scene memory-guided orienting, learned distractor suppression — implemented as spatiotopic preparatory bias (Stokes 2012) on a priority map. Located (when): temporal contextual cuing and LTM-based temporal anticipation (Olson & Chun 2001; Nobre & Stokes 2019). Unlocated (what): identity predictions from associative memory (see Key question 3: hippocampal reinstatement of a predicted shape in V1/V2 irrespective of location) and semantic/feature priming (Hutchinson & Turk-Browne 2012; Zhao 2013's structured colour).
- The architecture's "region = bracket in .where × .when" maps well onto the located forms; the unlocated forms (priming) need a separate channel — which the design already has — but note that the literature's unlocated priors still *land* on specific populations (category-selective cortex, V1/V2 patterns), i.e., they are "where in feature space", not "nowhere".
- The timescale hierarchy argues that a single architecture should let expectations arise from traces of different ages (previous word, this document's regularities, LTM) and that control migrates from active templates to learned traces with repetition (reduced CDA) — consistent with "moving a region into the past is retrieval" for recent traces, but LTM-based guidance may require re-entry into an active state (open per Nobre & Stokes 2019).

### Gaps
- Millisecond magnitudes of the contextual-cueing benefit in Chun & Jiang 1998 and of the memory-guided orienting benefit in Summerfield 2006 were not retrievable from the sources I could reach; both abstracts report direction and significance only.
- Jiang & Chun 2001 / Jiang & Leung 2005 effect sizes are not in the abstracts.

## Key question 3 — Hippocampal/associative prediction and its relation to attention

### Takeaway
Hippocampal pattern completion generates identity predictions that are reinstated in early visual cortex (what, not where); the strength of this hippocampal prediction tracks expectation effects in visual cortex across trials and individuals; and attention controls the hippocampal state that determines what gets encoded and stabilized.

### Cited Findings
- Pattern completion → predictive coding (primary, Nature Neuroscience 19(5):665–667; N = 24). Cue + action predicted an outcome image with 95% probability (predictable) or 50% (unpredictable). Sequence decoding was reliable in CA2–CA3–DG (t23 = 2.53, p = .02) and CA1 (t23 = 2.72, p = .01) but not in subiculum, V1 or V2; outcome decoding was reliable in V1 (t23 = 3.17, p = .004) and V2 (t23 = 2.51, p = .02) but not in hippocampal subfields; V1–V2 outcome decoding was more reliable on trials where CA–DG sequence decoding was correct (t23 = 2.32, p = .03); across participants, V1–V2 outcome decoding was predicted by CA–DG sequence decoding (robust r22 = .60, p = .002). No RT difference between predictable and unpredictable trials (F1,23 = 0.69). — [Hindy, Ng & Turk-Browne 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4284); full text [PMC4948994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4948994/).
- Hippocampus codes the predicted shape, visual cortex the presented one (primary, J Neurosci 38(31):6888–6899): auditory cues predicted visual shapes; "representations in visual cortex were dominated by whichever shape was presented, whereas representations in the hippocampus reflected only which shape was predicted by the cue. The strength of hippocampal predictions correlated across participants with the amount of expectation-related facilitation in visual cortex." — [Kok & Turk-Browne 2018](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0163-18.2018).
- Implicit anticipation in hippocampus after incidental VSL (see Key question 1) — [Turk-Browne et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0858-10.2010).
- Attention sets the hippocampal state and what is encoded — [Aly & Turk-Browne 2016 Cereb Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhv041); [Aly & Turk-Browne 2016 PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1518931113).
- Hippocampus specifically engaged in memory-guided spatial attention, correlated with the behavioural benefit — [Summerfield et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.01.021); hippocampus + dorsal frontoparietal network organize preparatory visual-cortex states — [Stokes et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1108555108).
- Regularities bias attention toward their location/feature even when uninformative about the target — [Zhao et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612460407).

### Inferences
- The hippocampal route is the clearest case of memory supplying an *unlocated* (identity) expectation that is then expressed in a located sensory map: the predicted outcome is reinstated as a V1/V2 pattern. For the architecture, "expectation as a predicted image per region" matches this: the predicted content is content-addressed from memory but realised at the region where the input is expected.
- Two-way dependence: attention gates what the hippocampus stabilises/encodes (Aly & Turk-Browne), and hippocampal retrieval in turn biases attention (Summerfield; Stokes; Goldfarb). A system in which the mask selects what memory learns, and memory then shapes the mask, is the empirically supported loop.
- Hindy 2016's null RT effect alongside robust neural prediction shows that prediction can exist without behavioural expression; surprise signals in the architecture need not be visible in output measures.

### Gaps
- Whether hippocampal predictions are generated for cues presented *outside* attention has not, to my knowledge from these sources, been tested directly; Turk-Browne 2010 used an unrelated cover task but the predictive images were foveal and attended.

## Key question 4 — Internal vs external attention: do they share mechanisms?

### Takeaway
Behaviourally and at the level of prefrontal control signals, orienting to a representation in working memory uses the same selection operations as orienting to a stimulus (equivalent retro-cue/pre-cue costs and benefits, equivalent N2pc for search within VSTM, overlapping frontoparietal networks, shared PFC control subspaces in monkeys). The implementation diverges downstream (parietal/visual cortex code selection and attention independently; episodic retrieval engages largely segregated parietal subregions), so "same controller, different substrates" is the current consensus.

### Cited Findings
- Taxonomy (review, Annual Review of Psychology 62:73–101): external attention selects "locations in space, points in time, or modality-specific input ... features ... or object representations that integrate over space, time, and modality"; internal attention selects "task rules, responses, long-term memory, or working memory. Working memory, in particular, lies closest to the intersection between external and internal attention." — [Chun, Golomb & Turk-Browne 2011](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.093008.100427).
- Retro-cues behave like pre-cues (primary, J Cogn Neurosci 15(8):1176–1194): "There were equivalent patterns of behavioral costs and benefits of cueing for both pre-cues and retro-cues"; replicated with a peripheral probe, ruling out response-criterion explanations; ERPs compared orienting to external vs internal spatial representations — [Griffin & Nobre 2003](https://doi.org/10.1162/089892903322598139).
- Overlapping networks (primary fMRI, J Cogn Neurosci 16(3):363–373): orienting to locations in a mental array vs in the world is "subserved by extensively overlapping networks. However ... a region of right parietal cortex selectively participated in orienting attention to the extrapersonal space, whereas several frontal lobe regions selectively participated in orienting attention within on-line mental representations." — [Nobre, Coull, Maquet, Frith, Vandenberghe & Mesulam 2004](https://doi.org/10.1162/089892904322926700).
- Object-based internal attention modulates category cortex (primary fMRI, Cerebral Cortex 17(9):2072–2083): retro-cueing a face vs a scene held in WM "modulated the activity in fusiform and parahippocampal gyri", with improved WM performance — [Lepsien & Nobre 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhl116).
- Same N2pc for search in memory (primary ERP, J Neurosci 29(25):8032–8038): "equivalent N2pcs, with similar time courses and scalp distributions, were also elicited when target items were identified within a VSTM representation" — [Kuo, Rao, Lepsien & Nobre 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0952-09.2009).
- Shared prefrontal control in monkeys (primary, Nature 592(7855):601–605): "similar representations in prefrontal cortex encoded the control of both selection and attention, suggesting that prefrontal cortex acts as a domain-general controller. By contrast, both attention and selection were represented independently in parietal and visual cortex." — [Panichello & Buschman 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03390-w).
- WM as internal attention (review, PB&R 20(2):228–242): a "give-and-take interdependence between attention directed toward either actively maintained internal representations (traditionally considered WM) or external perceptual stimuli (traditionally considered selective attention)"; WM contents capture external attention even when irrelevant — [Kiyonaga & Egner 2013](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-012-0359-y).
- Retro-cue review (AP&P 78(7):1839–1860): of six hypotheses, support for "(3) attended representations are strengthened in WM, (4) not-attended representations are removed from WM, (5) a retro-cue ... provides a head start for its retrieval before decision making, and (6) attention protects the selected representation from perceptual interference"; not for decay-protection (1) or prioritisation-for-comparison (2) — [Souza & Oberauer 2016](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-016-1108-5).
- Beyond sustained internal attention (review, TiCS 21(6):449–461): prioritisation "acts in multiple steps: first orienting towards and selecting a memory, and then reconfiguring its representational state ... Reconfiguration sets up an optimized perception-action mapping, obviating the need for sustained attention." — [Myers, Stokes & Nobre 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.03.010). The inside-out view of attention within WM — [van Ede & Nobre 2023](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-021422-041757).
- Attention-to-memory in parietal cortex (reviews): dorsal parietal = top-down attention to memory, ventral parietal = bottom-up capture by retrieved content — [Cabeza, Ciaramelli, Olson & Moscovitch 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2459); the AtoM hypothesis — [Ciaramelli, Grady & Moscovitch 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2008.03.022). Contrary meta-analytic anatomy: "predominantly divergent subregions of lateral PPC are engaged during acts of episodic retrieval and during goal-directed and reflexive attention" — [Hutchinson, Uncapher & Wagner 2009](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.919109); parietal regions for perceptual attention and episodic memory "are largely segregated and often show a push-pull relationship" — [Sestieri, Shulman & Corbetta 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2017.6).

### Inferences
- For design question Q(3): using one selection mechanism (the mask) for both perception and retrieval is supported at the control level — the same orienting operations, with the same spatial signatures (N2pc), work on stored layouts — which also supports the design's premise that stored items retain a .where that a region can be moved onto. Expect divergence in *what the mask touches*: internal selection reconfigures the selected item's state (Myers 2017; Panichello & Buschman: selection transforms the item's representation into a shared output subspace) rather than merely gating it.
- The AtoM debate warns that *episodic* retrieval (recollection of a past document/sentence) recruits parietal regions segregated from those of perceptual attention; so "moving a region into the past" may be a fair model of attention within short-term/working memory but is less directly supported for remote LTM retrieval, where the literature expects a distinct retrieval signal that then informs the attention apparatus (Hutchinson & Turk-Browne's indirect route).

### Gaps
- Souza & Oberauer 2016 quantify retro-cue benefits only in the (paywalled) full text; I could not retrieve a pooled effect size.
- Griffin & Nobre 2003's ERP conclusions (which components were shared vs distinct) are truncated in the abstract I could access.

## Key question 5 — Focus of attention within working memory and the direct-access region

### Takeaway
Working memory is best described as nested states: activated LTM, a capacity-limited direct-access region (~3–5 chunks), and a focus of attention of about one item; items outside the focus lose sustained neural activity yet remain retrievable ("activity-silent"), and whether they can guide attention is disputed (single-template vs two-item guidance). I found no study that directly tests whether *predictions* are generated from direct-access items outside the focus.

### Cited Findings
- Three states (primary, JEP:LMC 28(3):411–421): arithmetic latencies depended on the size of the active set but not the passive set; object-switch costs within the active set; "Participants took 2 s to encode a passive set so that it did not affect processing latencies"; results "support a model distinguishing 3 states of representations in working memory: the activated part of long-term memory, a capacity limited region of direct access, and a focus of attention." — [Oberauer 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.3.411). Elaborated as the embedded-components design — [Oberauer 2009, "Design for a working memory"](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0079-7421(09)51002-X) and — [Oberauer & Hein 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721412444727) (citations verified; chapter contents not re-read here).
- Capacity ~4 (BBS 24(1):87–114): Miller's seven "was meant more as a rough estimate"; the capacity limit "is only three to five chunks", observable when chunking/rehearsal are blocked — [Cowan 2001](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922); the embedded-processes framework of activated memory with a focus of attention — [Cowan 1988](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.104.2.163) (citation verified; abstract not available).
- Single-item focus from retrieval dynamics — [McElree 2006, "Accessing recent events"](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0079-7421(06)46005-9) (citation verified; content not re-read here — background knowledge: speed–accuracy tradeoff data showing fast access for about one item and slower, uniform access for the rest).
- Outside the focus, activity drops but memory persists (primary fMRI/MVPA, J Cogn Neurosci 24(1):61–79): "only items within the focus of attention elicited an active neural trace. Activity corresponding to representations of items outside the focus quickly dropped to baseline. Nevertheless, this information was remembered ... refocusing attention toward a previously unattended memory item can reactivate its neural signature." — [Lewis-Peacock, Drysdale, Oberauer & Postle 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_00140). Framework: "'activity-silent' working memory" — [Stokes 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2015.05.004). EEG "ping" reveals hidden states: "temporarily unattended items remain robustly coded in the hidden state", while a forget cue wipes the trace; hidden-state strength predicts WM-guided behaviour — [Wolff, Jochim, Akyürek & Stokes 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4546).
- Only the template guides attention (review, TiCS 15(7):327–334): "whereas active memory items function as an attentional template and directly affect perception, other, accessory items do not ... items compete for the status of 'attentional template' that contains only one object at a time." — [Olivers, Peters, Houtkamp & Roelfsema 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2011.05.004). Memory-related capture present with one colour in VWM, "no longer present for memory loads beyond a single item", restored when a cue selected one item — [van Moorselaar, Theeuwes & Olivers 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036229). Contradicted: "Reliable capture was observed when 2 colors were held in VWM and both colors were present as distractors" — [Hollingworth & Beck 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000230).
- A WM-held location and a learned location bias act independently and additively — [Gao & Theeuwes 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-020-02115-x).
- Retro-cue evidence that unattended WM items are weakened/removed rather than merely unprotected — [Souza & Oberauer 2016](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-016-1108-5).

### Inferences
- The design's "admitted contents are short-term memory" with seven regions resembles a direct-access region of several items plus whichever region is currently being read; the literature suggests a further asymmetry: one item/template at a time is in a state that interacts with perception (Olivers 2011; Panichello & Buschman 2021 "selection transforms the representation"), with others held in a latent form that does not drive perception until re-selected.
- By extension, there is no positive evidence that direct-access items outside the focus generate perceptual predictions; the closest data (van Moorselaar 2014 vs Hollingworth & Beck 2016) concern attentional guidance, and they disagree on whether two items can guide at once. A conservative reading: at most one or two prioritized representations supply active expectation; the rest are retrievable but inert.
- Lewis-Peacock 2012 and Wolff 2017 support the design's "moving a region into the past is retrieval": re-focusing reinstates an item's neural signature from a hidden state.

### Gaps
- No study located that measures expectation/surprise signals for stimuli predicted by a non-focal WM item; this is an open empirical question, not a settled one.
- McElree 2006, Oberauer 2009, Oberauer & Hein 2012 and Cowan 1988 were verified bibliographically but their text was not re-read.

## Key question 6 — Temporal attention and temporal expectation (the .when bracket)

### Takeaway
Attention selects in time as it does in space: cued temporal intervals yield behavioural benefits with partly distinct (left parietal/premotor) circuitry, learned sequential structure guides attention to a point in time, and recent reviews treat temporal expectation (rhythms, hazard rates, sequences, associations) as a first-class dimension of proactive attention drawn from memory across timescales.

### Cited Findings
- Spatial vs temporal orienting (primary PET/fMRI, J Neurosci 18(18):7426–7435): a temporal analogue of the Posner task with cues to 300 ms or 1500 ms intervals, factorially crossed with spatial cues; "Behavioral data showed benefi[ts]" (abstract truncated in source); asks "whether there exists a general system for allocating attentional resources, independent of stimulus dimension, or whether functionally specialized brain regions are recruited" — [Coull & Nobre 1998](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-18-07426.1998).
- Temporal attention review (NRN 19(1):34–48): "the brain makes use of various forms of temporal regularities in the environment to guide adaptive behaviour and influence neural processing" — [Nobre & van Ede 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2017.141).
- Learned sequences cue time, identity and location, relying "heavily on the element immediately preceding the target" — [Olson & Chun 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.5.1299).
- Statistical learning drives selection of "what to expect where and when" — [Theeuwes, Bogaerts & van Moorselaar 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.06.001); LTM guides "temporally structured anticipatory attention" across episodes — [Nobre & Stokes 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.08.030).
- Temporal regularities in an irrelevant stream bias spatial and feature attention — [Zhao et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612460407); VSL triggers hippocampal anticipation of the next item — [Turk-Browne et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0858-10.2010).

### Inferences
- A .where × .when bracket is consistent with the literature's treatment of time as a selectable dimension with its own cueing benefits and partly distinct neural substrate; the strongest learned temporal expectation is first-order (the immediately preceding element), which is the natural analogue for serial reading one word at a time.
- Temporal and spatial expectations combine (Coull & Nobre's factorial design; Theeuwes 2022's where-and-when), supporting a joint rather than separable mask.

### Gaps
- The behavioural benefit sizes in Coull & Nobre 1998 and the neural dissociation details (left intraparietal/premotor for temporal orienting) are in the body text, which I could not access; only the design is verified here.
