# Pre-attentive prediction and deviance detection in audition

Scope: MMN under inattention; MMN as hierarchical prediction error; deviance distraction and involuntary attention switching; own-name / cocktail-party; attenuation vs filter theories; inattentional deafness and load; computational models treating expectancy violation as salience. Every citation below was checked against PubMed (E-utilities), Crossref, or the primary full text on 2026-10-09; items that could not be verified are flagged UNVERIFIED. Primary studies and reviews are labelled in-line. Effect sizes and conditions are quoted from the primary abstracts or full texts where they were accessible.

Conventions: "primary" = empirical study; "review" = narrative/theoretical review; "model" = computational paper. Latencies are post-stimulus unless stated.

## Key Question 1: MMN without attention — classic claims, modulation vs abolition, and what regularities it encodes

### Takeaway
MMN is reliably elicited while listeners read, watch silent video, play Tetris, or perform a demanding RSVP task, so a change/prediction-error signal exists outside the focus of attention; but strongly focused channel-selective attention attenuates it (to <1 µV in Woldorff et al. 1991), attention can determine *which* regularities get formed (stream segregation), and the regularities it encodes extend from single features to inter-feature relations, patterns and abstract rules — while the P3a/P3b and "global" (seconds-scale) regularities need attention or awareness.

### Cited Findings
- Original MMN report (primary): Näätänen, Gaillard & Mäntysalo (1978), *Acta Psychologica* 42:313–329, "Early selective-attention effect on evoked potential reinterpreted" — the deviant-minus-standard negativity was observed for deviants in the unattended ear of a dichotic task. — [PubMed 685709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/685709/)
- Näätänen's theoretical position (review): Näätänen (1990), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 13:201–233, "The role of attention in auditory information processing as revealed by event-related potentials and other brain measures of cognitive function" (with author's response, BBS 13:261–288, "Automatic and attention-dependent processing of auditory stimulus information"). — [doi:10.1017/S0140525X00078407](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00078407); [doi:10.1017/S0140525X00078717](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00078717)
- The standard 2007 review (review): Näätänen, Paavilainen, Rinne & Alho (2007), *Clinical Neurophysiology* 118(12):2544–2590. Abstract: MMN "is elicited by any discriminable change in auditory stimulation but recent studies extended the notion of the MMN even to higher-order cognitive processes such as those involving grammar and semantic meaning. Moreover, MMN data also show the presence of automatic intelligent processes such as stimulus anticipation at the level of auditory cortex. In addition, the MMN enables one to establish the brain processes underlying the initiation of attention switch to, conscious perception of, sound change in an unattended stimulus stream." — [doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2007.04.026)
- "Reading a book" condition verified in a primary study: Alain & Woods (1997), *Psychophysiology* 34:534–546. Participants either responded to pattern changes in a designated ear or were "presented with the same sequences while reading a book (no response required). In all conditions, changes in the pattern elicited a mismatch negativity (MMN) that peaked at 160–220 ms poststimulus. MMN amplitude varied with attention: the amplitude was higher in response to deviant stimuli presented in the attended ear than to the deviant stimuli presented in the unattended ear or during reading." — [doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.1997.tb01740.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.1997.tb01740.x)
- Attention attenuates MMN strongly under optimized focusing (primary): Woldorff, Hackley & Hillyard (1991), *Psychophysiology* 28(1):30–42. Two dichotic experiments; Exp 1 ISIs 120–320 ms, intensity-decrement deviants; "the MMN elicited by the unattended-channel deviant was markedly reduced (peak amplitude of less than 1 microV) relative to the corresponding negative wave elicited by the attended-channel deviants (3–4 microV), as well as relative to previously reported MMNs (3–6 microV)". Exp 2 (ISIs 65–205 ms): unattended-channel MMN "barely discernible", attended ~3–4 µV. Conclusion: processing in unattended channels "can be attenuated or gated at an early sensory level under conditions of highly focused auditory selective attention." (A published comment followed in Psychophysiology 28(4):478–484.) — [doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.1991.tb03384.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.1991.tb03384.x)
- Attention determines which regularities exist (primary): Sussman, Ritter & Vaughan (1998), *Brain Research* 789:130–138. Alternating high/low tones at constant rate; "When subjects ignored the stimuli, the three-tone standard and deviant sequences contained within the high- and low-pitched tones did not emerge and no MMNs were obtained"; when attending to the high tones, "MMNs were obtained to the deviants within both the attended and unattended streams." — [doi:10.1016/s0006-8993(97)01443-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-8993(97)01443-1)
- Competition for deviance detection (primary): Sussman, Winkler & Wang (2003), *Psychophysiology* 40:430–435, "MMN and attention: competition for deviance detection". — [doi:10.1111/1469-8986.00045](https://doi.org/10.1111/1469-8986.00045)
- Sussman's context account (review): Sussman (2007), *Journal of Psychophysiology* 21(3–4):164–175, "A new view on the MMN and attention debate: the role of context in processing auditory events" — proposes that "the principal factor governing MMN is the sound context" and that MMN relies on multiple mechanisms within auditory scene analysis. — [doi:10.1027/0269-8803.21.34.164](https://doi.org/10.1027/0269-8803.21.34.164); [scinapse summary](https://scinapse.io/papers/1893102296)
- Sussman, Chen, Sussman-Fort & Dinces (2014), *Brain Topography* 27:553–564, "The five myths of MMN" (review): aims to dispel "the notion that MMN is a simple feature discriminator and that attention itself modulates MMN elicitation", emphasising "the highly context-dependent nature of MMN elicitation". — [doi:10.1007/s10548-013-0326-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10548-013-0326-6)
- Recent direct test of attentional load on physical vs abstract MMN (primary, 2024): Paavilainen & Ilola (2024), *Heliyon* 10(12):e33182. Physical deviants (higher pitch) and abstract deviants (descending pairs among ascending pairs) under three conditions — silent video, Tetris, attend-and-detect soft targets. "Both the physical and abstract deviants elicited in all conditions MMNs but no statistically significant amplitude differences between the conditions were found. The N2b and P3b components were elicited only in the attend condition and only by the soft target tones." Authors note silent video "usually keeps the participants' attention well enough directed away from the auditory stimuli." — [doi:10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e33182](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e33182)
- Abstract regularities (review): Paavilainen (2013), *International Journal of Psychophysiology* 88(2):109–123. MMN "also includes the detection and use of more complex, 'abstract', regularities based, for example, on relationships between various physical features of the stimuli or in patterns present in the auditory stream ... the central auditory system performs even at the pre-attentive, auditory-cortex level surprisingly 'cognitive' operations, such as generalization leading to simple concept formation, rule extraction and prediction of future stimuli. The information extracted often seems to be in an implicit form, not directly available to conscious processes ... the regularity violations can temporarily impair performance in the primary task." — [doi:10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2013.03.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2013.03.015)
- Abstract-rule violations processed without attention or awareness (primary): Schröger, Bendixen, Trujillo-Barreto & Roeber (2007), *PLoS ONE* 2(11):e1131. "violations of abstract auditory rules are processed even when the sounds are unattended. When subjects performed a task related to the sounds but not to the rule, rule violations impaired task performance and activated a network involving supratemporal, parietal and frontal areas although none of the subjects acquired explicit knowledge of the rule or became aware of rule violations." — [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0001131](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0001131)
- Memory-based vs adaptation controversy (reviews): Näätänen, Jacobsen & Winkler (2005), *Psychophysiology* 42:25–32, argue MMN "cannot be accounted for by differential activation of the afferent N1 transient detectors" and requires "a memory representation of the standard" ([doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.2005.00256.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2005.00256.x)); May & Tiitinen (2010), *Psychophysiology* 47:66–122, counter that MMN "is, in essence, a latency- and amplitude-modulated expression of the auditory N1 response, generated by fresh-afferent activity of cortical neurons that are under nonuniform levels of adaptation" ([doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.2009.00856.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2009.00856.x)). Winkler (2007), *J Psychophysiol* 21:147–163, "Interpreting the mismatch negativity" ([doi:10.1027/0269-8803.21.34.147](https://doi.org/10.1027/0269-8803.21.34.147)).
- Attention as gain on prediction error, prediction as top-down content (review): Schröger, Marzecová & SanMiguel (2015), *European Journal of Neuroscience* 41(5):641–664. "Auditory attention typically yields enhanced brain activity, whereas auditory prediction often results in attenuated brain responses. However, when strongly predicted sounds are omitted, brain responses to silence resemble those elicited by sounds ... attention may magnify the processing differences between predicted and unpredicted sounds ... prediction relates to predictions sent down from predictive models housed in higher levels ... and attention refers to gain modulation of the prediction error signal sent up to the higher level." — [doi:10.1111/ejn.12816](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12816)
- Recent compendium (review): Fitzgerald & Todd (2020), *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 11:468, "Making Sense of Mismatch Negativity" — reviews MMN as index of regularity-based prediction "over many timescales", including attention. — [doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00468](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2020.00468)
- Early indicators of prediction without attention (review): Bendixen, SanMiguel & Schröger (2012), *Int J Psychophysiol* 83:120–131: "the auditory system automatically uses stimulus predictability ... This type of predictive processing does not require attentional processing of the sounds or cognitive control of the predictions"; match signals (N1 attenuation, repetition positivity, early gamma) and mismatch signals (MMN, omission responses) "down to the level of the auditory middle-latency responses." — [doi:10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2011.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2011.08.003)
- Deviance detection at 20–40 ms and single-neuron SSA (review): Escera & Malmierca (2014), *Psychophysiology* 51:111–123: human correlates of deviance detection "at 20–40 ms from change onset" and stimulus-specific adaptation along the auditory hierarchy; MMN "seems to involve a cascade of neuronal processing that occurs at different successive levels." — [doi:10.1111/psyp.12156](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12156)
- Regularity representations as perceptual objects (review): Winkler, Denham & Nelken (2009), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 13(12):532–540: "The auditory system continuously searches for regularities within the acoustic signal ... Representations of the detected regularities produce predictions of upcoming sounds." — [doi:10.1016/j.tics.2009.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2009.09.003)

### Inferences
- The field's consensus is that attention *modulates but does not abolish* the MMN: with ordinary distraction tasks (video, Tetris, RSVP) the amplitude difference is nil or small (Paavilainen & Ilola 2024; Bekinschtein et al. 2009, see KQ2), while highly focused rapid-rate dichotic attention gates it to near-zero (Woldorff et al. 1991; Alain & Woods 1997). Sussman reframes this: attention changes the *context/organisation* (which streams exist) over which an otherwise automatic deviance detector operates.
- Pre-attentive regularity encoding is coarser than semantics but richer than single features: inter-feature relations (e.g., "the higher tone is the louder"), pattern alternations and abstract rules are encoded without awareness (Paavilainen 2013; Schröger et al. 2007). Lexical-level traces are also activated outside attention (see KQ5).
- Architectural reading: a prediction/subtraction stage over the unattended field is well supported, but its gain should be reducible by a strong attentional mask (Woldorff), and its *inputs* (what counts as a stream) can be set by attention (Sussman).

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full text of Näätänen et al. (2007) or Sussman (2007); claims about them rest on verified abstracts and secondary summaries.
- Alain & Woods (1997) report direction but the abstract gives no µV figures for the attended–unattended difference.

## Key Question 2: MMN as prediction error and the hierarchy of pre-attentive prediction (Garrido 2009; Bekinschtein 2009; Wacongne 2011; sleep and consciousness dissociations)

### Takeaway
The local-global paradigm shows a robust two-level dissociation: a local (transition-probability) prediction error at ~100–300 ms is present under mind-wandering, under a competing visual RSVP task, in vegetative-state patients and across sleep stages, whereas the global (seconds-scale, counting-type) regularity violation (P3b, ~300–600 ms) disappears when subjects are unaware of the global rule, distracted, asleep or unconscious; the local MMN itself is nonetheless modulated by higher-order expectation (reduced when the deviant is globally expected), and omission responses prove it is predictive rather than adaptive.

### Cited Findings
- Predictive-coding framing (review): Garrido, Kilner, Stephan & Friston (2009), *Clinical Neurophysiology* 120(3):453–463. MMN "reflects the brain's ability to perform automatic comparisons between consecutive stimuli and provides an electrophysiological index of sensory learning and perceptual accuracy"; reviews "adaptation" vs "model adjustment" hypotheses "and proposes predictive coding as a general framework that attempts to unify both." — [doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2008.11.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2008.11.029)
- Bekinschtein, Dehaene, Rohaut, Tadel, Cohen & Naccache (2009), *PNAS* 106(5):1672–1677 (primary). Design: series of 5 complex 50-ms sounds, SOA 150 ms (650 ms per series); 4 identical then a 5th identical (local standard) or different (local deviant); in a block one series type is frequent (80%) and the other rare (20%), so local and global deviance are orthogonal; each block starts with 20–30 global standards. Local effect: "2 major ERP effects within an early 130–300 ms temporal window: first a typical MMN, followed by a central positivity"; detected in 32/32 subjects; "No difference was observed for the local effect between the 3 groups" (count / mind-wander / visual interference). Global effect: present in all counting-task subjects; in the mind-wandering group (n=11) "the global effect decreased dramatically, and no significant effect was observed beyond 400 ms" (6/11 showed an early global effect); in the visual-interference group (n=10, letter-detection RSVP) "violation of the global regularity did not elicit any measurable ERP effect" (3/10 weak effects). Regularity-awareness score 4/4 (count), 2.3/4 (mind-wander), 0.4/4 (interference). Patients: of 4 vegetative-state patients 3 had a local effect and none a global effect; all 4 minimally-conscious patients had a local effect and 3 a global effect. fMRI: local violation activated bilateral STG incl. primary auditory cortex; global effect spanned prefrontal, cingulate, parietal and temporal regions. Authors: local effects "are highly suggestive of an automatic, nonconscious, and encapsulated mode of processing"; "being merely awake and aware is not enough to elicit a global effect ... a global effect was present only when subjects were conscious of the global regularity violations." — [doi:10.1073/pnas.0809667106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0809667106); full text [PMC2635770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2635770/)
- Wacongne, Labyt, van Wassenhove, Bekinschtein, Naccache & Dehaene (2011), *PNAS* 108(51):20754–20759 (primary, EEG+MEG, n=10). Participants "were instructed only to attend to the stimuli, not to count them" (i.e., attention was *on* the sounds; this study does not manipulate attention). Blocks where xxxxx or xxxxY was frequent (75%), 15% deviant series, 10% omissions. Local MMN "at around 120 ms after the onset of the fifth deviant tone" (EEG 85–150 ms, p=0.002) in both block types, i.e., "the fifth, locally deviant tone, although fully expected, still elicits an MMN"; but "the MMN amplitude was reduced on xxxxY compared with xxxxx blocks (EEG: 134–190 ms, P=0.014)". Second-level novelty: rare vs frequent sequences differed later (EEG 327–540 ms, p<0.00001), with "a complete inversion of the classical mismatch response" when the monotonous xxxxx was the rare sequence (a P3b to five identical tones). Omission of the 5th tone evoked an early response "peaking around 100 ms after the onset of the omitted tone", larger when a deviant was expected (xxxxY blocks; EEG 109–130 ms, p=0.03) — "exactly as expected from a hierarchy of predictive systems but cannot be accommodated easily by a single process of novelty detection"; adaptation/entrainment accounts predict the opposite. Conclusion: "The MMN reflects the operation of a temporally and conceptually limited prediction system that uses the recent past to predict the present, based solely on a compilation of the probabilities of the stimuli and their transitions ... it uses only a limited time window and is blind to the global overall rule or pattern." — [unicog PDF](https://www.unicog.org/publications/PNAS-2011-Wacongne-20754-9.pdf)
- Spiking model of the same claims (model): Wacongne, Changeux & Dehaene (2012), *Journal of Neuroscience* 32(11):3665–3678: predictive-coding network with "distinct input, predictive, and prediction error units" and STDP/NMDA learning of transition statistics accounts for "a frequency-dependent response to rare deviants, a response to unexpected repeats in alternating sequences (ABABAA…), a lack of consideration of the global sequence context, a response to sound omission, and a sensitivity of the MMN to NMDA receptor antagonists"; includes a new MEG validation. — [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5003-11.2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5003-11.2012)
- Attention and expectation act differently on the two levels (primary): Chennu, Noreika, Gueorguiev, Blenkmann, Kochen, Ibáñez, Owen & Bekinschtein (2013), *J Neurosci* 33(27):11194–11205. "Early first-level processing indexed by the MMN was sensitive to stimulus predictability: here, attentional precision enhanced early responses, but explicit top-down expectation diminished it ... later, second-level processing indexed by the P300: although sensitive to the degree of predictability, responses at this level were contingent on attentional engagement and in fact sharpened by top-down expectation. At the highest level, the drift of the CNV was a fine-grained marker of top-down expectation itself." — [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0114-13.2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0114-13.2013)
- Sleep (primary): Strauss, Sitt, King, Elbaz, Azizi, Buiatti, Naccache, van Wassenhove & Dehaene (2015), *PNAS* 112(11):E1353–E1362. "The global response, reflected in the P300, vanished during sleep ... The local mismatch response remained across all sleep stages (N1, N2, and REM sleep), but with an incomplete structure; compared with wakefulness, a specific peak reflecting prediction error vanished during sleep." Authors conclude sleep "leaves initial auditory processing and passive sensory response adaptation intact, but specifically disrupts both short-term and long-term auditory predictive coding." — [doi:10.1073/pnas.1501026112](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1501026112)
- Clinical replication (primary): Faugeras et al. (2012), *Neuropsychologia* 50(3):403–418: 65 bedside recordings in 49 non-communicating patients; "only conscious patients presented this proposed neural signature [global effect]"; local violations "led to an early response in auditory cortex, independent of attention or the presence of a concurrent visual task." — [doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.12.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.12.015)
- Bayesian-filtering model (model): Lieder, Stephan, Daunizeau, Garrido & Friston (2013), *PLoS Comput Biol* 9(11):e1003288 — MMN modelled as superposed fields of prediction-error units in a hierarchical dynamical model; reproduces deviant-probability and -magnitude effects on amplitude and latency. — [doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003288](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003288)
- Critical appraisal (review): Heilbron & Chait (2018), *Neuroscience* 389:54–73, "Great Expectations": for the assumptions "that neural responses are shaped by expectations and that these expectations are hierarchically organized – animal and human studies provide compelling evidence. The anticipatory, predictive nature of these expectations also enjoys empirical support, especially from studies on unexpected stimulus omission. However, for the existence of separate error and prediction neurons ... evidence is lacking ... results on [oscillatory signatures and the relation between attention and precision] are mixed or contradictory." — [doi:10.1016/j.neuroscience.2017.07.061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2017.07.061)

### Inferences
- The evidence does *require* prediction and prediction error outside attention, but places a hard ceiling on its scope: predictions based on local transition probabilities over a short window (hundreds of ms to a few seconds) are pre-attentive and even survive sleep partially; predictions that require maintaining a counting-type rule across trials/seconds in working memory require attention and awareness.
- The two levels interact: the pre-attentive MMN is partially cancelled by a higher-level expectation (Wacongne 2011) and enhanced by attentional "precision" (Chennu 2013) — consistent with a multiplicative gain (attention) applied to a subtractive error (prediction), as in Schröger et al. (2015).
- For a serial reader architecture: the analogue is that word-to-word (local) expectations can be computed and subtracted outside the attended region, whereas discourse-scale (global) expectations are only available for the attended, working-memory-held content.

### Gaps
- Whether the global effect's attention dependence reflects attention per se or conscious access is debated (Bekinschtein et al. and Strauss et al. interpret it as consciousness); I found no study dissociating the two for the global effect.
- Wacongne et al. (2011) did not manipulate attention; the hierarchical interaction under *inattention* is inferred from Bekinschtein et al. (2009) and Strauss et al. (2015).

## Key Question 3: Deviance distraction and involuntary attention switching — is capture by unexpectedness rather than novelty, and is it unconditional, set-contingent or load-dependent?

### Takeaway
Behavioural capture by a task-irrelevant sound is driven by violation of a learned expectancy (conditional-probability or sequence-level rule), not by novelty or rarity per se (Vachon et al. 2012; Parmentier et al. 2011); the detection stage (MMN) is load-insensitive, but the orienting/behavioural stage is reduced or abolished by working-memory load, encoding difficulty, foreknowledge and cueing, habituates when the deviation itself becomes predictable, and depends partly on the sound's informational value and on the listener's attentional set — so capture is real but conditional, not unconditional.

### Cited Findings
- Escera, Alho, Winkler & Näätänen (1998), *J Cognitive Neuroscience* 10(5):590–604 (primary, cross-modal oddball): visual odd/even RT task; each visual stimulus preceded by a standard tone (80%), slightly higher deviant tone (10%) or natural novel sound (10%). "Novel sounds prolonged the RT to successive visual stimuli by 17 msec ... Deviant tones, in turn, decreased the hit rate but did not significantly affect the RT." ERPs: MMN peaking at 150 ms to deviants; novels elicited enhanced N1 and a P3a with early (230 ms, central) and late (315 ms, right-frontal) subcomponents. Two mechanisms proposed: a transient detector (N1) for novels and a change detector (MMN) for deviants and novels. — [doi:10.1162/089892998562997](https://doi.org/10.1162/089892998562997)
- Schröger & Wolff (1998), *NeuroReport* 9(15):3355–3358 (primary): same protocol under three task-relevance conditions; "distractors elicited the MMN ... in each condition (even when the sounds were ignored) and subsequent N2b and P3 (reflecting orienting towards the distractor) when the sounds were attended"; introduces the Reorienting Negativity (RON). — [doi:10.1097/00001756-199810260-00003](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-199810260-00003)
- Is the MMN→P3a→RON chain tightly coupled? (primary) Horváth, Winkler & Bendixen (2008), *Biological Psychology* 79:139–147: "P3a can be elicited without concurrent N1-increase or MMN elicitation and without subsequent elicitation of the RON" — the stages "are not strongly coupled." — [doi:10.1016/j.biopsycho.2008.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2008.04.001)
- Separate change-detection vs orienting mechanisms (primary): Rinne, Särkkä, Degerman, Schröger & Alho (2006), *Brain Research* 1077:135–143: task-irrelevant intensity changes of ±3/6/9 dB; "discrimination performance deteriorated with increasing magnitude"; increments evoked N1 enhancement + MMN + P3a, decrements only MMN, yet both distracted — "P3a may not be regarded as a general index of attentional shift." — [doi:10.1016/j.brainres.2006.01.043](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2006.01.043)
- Parmentier (2014), *Psychological Research* 78(3):321–338 (review): deviance distraction "(1) derives from the time penalty associated with the involuntary orientation of attention to and away from the deviant sound ... and (2) is mediated by a number of factors (some increasing distraction, such as aging or induced emotions; some decreasing it, such as a memory load or cognitive control). Contrary to the received view that deviants ineluctably elicit distraction, recent work demonstrates that it is contingent upon auditory distractors acting as unspecific warning signals in the service of goal-oriented behavior, and that deviants do not elicit distraction because they are rare but because they violate the cognitive system's predictions (which can be manipulated through implicit rule learning or explicit cueing) ... the capture of attention by spoken deviant sounds is followed by an involuntary evaluation of their semantic properties ... behavioral deviance distraction is not the mere byproduct of the mismatch negativity, P3a and re-orientation negativity." — [doi:10.1007/s00426-013-0534-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-013-0534-4)
- Vachon, Hughes & Jones (2012), *JEP: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 38(1):164–177, "Broken expectations" (primary; full text read). Paradigm: visual serial recall with a to-be-ignored spoken letter sequence whose voice (female/male) alternated every 5 trials ("quintets"). Exp 1 (n=37; 90 trials = 18 quintets): recall was poorest on Trial Position 1 of each quintet, i.e., the voice-change trial (main effect of trial position with linear trend F(1,36)=60.48, p<.001; quadratic F(1,36)=13.14); the effect was "confined to Trial Position 1". Crucially, no capture on first encounter: within the first quintet there was no trial-position effect (F<1, d<0.11) whereas in the second quintet (first voice change) there was (F(4,144)=7.66, p<.001, d=0.92); quintet × position interaction F(4,144)=5.87. Habituation: the capture effect "waned over the course of the experiment", attributable "mainly to the first few such changes" (the across-quintet parallelism test became non-significant once Quintets 2–4 were removed). Exp 2 (n=24; 70 trials): replicated no first-trial capture (first quintet d<0.36 vs second quintet F(4,92)=6.10, d=1.03; position-1 difference t(23)=3.45, p=.002, d=1.44) and habituation across quintets 2–9; then, in Quintet 11, an *unpredictable* voice change at Trial Position 2 (breaking the change-every-5 rule) reinstated capture: recall poorer at Positions 2 and 3 of Quintet 11 (trial-position effect F(4,92)=6.79, p<.001, d=1.09; absent in Quintets 9, 10, 12–14, Fs<1). The authors frame this against Sokolov's neuronal-model/OR novelty account and in favour of expectancy violation, noting that predictability effects on single deviants were known (e.g., Sussman, Winkler & Schröger 2003) but that this is "the first time ... that such a predictability effect has been demonstrated for a pattern that is relatively large-scale." — [Royal Holloway PDF](https://repository.royalholloway.ac.uk/items/00fe839e-32d4-b688-d168-f33dd5ad3c6c/10/Vachon_Hughes_Jones_2012_.pdf)
- Predecessor deviation effect (primary): Hughes, Vachon & Jones (2005), *JEP:LMC* 31(4):736–749, "Auditory attentional capture during serial recall: violations at encoding of an algorithm-based neural model?" — [doi:10.1037/0278-7393.31.4.736](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.31.4.736)
- Novelty vs expectation vs change dissociated (primary): Parmentier, Elsley, Andrés & Barceló (2011), *Cognition* 119(3):374–380: in a cross-modal oddball, base rate and conditional probability were manipulated orthogonally; "behavioral distraction does not result from a sound's novelty per se but from the violation of the cognitive system's expectation based on the learning of conditional probabilities and, to some extent, the occurrence of a perceptual change from one sound to another." — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2011.02.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2011.02.001)
- Informational value is necessary for *behavioural* distraction in oddball tasks (primary): Parmentier, Elsley & Ljungberg (2010), *Cognition* 115(3):504–511: "(1) behavioral novelty distraction is only observed when the sound announces the occurrence and timing of an upcoming visual target (as is the case in all past research); (2) ... no such distraction is observed for deviant sounds conveying no such information; and ... (3) deviant sounds can actually facilitate performance when these, but not the standards, convey information." — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2010.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.03.002)
- But the orienting ERP is automatic even then (primary): Wetzel, Schröger & Widmann (2013), *Psychophysiology* 50(9):920–930: "Independent from the informational content, novel, and deviant sounds elicited the P3a ... In contrast, impaired performance was only observed if target-related information was provided ... distractor sounds are automatically evaluated as potentially significant, but ... the consequences for behavior depend on further processes." — [doi:10.1111/psyp.12072](https://doi.org/10.1111/psyp.12072)
- Cognitive control: cueing abolishes distraction (primary): Parmentier & Hebrero (2013), *JEP:LMC* 39(5):1635–1641: "cues predicting the presentation of deviant sounds eliminated behavioral distraction irrespective of the time available to process them." — [doi:10.1037/a0032421](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032421)
- Working-memory/task load reduces the orienting stage but not detection (primary): Berti & Schröger (2003), *Eur J Neurosci* 17(5):1119–1122: "distraction effects were still present but reduced markedly with higher task demands ... the preattentive change detection system underlying distraction was not modulated by task demand whereas distraction per se was." — [doi:10.1046/j.1460-9568.2003.02527.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1460-9568.2003.02527.x). SanMiguel, Corral & Escera (2008), *J Cogn Neurosci* 20(7):1131–1145: with 1-back WM load (vs no-memory control), novel sounds (20%) produced less distraction "both behaviorally and as indexed by an attenuation of the late phase of the novelty-P3. The transient/change detection mechanism indexed by MMN was not affected"; the authors note this "contradict[s] predictions of the load theory of selective attention and cognitive control" (which predicts more distraction under WM load). — [doi:10.1162/jocn.2008.20078](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.20078)
- Encoding difficulty and foreknowledge abolish the deviation effect (primary): Hughes, Hurlstone, Marsh, Vachon & Jones (2013), *JEP:HPP* 39(2):539–553: capture by a voice deviation in irrelevant speech "was abolished when focal-task engagement was promoted either by increasing the difficulty of encoding the visual to-be-remembered stimuli (by reducing their perceptual discriminability) or by providing foreknowledge of an imminent deviation", whereas the changing-state effect was not modulated; individual WM capacity predicted the deviation effect but not the changing-state effect. — [doi:10.1037/a0029064](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029064). Review of this "duplex-mechanism" account: Hughes (2014), *PsyCh Journal* 3:30–41 — [doi:10.1002/pchj.44](https://doi.org/10.1002/pchj.44)
- Attentional-set contingency in auditory singleton capture (primary): Dalton & Lavie (2004), *JEP:HPP* 30(1):180–193 — irrelevant singletons (unique on frequency/intensity/duration) produced search costs — [doi:10.1037/0096-1523.30.1.180](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.30.1.180); Dalton & Lavie (2007), *Perception & Psychophysics* 69(2):162–171: "singleton interference was eliminated when the target was not a singleton ... auditory attentional capture depends on the observer's attentional set ... The suggestion that hearing might act as an early warning system that would always be tuned to unexpected unique stimuli must therefore be modified." — [doi:10.3758/bf03193739](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193739)
- Categorical (semantic) deviation is *not* set-contingent (primary, 7 experiments): Vachon, Marsh & Labonté (2020), *JEP: General* 149(7):1360–1397: a categorical change in ignored speech (e.g., a digit among letters) disrupts visual serial recall; "distraction by categorical deviation is noncontingent on the activated task set and appears resistant to top-down control manipulations", implying "the semantic content of the irrelevant sound can be extracted preattentively". — [doi:10.1037/xge0000714](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000714)
- Semantic analysis follows novelty detection (primary): Parmentier (2008), *Cognition* 109(3):345–362: novel spoken words incongruent with the visual target (e.g., "left" before a right arrow) disrupted beyond congruent novels; "No semantic effect was observed for congruent and incongruent standards, suggesting that novelty detection is necessary for involuntary semantic processing"; the novelty effect grew with deviance magnitude and decreased over the task, the semantic effect did neither. — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2008.09.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2008.09.005)
- Threat/taboo content (primary): Röer, Körner, Buchner & Bell (2017), *Emotion* 17(4):740–750: taboo words in steady-state or changing-state irrelevant speech disrupted serial recall more than neutral words; the effect "did not habituate and it did not differ between individuals with high and low working memory capacity"; but a *single* deviant taboo word produced no extra effect — authors favour "a functional view of auditory distraction, according to which the to-be-ignored modality is routinely monitored for potentially important stimuli." — [doi:10.1037/emo0000274](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000274)
- Regularity per se does not capture (primary): Southwell, Baumann, Gal, Barascud, Friston & Chait (2017), *Phil Trans R Soc B* 372:20160105: EEG showed rapid gain increase to regular (REG) vs random (RAND) tone-pip sequences, but in two behavioural tasks "we consistently find no evidence for the exogenous capture of attention by regular acoustic patterns ... In fact, as demonstrated in Experiment 2, random stimuli can be more distracting than regular ones." — [doi:10.1098/rstb.2016.0105](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0105)
- Violation, not emergence, drives phasic arousal when task-irrelevant (primary): Zhao, Chait, Dick, Dayan, Furukawa & Liao (2019), *Nature Communications* 10:4030: 20-Hz tone-pip sequences "too rapid for naive listeners to explicitly follow"; "Exp1 and Exp2 measured responses to transitions when they were not behaviorally relevant"; pupil dilation responses "are consistently evoked by STEP and REG10-RAND20 transitions, but not RAND20-REG10 transitions" (STEP PDR began ~300 ms post-transition, peak 1520 ms) — "When not behaviourally relevant, only abrupt structural violations evoke a PDR", interpreted as norepinephrine signalling unexpected uncertainty. — [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12048-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12048-1)
- Habituation of the irrelevant-sound effect itself (primary): Bell, Röer, Dentale & Buchner (2012), *JEP:LMC* 38:1542–1557, "Habituation of the irrelevant sound effect: evidence for an attentional theory of short-term memory disruption". — [doi:10.1037/a0028459](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028459)
- Implicit vs explicit measures (review): Dalton & Hughes (2014), *Psychol Res* 78(3):313–320. — [doi:10.1007/s00426-014-0557-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-014-0557-5)

### Inferences
- The best-supported answer to "novelty or unexpectedness?" is unexpectedness: on first encounter nothing is captured; capture appears only once a rule has been learned and broken, decays as the breaking itself becomes rule-governed, and returns when the higher-order rule is broken (Vachon et al. 2012; Parmentier et al. 2011). This is exactly a prediction-error account at more than one level.
- "Unconditional vs contingent vs load-dependent": detection (MMN, P3a) behaves nearly unconditionally; the *behavioural switch* is contingent — reduced or eliminated by WM load, encoding load, foreknowledge/cueing, task set (singleton detection mode) and informational irrelevance. The conflict between Parmentier (2010: no distraction without informational value) and the irrelevant-sound deviation effect (Hughes/Vachon: capture by wholly irrelevant speech during encoding) is unresolved in the literature; both agree capture is modulated by task engagement (Hughes et al. 2013).
- Size: behavioural costs are small (tens of ms RT; a few percentage points of recall), consistent with a brief orienting-and-return rather than a full re-allocation.
- Two kinds of unattended content break through non-contingently: categorical/semantic deviation within speech (Vachon 2020) and self-relevant or threatening words (Röer et al. 2013, 2017; KQ4) — suggesting that the unattended-channel representation reaching the capture mechanism is at least lexical-categorical for a single competing speech stream.

### Gaps
- I did not read Parmentier (2014) in full; typical RT costs quoted there (and the aging/emotion moderators) are reported only at the level of the abstract.
- No direct test was found of whether an *unattended-stream* deviant captures when the attentional set is on a different feature dimension of the same stream (the auditory analogue of contingent capture is only tested with singletons, Dalton & Lavie 2007).

## Key Question 4: Own-name / cocktail party — what fraction notice, and what it says about semantic processing of the unattended channel

### Takeaway
About one-third of shadowers notice their own name in the rejected channel (33% Moray 1959; 34.6% Wood & Cowan 1995), rising to 65% in low-working-memory-span and falling to 20% in high-span listeners (Conway et al. 2001); noticing is accompanied by shadowing errors on the next items, i.e., a brief attention shift, and ERP work shows an own-name-specific negativity at 170–270 ms that does not habituate even under video-game distraction — so unattended speech receives at least name-level identification, but whether this reflects routine semantic analysis or lapses of selection remains contested.

### Cited Findings
- Moray (1959), *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* 11:56–60, "Attention in dichotic listening: affective cues and the influence of instructions" (primary). Reported, per later replications, that roughly 33% of participants noticed their own name in the ignored channel. — [doi:10.1080/17470215908416289](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470215908416289); the 33% figure is as summarised by [Conway et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03196169)
- Wood & Cowan (1995), *JEP:LMC* 21(1):255–260 (primary, n=34): "only 34.6% of the participants recalled hearing their name in the channel to be ignored. Only those participants showed on-line evidence of attention shifts, and those shifts occurred only for the two items following the name ... participants who detected their name monitored the irrelevant channel for a short time afterward." — [doi:10.1037//0278-7393.21.1.255](https://doi.org/10.1037//0278-7393.21.1.255)
- Wood & Cowan (1995), *JEP: General* 124(3):243–262 (primary, 4 experiments, forward→backward speech change in the irrelevant channel): "Only participants who shifted attention toward the irrelevant channel during the backward speech later recalled hearing it. In those whose attention shifted, shadowing errors peaked dramatically about 15 s after the change. There was no evidence of direct or indirect memory for phrases presented in the irrelevant channel. The results contradict models of attention stating that listeners process task-irrelevant information extensively without diverting resources used in shadowing." — [doi:10.1037//0096-3445.124.3.243](https://doi.org/10.1037//0096-3445.124.3.243)
- Conway, Cowan & Bunting (2001), *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 8(2):331–335 (primary): low-span participants detected their name 65% of the time vs 20% for high-span; "subjects who detect their name in the irrelevant message have relatively low working-memory capacities, suggesting that they have difficulty blocking out, or inhibiting, distracting information." — [doi:10.3758/bf03196169](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03196169); [PDF](https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/conway-et-al-pbr-2001.pdf)
- Own name in irrelevant speech disrupts visual serial recall (primary): Röer, Bell & Buchner (2013), *Journal of Cognitive Psychology* 25(8):925–931, "Self-relevance increases the irrelevant sound effect: attentional disruption by one's own name" — more serial-recall errors when irrelevant sentences contained the participant's own name than a yoked control's name (as summarised in a later PMC article). — [doi:10.1080/20445911.2013.828063](https://doi.org/10.1080/20445911.2013.828063); summary in [PMC6157865](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6157865/)
- ERP evidence of pre-attentive own-name discrimination (primary): Tateuchi, Itoh & Nakada (2012), *Psychophysiology* 49(6):786–791: subjects played a video game and ignored names; "A P3a-like frontal positivity (P440, 440 ms) indexing OR was elicited by SON only when it was the rare stimulus and its amplitude decreased with the repeated presentation of SON. Preceding the P440, SON consistently elicited an early frontal negativity (SON negativity, 170–270 ms), including when SON was the high-probability stimulus, and unlike the P440, this negativity did not habituate" — "early preattentive processing of speech sounds distinguishes SON from other names irrespective of short-term stimulus context, and ... this culminates in an OR only when SON is evaluated as being contextually meaningful." — [doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.2012.01363.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8986.2012.01363.x)
- Own name without consciousness (primary): Perrin et al. (2006), *Archives of Neurology* 63(4):562–569: "A P3 component was observed in response to the patient's name in all patients with locked-in syndrome, in all MCS patients, and in 3 of 5 patients in a VS"; P3 latency delayed in MCS/VS; "a P3 response does not necessarily reflect conscious perception." — [doi:10.1001/archneur.63.4.562](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneur.63.4.562)
- Alternative "functional monitoring" interpretation (primary): Röer et al. (2017) taboo-word study (KQ3) argues the ignored modality "is routinely monitored for potentially important stimuli (e.g., self-relevant or threatening information)". — [doi:10.1037/emo0000274](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000274)

### Inferences
- The one-third figure is stable across 1959–2001 replications; its dependence on WM span implies that noticing reflects *failures of maintained selection* (late filtering by control) at least as much as unconditional breakthrough — the high-span 20% is the floor for "truly unattended" semantic identification of a maximally self-relevant word.
- The 170–270 ms non-habituating own-name negativity (Tateuchi et al. 2012) and P3 in some vegetative patients (Perrin et al. 2006) support a stimulus-driven, context-independent recognition of a highly over-learned word form outside attention; the subsequent orienting (P3a/P440) is context-dependent (rare vs frequent) — the same detection-vs-orienting split seen for tones.
- Wood & Cowan's backward-speech result (no memory without an attention shift; shadowing errors ~15 s after the change) argues against extensive unattended semantic analysis of ordinary content.

### Gaps
- Moray (1959) itself could not be read; participant numbers (classically 12 subjects) and the exact proportion come from secondary summaries.
- Röer et al. (2013) effect size not obtained (journal not indexed in PubMed; abstract not retrieved).

## Key Question 5: Attenuation vs early/late selection — Broadbent, Treisman, Deutsch & Deutsch, Lachter et al. (2004), and the current state

### Takeaway
Lachter, Forster & Ruthruff (2004) revived early selection by arguing that every classic demonstration of unattended-word identification can be explained by brief "slippage" of attention, and showed no priming when slippage was prevented; dichotic priming studies agree that lexical priming from the unattended ear appears only when the prime is salient/segregated (distinct F0) or noticed; ERP/MEG work splits the difference — N400 semantic priming survives passive listening but not dichotic rejection, lexical memory-trace activation (~50–120 ms) is attention-independent, and in two-talker speech lexical and semantic tracking is restricted to the attended talker while acoustic-phonetic and even phrasal-structure tracking of the ignored talker persists.

### Cited Findings
- Broadbent (1958), *Perception and Communication*: the filter theory holds that "people do not process unattended stimuli beyond the analysis of basic physical properties" (as stated by Lachter et al. 2004). — [doi:10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.880](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.880)
- Treisman (1960), *QJEP* 12:242–248, "Contextual cues in selective listening" (primary): the classic demonstration that shadowers' responses can follow the message when it switches to the rejected ear — the basis of the attenuation model. Verified bibliographically only. — [doi:10.1080/17470216008416732](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470216008416732). Treisman (1964), *American Journal of Psychology* 77:206–219, "Verbal cues, language, and meaning in selective attention" — [PubMed 14141474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14141474/)
- Deutsch & Deutsch (1963), *Psychological Review* 70:80–90, "Attention: some theoretical considerations" — late-selection proposal (all inputs analysed for meaning; selection at response). — [PubMed 14027390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14027390/)
- Lachter, Forster & Ruthruff (2004), *Psychological Review* 111(4):880–913 (review + 5 primary experiments): "A careful review of this evidence ... reveals strong reasons to doubt that these irrelevant stimuli were in fact unattended ... When steps were taken to ensure that irrelevant stimuli were not attended, these stimuli produced no priming effects. Hence, the authors found no evidence that unattended stimuli can be identified. The results support a modern version of Broadbent's selective theory"; the apparent evidence is accounted for by rapid spatial-attention shifts ("slippage") to the irrelevant words. — [doi:10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.880](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.880)
- Dichotic lexical priming (primary, 6 experiments): Dupoux, Kouider & Mehler (2003), *JEP:HPP* 29(1):172–184: "When the prime was acoustically salient, a cross-channel priming effect emerged, and participants were aware of the prime. When the prime was less salient, no identity priming was found, and participants failed to notice the prime ... Results are inconsistent with models of late filtering, which predict equal priming irrespective of prime saliency." — [doi:10.1037//0096-1523.29.1.172](https://doi.org/10.1037//0096-1523.29.1.172)
- Priming from unattended *continuous* speech when voices are segregable (primary): Rivenez, Darwin & Guillaume (2006), *JASA* 119(6):4027–4040: "Primes speeded category detection by 25 ms when the two messages were in a difference F0 range ... disappeared when there was no F0 range difference ... replicated when participants were compelled to focus on the attended message in order to perform a second task." — [doi:10.1121/1.2190162](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.2190162)
- ERP in dichotic listening (primary, n=32): Bentin, Kutas & Hillyard (1995), *JEP:HPP* 21(1):54–67: "The N400 elicited by unattended words was insensitive to semantic manipulation. Recognition memory was better for attended than for unattended words. However, the percentage of false positives was elevated equally for lures that were semantically related to 'old' words, whether they had been attended or unattended. Words that were initially attended induced similar repetition effects in a lexical decision task as words that were initially unattended. Hence, both attended and unattended words are semantically processed and activate semantic representations." — [doi:10.1037//0096-1523.21.1.54](https://doi.org/10.1037//0096-1523.21.1.54)
- N400 priming under cross-modal inattention (primary): Relander, Rämä & Kujala (2009), *J Cogn Neurosci* 21(8):1511–1522: with attention directed to another modality (passive task), "A significant N400 effect was found in all tasks. The effect was stronger in the word and the phonological tasks than in the passive task ... The N400 effect in the passive task indicates that semantic priming occurs even when spoken words are not actively attended." — [doi:10.1162/jocn.2009.21127](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2009.21127)
- Lexical/syntactic MMN outside attention (review): Pulvermüller & Shtyrov (2006), *Progress in Neurobiology* 79(1):49–71: "even under attentional withdrawal, MMN size and topography reflect the activation of memory traces for language elements ... words elicit a larger MMN than meaningless pseudowords ... At the syntactic level, MMN size was found to reflect whether a word string conforms to abstract grammatical rules ... lexical, semantic and syntactic information can be processed by the central nervous system outside the focus of attention in a largely automatic manner." — [doi:10.1016/j.pneurobio.2006.04.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pneurobio.2006.04.004)
- Attention × lexicality timing (primary): Shtyrov, Kujala & Pulvermüller (2010), *J Cogn Neurosci* 22(7):1465–1478: "Under nonattend conditions, the word-elicited response (peaking at approximately 120 msec) was larger than that to pseudowords ... when attention was directed toward the auditory input, such word–pseudoword difference disappeared. Whereas responses to words seemed unchanged by attentional variation, early pseudoword responses were modulated significantly by attention ... attention effects on lexical processing accumulating after 150–200 msec." — [doi:10.1162/jocn.2009.21292](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2009.21292). Related: Shtyrov, Kimppa, Pulvermüller & Kujala (2011), *NeuroImage* 55:658–668, ERPs reflect the frequency of *unattended* spoken words — [PubMed 21146619](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21146619/); MacGregor, Pulvermüller, van Casteren & Shtyrov (2012), *Nature Communications* 3:711, word–pseudoword MEG dissociation "emerging ∼50 ms after acoustic information required for word identification first becomes available" — [doi:10.1038/ncomms1715](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1715)
- Two-talker neural tracking — lexical processing only for the attended talker (primary): Brodbeck, Hong & Simon (2018), *Current Biology* 28(24):3976–3983: a response reflecting "incremental integration of phonetic information for word identification" at ~114 ms relative to phoneme onset; in a cocktail-party condition "neural lexical processing of only the attended, but not the unattended, speech stream. Thus, while responses to acoustic features reflect attention through selective amplification of attended speech, responses consistent with a lexical processing model reveal categorically selective processing." — [doi:10.1016/j.cub.2018.10.042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.10.042)
- Acoustic features of the ignored talker are recovered (primary): Brodbeck, Jiao, Hong & Simon (2020), *PLoS Biology* 18(10):e3000883: "Early (approximately 70-millisecond) responses to nonoverlapping spectrotemporal features are seen for both talkers ... masked ... representations persist, but ... with an approximately 20-millisecond delay ... auditory cortex recovers acoustic features that are masked in the mixture, even if they occurred in the ignored speech." — [doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000883](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000883)
- Semantic (N400-like) tracking depends on comprehension/attention (primary): Broderick, Anderson, Di Liberto, Crosse & Lalor (2018), *Current Biology* 28(5):803–809: semantic-dissimilarity regression produced "a prominent negativity at a time lag of 200–600 ms on centro-parietal EEG channels"; across time-reversed speech, "cocktail party attention, and audiovisual speech-in-noise", the response "was very sensitive to whether or not subjects understood the speech they heard." — [doi:10.1016/j.cub.2018.01.080](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.01.080)
- Counter-evidence: phrasal structure of *task-irrelevant* speech is tracked (primary, MEG, n=30, Hebrew): Har-shai Yahav & Zion Golumbic (2021), *eLife* 10:e65096: with hierarchical frequency-tagging, "the phrasal structure of structured task-irrelevant stimuli was represented in the neural response in left inferior frontal and posterior parietal regions, indicating that selective attention does not fully eliminate linguistic processing of task-irrelevant speech. Additionally, neural tracking of to-be-attended speech in left inferior frontal regions was enhanced when competing with structured task-irrelevant stimuli." — [doi:10.7554/eLife.65096](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.65096)
- Method background (primary): Ding, Melloni, Zhang, Tian & Poeppel (2016), *Nature Neuroscience* 19(1):158–164: cortical tracking of word/phrase/sentence rhythms "dissociated from the encoding of acoustic cues and from the predictability of incoming words." — [doi:10.1038/nn.4186](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4186)
- Semantic auditory distraction depends on process similarity, not content (primary): Marsh, Hughes & Jones (2009), *Cognition* 110(1):23–38: meaningful irrelevant speech disrupted free recall by category more than meaningless sound, and more when semantically related, but "only when instructions emphasized recall by category rather than by serial order." — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2008.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2008.08.003)

### Inferences
- The current state is a qualified early-selection-with-leakage position: identification of unattended words to the point of *priming or memory* requires either attentional slippage (Lachter et al.) or sufficient perceptual segregation/salience of the irrelevant stream (Dupoux; Rivenez), while pre-lexical and lexical *memory-trace activation* (word vs pseudoword, frequency, own name) occurs automatically within ~50–200 ms and is then gated by attention for everything downstream (N400 context integration, lexical tracking in multi-talker scenes).
- Har-shai Yahav & Zion Golumbic (2021) is the main recent challenge: structure-level (phrase) tracking of ignored speech in left IFG — but it used syllable-rate structured stimuli with frequency tagging, not natural narrative; it conflicts in emphasis with Brodbeck et al. (2018) and Broderick et al. (2018) and is not yet reconciled in the literature.
- For the architecture: unattended text-like input should be expected to activate word-form/lexical nodes (and self-relevant or category-deviant items) but not to be integrated into sentence-level meaning unless admitted by the mask.

### Gaps
- Treisman (1960) quantitative details (number of subjects, frequency of ear-following intrusions) could not be retrieved from a primary source (publisher page 403).
- Broadbent (1958) is a book; verified only via Lachter et al.'s description.
- Ding et al. (2018, eLife, "Attention is required for knowledge-based sequential grouping") was not found in PubMed under the query used — UNVERIFIED and omitted.

## Key Question 6: Inattentional deafness — under what load do unattended sounds go unnoticed?

### Takeaway
A clearly audible 19-s "I'm a gorilla" voice is missed by ~70% of listeners attending to a conversation of the opposite sex (vs ~10% attending to same-sex voices), a brief tone is missed by 79% under high visual perceptual load, MEG shows the ~100-ms auditory evoked response and the P3 both suppressed under high visual load, yet manipulations of *auditory* perceptual load repeatedly fail to reduce auditory distractor processing — so cross-modal and feature-set-based inattention produce deafness, whereas within-audition capacity appears to have surplus.

### Cited Findings
- Dalton & Fraenkel (2012), *Cognition* 124(3):367–372 (primary): "the absence of attention can leave people 'deaf' to the presence of an 'auditory gorilla' which is audible for 19s and clearly noticeable under full attention ... the first ever demonstration of sustained inattentional deafness ... within a lifelike, three-dimensional auditory scene in which the unnoticed stimulus moves through the middle of several other dynamic auditory stimuli." — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2012.05.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2012.05.012). Secondary summaries of the numbers: male gorilla voice entering 33 s into a 69-s scene; 90% of those attending the men's conversation noticed vs 30% of those attending the women's; a second study moving the gorilla to the women's side still left 55% of women-attenders unaware. — [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/introducing-inattentional-deafness-noisy-gorilla-thats-missed); NBC coverage reports 45 participants and the same 70%/10% miss rates — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/body-odd/did-he-just-say-hes-gorilla-flna887652) (participant count differs between secondary sources: 40 vs 45)
- Macdonald & Lavie (2011), *Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics* 73(6):1780–1789 (primary, 3 experiments): cross-shape colour (low load) vs subtle line-length (high load) discrimination with a pure tone on the final trial; "Failures to notice the presence of this tone (i.e., inattentional deafness) reached a rate of 79% in the high-visual-load condition, significantly more than in the low-load condition." — [doi:10.3758/s13414-011-0144-4](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-011-0144-4)
- Raveh & Lavie (2015), *APP* 77(2):483–492 (primary, 4 experiments): "auditory detection sensitivity was consistently reduced with higher [visual] load, and ... this effect persisted even when the auditory detection response was made first ... and when the auditory stimulus was highly expected (50% present)." — [doi:10.3758/s13414-014-0776-2](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13414-014-0776-2)
- Neural locus (primary, MEG): Molloy, Griffiths, Chait & Lavie (2015), *J Neurosci* 35(49):16046–16054: high visual-search load "led to increased early visual evoked responses (within 100 ms from onset). - Auditory perceptual load does *not* gate auditory distractors (primary, 4 experiments): Murphy, Fraenkel & Dalton (2013), *Cognition* 129(2):345–355: "we consistently failed to find support for the role of perceptual load in auditory selective attention ... the auditory system ... is likely to have surplus capacity to process auditory information from other streams, regardless of the perceptual load in the attended stream ... the auditory modality acting as an 'early-warning' system." — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2013.07.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2013.07.014)
- Murphy, Spence & Dalton (2017), *Hearing Research* 352:40–48 (review): "less research has addressed the question of whether the same principles hold in audition and, to date, the existing literature provides a mixed picture ... this question still awaits resolution." — [doi:10.1016/j.heares.2017.02.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heares.2017.02.005)

- Pre-attentive organisation is robust to load (primary): Masutomi, Barascud, Kashino, McDermott & Chait (2016), *JEP:HPP* 42(3):386–400: repetition-based sound segregation under visual MOT, RSVP digit encoding or demanding auditory monitoring decoys showed "a small effect of attention that is nonspecific ... segregation performance remained high despite the presence of a concurrent, objectively demanding decoy task." — [doi:10.1037/xhp0000147](https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000147)

### Inferences
- Load that matters is cross-modal perceptual load (visual search/discrimination difficulty) and feature-set mismatch (attending to the other sex's voices), both of which suppress even early (~100 ms) auditory evoked responses — i.e., the mask can reach the sensory stage, consistent with Woldorff et al. (1991) in KQ1.
- Within audition, extra load on the attended stream does not reduce distractor processing (Murphy et al. 2013), which fits the "early-warning" role and the MMN's load-insensitivity (Berti & Schröger 2003; SanMiguel et al. 2008).
- The gorilla result is a *sustained* failure (19 s) despite the stimulus being a deviant human voice — unexpectedness alone does not guarantee capture when the attentional set is tuned to a different feature range; this bounds any "unconditional capture" claim.

### Gaps
- The exact Dalton & Fraenkel miss percentages and n come from press/BPS summaries, not the primary PDF (repository link looped); treat 70%/10% (or 30%/90% noticing) as approximate.
- No study was found that measures inattentional deafness as a function of *semantic* load (e.g., reading comprehension difficulty) rather than perceptual load.

## Key Question 7: Computational and quantitative models treating expectancy violation as a salience term

### Takeaway
The best quantitative work ties auditory brain responses to ideal-observer information measures: sustained MEG/EEG activity tracks the precision/predictability of tone sequences in naive listeners performing an incidental visual task (Barascud et al. 2016), Bayesian change-detection models fit human detection of statistical change and anchor EEG deviance responses (Skerritt-Davis & Elhilali 2018), and phasic pupil-linked arousal fires to structural violations but not to emerging regularity when the sounds are irrelevant (Zhao et al. 2019) — whereas predictability itself is not salient (Southwell et al. 2017), and feature-contrast saliency maps (Kayser et al. 2005) capture a different, non-predictive notion of salience.

### Cited Findings
- Barascud, Pearce, Griffiths, Friston & Chait (2016), *PNAS* 113(5):E616–E625 (primary + model): behaviour "on par with an ideal observer model" (PPM-style variable-order Markov model of Pearce); MEG/fMRI listeners "were naïve to the auditory stimuli and attended to an incidental visual task [n-back]; the observed brain responses can therefore be taken as reflecting largely automatic processes"; "estimates based on brain response latencies from naïve, passively listening participants (experiments 2 and 4) were essentially identical to those obtained behaviorally (experiment 1)"; "dynamic changes in tonic activity precisely correlate with the expected precision or predictability of ongoing auditory input"; sources: interaction of primary auditory cortex, hippocampus and inferior frontal gyrus; the authors argue the data "are also not consistent with explanations in terms of attention effects" because rapid *gain decreases* are hard to explain by attention. — [doi:10.1073/pnas.1508523113](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1508523113)
- Sohoglu & Chait (2016), *eLife* 5:e19113 (primary): "MEG in passive listeners and those actively detecting appearance events revealed increased sustained activity in auditory and parietal cortex in REG relative to RAND scenes, emerging ~400 ms of scene-onset ... The effect of temporal structure on appearance-evoked responses was delayed when listeners were focused on the scenes relative to when listening passively, consistent with the notion that attention reduces 'surprise'." — [doi:10.7554/eLife.19113](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.19113)
- Southwell et al. (2017) — EEG gain increase for REG vs RAND but no attentional capture by regularity; RAND more distracting (see KQ3). — [doi:10.1098/rstb.2016.0105](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0105)
- Zhao et al. (2019) — pupil-linked NE "interrupt" to violations only, framed as unexpected uncertainty (Yu–Dayan) tracked "on rapid time scales relevant to sensory signals" (see KQ3). — [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12048-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12048-1)
- Skerritt-Davis & Elhilali (2018), *PLoS Comput Biol* 14(5):e1006162 (primary + model): change detection in randomness of pitch sequences; "a perceptual model based on Bayesian inference shows a capacity in the brain to track higher-order statistics ... the inference model facilitates analysis of neural electroencephalography (EEG) responses ... ### Inferences
- A salience term of the form "prediction error weighted by precision" has direct empirical anchors in audition: (i) sustained responses that index precision and are present without attention (Barascud), (ii) phasic arousal that fires on model failure (REG→RAND, STEP) but not on model confirmation (RAND→REG) when irrelevant (Zhao), and (iii) behavioural capture that scales with expectancy violation and habituates as the violation becomes predictable (Vachon). Novelty-as-unfamiliarity and regularity-as-such are both empirically rejected as salience terms.
- The two-level structure (local MMN blind to global context; global P3b needs attention) suggests the salience term computed outside attention should be driven by *local* transition-probability error, while higher-order error only arises for content already admitted to working memory.
- Attention appears to "reduce surprise" (Sohoglu & Chait 2016) and to sharpen higher-level prediction (Chennu 2013) — i.e., the mask does not just pass content; it also changes the predictive model's precision for admitted content.

### Gaps
- I found no published computational model of *behavioural deviance distraction* (RT cost as a function of surprise) fitted to the Escera/Parmentier paradigms; Zhao et al. (2019) and Barascud et al. (2016) are physiological, and Southwell et al. (2017) is behavioural but tests regularity, not violation magnitude.
- An auditory "SUN"-style natural-statistics saliency model attributed to Tsuchida & Cottrell (2012) could not be located in Crossref under that title — UNVERIFIED, omitted.

## Synthesis for the two design questions

### Takeaway
(1) Yes: prediction and prediction error demonstrably operate outside attention, but only for local, short-window regularities — single features, inter-feature relations, short patterns/abstract rules and over-learned word forms (own name, word vs pseudoword) — not for seconds-scale global rules or for sentence-level semantic integration; (2) an unexpected unattended item does capture attention, but by expectancy violation rather than novelty, through a two-stage process in which detection/orienting signals are near-unconditional while the behavioural switch is contingent on attentional set, informational value, working-memory/encoding load and foreknowledge, and habituates once the violation is itself predictable.

### Inferences
- For the serial-reading architecture: a predicted-image subtraction over the *whole* activated field (outside the seven regions) is licensed by the auditory evidence, provided the prediction is local (previous one or few items; feature/word-form level) and its output is treated as a candidate salience signal rather than as admitted content. A second, richer prediction (discourse/global) should live only inside the attended/STM contents.
- The capture rule suggested by the evidence: salience = magnitude of local prediction error × (1 − predictability of the error itself) × gain, where gain is lowered by focal-task load and by distance from the current attentional set (feature range), raised for over-learned self-relevant forms, and where the MMN-like detection is always computed but the mask-reallocation is thresholded. Habituation of capture to repeated rule-governed deviations (Vachon) is naturally produced if the "predictability of the error" term is learned at a higher order.
- The two reviewed asymmetries — regularity emergence does not capture (Southwell; Zhao) whereas violation does — argue that priming/confirmation should *reduce* salience, and only unexplained error should feed the mask.

### Gaps
- No single study simultaneously manipulates attention, load and expectancy violation level in audition with both ERP and behavioural measures across feature, pattern and semantic levels; the hierarchy sketched above is assembled across paradigms.
- Quantitative transfer to text (word-by-word) is untested: the auditory literature's "local" window is hundreds of ms to a few seconds of tones/syllables, and whether word-level predictions in reading behave like local transition probabilities (pre-attentive) or like global rules (attention-dependent) is not established here.
