## 1. Serial order representation: chaining vs. positional vs. competitive-queuing codes; do animals learn item-item links or positions on an abstract ordered scale?

### Takeaway
Established: simple item-to-item chaining fails as an account of human immediate serial recall and of monkeys' learned lists; the data favour position/rank codes read out by a competitive-queuing process, and monkeys carry ordinal-position knowledge across lists and across tasks. Contested: whether that code is a spatial "line", a rank category, or an emergent conjunctive code, and how far non-primates share it (classic pigeon data look like end-anchored rules; later tests show positional knowledge in pigeons too).

### Cited Findings

Framing and human models
- **[S]** Lashley's (1951) formulation of the serial-order problem posits that all serial elements are represented in parallel before the action begins, and that errors are most likely between elements with nearly equal prior activation; chaining of each response to the next cannot explain this. — [Averbeck, Chafee, Crowe & Georgopoulos 2002, PNAS 99:13172](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.162485599)
- **[R]** Chaining makes a testable prediction: in lists alternating confusable and non-confusable letters, a confusable item should raise errors on the item that follows it. Three experiments found the opposite: errors were confined to the confusable items ("sawtooth" curves) and confusability did not affect what followed. The authors conclude the pattern is "inconsistent with the chaining hypothesis", and add three constraints any model must meet: transpositions are mostly local, confusable items swap with each other, repeats are rare and far apart. — [Henson, Norris, Page & Baddeley 1996, QJEP 49A:80](https://doi.org/10.1080/713755612)
- **[B]** The main non-chaining model families: (a) positional models that bind each item to a position code, e.g. start and end markers in the Start-End Model — [Henson 1998, Cognitive Psychology 36:73](https://doi.org/10.1006/cogp.1998.0685); (b) context/timing-signal models with competitive queuing — [Burgess & Hitch 1999, Psychological Review 106:551](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.3.551); (c) ordinal models with a primacy gradient of activation plus suppression of each recalled item and no position codes — [Page & Norris 1998, Psychological Review 105:761](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.105.4.761-781); (d) oscillator-based temporal context — [Brown, Preece & Hulme 2000, Psychological Review 107:127](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.107.1.127). The chaining family is represented by TODAM — [Lewandowsky & Murdock 1989, Psychological Review 96:25](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.96.1.25).
- **[B]** A recurrent network trained on serial recall, with no built-in position markers and no item-item chaining, reproduces the benchmark error patterns by developing conjunctive item-by-rank codes; it is the main "emergent" alternative to explicit positional models. — [Botvinick & Plaut 2006, Psychological Review 113:201](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.113.2.201)
- **[R]** A cross-domain review (verbal, visual, spatial short-term memory) concludes that sequences in all three are planned and controlled by competitive queuing; for verbal memory the supported principles are a primacy gradient, position marking, response suppression and cumulative matching, with weaker direct evidence in the visual and spatial domains. — [Hurlstone, Hitch & Baddeley 2014, Psychological Bulletin 140:339](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034221)

Neural evidence for parallel planning and rank codes
- **[R]** Monkeys copying geometric shapes: prefrontal ensemble patterns for all upcoming segments were present before drawing began, and the rank of each segment's representational strength predicted its serial position; error trials fit Lashley's near-equal-strength prediction. — [Averbeck et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.162485599)
- **[R]** Rank-selective neurons ("first", "second", "third") were found in SMA, pre-SMA, supplementary eye field and dorsolateral PFC of macaques; they were "rank generalists", preferring the same rank whether the monkey ordered three actions or three objects. — [Berdyyeva & Olson 2010, J Neurophysiol 104:141](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00639.2009)
- **[B]** Lateral prefrontal neurons code the temporal rank of objects, object identity, and their conjunction. — [Ninokura, Mushiake & Tanji 2004, J Neurophysiol 91:555](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00694.2003)
- **[R]** Two-photon imaging of thousands of macaque PFC neurons during spatial sequence memory: the delay-period state space decomposes into a sum of low-dimensional "rank subspaces", one per ordinal position, each storing the location held at that rank; the subspaces generalize to novel sequences, and item-rank binding is a population-level property, not a single-neuron one. — [Xie et al. 2022, Science 375:632](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm0204)
- **[R]** Human MEG (N = 43 across two studies): during rest, replay follows a learned rule-defined order, not the experienced order (peak lag 40 ms). Decoders reveal factorized codes: an abstract position-in-sequence code and a sequence-identity code that transfer to new objects; during replay the position code leads the item code by about 50 ms, and position-code sequences replay before the new objects have even been seen. — [Liu, Dolan, Kurth-Nelson & Behrens 2019, Cell 178:640](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6657653/)
- **[R]** Monkey intraparietal sulcus: enumerating items one by one in time and estimating a simultaneous dot array engage different neuron populations; a third population codes the resulting cardinality regardless of how it was delivered. Sequential position and set size converge only at a late stage. — [Nieder, Diester & Tudusciuc 2006, Science 313:1431](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1130308)
- **[B]** A widely used taxonomy separates five kinds of sequence knowledge with different mechanisms and substrates: transition/timing knowledge, chunking, ordinal knowledge, algebraic patterns, and nested tree structures (the last proposed as possibly human-specific). Item-item transition learning and ordinal-position knowledge are treated as distinct systems. — [Dehaene, Meyniel, Wacongne, Wang & Pallier 2015, Neuron 88:2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.019)

Animals: simultaneous chaining
- **[R]** In the simultaneous-chaining paradigm all list items are displayed at once in a spatial arrangement that changes every trial, with no feedback after individual responses (only after the full sequence), so neither a motor chain nor stimulus-response feedback can guide performance; the subject must consult an internal representation. — [Terrace 2010, Comparative Cognition & Behavior Reviews 5:23](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/); original pigeon method in [Straub & Terrace 1981](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03209775)
- **[R]** Pigeons: learned 4-item colour lists (chance about .04) and 5-item lists, but on two-item subset tests from a 5-item list they were above chance on only 7 of 10 pairs, failing the three interior pairs BC, BD, CD. Their latencies did not vary with the list position of the first item or with the gap between items. Terrace's summary rule set: respond first to A, last to E, to anything else by default. — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[R]** Monkeys on the same subset tests order all pairs including interior ones. Latency to the first item rises with that item's position in the list, and (in the 5-item cebus data) latency to the second item rises with the number of intervening items, which Terrace reads as scanning an internal representation from the start. — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/); primary monkey data in [D'Amato & Colombo 1988, JEP:ABP 14:131](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.14.2.131) **[B]**
- **[R]** Derived-list test, rhesus monkeys: after four 4-item photograph lists, new lists were built taking one item from each old list. When each item kept its old ordinal position the list was acquired with almost no errors (criterion met in the minimum 120 trials; one animal needed 180 on one list); when positions were changed the list was as hard as a new one. Item-item associations predict no difference, because no adjacent pair was ever trained. — [Chen, Swartz & Terrace 1997, Psychological Science 8:80](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1997.tb00687.x); numbers from [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[R]** Serial expertise: 4 naive rhesus monkeys learned 3-, 4- and 7-item photograph lists by trial and error (chance 1/6, 1/24, 1/5,040). Successive 7-item lists took a mean of 31.5, 17.5, 13 and 12.25 sessions. Tested on all pairs of the 28 items from four 7-item lists, they ordered pairs drawn from different lists correctly 91% of the time on first presentation, i.e. by ordinal position alone. — [Terrace, Son & Brannon 2003, Psychological Science 14:66](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.01420); session counts from [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[R]** In those pair tests accuracy rose and latency fell with ordinal distance between the items, within and between lists. Terrace contrasts the predictions: an associative chain predicts worse and slower performance at larger distances (more links to traverse); a spatial representation predicts the reverse; the spatial prediction was confirmed. A "magnitude" effect also appeared: latency rose with the position of the first item (median 2,180 ms for AB vs. 3,374 ms for FG). — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[B]** Macaques trained on several three-image lists treat images as members of ordinal categories: sequences that mix items from different lists but preserve each item's ordinal slot are performed readily, and intrusion errors come from the same ordinal slot of another list. — [Orlov, Yakovlev, Hochstein & Zohary 2000, Nature 404:77](https://doi.org/10.1038/35003571)
- **[R]** Pigeons revisited: trained on three 4-item lists and tested on derived lists, pigeons performed markedly better when ordinal positions were maintained than when they were changed, paralleling the primate result; the authors conclude ordinal knowledge is "not unique to the primate lineage". — [Scarf & Colombo 2011, JEP:ABP 37:483](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023695); see also the title-level claim of a positional coding mechanism after multiple three-item lists in [Scarf & Colombo 2010, Animal Cognition 13:653](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-010-0315-z) **[B]**
- **[R]** Three rhesus monkeys transferred list knowledge in both directions between simultaneous chaining and pairwise transitive-inference tasks, with benefits from the first transfer trial; error patterns in both tasks were best fit by items occupying positions with Gaussian uncertainty on one linear continuum. The authors note that to perform a simultaneous chain perfectly a subject "need only know the ordinal position of each item and the last item it touched". — [Jensen, Altschul, Danly & Terrace 2013, PLoS ONE 8:e70285](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3729468)

A human overlearned list (the alphabet) shows both signatures
- **[B]** "What letter comes after/before X?" latencies show a sawtooth pattern consistent with storage in chunks searched forward from the chunk's start; "before" questions are slower than "after". — [Klahr, Chase & Lovelace 1983, JEP:LMC 9:462](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.9.3.462)
- **[S]** The same list yields a symbolic distance effect in order judgments (far-apart letters are compared faster), and latency grows with the alphabetic position of the first letter. — [Hamilton & Sanford 1978, QJEP 30:33](https://doi.org/10.1080/14640747808400652), as described in [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)

### Inferences
- The literature does not support "A is followed by B" as the stored unit of a learned list in primates. What transfers is a per-item position or rank value. That matters for any design that hopes to grow conceptual order out of successor links: the animal and human data say the ordered scale is carried by item-to-position bindings, with successor relations derivable from them, not the other way round.
- Chain-like and line-like signatures coexist and depend on the question asked. "What comes next" and production tasks show forward-directional, chunked access (alphabet, counting-on); comparison tasks show distance effects. This is a task dissociation inside one memorized list, and it weakens any claim that one read-out mechanism serves both.
- The neural data (rank-generalist neurons, rank subspaces, factorized position codes in replay) show that position is represented separately from content and reused across sequences. That is a structural code a second system could align with magnitude; it is not itself a magnitude code.
- Species differences are graded, not categorical: the classic pigeon failure on interior pairs was not replicated with derived-list tests.

### Gaps
- Lashley (1951), Houghton (1990, competitive queuing), and Ebbinghaus's derived-list work were not retrieved as primary sources; they are cited here only through later papers.
- Jensen, Terrace & Ferrera-group papers specifically on absolute vs. relative ordinal position (cited in their 2019 review as Kao et al. 2018) could not be located before the search budget ran out.
- No source was read on whether rank-selective neurons in monkey frontal cortex respond to numerosity, i.e. whether rank and cardinal-number tuning are the same cells. Nieder et al. 2006 suggests partial separation in parietal cortex only.
- Model details for Henson (1998), Burgess & Hitch (1999), Page & Norris (1998) and Botvinick & Plaut (2006) are from prior knowledge, not re-read.

## 2. Transitive inference: is an inferred linear order built from adjacent-pair learning, and by what mechanism?

### Takeaway
Established: training on adjacent pairs alone yields correct choices on untrained pairs, with a symbolic distance effect, in every vertebrate tested and in simple neural networks. Contested: the mechanism. Value-transfer and other model-free accounts fail several critical tests in monkeys, and human fMRI shows a one-dimensional rank code in frontoparietal cortex; but recent theory shows transitive generalization follows from ordinary learning whenever items are coded additively, so the behaviour alone does not prove an explicit "mental line".

### Cited Findings

Classic results and the associative challenge
- **[B]** Children of 4-6 years made transitive inferences about stick length (e.g. B vs. D after training A>B, B>C, C>D, D>E) once they had been trained to remember the premises; earlier failures were attributed to memory, not logic. — [Bryant & Trabasso 1971, Nature 232:456](https://doi.org/10.1038/232456a0)
- **[S]** Squirrel monkeys solved the five-term non-verbal version, opening the animal literature. — [McGonigle & Chalmers 1977, Nature 267:694](https://doi.org/10.1038/267694a0), as described in [Jensen, Terrace & Ferrera 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710392/)
- **[S]** Pigeons also choose B over D; von Fersen and colleagues explained this without inference through value transfer: the unreinforced member of each training pair picks up some value from its reinforced partner, producing a graded value ordering. — [von Fersen, Wynne, Delius & Staddon 1991, JEP:ABP 17:334](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.17.3.334), as described in [Jensen et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710392/)
- **[R]** A review of the animal literature concluded reinforcement-based models were then in the stronger empirical and theoretical position, and that animal transitive inference should be treated as a product of reinforcement history. — [Vasconcelos 2008, Behavioural Processes 78:313](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2008.02.017)
- **[B]** A computational analysis argued that rodent "transitive" choices and their loss after hippocampal damage can be produced by associative strengths over conjunctive codes, without relational flexibility. — [Frank, Rudy & O'Reilly 2003, Hippocampus 13:341](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.10084)
- **[R]** Transitive inference has been reported in every vertebrate species tested (per two reviews cited there). — [Jensen et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710392/). Title-level reports extend it to fish and to paper wasps **[B]** — [Grosenick, Clement & Fernald 2007, Nature 445:429](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05511); [Tibbetts et al. 2019, Biology Letters 15:20190015](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2019.0015)

Evidence against purely associative accounts (monkeys)
- **[R]** Review and simulation of six models (model-free: Q-learning, value transfer, REMERGE; model-based: RL-Elo, sequential Monte Carlo, Betasort) against monkey data. Q-learning shows no transfer; value transfer gives a distance effect far weaker than observed; REMERGE gives a reversed distance effect at transfer. Value transfer wrongly predicts disruption after massed presentation of one pair (Lazareva & Wasserman 2006, 2012). List linking (two lists joined by one new pair behave as one long list; Treichler & Van Tilburg 1996) had no computational account. Verdict: "Model-based approaches perform better under a wider range of scenarios, but no single model explains the full scope of behaviors". — [Jensen, Terrace & Ferrera 2019, Frontiers in Neuroscience 13:878](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710392/); primary sources [Lazareva & Wasserman 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2011.12.001) **[B]**, [Treichler & Van Tilburg 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.22.1.105) **[B]**
- **[R]** Four rhesus monkeys, 7-item lists, adjacent pairs only in training, all 21 pairs at test. When reward size was made to run against list rank (correct choices of early items paid least), learning slowed but monkeys still reached above-chance rule-based choices with a distance effect larger than the reward effect; model-free Q-learners could not exceed about 60% in that condition. The inferred representation late in training resembled a uniformly spaced linear order, not expected reward. — [Jensen, Alkan, Ferrera & Terrace 2019, Science Advances 5:eaaw2089](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7032924/)
- **[R]** Betasort represents each item as a beta distribution over a unit line (position plus uncertainty) and updates all items' positions implicitly on each trial. — [Jensen et al. 2015, PLoS Comput Biol 11:e1004523](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004523), as summarized in [Jensen et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710392/)
- **[R]** Dorsolateral prefrontal neurons in two monkeys doing a demanding transitive-inference task carried signals supporting "an abstract, mental schema of ordered items", reported as independent of the reward value experienced for single items. — [Brunamonti et al. 2016, J Neurosci 36:1223](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1473-15.2016)

Hippocampus
- **[R]** Rats learned four overlapping odour discriminations. Rats with the hippocampus disconnected (fornix transection, or perirhinal/entorhinal removal) learned the premise pairs normally but showed no transitive inference on the untrained pair. — [Dusek & Eichenbaum 1997, PNAS 94:7109](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC21293)
- **[B]** In humans, hippocampal damage impairs transitive inference, and in controls success on the critical pair goes with awareness of the hierarchy. — [Smith & Squire 2005, J Neurosci 25:10138](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2731-05.2005). Inference across more distant items improves after a delay containing sleep. — [Ellenbogen et al. 2007, PNAS 104:7723](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0700094104)
- **[B]** Two hippocampal-system models do transitive inference without a stored line: REMERGE (recurrent interaction among stored pair memories at retrieval) — [Kumaran & McClelland 2012, Psychological Review 119:573](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028681); and the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine, which learns reusable structural codes in entorhinal cortex and binds them to items in hippocampus — [Whittington et al. 2020, Cell 183:1249](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.024). Humans who learn two social hierarchies by pairwise comparison combine them into a two-dimensional map in hippocampal-entorhinal cortex — [Park, Miller, Nili, Ranganath & Boorman 2020, Neuron 107:1226](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.06.030)

Neural geometry in humans
- **[R]** N = 34 adults learned two sets of six novel objects by adjacent-pair comparisons only (training accuracy 95.6%). In the scanner, untrained non-adjacent pairs were answered more accurately than trained adjacent pairs (96.7% vs. 86.0%); accuracy rose about 3.4% and RT fell about 72 ms per rank of distance. Before any linking, people spontaneously matched ranks across the two sets. Multivoxel patterns in posterior parietal and dorsomedial prefrontal cortex formed a one-dimensional rank line, with the two sets as overlapping parallel lines and cross-set decoding of rank. After only 20 trials on the single pair that links the two sets (items 6 and 7), the neural code elongated into a single line for higher performers; the amount of elongation correlated with behavioural integration (r = 0.49 PPC, r = 0.40 dmPFC). — [Nelli, Braun, Dumbalska, Saxe & Summerfield 2023, Neuron 111:1504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10618408/)
- **[R]** Same paper, modelling: a two-layer network trained by online gradient descent on adjacent pairs reproduced human choice matrices (r = 0.98) and formed the same overlapping rank lines in its hidden layer. It could not assemble the two lists after the link (it learned the link as an isolated exception) unless updates were weighted by a learned certainty about each relation. — [Nelli et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10618408/)
- **[R]** EEG, N = 46: the multivariate pattern evoked by six arbitrary images whose reward probabilities (0.05 to 0.95) were learned by trial and error aligned with the pattern evoked by the digits 1 to 6 (reliable about 300-650 ms); the worst bandit looked like "1", the best like "6". The shared code was low-dimensional. The authors' reading: learned ranks are mapped onto an existing abstract magnitude scale. — [Luyckx, Nili, Spitzer & Summerfield 2019, eLife 8:e42816](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405242/)
- **[B]** Title-level convergent imaging: frontal and parietal activation in human transitive inference — [Acuna et al. 2002, Cerebral Cortex 12:1312](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/12.12.1312); parietal cortex tracks the symbolic distance effect in relational reasoning — [Hinton, Dymond, von Hecker & Evans 2010, Neuroscience 168:138](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2010.03.052); numbers and verbal transitive series have "overlapping and distinct" neural representations — [Prado, Noveck & Van Der Henst 2010, Cerebral Cortex 20:720](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhp137)

Recent theory: transitive generalization without an explicit order
- **[R]** Any learner with an additive representation of the two presented items "necessarily implements a ranking system" and so generalizes transitively. Partly conjunctive representations still do so under norm-minimizing learning, governed by one "conjunctivity factor" (0 = additive, 1 = fully conjunctive; a random ReLU network is about 0.15). Fully conjunctive codes (each pair its own unit) cannot generalize transitively. The symbolic distance effect, terminal-item effect and a memorization effect fall out. Deep networks in the feature-learning ("rich") regime do worse and can violate the ranking pattern. — [Lippl, Kay, Jensen, Ferrera & Abbott 2024, PNAS 121:e2314511121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11252811/)
- **[R]** Recurrent networks trained on a working-memory version of the task generalize perfectly "despite lacking overt transitive structure prior to training", reproduce classic behavioural signatures, and come in more than one solution type; a large human experiment was inconsistent with the most intuitive solution class. — [Kay et al. 2024, PLoS Comput Biol 20:e1011954](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011954)
- **[R]** Simple reinforcement learning infers transitive relations better when it updates only the winner (or only the loser) of each pair; four human experiments (n = 145) support such asymmetric updating, which also yields compressed value scales. — [Ciranka et al. 2022, Nature Human Behaviour 6:555](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01263-w)

Distance effects
- **[B]** The numerical distance effect: time to pick the larger of two digits falls as their difference grows. — [Moyer & Landauer 1967, Nature 215:1519](https://doi.org/10.1038/2151519a0)
- **[R]** The same accuracy and latency functions (distance and magnitude effects) are obtained from monkeys and humans ordering arbitrary photograph lists and ordering numerosities; Terrace reports the functions for college students and monkeys as indistinguishable in form. — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)

### Inferences
- Yes, a linear order is built from adjacent-pair learning. The minimal sufficient mechanism is not chaining and not explicit logic: it is assigning each item its own scalar (or low-dimensional) value that pairwise training pushes apart. Lippl et al. make this exact: item-wise (additive) coding gives transitivity for free; pair-wise (conjunctive) coding, which is what "A is followed by B" link storage amounts to, gives none.
- This creates a tension for the shared-mechanism hypothesis. A long-term store of successor links is the conjunctive extreme; an ordered concept needs the additive extreme. The two can share a learning rule, but the representation that supports inference is a different one from the representation that supports recall of the next item.
- Monkey data (reward-gradient reversal, list linking, cross-task transfer) and human data (rapid re-assembly after one linking pair) need something beyond incremental value learning: uncertainty-weighted updating that moves whole sets of items together. That is the closest thing in this literature to a general "structure" operation.
- Luyckx et al. and Nelli et al. are the strongest positive evidence for a shared ordered code: arbitrary learned ranks and number symbols evoke aligned patterns, in the same dorsal frontoparietal regions that carry number.

### Gaps
- Bryant & Trabasso, McGonigle & Chalmers and von Fersen et al. were not re-read as primary sources.
- No lesion or inactivation study was found that tests whether the frontoparietal rank line (Nelli et al.) is necessary for inference, as opposed to the hippocampal system (Dusek & Eichenbaum). The two literatures point at different structures and were not reconciled in anything read.
- Parietal single-unit recordings during transitive inference from the Ferrera group (2020 onward) and any 2025-2026 follow-ups to Nelli et al. were not searched.
- Value-transfer vs. relational accounts in pigeons specifically (Lazareva & Wasserman) are reported only through the Jensen et al. review.

## 3. The count list as a placeholder structure: how children map the memorized count sequence onto quantities, and whether a successor function is induced from the list

### Takeaway
Established: children recite the count list and count objects correctly long before the words carry cardinal meaning, then acquire "one", "two", "three" (sometimes "four") one at a time before a change in which counting is used to produce any set. Contested: whether that change is an induction of the successor function over the list. Later work shows many new cardinal-principle knowers lack successor and ordering knowledge, that it generalizes only around 5.5-6 years, and that it tracks mastery of the generative rules of the count list.

### Cited Findings

The stage sequence
- **[R]** Around age 2 children recite the counting words in order "without numerical meaning" (compared by the authors to reciting the ABCs); around 2;6 they become one-knowers; over the following months they pass through two-, three- and sometimes four-knower stages before grasping the cardinality principle. — [Odic, Le Corre & Halberda 2015, Cognition 138:102](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.01.008) (introduction)
- **[B]** The original evidence is from Give-a-Number and How-Many tasks: 2- and 3-year-olds who count flawlessly still grab a handful when asked for "three", and learn the first few number-word meanings in order over roughly a year. — [Wynn 1990, Cognition 36:155](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277%2890%2990003-3); [Wynn 1992, Cognitive Psychology 24:220](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285%2892%2990008-P)
- **[B]** The count sequence itself develops through levels: an unsegmented string, an unbreakable list that must be started from "one", a breakable chain that can be entered anywhere, a numerable chain whose words can themselves be counted, and a bidirectional chain usable forward and backward. — [Fuson 1988, Children's Counting and Concepts of Number](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-3754-9)
- **[R]** The same one-, two-, three-knower trajectory is found in Tsimane' children (Bolivian farmer-foragers), but substantially delayed; the authors conclude the stages, not their timing, are a fundamental property of number-concept acquisition. — [Piantadosi, Jara-Ettinger & Gibson 2014, Developmental Science 17:553](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12078)
- **[R]** Children learning Slovenian or Saudi Arabic, languages with dual marking, learn "two" faster than in any previously studied language even with less counting experience, so number words heard in quantificational structures outside the count routine also drive early meanings. — [Almoammer et al. 2013, PNAS 110:18448](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1313652110)

Bootstrapping and the successor function
- **[B]** Carey's Quinian bootstrapping account: the count list is first learned as an ordered set of meaningless placeholders; "one" to "four" get meanings from representations of small sets; the child then notices that the order of those known words matches sets differing by one and generalizes: the next word names a set with one more. — [Carey 2009, The Origin of Concepts](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195367638.001.0001)
- **[R]** 3- and 4-year-olds estimating sets without counting show the signature of small-set ("enriched parallel individuation") representations for "one" to "four"; the authors conclude "analog magnitudes play no role" in acquiring the counting principles. Children could not estimate sets beyond four without counting until about six months after becoming cardinal-principle knowers. — [Le Corre & Carey 2007, Cognition 105:395](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880652)
- **[R]** N = 73 children aged 2;10 to 4;3; 71 could count to ten and all to at least eight. Direction task (after adding an item, is it the earlier or later number word?): no-, one-, two- and three-knowers at chance; four-knowers and high-numeral (cardinal-principle) knowers above chance. Unit task (adding one means exactly the next word, adding two means the word after): only high-numeral knowers above chance (t(28) = 4.61, p < .001); four-knowers failed. Most subset-knowers nonetheless counted well and knew the last word answers "how many". Conclusion: only cardinal-principle knowers "understand how counting implements the successor function". — [Sarnecka & Carey 2008, Cognition 108:662](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8dh3972h)

Challenges to a single semantic induction
- **[R]** 84 children classified as cardinal-principle knowers: many did not reliably know which of two counted numbers is larger or that successive numbers differ by one; this knowledge tracked how high they could count and their estimation skill, and was item-specific (better for 5 than for 25). The authors find little support for an induction over the whole list. — [Davidson, Eng & Barner 2012, Cognition 123:162](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22245033/)
- **[S]** Children generate successors for all numbers and judge that every number has a successor only around 5.5 to 6 years, about two years after becoming cardinal-principle knowers. — [Cheung, Rubenson & Barner 2017, Cognitive Psychology 92:22](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2016.11.002); the age is corroborated in the abstract of [Chu, Cheung, Schneider, Sullivan & Barner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12875) **[R]**
- **[R]** N = 127 preschoolers, pretest-posttest: children can acquire the cardinal principle without understanding succession or exact ordering; cardinal-principle knowledge predicted later improvement on both. Title: "Meaning before order". — [Spaepen, Gunderson, Gibson, Goldin-Meadow & Levine 2018, Cognition 180:59](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.06.012)
- **[R]** N = 136 children aged 3.5 to 6: successor knowledge was related both to mastery of the rules that generate the count list and to "+1" arithmetic training, but children with strong successor knowledge still struggled with the arithmetic form, pointing to extraction of recursive counting rules as the likelier source. — [Schneider, Sullivan, Guo & Barner 2021, Child Development 92:e476](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13524)
- **[R]** 4- and 5-year-olds who show a productive counting rule (can generate number words beyond their rote range from the decade structure) are more likely to believe numbers never end; such rule knowledge was not directly linked to successor knowledge itself. — [Chu et al. 2020, Cognitive Science 44:e12875](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12875). A cross-linguistic test of whether children use language structure to discover the recursive counting rules is in [Schneider et al. 2020, Cognitive Psychology 117:101263](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2019.101263) **[B]**
- **[R]** Children scored as five- or six-knowers on Give-a-Number can compare only the numbers they know, like one- to four-knowers; the authors argue cardinal-principle knowledge is over-attributed by current criteria. — [Krajcsi & Reynvoet 2024, Developmental Science 27:e13430](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.13430)
- **[R]** 63 Tsimane' children aged 4-11: 14 of 26 full counters were perfect on exact-equality transformations (the rest erred, mostly on substitution), while 6 of 37 subset-knowers were perfect (more than chance). Conclusion: "mastery of counting is neither necessary nor sufficient for understanding the logic of exact number". — [Jara-Ettinger, Piantadosi, Spelke, Levy & Gibson 2017, Developmental Science 20:e12459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5319921/)
- **[B]** Title-level related findings: subset-knowers use one-to-one correspondence to track set identity but not numerical equality — [Izard, Streri & Spelke 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogpsych.2014.01.004); knowing the preceding number, not only the next, marks mature understanding of the sequence — [Sella & Lucangeli 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2019.104104); a review of current theoretical challenges — [Sella, Slusser, Odic & Krajcsi 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12428)

Critiques and alternatives
- **[S]** Rips, Asmuth and Bloomfield argue that bootstrapping presupposes what it explains: the first few word meanings plus the list are equally consistent with cyclic (modular) number systems, so the child must already have the structure of the natural numbers to pick the right generalization. — [Rips, Asmuth & Bloomfield 2006, Cognition 101:B51](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2005.12.001), as characterized in [Piantadosi, Tenenbaum & Goodman 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** Their BBS target article holds that natural-number concepts do not clearly grow out of infant quantitative abilities and proposes a top-down route through mathematical schemas. — [Rips, Bloomfield & Asmuth 2008, BBS 31:623](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X08005566). A reply defending inductive learning of the natural numbers: [Margolis & Laurence 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2007.03.003) **[B]**
- **[B]** The principles-first tradition (Gelman & Gallistel 1978) holds that counting principles guide learning from the start. Its later form posits an innate representation of exactly ONE plus a recursive successor rule as the generative basis of integer concepts. — [Leslie, Gelman & Gallistel 2008, TiCS 12:213](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2008.03.004)
- **[B]** Spelke attributes natural number to the combination of core systems through natural language, emphasizing the generative grammar of number expressions over the rote count routine. — [Spelke 2017, Language Learning and Development 13:147](https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2016.1263572)
- **[R]** Carey and Barner's two-phase restatement: first, small exact meanings learned item by item; second, those meanings combined with "recursive counting procedures". They reject approximate magnitudes as the origin of integer concepts both empirically and in principle. — [Carey & Barner 2019, TiCS 23:823](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2019.07.004); the non-perceptual, language-and-procedure origin is argued at length in [Barner 2017, J Child Language 44:553](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305000917000058) **[B]**

A non-human induction from list position
- **[R]** Alex, a grey parrot who could label sets of 1-6 and Arabic numerals 1-6 and order those numerals, was taught vocal labels for the numerals 7 and 8 and their order relative to 6, never with sets. Untrained, he ordered 7 and 8 against the other numerals (21/26) and then labelled sets of seven and eight objects (22/30 on first trials; chance 1/3) and picked out sets by "seven" and "eight" (12/12). The authors call this an inference of cardinal value from ordinal position; they note it was "not fully warranted by his training", rests on one animal, and that with the most conservative chance level the seven-item result was p = .07. — [Pepperberg & Carey 2012, Cognition 125:219](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3434310/)
- **[R]** Same paper: the chimpanzees Ai and Sheba showed no such inference; their numerals were trained piecemeal, order was taught separately, and neither showed savings when learning larger numerals. — [Pepperberg & Carey 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3434310/)

### Inferences
- The count list is the clearest real case of a rote serial list that precedes and then scaffolds an ordered concept. But the developmental record says the list alone does nothing for one to two years: reciting and one-to-one tagging coexist with no cardinal meaning beyond three or four.
- What changes at the cardinal-principle transition is procedural (use counting to make a set) more than conceptual. Explicit order and successor knowledge come after meaning, are item-specific at first, and generalize when the child masters the generative morphology of number words. So "sequence becomes conceptual order" happens in at least three separable steps: list, list-to-set procedure, then abstract successor rule.
- The Tsimane' data cut both ways for a shared mechanism: the stage order is universal, yet exact-equality logic and counting mastery come apart in both directions.
- Alex is an existence proof that ordinal position on a symbol list can be converted into cardinal meaning by a non-human, but only after cardinal meanings for the earlier symbols already existed. The list supplied the extension, not the first meanings.

### Gaps
- Carey (2009), Gelman & Gallistel (1978), Wynn (1990, 1992), Fuson (1988) and Spelke (2017) were not re-read; their summaries are from prior knowledge.
- Cheung et al. (2017) sample size and task details were not verified from the paper itself.
- No training study was found that manipulates count-list knowledge alone (e.g. teaching a longer rote list) and measures cardinal or successor knowledge; the causal role of the list is inferred from correlations and from the Alex case.
- Whether backward-sequence knowledge (Sella & Lucangeli 2020) precedes or follows successor knowledge was not checked beyond the title.

## 4. Piantadosi, Tenenbaum & Goodman 2012: is recursion the key step? What do later computational accounts say?

### Takeaway
In the 2012 model recursion is the representational step that separates a cardinal-principle knower from a subset-knower, but it is a recursive procedure over sets that walks the count list, not a successor function, and the ordered list is assumed as a given primitive. Later accounts keep program induction but move away from "+1 from the list" as the foundation; no neural-network account read here reproduces the transition.

### Cited Findings
- **[R]** The model does Bayesian inference over lambda-calculus expressions that map sets to number words. Its Table 1 primitives are set tests (singleton?, doubleton?, tripleton?), set operations (select, set-difference, union, intersection), logic (if, and, or, not), three operations on the counting routine (next, prev, equal-word?), and recursion (L). The table contains no approximate-magnitude primitive. — [Piantadosi, Tenenbaum & Goodman 2012, Cognition 123:199](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** The count list is not learned by the model. The authors state that the sequence of number words "is known to children before they start to learn the words' numerical meanings" and therefore provide it through next and prev, which "operate on the domain of words, not on the domain of sets or numerical representations". — [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** Recursion enters as a primitive L that evaluates the whole current hypothesis on a smaller set. The cardinal-principle hypothesis says: if the set is a singleton return "one", otherwise return next of L applied to the set minus one selected element. The authors stress: "L is crucially not a successor function"; it is more basic, and can express a successor function among many other functions. — [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** The prior penalizes recursion with a free parameter, justified by the unbounded stack memory recursion needs. With no penalty the model becomes a cardinal-principle knower too early (right after the one-knower stage); with a very heavy penalty it lingers in a long four-knower stage. The timing of the transition therefore depends on that parameter and on which set-size primitives exist. — [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** The model reproduces the one-, two-, three-knower sequence followed by an abrupt transition, driven by the amount of data and the power-law frequency of number words, not by analogy over the first few meanings as in Carey's account. It also learns singular/plural and a Mod-5 system when given those data, and prefers the natural-number hypothesis over modular ones because Mod-N is not a primitive (the answer to Rips et al.). — [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** Stated limits: knowledge that one more element means one step further on the list is "only implicit in the computations"; explicit knowledge of successorship "does not come for free". The model does not address how children learn that next always yields a new word (infinity). It predicts that a child who recited the number words in some other order would not make the transition. — [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)
- **[R]** The 2023 follow-up is a tutorial plus a revised model that "acquires a counting procedure using observations of sets and words", framed as algorithm learning and as responding to critiques of the 2012 version. — [Piantadosi 2023, Child Development 94:1472](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.14031)
- **[B]** The wider programme treats conceptual development as program induction and code revision ("the child as hacker"). — [Rule, Tenenbaum & Piantadosi 2020, TiCS 24:900](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.005)
- **[R]** A 2025 proposal argues for a third origin of exact number besides counting-with-successor and refinement of the approximate system: a language of thought shared by arithmetic and geometry with three primitives (repetition, concatenation, recursive embedding). On this view +1 is one of the simplest expressions, not the foundation; numbers need not be built serially ("having the concept 19 is not necessary before understanding 20"); and children within their counting range often cannot judge ordinal position, successors or relative size. The approximate system is described as underlying intuitions of approximate quantity, not the emergence of exact arithmetic. — [Dehaene, Sablé-Meyer & Ciccione 2025, TiCS 29:526](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618345/)
- **[R]** Neural-network models of the successor function: one-hot networks (mapped by the authors to rote count-list memorization) and place-value networks (mapped to learning number-naming rules), both trained on N to N+1 for 0-98. The place-value model shows structured representations with a similarity drop at decade boundaries. The abstract reports no test beyond the trained range and no subset-knower stages. — [Gupta & Varma 2023, arXiv:2311.15194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15194)
- **[R]** On recursion as a capacity: recursive sequence generation is reported in monkeys, children, U.S. adults and native Amazonian adults (per the title); the one-line abstract states that the ability "is early developing and found across cultures, but is not unique to human thought". Task details (centre-embedded bracket-like sequences; extra training for monkeys) are from memory and unverified. — [Ferrigno, Cheyette, Piantadosi & Cantlon 2020, Science Advances 6:eaaz1002](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz1002). A counter-position at title level **[B]**: centre-embedded structures in baboons are "a by-product of associative learning and working memory constraints". — [Rey, Perruchet & Fagot 2012, Cognition 123:180](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2011.12.005)
- **[R]** Adults with severe agrammatic aphasia after large left perisylvian lesions solved calculations requiring recursive, structure-dependent operations (bracketed expressions), showing mathematical recursion independent of language grammar in the mature system. — [Varley, Klessinger, Romanowski & Siegal 2005, PNAS 102:3519](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0407470102)

### Inferences
- The model's answer to "is recursion the key step?" is yes, in a specific sense: the cardinal-principle hypothesis is the first one in the sequence that calls itself, and a cost on recursion is what delays it. But three things the shared-mechanism hypothesis would want are absent. The serial list is an input, not something the same mechanism learns. The recursion runs over sets (peel one element, step one word), so list order and set order are tied by a procedure that visits both, not by one representation. And explicit successor knowledge is a further step the model leaves open, matching the developmental lag in question 3.
- The model does capture one real dependency: without a consistently ordered list there is nothing for the recursive procedure to step along. That is the strongest formal statement in this literature of the list as a necessary scaffold.
- Recursion in the model is a hypothesis-space resource with a cost, not a mechanism shared with sequence memory. Evidence that monkeys can produce centre-embedded sequences, and that agrammatic patients keep arithmetic recursion, suggests recursion is not unitary and not tied to one faculty; that argues for testing, not assuming, a common recursive operation.
- The 2025 language-of-thought proposal is a direct rival to "order from the successor list": it derives exact number from repetition and nesting applied to sets, which is closer to a part-whole hierarchy than to a chain.

### Gaps
- The revised 2023 model's primitives and its handling of the count list were not read beyond the abstract.
- No connectionist or deep-learning model was found that shows subset-knower stages and a cardinal-principle transition from learning a count sequence; the one neural-network paper located does not claim this. Work of this kind from 2024-2026 was not searched.
- Formal claims that the successor function derives from a general recursive combinatorial operation in language (Hauser, Chomsky & Fitch 2002 and later) were not sourced; the 2012 paper cites that tradition only as motivation for including recursion.

## 5. Mapping symbols to magnitudes: calibration of the approximate number system to number words, direction asymmetries, and the symbol-grounding debate

### Takeaway
Established: adults can go both ways between number words and approximate magnitudes, and the mapping can be recalibrated by brief feedback. Established in children: the two directions come apart; word-to-magnitude works before magnitude-to-word, so the link is not symmetric when first formed. Contested: whether symbolic number meaning is grounded in the approximate system at all; two 2016 reviews conclude it is not, and propose symbol-symbol (order) associations instead.

### Cited Findings

Calibration and the structure of the mapping
- **[S]** Adults estimating dot arrays shift all their estimates after feedback on a single reference array; the authors model a "response grid" that interfaces the analogue number line with number words and can be stretched or shifted as a whole. — [Izard & Dehaene 2008, Cognition 106:1221](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2007.06.004), as described in later papers returned by search
- **[R]** Two mechanisms connect number words to non-verbal magnitudes: associative mappings (item-by-item links) and structure mappings (a holistic inference relating the two systems as wholes); both operate in children's estimation, with the inferential component substantial even early. — [Sullivan & Barner 2014, Child Development 85:1740](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12211); adult basis in [Sullivan & Barner 2013, QJEP 66:389](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2012.715655) **[B]**
- **[B]** Five-year-olds map number words within their counting range onto approximate numerosities, but not words beyond that range. — [Lipton & Spelke 2005, Child Development 76:978](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2005.00891.x)

Direction asymmetry
- **[R]** 62 children aged 2;7 to 4;6 (11 one-knowers, 7 two-knowers, 8 three-knowers, 36 cardinal-principle knowers) did both directions. Magnitude-to-word (say how many dots were flashed): slopes in the 6-10 range were not above zero for two/three-knowers (M = 0.05) or even for cardinal-principle knowers as a group (M = 0.21, p = .11). Word-to-magnitude (tap fast "N times" without counting): slopes in the 6-10 range were positive for two/three-knowers (M = 0.42, p < .05) and cardinal-principle knowers (M = 0.52, p < .001). Main effect of task F(1,59) = 5.89, p = .02. One-knowers showed neither mapping. — [Odic, Le Corre & Halberda 2015, Cognition 138:102](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.01.008)
- **[R]** Their conclusion: the mapping "is not functionally bidirectional in early development", and the direction from number words to the approximate system is established first; precision keeps improving afterwards. They offer this as reconciling Le Corre & Carey (2007: no magnitude-to-word mapping until well after the cardinal principle) with Wagner & Johnson (2011: subset-knowers give approximately more for larger number words). — [Odic et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.01.008); the two prior studies are [Le Corre & Carey 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880652) and [Wagner & Johnson 2011, Cognition 119:10](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.11.014) **[S]**
- **[R]** Even in adults the two directions show opposite biases: slight under-estimation going from magnitude to word and slight over-production going from word to magnitude (cited from Crollen et al. 2011). — [Odic et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.01.008)
- **[R]** Children aged 6 to 8 can map in both directions between symbolic and non-symbolic number; the ability develops across those years and predicts school mathematics beyond standard symbolic and non-symbolic tasks. — [Mundy & Gilmore 2009, JECP 103:490](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2009.02.003)
- **[B]** Title-level: approximate number-word knowledge is present before the cardinal principle. — [Gunderson, Spaepen & Levine 2015, JECP 130:35](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.09.008)
- **[R]** Against early magnitude grounding: new data are reported as showing that magnitude-to-word mappings "do not underlie the meanings of number words early in development". — [Carey, Shusterman, Haward & Distefano 2017, Cognition 168:243](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2017.06.022)

The symbol-grounding debate
- **[R]** Review conclusion: current evidence "does not support the notion that number symbols are grounded in the ANS"; any association that exists must be mediated by cognitive control. — [Leibovich & Ansari 2016, Canadian J Exp Psychol 70:12](https://doi.org/10.1037/cep0000070)
- **[R]** A second review examines four standard arguments for the mapping account and finds each questionable. (1) Animal and infant numerosity discrimination is silent on how symbols get meaning. (2) Distance and size effects for symbols can arise from decision processes or number-word frequency, and occur for letters and military ranks; in a matching task digit pairs showed no distance effect while dot and mixed pairs did, and only the latter two correlated. (3) Shared intraparietal activation is disputed: cross-notation adaptation and decoding were reported (Piazza et al. 2007; Eger et al. 2009) but later pattern analyses found no shared representation (Bulthé et al. 2014; Lyons et al. 2015). (4) The link from non-symbolic acuity to mathematics is small (meta-analytic r about 0.20-0.24, roughly 4-5% of variance) and partly explained by inhibitory control. — [Reynvoet & Sasanguie 2016, Frontiers in Psychology 7:1581](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061812/)
- **[R]** Their alternative: small number words are mapped onto the object-tracking system (up to about four); larger symbols get meaning mainly through order associations with other symbols via the count list. Supporting points they cite: 3-5-year-olds can compare number words above four without knowing their cardinal values (Davidson et al. 2012); children map number words to small sets first, digits to small sets second, and words to digits last (Benoit et al. 2013). — [Reynvoet & Sasanguie 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061812/)
- **[S]** Comparing a digit with a dot array is markedly harder than comparing two dot arrays, read as a cost of linking two distinct representations ("symbolic estrangement"). — [Lyons, Ansari & Beilock 2012, JEP:General 141:635](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027248), as described in [Reynvoet & Sasanguie 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061812/)
- **[R]** Error rates, reaction times and diffusion-model drift rates fit an analogue (Weber-law) model for dot comparison but are "systematically biased" for digit comparison; the authors propose a discrete semantic network for symbolic number. A companion study found distance and size effects uncorrelated between digit and dot tasks. — [Krajcsi, Lengyel & Kojouharova 2018, Frontiers in Psychology 9:124](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5817629/)
- **[R]** In bilateral intraparietal sulcus, fMRI pattern similarity between dot numerosities follows the overlap predicted by analogue tuning curves; for digits the similarity field is flat, "more consistent with discrete, categorical representation"; a digit and the matching number of dots share no number-specific pattern, although overall activation scales with log number in both formats. — [Lyons, Ansari & Beilock 2015, Human Brain Mapping 36:475](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.22641)
- **[B]** A neural model in which symbolic input reuses number-selective units but with sharper (near-exact) tuning than non-symbolic input. — [Verguts & Fias 2004, J Cogn Neurosci 16:1493](https://doi.org/10.1162/0898929042568497)
- **[R]** N = 1,391 children, grades 1-6: symbolic magnitude comparison is the best predictor of arithmetic in grades 1-2; the unique contribution of symbolic ordinal judgment rises steadily and overtakes all others by grade 6; non-symbolic comparison is never a unique predictor. — [Lyons, Price, Vaessen, Blomert & Ansari 2014, Developmental Science 17:714](https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12152)

### Inferences
- Is the word-to-magnitude mapping symmetric? In adults it is bidirectional but with opposite systematic biases in the two directions. In children the two directions are acquired at different times, word-to-magnitude first. The evidence favours two directed mappings that later cohere, not one symmetric association.
- The order of acquisition is informative: the direction from the symbol to the magnitude appears before the cardinal principle; the direction from magnitude to symbol appears after. Naming a perceived quantity is the harder, later skill.
- Calibration by a single anchor (Izard & Dehaene) and structure mapping (Sullivan & Barner) both describe aligning two already-ordered systems as wholes. That presupposes each side is ordered independently, the count list by learned sequence and the magnitude system by its own metric, which is evidence for two orders linked by alignment, not one order.
- The grounding debate is unresolved, but the weight of 2012-2018 evidence moved toward symbols being organised mainly by relations to other symbols (order), with only a loose and effortful tie to analogue magnitude.

### Gaps
- Izard & Dehaene (2008) was not read directly; the description comes from citing papers.
- Odic et al.'s second experiment and their coefficient-of-variation analyses were not extracted.
- No study was found testing direction asymmetry for symbols and non-numerical intensities (loudness, brightness, effort); the closest material is the authors' own remark that the question generalizes to other magnitude dimensions.
- The Leibovich & Ansari review was paywalled; only its abstract-level conclusions are reported.
- Asymmetries in cross-notation fMRI decoding (Eger et al. 2009) are discussed under question 6 and remain unverified in direction.

## 6. Neural evidence: how arbitrary symbols attach to magnitudes; animals using numerals; do ordinal sequences (letters, months) and magnitude share machinery?

### Takeaway
Established: in monkeys the numeral-numerosity link is carried by prefrontal "association" neurons (23% of cells) with almost none in parietal cortex (2%), and monkeys, chimpanzees and a parrot use learned symbols ordinally and cardinally. Established with caveats: human intraparietal sulcus is active for ordering letters and months as well as numbers. Contested: whether that is one representation. Pattern analyses separate numerical from alphabetical order, and symbolic order judgments engage premotor, not parietal, cortex.

### Cited Findings

Attaching shapes to numerosities
- **[R]** Two rhesus monkeys learned over months to associate Arabic numerals 1-4 with 1-4 dots (accuracy 87% dots, 88% shapes). Of 692 lateral prefrontal neurons, 157 (23%) were "association neurons" tuned to the same value in both formats; of 437 intraparietal neurons, 8 (about 2%). Association-neuron coding weakened on error trials. The authors call PFC "the prime source for the linking of signs to numerical categories in monkeys". — [Diester & Nieder 2007, PLoS Biology 5:e294](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0050294)
- **[B]** Background: numerosity-tuned neurons with Weber-law tuning are found in monkey prefrontal and posterior parietal cortex. — [Nieder 2016, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 17:366](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.40)
- **[B]** Human medial temporal lobe single units respond selectively to numerosities and to numerals, in largely separate populations; details of proportions and of any distance effect for symbol-tuned cells were not re-verified. — [Kutter, Bostroem, Elger, Mormann & Nieder 2018, Neuron 100:753](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.08.036)
- **[S]** Human fMRI: cross-notation adaptation between dots and digits in intraparietal sulcus (Piazza et al. 2007) and shared multivoxel patterns (Eger et al. 2009), versus notation-specific parietal responses (Cohen Kadosh et al.) and no cross-format generalization (Bulthé et al. 2014; Lyons et al. 2015). — [Reynvoet & Sasanguie 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061812/); primary sources [Piazza, Pinel, Le Bihan & Dehaene 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.11.022), [Eger et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.08.047), [Cohen Kadosh et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.12.025), [Bulthé, De Smedt & Op de Beeck 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2013.10.049) **[B]**

Animals using symbols for quantity
- **[R]** Three rhesus macaques learned 26 symbols (digits 0-9, then 16 letters) for 0-25 drops of liquid by choosing between pairs. In an addition task (two symbols vs. one), performance exceeded chance, and a novel symbol set transferred to addition within about 10 days, which the authors say rules out memorizing the 351 pairs. The smaller addend was heavily under-weighted (weight 0.10 in the first 10 days vs. 0.89 for the larger; 0.34 by month 5; with dots 0.64 vs. 1.03). Best-fitting model: each addend's value is normalized by the other ("dynamically shifting, relative scaling"); no evidence of a fixed logarithmic scale for symbols. — [Livingstone et al. 2014, PNAS 111:6822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4020100/)
- **[B]** Juvenile macaques learn such symbols better than adults and develop symbol-selective regions in inferotemporal cortex. — [Srihasam, Mandeville, Morocz, Sullivan & Livingstone 2012, Neuron 73:608](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.12.022)
- **[R]** Two rhesus monkeys trained to touch exemplars of numerosities 1-4 in ascending order, with size, shape and colour varied, then ordered pairs of novel numerosities 5-9 without reward. On 150 trial-unique 1-4 lists accuracy stayed near 40% (chance below .05). — [Brannon & Terrace 1998, Science 282:746](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.282.5389.746); test details from [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[R]** Control: one monkey trained on the non-monotonic order 3-1-4-2 showed no improvement across 13 stimulus sets, then improved immediately when the required order became ascending. A descending rule (4-3-2-1) was learnable. Terrace's conclusion: monkeys perceive ordinal relations between numerosities; the numerosities are not treated as arbitrary categories that can be listed in any order. — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/); primary report [Brannon & Terrace 2000, JEP:ABP 26:31](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.26.1.31) **[B]**
- **[B]** Chimpanzee Ai labelled set sizes with Arabic numerals and, separately, touched numerals in ascending order; zero was introduced into both skills, with transfer between the cardinal and ordinal uses reported as incomplete. — [Matsuzawa 1985, Nature 315:57](https://doi.org/10.1038/315057a0); [Biro & Matsuzawa 2001, Animal Cognition 4:193](https://doi.org/10.1007/s100710100086); sequential responding to numerals in [Tomonaga & Matsuzawa 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/s100710050045). Chimpanzee Sheba labelled and summed with numerals — [Boysen & Berntson 1989](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7036.103.1.23); chimpanzees' ordinal and transitive use of numerals — [Boysen, Berntson, Shreyer & Quigley 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7036.107.2.208). Macaques choose the larger of two Arabic numerals for food, including novel pairings — [Washburn & Rumbaugh 1991, Psychological Science 2:190](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1991.tb00130.x)
- **[R]** Chimpanzee cardinal and ordinal numeral skills were trained separately and showed no savings for later numerals, in contrast to the parrot Alex's inference from list position (question 3). — [Pepperberg & Carey 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3434310/)

Non-numerical order in intraparietal sulcus
- **[R]** fMRI, N = 17: comparing two-digit numbers (which is larger), letters (which is later in the alphabet) and colour saturation, each against a dimming control. Numbers and letters jointly activated anterior horizontal intraparietal sulcus bilaterally (about -39, -39, 36 and 45, -36, 48), overlapping the standard number region; no parietal region was number-specific. Conclusion: this region codes abstract ordinal information, "a common neural mechanism". Caveats: marginal ROI statistics; shared activation does not show shared neurons. — [Fias, Lammertyn, Caessens & Orban 2007, J Neurosci 27:8952](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6672167/)
- **[S]** A multivariate re-analysis of those data with support-vector machines found a clear dissociation between numerical and alphabetical order within bilateral horizontal intraparietal sulcus. — Zorzi, Di Bono & Fias 2011 (NeuroImage), as described in [Rubinsten & Sury 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079) and noted in [Attout et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3954845/)
- **[R]** Generating months and generating numbers both activate intraparietal sulcus more than generating animals; no difference between numbers and months, suggesting activation "might not be modulated by the additional quantity information carried by numbers". — [Ischebeck et al. 2008, Human Brain Mapping 29:894](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.20433)
- **[R]** fMRI, N = 26: distance effects in order short-term memory for letters, alphabetical order judgment, and numerical order judgment overlap in left anterior intraparietal sulcus only (-36, -50, 40; 19 voxels). Right anterior sulcus appeared more number-specific. Behavioural distance effects correlated only between the two letter tasks (r = .55) once a luminance control was partialled out. Authors: domain-general ordinal comparison in left intraparietal sulcus; task mismatches and weak cross-task consistency are acknowledged. — [Attout, Fias, Salmon & Majerus 2014, PLoS ONE 9:e92049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3954845/)
- **[R]** Intraparietal activation follows participants' strategy in both a magnitude task (normal distance effect) and an order-checking task (reverse distance effect); the authors conclude the region "represents a mental number line" accessed differently by the two tasks. — [Franklin & Jonides 2009, J Cogn Neurosci 21:2114](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn.2008.21181)
- **[R]** fMRI, N = 33: ordinal (is this triplet in order?) and cardinal (which is greater?) judgments on digits and on dot arrays. Dots: ordering and comparison overlapped heavily (40.2% of ordering voxels; right anterior intraparietal sulcus, more active for ordering). Digits: no conjunction survived; 1.1% overlap, below the 2.5% expected by chance. Digit ordering activated pre-SMA and left dorsal and ventral premotor cortex with no parietal region. Behaviour: digits in correct order showed a reversed distance effect (-0.35, p = .011); dots showed canonical effects everywhere. Interpretation: symbolic order is retrieved from the count list via premotor circuits; non-symbolic order is computed by iterated magnitude comparison. — [Lyons & Beilock 2013, J Neurosci 33:17052](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6618433/)
- **[S]** Children with developmental dyscalculia differ from controls in right inferior parietal activation during symbolic ordinal processing. — [Kaufmann et al. 2009, Cognitive Development 24:486](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogdev.2009.09.001), as described in [Rubinsten & Sury 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079)
- **[R]** Adults: number comparison and number ordering both predict arithmetic, ordering more strongly and mediating the comparison effect; the same holds for comparison and ordering of months, with matching canonical or reverse distance effects. The authors conclude the relevant processes "are not specific to the domain of numbers". — [Morsanyi, O'Mahony & McCormack 2017, QJEP 70:2497](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2016.1246577)
- **[B]** Title-level: prefrontal and parietal mechanisms for order information in working memory — [Marshuetz et al. 2000, J Cogn Neurosci 12 Suppl 2:130](https://doi.org/10.1162/08989290051137459); review of numerical ordinality — [Lyons, Vogel & Ansari 2016, Progress in Brain Research 227:187](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.pbr.2016.04.010)

### Inferences
- Is the attachment bidirectional at the neural level? In monkeys the association neurons respond to either format with the same tuning, which is a symmetric code at the single-cell level, but it sits in prefrontal cortex, apart from the parietal magnitude code. The link is a third representation joining two others, not a re-labelling of the magnitude neurons.
- Monkey symbol values behave unlike a stable magnitude scale: context-normalized, with gross under-weighting in combination. Learned symbols acquire value order quickly, but composing them is a separate and much weaker ability.
- Shared machinery for letters, months and numbers is supported at the level of regional activation and behavioural correlations, and undercut at the level of activation patterns and by the digit-ordering result. The most defensible reading: a domain-general ordinal comparison process in anterior intraparietal sulcus operating over domain-specific codes.
- The Brannon & Terrace control is a clean asymmetry between the two kinds of order. An arbitrary item list can be learned in any order; a numerosity list could not be learned in a non-monotonic order. Magnitude order is intrinsic to the representation; list order is imposed on it.
- Lyons & Beilock's double pattern (reversed distance effect and premotor activation for ordered digits) is the neural counterpart of the chain-like signature in question 1: the count list keeps its rote, sequential access route in adults, alongside magnitude comparison.

### Gaps
- No searches could be run for this section. Diester & Nieder's follow-up work, human single-unit studies after 2018, and any 2023-2026 studies of symbol learning in monkeys or of ordinal coding in parietal cortex are not covered.
- The Zorzi, Di Bono & Fias (2011) paper was not located by DOI and is reported only through two papers that describe it.
- The direction of cross-format decoding asymmetry in Eger et al. (2009) (the researcher's recollection is that a classifier trained on digits predicted dots but not the reverse) could not be verified and should not be cited without a page check.
- Kutter et al. (2018) proportions, and the Biro & Matsuzawa (2001) zero-transfer result, are from memory.
- No study was found that records from the same neurons during a learned arbitrary list task and a numerosity task, which is the direct test of shared machinery.

## 7. SNARC and spatial-numerical associations as evidence for a shared ordered representation; ordinal position-space effects in working memory

### Takeaway
Established: left-right response advantages track position in ordered sets for numbers, letters, months, days and freshly learned lists, so the spatial effect is about order, not quantity. Contested: whether one mechanism produces them. Position in a just-memorized sequence can replace the long-term number-space effect, yet both can appear together and are uncorrelated across people, which favours two codings (long-term order and temporary position binding) sharing an attentional process.

### Cited Findings
- **[B]** The SNARC effect: in parity judgment, small numbers are answered faster with the left hand and large numbers with the right; the association depends on relative magnitude within the tested range and on reading direction. — [Dehaene, Bossini & Giraux 1993, JEP:General 122:371](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.122.3.371)
- **[B]** The same left-early, right-late association is found for letters and months, and for days of the week, including when order is irrelevant to the task. — [Gevers, Reynvoet & Fias 2003, Cognition 87:B87](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277%2802%2900234-2); [Gevers, Reynvoet & Fias 2004, Cortex 40:171](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452%2808%2970938-9)
- **[B]** A SNARC-like effect appears for a newly learned arbitrary word sequence ("the SNARC effect in serial learning"). — [Previtali, de Hevia & Girelli 2010, Experimental Brain Research 201:599](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-009-2063-3)
- **[S]** Ordinal position effect: participants hold a short sequence of centrally presented items (fruit and vegetable names in the description read) in order; retrieving early items speeds left-hand responses and late items right-hand responses. Later studies found that number magnitude stops interacting with space once the numbers are held as a working-memory sequence (van Dijck et al. 2013, 2014), that later sequence items shift attention rightward in a cueing task, and that the effect extends to letters. — [van Dijck & Fias 2011, Cognition 119:114](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.12.013), as described in [Abrahamse, van Dijck, Majerus & Fias 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00932/full)
- **[R]** The "mental whiteboard" hypothesis: position markers in serial-order working memory are coordinates in an internal space; retrieval is selection by spatial attention; the default direction is left to right. The authors present it as specifying the nature of the position markers in models such as Henson (1998) and Brown et al. (2000), and name intraparietal sulcus as the likely hub, while stating that no study has tested the neural substrate directly. Boundary conditions they list: sequences of only 2-4 items, some effects tentative, and the effect appears only when items must be checked against the memorized sequence (Ginsburg et al. 2014). They also cite a SNARC-like pattern in chimpanzees for learned numeral sequences (Adachi 2014). — [Abrahamse et al. 2014, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 8:932](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00932/full)
- **[R]** Direct test of one vs. two codes. Experiment 1 (N = 42): blocks requiring a response to every digit showed SNARC and no ordinal position effect; go/no-go blocks on memorized digits showed the ordinal position effect and no overall SNARC. Experiment 2 (N = 52), with block types intermixed: both effects appeared together, and they were uncorrelated across individuals (r = 0.019). Conclusion: two coexisting spatial codings, one for long-term order and one for temporary position bindings formed at retrieval, possibly sharing an attentional process. — [Ginsburg & Gevers 2015, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 9:8](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00008/full)
- **[B]** Title-level convergence: spatial-numerical and ordinal positional associations "coexist in parallel". — [Huber, Klein, Moeller & Willmes 2016, Frontiers in Psychology 7:438](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00438)
- **[B]** Patients with left neglect after right-hemisphere damage misplace the midpoint of a number interval toward the larger number, as in physical line bisection. — [Zorzi, Priftis & Umiltà 2002, Nature 417:138](https://doi.org/10.1038/417138a)
- **[B]** A follow-up comparing bisection of numerical intervals with letter and month intervals in neglect reported a different pattern for the non-numerical sequences (from memory; not re-read). — [Zorzi, Priftis, Meneghello, Marenzi & Umiltà 2006, Neuropsychologia 44:1061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2005.10.025)
- **[R]** The whiteboard authors reinterpret the neglect number-bisection bias as possibly reflecting working memory, not only a long-term mental number line (citing Doricchi et al. 2005, 2009). — [Abrahamse et al. 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00932/full)
- **[B]** Title-level developmental point: preschoolers "use space, rather than counting, to infer the numerical magnitude of digits". — [Sella, Berteletti, Lucangeli & Zorzi 2017, Cognition 158:56](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2016.10.010)

### Inferences
- SNARC-type effects are evidence that ordered sets of any content, including a list memorized minutes ago, are bound to a spatial frame. They support a common format for order (positions in a space) but not a common format for order and magnitude: the effects go with ordinal position even when magnitude is absent or controlled.
- The working-memory findings show position codes are assigned flexibly and can override the canonical number order, which matches the factorized position codes in question 1. The long-term number-space association looks like a consolidated instance of the same binding, but Ginsburg & Gevers' zero correlation warns against treating them as one mechanism.
- For the question of how rote order becomes conceptual order, this is a candidate bridge: bind list items to positions in an internal space, and comparative judgments (distance effects, spatial compatibility) come with the format. It does not by itself supply quantity.

### Gaps
- van Dijck & Fias (2011), Dehaene et al. (1993), Gevers et al. (2003, 2004), Previtali et al. (2010) and both Zorzi neglect papers were not read directly this session.
- Cultural and reading-direction moderators, the claim that number-space mapping is not innate, and animal number-space studies (newborn chicks) could not be searched; their status as contested is known but unsourced here.
- No study was found testing whether an ordinal position effect arises for sequences of felt intensities or other non-symbolic magnitudes held in memory.

## 8. Evidence against a single mechanism: where sequence order and magnitude/concept order dissociate

### Takeaway
Established: sequence knowledge and quantity knowledge dissociate in both directions in single patients, in children (list without meaning; cardinal principle without successor or order knowledge; counting neither necessary nor sufficient for exact equality), and in adult imaging (digit order vs. digit magnitude). The dissociations are strong enough to rule out a single undifferentiated mechanism; they are compatible with distinct representations that share a comparison process or a learning rule.

### Cited Findings

Neuropsychology
- **[S]** Patient SE (left frontal infarct) was "unable to access the cardinal meaning of numbers" and showed an inverse distance effect in number comparison, yet answered "which number comes next?" correctly: sequence meaning spared, cardinal meaning impaired. — [Delazer & Butterworth 1997, Cognitive Neuropsychology 14:613](https://doi.org/10.1080/026432997381501), as described in [Rubinsten & Sury 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079)
- **[S]** The reverse pattern: a patient with Gerstmann syndrome after a parietal lesion was impaired at processing order for numbers, letters, days and months, with quantity processing relatively preserved. — [Turconi & Seron 2002, Cortex 38:911](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452%2808%2970069-8), as described in [Rubinsten & Sury 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079). (The secondary source gives the lesion as right parietal; Gerstmann syndrome is classically left parietal; lesion side unverified.)
- **[S]** Not all cases dissociate: one Gerstmann case showed joint impairment of number and letter comparison, another preserved number comparison with impaired letter comparison. — [Fias et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6672167/) (discussion)
- **[B]** Patient CG, after a left-hemisphere lesion, lost essentially all number knowledge above four in every task while non-numerical knowledge, including other ordered verbal series, was reported preserved. — [Cipolotti, Butterworth & Denes 1991, Brain 114:2619](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/114.6.2619)
- **[B]** Double dissociation "between rote verbal and quantitative knowledge of arithmetic": one patient with an inferior parietal lesion lost quantity-based operations (subtraction, bisection, comparison) with rote multiplication facts spared; another with a left subcortical lesion lost rote verbal sequences and tables with quantity knowledge spared. — [Dehaene & Cohen 1997, Cortex 33:219](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-9452%2808%2970002-9)
- **[B]** Approximate quantity and exact number words are "dissociable systems" in two further patients. — [Lemer, Dehaene, Spelke & Cohen 2003, Neuropsychologia 41:1942](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0028-3932%2803%2900123-4). Number knowledge can be spared in semantic dementia while other concepts are lost — [Cappelletti, Butterworth & Kopelman 2001, Neuropsychologia 39:1224](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0028-3932%2801%2900035-5); number words can dissociate from other words in reading and writing — [Butterworth, Cappelletti & Kopelman 2001, Nature Neuroscience 4:784](https://doi.org/10.1038/90484)
- **[R]** Three men with severe agrammatic aphasia kept all basic calculation procedures, including structure-dependent bracketed expressions. — [Varley et al. 2005, PNAS 102:3519](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0407470102)

Ordinal vs. cardinal processing in healthy and dyscalculic adults
- **[R]** Adults with developmental dyscalculia (13 vs. 15 controls for dots; 9 vs. 14 for digits) showed normal ratio (quantity) effects but abnormal ordinality effects when judging whether three dot arrays or numerals were in order. The authors propose ordinality and quantity as separate systems, with dyscalculia possibly an ordinal deficit, while calling the two-system idea tempting but not directly demonstrated by their data. — [Rubinsten & Sury 2011, PLoS ONE 6:e24079](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079)
- **[S]** Order judgments on digit pairs give a reverse distance effect (adjacent, correctly ordered items are fastest), unlike magnitude comparison. — [Turconi, Campbell & Seron 2006, Cognition 98:273](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2004.12.002), as described in [Rubinsten & Sury 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0024079)
- **[R]** Digit ordering and digit comparison share almost no activated voxels (1.1%), whereas dot ordering and dot comparison share 40.2%; see question 6. — [Lyons & Beilock 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6618433/)
- **[S]** Judging ordinality of digits is unrelated to magnitude comparison and independently predicts arithmetic; dyscalculic children are slower on ordering but not on comparison (Attout & Majerus 2015). — [Goffin & Ansari 2016, Cognition 150:68](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2016.01.018), as described in [Reynvoet & Sasanguie 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5061812/)
- **[B]** Title-level: ERP evidence for "differential processing of numerical quantity and order" — [Turconi, Jemel, Rossion & Seron 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogbrainres.2004.05.003); "differential processing of symbolic numerical magnitude and order in first-grade children" — [Vogel, Remark & Ansari 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.07.010); numerical ordering ability mediates the relation between approximate number acuity and arithmetic — [Lyons & Beilock 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2011.07.009)

Counting-sequence knowledge vs. number concept
- **[R]** Children who recite to ten and count objects accurately can still be subset-knowers who fail to give "three", and fail to see that adding one means the next word. — [Sarnecka & Carey 2008](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8dh3972h)
- **[R]** Cardinal-principle knowers often lack ordering and successor knowledge for numbers in their own count range. — [Davidson et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22245033/); [Spaepen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.06.012)
- **[R]** Counting mastery is "neither necessary nor sufficient" for understanding exact equality. — [Jara-Ettinger et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5319921/)
- **[B]** Title-level: cardinal uses of number words are acquired before ordinal uses ("One first?"). — [Colomé & Noël 2012, JECP 113:233](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.03.005)
- **[S]** Magnitude without a count list: groups whose languages lack exact number words still represent approximate quantity; they fail to encode exact cardinalities of moderate size, though one-to-one matching of visible sets remains possible. — [Pica, Lemer, Izard & Dehaene 2004, Science 306:499](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1102085); [Gordon 2004, Science 306:496](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1094492); [Frank, Everett, Fedorenko & Gibson 2008, Cognition 108:819](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2008.04.007); as characterized in [Piantadosi et al. 2012](https://web.stanford.edu/~ngoodman/papers/COGNIT2374.pdf)

Symbol code vs. magnitude code; sequence memory vs. magnitude
- **[R]** Digits are coded categorically and dots analogically within the same parietal region, with no shared number-specific pattern. — [Lyons, Ansari & Beilock 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.22641)
- **[R]** In monkeys the symbol-numerosity association is prefrontal; parietal numerosity neurons almost never carry it. — [Diester & Nieder 2007](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0050294)
- **[R]** Monkeys could not learn a non-monotonic order over numerosities although arbitrary photograph lists are learned in any order. — [Terrace 2010](https://comparative-cognition-and-behavior-reviews.org/vol5_terrace/)
- **[B]** Memory for the order of a sequence of events depends on the hippocampus in rats, dissociably from recognition of the items. — [Fortin, Agster & Eichenbaum 2002, Nature Neuroscience 5:458](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn834). Inference over overlapping pairs also needs hippocampal connections while pair learning does not — [Dusek & Eichenbaum 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC21293) **[R]**
- **[R]** Formal result: pair-specific (fully conjunctive) codes cannot support transitive generalization; item-wise additive codes necessarily do. — [Lippl et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11252811/)

Evidence on the other side (for shared resources), for balance
- **[R]** Aligned EEG patterns for digits and learned reward ranks — [Luyckx et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405242/); a rank line for arbitrary objects in number-related frontoparietal cortex — [Nelli et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10618408/); common anterior intraparietal activation for numbers, letters and months — [Fias et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6672167/), [Ischebeck et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.20433); ordering of months predicts arithmetic like ordering of numbers — [Morsanyi et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2016.1246577); monkeys transfer one list representation between a sequence-production task and a pairwise inference task — [Jensen et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3729468)
- **[B]** Relational-complexity theory treats transitive inference, class inclusion and hierarchical classification as structurally equivalent (ternary-relational) and reports that children's performance on them, and on cardinality tasks, is linked through a common capacity factor. This is evidence for a shared capacity limit, not for a shared representation. — [Halford, Wilson & Phillips 1998, BBS 21:803](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X98001769); [Andrews & Halford 2002, Cognitive Psychology 45:153](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0285%2802%2900002-6)

### Inferences
- At least four things separate under some condition: (a) the rote count sequence, (b) ordinal relations among symbols, (c) exact cardinal meaning, (d) approximate magnitude. Patients lose (c) keeping (a); lose rote sequences keeping quantity; children have (a) without (c), and (c) without (b); cultures have (d) without (a); adult brains process (b) and symbolic magnitude in non-overlapping voxels.
- What does not dissociate as cleanly is non-numerical order and numerical order: letters, months, learned ranks and numbers repeatedly behave alike and co-activate. The supported shared component is "order over items", not "order equals magnitude".
- A fair summary for a designer: the literature supports a common format for ordered sets (item-to-position or item-to-rank bindings, comparable by distance) that both a learned sequence and a magnitude scale can be expressed in, and a separate, effortful, directional alignment between a symbol list and a magnitude scale. It does not support deriving the ordered concept from successor links by one recursive mechanism, and the one place recursion is formally required (the cardinal-principle procedure) treats the list as a given.
- The hypothesis also mentions hierarchies of kinds or parts. Nothing read here tests whether sequence learning and class-inclusion or part-whole hierarchy share a mechanism; the only pointer is relational-complexity theory, which claims equal structural complexity and a shared capacity limit.
