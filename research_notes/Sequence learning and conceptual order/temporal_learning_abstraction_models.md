# Temporal sequence learning as the mechanism that builds abstraction hierarchies: models that unify the two and models that separate them

Researcher notes, compiled 2026-11-27. The hypothesis under test (sequence learning and the building of conceptual order share one recursive mechanism) is treated as open, not assumed.

## 1. Symmetric vs. asymmetric synaptic structure: is "one-directional association = sequence, bidirectional association = attractor/assembly" accurate, and what happens when both A->B and B->A are trained?

### Takeaway
The slogan is a fair first approximation of Hopfield-type theory (symmetric coupling gives an energy landscape with fixed points; an asymmetric, delayed coupling gives flow from one state to the next), but it is wrong in three specifics: working sequence networks need both kinds of coupling at once, symmetric links between two different consecutive patterns give graded correlated attractors or a resonant pair rather than a clean merge, and one temporally asymmetric rule can yield either outcome depending on stimulus timing. Whether learning A->B also yields B->A is an empirical variable: yes for human word pairs, no for serial lists, most non-human animals, and autoregressive language models.

### Cited Findings

**Classic attractor and sequence networks**
- Hopfield (1982), "Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities", PNAS 79(8):2554-2558. The abstract lists time-sequence retention among the model's emergent properties alongside content-addressable memory [AB]. — [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC346238/)
- Sompolinsky & Kanter (1986), "Temporal association in asymmetric neural networks", Physical Review Letters 57(22):2861-2864. A network that recalls time sequences and cycles of patterns; the key ingredient is asymmetric synapses with a slow dynamic response; includes simulations and an analysis of thermal noise; proposed as a model of rhythmic motor pattern generation [AB] (sim, theory). — [Hebrew University record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/temporal-association-in-asymmetric-neural-networks/)
- Kleinfeld (1986), "Sequential state generation by model neural networks", PNAS 83(24):9469-9473. Sequence generation depends on the interplay of two sets of synapses: one set stabilises the network in its current memory state, the second set acts with a time delay and causes the specified transition to the next memory; numerical examples cover intact, noisy and missing connections; a way to recognise externally generated sequences is proposed [AB] (sim). — [PMC387161](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387161)
- One rule or two (Sompolinsky-Kanter, Kleinfeld): two weight components with two different roles and two different time constants. Sequence is not produced by asymmetric coupling alone; each state must also be self-stabilised by a symmetric component. — [PMC387161](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC387161)
- Kosko (1988), "Bidirectional associative memories", IEEE Trans. Systems, Man, and Cybernetics 18(1):49-60. A two-layer heteroassociative network whose single weight matrix is used in both directions (A to B and B to A); activity reverberates until a bidirectionally stable state is reached, which corresponds to a local minimum of an energy function (secondary description; Kosko's 2021 follow-up states that every rectangular matrix is bidirectionally stable) [AB] (theory). This is the cleanest formal answer to "what if both A->B and B->A are stored": the pair becomes a fixed point, not a sequence. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/21.87054); [secondary summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidirectional_associative_memory)
- Chaudhry, Zavatone-Veth, Krotov & Pehlevan (2023), "Long Sequence Hopfield Memory", NeurIPS 36. Sequence memory in Hopfield-like networks uses temporally asymmetric Hebbian rules and has limited capacity because of interference between memories; a nonlinear (dense-associative-memory) interaction term gives new capacity scaling laws that beat traditional models, verified numerically [AB] (theory, sim). — [arXiv 2306.04532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04532)

**What symmetric coupling between successive patterns does**
- Griniasty, Tsodyks & Amit (1993), "Conversion of temporal correlations between stimuli to spatial correlations between attractors", Neural Computation 5(1):1-17. A small modification of a Hopfield matrix that stores a fixed sequence of uncorrelated patterns gives a network whose attractors are correlated with several learned patterns: uncorrelated stimuli evoke correlated attractors, so temporal order is converted into spatial correlation. Correlations are significant up to a separation of five patterns in the sequence, a value that is robust over a range of parameters, and this matches the Miyashita recordings. A simple unsupervised learning dynamics produces the matrix when sequences are repeatedly shown in fixed order [AB] (theory, sim). The added coupling links each pattern to its sequence neighbour in both directions with equal strength [BIB-level recollection of the model's form; re-check]. — [Neural Computation record](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1993/griniasty1993neco-conversion)

**Plasticity rules that produce direction-specific or bidirectional links**
- Markram, Lübke, Frotscher & Sakmann (1997), Science 275:213-215, and Bi & Poo (1998), J. Neurosci. 18(24):10464-10472, are the standard experimental sources for spike-timing-dependent plasticity, in which the sign of synaptic change depends on the order of pre- and postsynaptic spikes [BIB] (data). — [Markram et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5297.213); [Bi & Poo](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-24-10464.1998)
- Clopath, Büsing, Vasilaki & Gerstner (2010), "Connectivity reflects coding: a model of voltage-based STDP with homeostasis", Nature Neuroscience 13(3):344-352. In a simulated recurrent spiking network one voltage-based rule gave mostly unidirectional strong connections under temporal coding (spatio-temporal input correlations) and mostly bidirectional strong connections under rate coding (spatial correlations only) [AB] (sim). — [Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2479)
- Mishra, Kim, Guzman & Jonas (2016), Nature Communications 7:11552. CA3-CA3 recurrent synapses show a temporally symmetric, broad STDP window (half-width about 150 ms): pairing potentiates regardless of spike order; in autoassociative network models storage and recall were more robust with the symmetric rule than with an asymmetric one [AB] (data, sim). — [PMC4869174](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4869174)
- Pereira & Brunel (2020), "Unsupervised learning of persistent and sequential activity", Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience 13:97 [FT] (sim, theory). One temporally asymmetric Hebbian rule (postsynaptic rate now, presynaptic rate a delay D earlier) plus a multiplicative homeostatic variable. Which structure is learned depends on stimulus duration T and inter-stimulus gap Δ relative to D: long, well separated stimuli strengthen only recurrent (within-population) weights and give persistent activity, a fixed-point attractor; short, contiguous stimuli strengthen only feedforward weights and give sequential activity; when Δ < D < T both grow, giving sequences that end in persistent activity, with strong bidirectional connections in some intermediate cases. Hebbian plasticity alone drove all weights to the maximum, and simple synaptic normalisation did not rescue either regime; the homeostatic term was required. — [PMC6978734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6978734)
- Gillett, Pereira & Brunel (2020), "Characteristics of sequential activity in networks with temporally asymmetric Hebbian learning", PNAS 117(47):29948-29958. Theory of transient sequential retrieval in sparsely connected rate networks; the sparseness of recalled sequences is controlled by nonlinearities in the rule [AB] (theory, sim). — [PMC7703604](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7703604)
- Fiete, Senn, Wang & Hahnloser (2010), Neuron 65(4):563-576. Earlier studies found STDP alone could not organise long sequences; STDP combined with heterosynaptic competition within single neurons self-organises long unary chains even without sequential training input, with chain lengths distributed roughly as 1/L [AB] (sim). — [University of Bern record](https://boris.unibe.ch/1257)
- Diesmann, Gewaltig & Aertsen (1999), "Stable propagation of synchronous spiking in cortical neural networks", Nature 402:529-533, is the standard analysis of synfire-chain propagation through feedforward groups of neurons [BIB] (sim, theory). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/990101)
- Litwin-Kumar & Doiron (2014), Nature Communications 5:5319; Zenke, Agnes & Gerstner (2015), Nature Communications 6:6922; Chenkov, Sprekeler & Kempter (2017), PLoS Computational Biology 13:e1005359. Spiking-network studies in which assemblies are groups with strong reciprocal connections formed and kept stable by plasticity, and in which replayable assembly sequences combine recurrent connections inside each assembly with feedforward connections between assemblies [BIB] (sim). — [Litwin-Kumar & Doiron](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6319); [Zenke et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7922); [Chenkov et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005359)

**Does one-directional training give the reverse link? Behavioural and neural data**
- Kahana (2002), "Associative symmetry and memory theory", Memory & Cognition 30(6):823-840. For studied pairs, forward and backward cued recall are about equal, and Kahana's proposed test (correlating forward and backward recall of the same pairs) gave a near-perfect correlation, later replicated for word pairs; this supports a holistic, symmetric pair representation over two independent directional links (described through later papers that summarise it) [AB] (data). — [Springer record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03195769); [replication context](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758%2Fs13421-019-00950-4)
- Caplan, Glaholt & McIntosh (2006), "Linking associative and serial list memory: pairs versus triples", J. Exp. Psychol.: LMC 32(6):1244-1265, compares the symmetric behaviour of pairs with the directional behaviour of short serial lists [BIB] (data). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.32.6.1244)
- Howard & Kahana (2002), "A distributed representation of temporal context", J. Mathematical Psychology. In free recall, transitions between items show a contiguity effect with a robust forward asymmetry; the temporal context model explains both through a slowly drifting context rather than direct item-to-item links [AB] (data, theory). — [paper PDF](https://people.bu.edu/marc777/docs/HowardKahana02-JMP.pdf)
- Lionello-DeNolf (2009), "The search for symmetry: 25 years in review", Learning & Behavior 37(2):188-203 [FT] (data review). Of 24 non-human studies of emergent symmetry from 1982 to 2007, only three reported clear evidence (two used the same sea lions, one used pigeons), ten were mixed and eleven found none; about 41% of animals showed some evidence. Standard conditional-discrimination training is judged insufficient by itself; multiple-exemplar training and control of stimulus location matter. — [PMC2685068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- Sidman, Rauzin, Lazar, Cunningham, Tailby & Carrigan (1982), J. Exp. Analysis of Behavior 37:23-44: most children showed symmetry after one-directional conditional-discrimination training, none of the monkeys or baboons did (as summarised in later reviews) [BIB/AB] (data). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1901/jeab.1982.37-23)
- Schapiro, Kustner & Turk-Browne (2012), Current Biology 22(17):1622-1627. After passive exposure to a stream containing hidden object pairs in fixed temporal order, paired objects became more similar in medial temporal lobe activity patterns; most regions showed bidirectional (order-free) shaping, while CA2/3/DG showed a forward-looking, predictive pattern [AB] (data). — [PMC3443305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3443305)
- Autoregressive language models trained on "A is B" do not acquire "B is A" (see section 5). — [Berglund et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288)

### Inferences
- A more accurate statement than the slogan: the symmetric part of a coupling matrix defines an energy landscape (completion, persistence), and the antisymmetric or delayed part defines flow across it (order). Any working sequence memory in this tradition has both. A design that stores a transition matrix can read "sequence" off its antisymmetric part and "assembly" off its symmetric part.
- Training both A->B and B->A between two distinct items does not, in these networks, produce a single new item. It produces either a resonant pair (BAM) or overlapping attractors whose overlap falls off with sequence distance (Griniasty et al.). That is a similarity gradient. Turning it into a discrete unit one level up needs an extra step (a threshold and the allocation of a new unit) that the attractor models do not contain.
- The same local rule yields both structures; the control variable is timing. Co-activity that is long relative to the plasticity delay gives reciprocal links; rapid succession gives one-way links (Clopath et al.; Pereira & Brunel). A medium-term co-occurrence trace therefore tends to build assemblies, and an order-sensitive trace tends to build chains.
- Symmetry is not free. Human paired associates are symmetric, but serial lists, most animal conditional discriminations and next-token language models are not. A rule that merges two units because each predicts the other is therefore testing for something that directional learning does not supply by default; both directions have to be learned separately or supplied by a symmetric rule (as in CA3).
- The brain appears to keep both readouts of one experience: order-free similarity in cortex and directional prediction in CA3/DG (Schapiro et al. 2012).

### Gaps
- Hopfield (1982) body text could not be opened this session. My recollection is that the paper reports adding a non-symmetric term linking each stored state to its successor and obtaining only short sequences (about four states); this is unverified here.
- The threshold on the relative strength of the asymmetric term in Sompolinsky-Kanter, and the exact overlap values in Griniasty et al., were not checked against the papers.
- No source was located that trains both directions with temporally asymmetric STDP in a spiking network and reports whether the outcome is an assembly, a two-cycle, or mutual cancellation, beyond the regime maps in Clopath et al. and Pereira & Brunel.
- Results on attractors in strongly diluted asymmetric networks (which show that symmetry is sufficient but not necessary for fixed points) were not sourced.

## 2. Temporal contiguity producing similarity or invariance (Miyashita; Sakai & Miyashita; Griniasty, Tsodyks & Amit; Földiák; Wallis & Rolls; Wiskott & Sejnowski; Li & DiCarlo)

### Takeaway
This is the best-supported bridge from time to abstraction: physiology and models agree that stimuli experienced close together in time come to evoke similar responses, and one contiguity rule applied at every layer builds an invariance hierarchy. But these learners use temporal adjacency, not temporal order, so they produce similarity without retaining sequence knowledge, and they cannot tell "two views of one thing" from "two different things that follow each other".

### Cited Findings

**Physiology**
- Miyashita (1988), "Neuronal correlate of visual associative long-term memory in the primate temporal cortex", Nature 335:817-820. Neurons in anterior ventral temporal cortex acquired, through training, selectivity that reflects learned association between pictures [AB] (data). In the experiment the pictures were learned in a fixed serial order and a neuron's responses to pictures were correlated as a function of their serial distance [BIB-level recollection]. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/335817a0)
- Sakai & Miyashita (1991), "Neural organization for the long-term memory of paired associates", Nature 354:152-155. In a pair-association task two classes of anterior temporal neurons appeared: cells responding selectively to both members of a learned pair (pair-coding), and cells whose delay activity anticipated the associate (pair-recall) [AB] (data). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/354152a0)
- Messinger et al. (2001), PNAS: neuronal representations of stimulus associations develop in the temporal lobe during learning [AB] (data). — [PMC59829](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC59829)
- Li & DiCarlo (2008), Science 321:1502-1507. Altering the natural temporal contiguity of visual experience (an object swapped for another across a position change) changed the position tolerance of single inferotemporal neurons; the change was significant after about one hour and grew with exposure [AB] (data). — [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1641475); [lab page](https://dicarlolab.mit.edu/?p=216)
- Li & DiCarlo (2010), Neuron 67:1062-1075: the same manipulation reshapes size tolerance [AB] (data). — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/96054)
- Cox, Meier, Oertelt & DiCarlo (2005), Nature Neuroscience 8:1145-1147, and Wallis & Bülthoff (2001), PNAS 98:4800-4804, are the human psychophysics counterparts: altered temporal association of images changes which images are treated as the same object [BIB] (data). — [Cox et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1519); [Wallis & Bülthoff](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.071028598)
- Schapiro, Kustner & Turk-Browne (2012): temporal regularities alone raise representational similarity of paired objects in human medial temporal lobe (see section 1) [AB] (data). — [PMC3443305](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3443305)

**Models**
- Griniasty, Tsodyks & Amit (1993): attractor network that turns temporal neighbourhood into correlated attractors (details in section 1) [AB] (theory, sim). Related follow-ups: Amit, Brunel & Tsodyks (1994), J. Neurosci. 14:6435-6445; Brunel (1996), Neural Computation 8:1677-1710 (Hebbian learning of context); Yakovlev, Fusi, Berman & Zohary (1998), Nature Neuroscience 1:310-317 (inter-trial activity as the vehicle that links successive stimuli) [BIB]. — [Griniasty et al.](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1993/griniasty1993neco-conversion); [Amit et al.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-11-06435.1994); [Brunel](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1996.8.8.1677); [Yakovlev et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/1131)
- Földiák (1991), "Learning invariance from transformation sequences", Neural Computation 3(2):194-200. A Hebbian rule in which the postsynaptic term is a decaying trace (running average) of the unit's activity; trained on sequences of a pattern undergoing a transformation, units become invariant to retinal shift. The low-pass filter embodies the assumption that useful features are stable over time [AB] (sim). — [Neural Computation record](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1991/foldiak1991neco-learning)
- Wallis & Rolls (1997), "Invariant face and object recognition in the visual system", Progress in Neurobiology. VisNet, a layered competitive network trained with a trace rule equivalent to Földiák's, tests the proposal that a short-term memory trace in the Hebbian rule captures what stays invariant as an object transforms [AB] (sim). VisNet has four layers [AB]. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0301-0082%2896%2900054-8); [Rolls & Milward 2000](https://www.oxcns.org/papers/275_Rolls+Milward00.pdf); [Rolls 2012 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3378046)
- Wiskott & Sejnowski (2002), "Slow feature analysis: unsupervised learning of invariances", Neural Computation 14(4):715-770. Nonlinear expansion of the input followed by principal component analysis of the expanded signal and its time derivative extracts the most slowly varying features; applied repeatedly to learn more complex functions, and assembled into a hierarchical network of SFA modules as a simple model of the visual system. Performance degrades when one network must learn several invariances at once [AB] (sim, theory). — [authors' abstract page](https://www.ini.rub.de/PEOPLE/wiskott/Abstracts/WisSej2002.html)
- Sprekeler, Michaelis & Wiskott (2007), PLoS Computational Biology 3(6):e112. Slow feature analysis can be implemented by a modified Hebbian rule related to the trace rule, and for linear Poisson neurons by an STDP window whose shape and time scale match physiological data [AB] (theory). — [PMC1904380](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1904380)
- Franzius, Sprekeler & Wiskott (2007), PLoS Computational Biology 3(8):e166. A hierarchy of SFA nodes followed by sparse coding, fed with the visual stream of a simulated moving animal, yields place, head-direction or spatial-view cells depending only on movement statistics [AB] (sim, theory). — [PMC1963505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1963505)
- Illing, Ventura, Bellec & Gerstner (2021), NeurIPS. CLAPP: each layer minimises its own contrastive predictive loss with Hebbian, purely local updates and no backpropagated errors between layers; stacked layers build deep hierarchical representations of images, speech and video [AB] (sim). — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/feade1d2047977cd0cefdafc40175a99-Abstract.html)

**One rule or two**
- VisNet, hierarchical SFA and CLAPP each apply one temporal rule at every level of a fixed layered architecture and obtain increasing invariance with depth. So one rule applied recursively does build the abstraction hierarchy here; the levels themselves are given by the architecture, not created by learning. — [Wiskott & Sejnowski](https://www.ini.rub.de/PEOPLE/wiskott/Abstracts/WisSej2002.html); [Illing et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/feade1d2047977cd0cefdafc40175a99-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- These are contiguity learners, not sequence learners. Slowness and trace objectives are indifferent to the direction of time; the Griniasty coupling is symmetric. The output is "these belong together", with order discarded. So this family supports "time builds abstraction" but not "sequence knowledge and abstraction come from one rule": the sequence is consumed, not kept.
- The mechanism cannot distinguish invariance (two views of one object) from association (two different objects that reliably follow each other). Li & DiCarlo's swap experiment shows the cortex will merge different objects if their timing says so, and Miyashita's arbitrary picture pairs acquire shared coding by the same route. A design that promotes temporally contiguous units to one higher unit inherits this ambiguity: the result is an association cluster unless something else constrains it.
- The physiological effects are graded similarity changes in a fixed population, not the allocation of a new unit one level up.

### Gaps
- Miyashita (1988) stimulus counts and the correlation-by-serial-distance values were not re-read; the published percentage of pair-coding neurons by area appeared in a search snippet but could not be tied to a specific URL.
- Whether VisNet applies the trace rule at every one of its four layers or only the upper ones in each published variant was not verified.
- Recent self-supervised video models that learn from temporal contiguity at scale were not surveyed.

## 3. Hierarchical temporal memory and the memory-prediction framework: does each level run sequence memory and pass a stable name for the sequence upward?

### Takeaway
In the theory, yes: each region learns sequences, emits a slowly changing code for the sequence it is in, and that code becomes an element of sequences one level up. In published simulations this was realised only in the 2009 Markov-chain version; the later biologically detailed HTM is a single layer with no hierarchy, the pooling step never received a peer-reviewed demonstration, and since 2017 the group's theory has moved from temporal sequences to sensorimotor reference frames.

### Cited Findings
- Hawkins, George & Niemasik (2009), "Sequence memory for prediction, inference and behaviour", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 364:1203-1209 [FT]. States the framework in HTM terms: each memory region learns common sequences of patterns; relatively constant representations of sequences are passed up and become elements of sequences in the parent region; fast-changing input therefore becomes slower-changing with height; predictions flow down (all proposal). What is simulated is only a state-splitting learner for variable-order sequences (adapted from dynamic Markov coding), trained on motion capture from 32 joints quantised to 66 points, 239 poses covering four actions plus reversed walking; it found five sequence groups matching the actions (sim). Identifying sequences for the parent region and hierarchy-wide prediction are left as current or future work. — [PMC2666719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2666719/)
- George & Hawkins (2009), "Towards a mathematical theory of cortical micro-circuits", PLoS Computational Biology 5(10):e1000532 [FT]. A node is a coincidence detector plus a mixture of Markov chains. Learning: memorise coincidence patterns of the children's outputs; learn a transition matrix over coincidences and partition it (for example by graph partitioning) into temporal groups, each a Markov chain. The feedforward output to the parent is a vector with one entry per Markov chain, so the parent sees sequence identities, not individual patterns. Every node in the tree uses the same learning and inference algorithm (Bayesian belief propagation), and higher-level states change more slowly (sim, theory). Demonstrations cited: invariant recognition of grey-scale images, speaker identification, motion-capture grouping, and a subjective-contour effect from feedback. Acknowledged gaps: feedback does not alter the feedforward output; the equations assume singly connected networks; duration is handled by an external timing signal; variable-speed inference is future work; no heuristic for the number of stored coincidences. — [PMC2749218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2749218)
- Hawkins & Ahmad (2016), "Why neurons have thousands of synapses, a theory of sequence memory in neocortex", Frontiers in Neural Circuits 10:23 [FT]. Neurons with many dendritic segments; each segment holds potential synapses with a scalar permanence that is incremented or decremented Hebbian-style and thresholded to a binary weight. High-order sequences are represented by which cell within a minicolumn fires, so a shared subsequence (BC in ABCD versus XBCY) gets a different code in each context. Simulation: 2048 minicolumns of 32 cells; on a stream with embedded six-element sequences the network reached the 50% ceiling after about 2500 elements, against about 33% for a first-order network; little loss up to 40% cell death (sim). The paper models one cellular layer: no hierarchy, no temporal pooling, no stable sequence code sent upward. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2016.00023/full)
- Cui, Ahmad & Hawkins (2016), "Continuous online sequence learning with an unsupervised neural network model", Neural Computation 28(11):2474-2504, benchmarks the same single-layer sequence memory on streaming prediction [BIB] (sim). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00893)
- Temporal pooling. A Numenta patent describes a union processor whose elements stay active longer than one time step and pool the patterns of a learned sequence into a more stable representation; an unreviewed 2015 preprint describes layer 2/3 cells forming a stable, slowly changing code as layer 4 moves through a sequence; a secondary source notes that temporal pooling is not well understood and that its meaning changed as the algorithms changed [AB] (proposal). No peer-reviewed quantitative evaluation of the pooling step was located. — [patent US10776687](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10776687); [preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08255); [secondary source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_temporal_memory)
- Hawkins, Lewis, Klukas, Purdy & Ahmad (2019), "A framework for intelligence and cortical function based on grid cells in the neocortex", Frontiers in Neural Circuits 12:121 [FT]. The Thousand Brains proposal: every column in every region learns complete object models in a location (grid-cell-like) reference frame; hierarchy remains but is no longer a ladder of feature extractors; sequences are demoted to behaviours of objects, represented as sequences of displacement vectors; objects compose through predicted displacement cells; abstract knowledge is conjectured to be stored in location spaces, with thinking as movement through them. No simulations in this paper; the cell types are untested predictions (proposal). Companion: Hawkins, Ahmad & Cui (2017), Frontiers in Neural Circuits 11:81 [BIB]. — [PMC6336927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6336927); [2017 paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2017.00081)
- Clay, Leadholm & Hawkins (2024), "The Thousand Brains Project: a new paradigm for sensorimotor intelligence" (arXiv) [FT, first two thirds]. Learning modules share one input-output interface and can be stacked; a higher module uses the lower module's recognised object identity as a feature, which is how compositional objects are to be built. Object behaviours and abstract concepts are listed as target capabilities; a dataset with objects that change over time exists but had not been tested (proposal, partial implementation). — [arXiv 2412.18354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18354)
- Leadholm, Clay, Knudstrup, Lee & Hawkins (2025), "Thousand-brains systems: sensorimotor intelligence for rapid, robust learning and inference" (arXiv) [FT, first half]. Evaluation is 3D object recognition and pose estimation on 77 YCB objects presented in isolation: 98.6% accuracy on learned rotations, 95.1% with feature noise, 93.0% on novel rotations, 88.1% with both. No stacked-hierarchy, compositional-object, behaviour or abstract-concept experiment appears in the part read; the system is described as at an early stage (sim; authors' own evaluation, no independent replication found). — [arXiv 2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04494)

**One rule or two**
- In George & Hawkins (2009) one node algorithm is replicated at every level, and that algorithm has two sub-steps: spatial pooling into coincidences and temporal grouping of coincidences into Markov chains. The abstraction step is the temporal one: the name of a chain is the unit handed upward. This is the closest published realisation of "a recurring temporal pattern at one level becomes a single unit one level up", using sequence learning as the mechanism of abstraction. — [PMC2749218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2749218)

### Inferences
- HTM's answer to the hypothesis is affirmative in design and thin in evidence. The recursive scheme (sequence memory, then a stable name, then sequence memory over names) was specified in 2004-2009 but the only multi-level implementation uses a batch partition of a transition matrix inside a fixed tree, with no feedback effect on the upward signal.
- The 2016 neuron-level model solved the hard part of sequence memory (context-dependent codes for high-order sequences) and in doing so made upward naming harder, because the same input now has many context-specific codes that a pooling stage must reunite. That pooling stage is the unpublished piece.
- The later shift to location-based models is itself evidence that the originators did not find temporal sequence alone sufficient to build object-level abstraction; they added a second organising signal (movement-derived location).
- HTM groups coincidences that follow each other in time. That is a contiguity criterion (things that occur in sequence are one cause), not a substitutability criterion; it yields invariance and part-whole structure, not categories of interchangeable items.

### Gaps
- On Intelligence (Hawkins with Blakeslee, 2004) was not consulted directly; the framework is cited here through the 2009 papers by the same authors.
- The earlier Numenta vision implementation and its accuracy figures were not retrieved.
- The second halves of both Thousand Brains Project papers (heterarchy, action policy, efficiency results) were not read.

## 4. Hierarchical predictive coding with separated timescales (Rao & Ballard; Friston; Kiebel, Daunizeau & Friston; Hasson; Yamashita & Tani; Tani)

### Takeaway
One learning principle (minimise prediction error, usually by gradient descent through time) applied throughout a network with different time constants gives a functional hierarchy in which fast units hold reusable primitives and slow units hold their ordering. The hierarchy is temporal abstraction, not categorical abstraction, and in the best-known demonstrations the timescale separation is set by the modeller, not discovered.

### Cited Findings
- Rao & Ballard (1999), "Predictive coding in the visual cortex", Nature Neuroscience 2(1):79-87. A hierarchical network in which feedback carries predictions of lower-level activity and feedforward connections carry residual errors; trained on natural images it develops simple-cell-like receptive fields, and error-carrying units show end-stopping and other extra-classical effects [AB] (sim). The hierarchy in this paper is spatial (image patches), not temporal [BIB-level recollection]. — [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10195184/)
- Friston (2005), "A theory of cortical responses", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 360:815-836, and Friston & Kiebel (2009), "Predictive coding under the free-energy principle", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 364:1211-1221, give the general hierarchical free-energy formulation [BIB] (theory). — [Friston 2005](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2005.1622); [Friston & Kiebel 2009](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2008.0300)
- Kiebel, Daunizeau & Friston (2008), "A hierarchy of time-scales and the brain", PLoS Computational Biology 4(11):e1000209 [FT]. Claim: cortical anatomy recapitulates the temporal hierarchy of environmental dynamics. Model: a two-level generative model of birdsong with a Lorenz attractor at each level; the slow level's output sets a control parameter of the fast level. The time constants (0.25 s and 2 s), the number of levels and the coupling are fixed by the modeller. Recognition is variational free-energy minimisation with errors passed up and predictions down. Simulations: the model tracks both levels; after an omission the lower level decays within about 100 ms while the upper level keeps following the expected sequence; removing the upper level shrinks omission responses and makes the model fail under noise (sim, proof of concept). Reviews evidence for a caudal-to-rostral increase in timescale. — [PMC2568860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2568860)
- Kiebel, von Kriegstein, Daunizeau & Friston (2009), "Recognizing sequences of sequences", PLoS Computational Biology 5(8):e1000464, extends this to hierarchies of sequential dynamics for speech-like input [BIB] (sim). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000464)
- Hasson, Yang, Vallines, Heeger & Rubin (2008), "A hierarchy of temporal receptive windows in human cortex", J. Neurosci. 28(10):2539-2550. With films played forward, backward or scrambled at several scales, early visual areas respond reliably regardless of temporal disruption, while superior temporal sulcus and precuneus need about 12 s of intact structure and lateral sulcus, temporo-parietal junction and frontal eye field about 36 s [AB] (data). — [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18322098/)
- Lerner, Honey, Silbert & Hasson (2011), J. Neurosci. 31(8):2906-2915, maps the same hierarchy in auditory and language areas with a narrated story [AB] (data). — [PMC3089381](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3089381)
- Murray et al. (2014), "A hierarchy of intrinsic timescales across primate cortex", Nature Neuroscience 17:1661-1663 [BIB] (data); Baldassano, Chen, Zadbood, Pillow, Hasson & Norman (2017), "Discovering event structure in continuous narrative perception and memory", Neuron 95:709-721 [BIB] (data). — [Murray et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3862); [Baldassano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.06.041)
- Yamashita & Tani (2008), "Emergence of functional hierarchy in a multiple timescale neural network model: a humanoid robot experiment", PLoS Computational Biology 4(11):e1000220 [FT]. A continuous-time recurrent network of 180 units: 100 input-output units (time constant 2), 60 fast context units (5) and 20 slow context units (70); slow units have no direct connection to input-output. Trained by backpropagation through time (learning rate 5e-4, 5000 iterations) on hand-guided sensorimotor sequences of a humanoid robot. Fast units came to encode reusable movement primitives that generalised over object position; slow units encoded the switching between primitives. New sequences of old primitives were learned by updating only the slow units' connections, with the fast-unit primitives preserved. Control: equal time constants performed significantly worse (p < 0.01); a slow-to-fast ratio above 5 performed best. Limits stated: 180 rate units, 8 degrees of freedom, simple tasks, only a macro-level correspondence to the brain (sim, robot). — [PMC2570613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2570613/)
- Tani & Nolfi (1999), "Learning to perceive the world as articulated: an approach for hierarchical learning in sensory-motor systems", Neural Networks 12:1131-1141, and Tani (2014), "Self-organization and compositionality in cognitive brains: a neurorobotics study", Proceedings of the IEEE 102(4):586-605, are the predecessor (prediction-error-driven segmentation with a higher level learning the sequence of segments) and the review of this programme's compositionality claims [BIB]. — [Tani & Nolfi](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080%2899%2900060-X); [Tani 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPROC.2014.2308604)
- Koutník, Greff, Gomez & Schmidhuber (2014), "A Clockwork RNN", ICML (PMLR 32:1863-1871): hidden layer split into modules with fixed, different clock rates; fewer parameters and better results than plain RNN and LSTM on audio generation and spoken-word classification in preliminary experiments [AB] (sim). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/koutnik14.html)
- Chung, Ahn & Bengio (2017), "Hierarchical multiscale recurrent neural networks", ICLR: learned binary boundary detectors choose UPDATE, COPY or FLUSH at each layer and step, so timescales are discovered instead of preset; trained with a straight-through estimator; the discovered boundaries resemble the intrinsic structure of character and handwriting sequences [AB] (sim). — [arXiv 1609.01704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01704)
- Botvinick & Plaut (2004), "Doing without schema hierarchies: a recurrent connectionist approach to normal and impaired routine sequential action", Psychological Review 111(2):395-429: a recurrent network trained on routine action sequences shows hierarchically structured behaviour with no explicit hierarchy of schema units [BIB] (sim). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.2.395)

**One rule or two**
- One learning rule throughout (prediction-error minimisation). The hierarchy comes from a second ingredient that is not a learning rule: a difference in time constants, imposed by the designer (Kiebel et al.; Yamashita & Tani; Clockwork RNN) or learned as boundary decisions (Chung et al.). — [PMC2570613](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2570613/); [arXiv 1609.01704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01704)

### Inferences
- This family supports a specific, limited version of the shared-mechanism idea: sequence learning with one rule gives a hierarchy of temporal grain (primitive, sequence of primitives). It does not give categories or variables, and it creates no new units; the levels pre-exist as populations with different time constants.
- The Yamashita-Tani control is informative for any design with a medium-term memory acting as promoter: remove the timescale gap and the hierarchy does not form. The gap is the architectural prior doing the work that a promotion rule would otherwise do.
- The temporal-receptive-window and intrinsic-timescale data show that cortex has a timescale hierarchy. They do not show that the same learning rule operates at each level, or that the hierarchy is learned from sequences.
- Botvinick & Plaut is a caution in the other direction: hierarchically organised behaviour can arise in a system with no levels at all, so observing hierarchical behaviour does not demonstrate a hierarchical mechanism.

### Gaps
- Whether a timescale hierarchy can self-organise from sequence prediction with a local, biologically plausible rule and no preset time constants was not established from the sources read.
- Hierarchical joint-embedding predictive architectures and deep video predictive-coding networks were not sourced this session and are omitted.
- Tani's later variational models and his 2016 book were not consulted.

## 5. Compression of history (Schmidhuber's chunker; Elman's "Finding structure in time"; language models and the reversal curse)

### Takeaway
Schmidhuber's history compressor is a literal instance of one predictive rule applied recursively to build levels: each level learns to predict its own input and passes up only what it failed to predict. Next-item prediction also yields category structure (Elman) and, at scale, linearly decodable syntax and concept directions, but as soft geometry in one representation space, not as stacked discrete levels; and the reversal curse shows that directional prediction does not produce symmetric equivalence.

### Cited Findings
- Schmidhuber (1992), "Learning complex, extended sequences using the principle of history compression", Neural Computation 4(2):234-242 [FT]. Principle: a sequence can be reconstructed from the unpredicted inputs and the times at which they occurred, so expected inputs can be ignored without loss. Architecture 1, a self-organising multilevel predictor hierarchy: every level is a recurrent network trained to predict its own next input; when level s stops improving it is frozen and a new level s+1 is created; level s+1 is updated only when level s fails to predict, receiving the unpredicted input plus a representation of the time step. Higher levels therefore see shorter sequences and have shorter credit-assignment paths. Architecture 2 collapses the hierarchy into two networks: the chunker receives the automatizer's unpredicted inputs, and the automatizer is additionally trained to predict the chunker's internal state, so more inputs become predictable and the chunker is used less. Experiment (run by Hochreiter): a 20-step-lag task with 22 symbols in a continuous stream; a conventional recurrent network showed no significant improvement after more than one million training sequences; the chunking system solved it in under 5,000 sequences in 13 of 17 runs and under 35,000 in the rest, with error propagated only three steps back. Stated limits: the number of levels needed is not known in advance; levels are explicitly separated; the two-network version has no stability proof (sim, one task). — [paper PDF](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/rnn/1992-schmidhuber.pdf); [record](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1992/schmidhuber1992neco-learning-a)
- A later paper by the same group characterises the history compressor as a stack of recurrent networks running at different time scales, in which a higher network receives input only when the one below fails to predict [AB]. — [Clockwork RNN text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/koutnik14.html)
- Elman (1990), "Finding structure in time", Cognitive Science 14:179-211 [FT]. Simple recurrent network trained only to predict the next element. In a letter-stream task the prediction error serves as a cue to word boundaries. In the word task, 29 lexical items in 13 classes and 15 sentence templates gave 10,000 two- and three-word sentences, a stream of 27,354 words; the network had 31 input and output units and 150 hidden and context units. **One rule or two**
- Schmidhuber: one rule (predict your own next input), applied recursively, creates the levels. The higher level is a temporally coarser sequence of surprises, not a category. — [paper PDF](https://www.gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/rnn/1992-schmidhuber.pdf)
- Elman and language models: one objective, no levels, no discrete units. Categories appear because items with the same predictive consequences are driven to similar internal codes. — [paper PDF](https://papers.baulab.info/papers/Elman-1990.pdf)

### Inferences
- The history compressor is the cleanest existing statement of a recursive mechanism where sequence learning builds order: what a level cannot predict is the alphabet of the next level. Its criterion for promotion is failure of prediction. This is the opposite of chunking by recurring co-occurrence, where success of prediction (a reliable pair) is what gets promoted. The two criteria build different hierarchies: surprises upward (boundaries, events) versus regularities upward (chunks).
- Prediction learning produces categories by a route that is not mutual prediction. Elman's nouns cluster because they occur in the same contexts, not because one noun predicts another. The operative statistic is shared context (substitutability).
- The reversal curse and the influence-function result are direct evidence that sequence learning in one direction does not create a symmetric equivalence in the learned weights. Human pairs are symmetric (section 1), so this is a real gap between next-token learners and human association, and it argues that an equivalence-class operation must be specified separately from directional sequence learning, with its own bidirectional evidence.
- Probing results show hierarchy and concept directions are present in trained language models, but they are evidence about what large distributed predictors can represent, not evidence for a recursive level-building rule: there is no point at which a new unit is created.

### Gaps
- No published replication or scaled-up test of Schmidhuber's multilevel hierarchy on natural data was located this session.
- Whether the reversal curse persists in 2025-2026 frontier models with other training objectives was not checked.
- The details of the agreement and hierarchical-generalisation studies (Linzen, Gulordava, McCoy) are cited from bibliographic records only.
- Probing claims are sensitive to probe design; control-task critiques of probing were not surveyed.

## 6. Predictive maps and structure learning (successor representation; temporal context model; Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine; clone-structured cognitive graphs; Schapiro et al.; Gluck & Myers): do states that predict each other, or that predict the same things, come to share a representation?

### Takeaway
Yes on both counts, and the two are different statistics that coincide only for undirected, clustered transition structure. States that predict the same successors get similar codes in every predictive-map model; states that predict each other (mutually reachable under symmetric transitions) end up in the same community of the map; under one-way transitions the map is asymmetric and expresses prediction, not similarity. In all these models the result is graded similarity; turning it into higher-level units takes a further operation (spectral decomposition, community detection, or a second pathway).

### Cited Findings
- Dayan (1993), "Improving generalization for temporal difference learning: the successor representation", Neural Computation 5(4):613-624. Represents each state by its expected discounted future occupancy of all states, introduced to generalise between states with similar successors; value factorises into this representation and a reward term [AB] (theory, sim). — [paper PDF](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~dayan/papers/d93b.pdf); [later description](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00282)
- Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (2017), "The hippocampus as a predictive map", Nature Neuroscience 20:1643-1653 [FT]. Place cells modelled as successor-representation units learned by temporal-difference updates. Under a directed policy on a track, fields skew backward against the direction of travel (asymmetric map). On the community-structured graph of Schapiro et al., rows of the learned matrix cluster by community, because states in one community are visited close together in time and so predict one another; larger discount factors widen fields until they cover a whole compartment. The eigenvectors of the matrix give grid-like fields, do not depend on the discount factor, and split a compartmentalised environment across its bottlenecks, which the authors propose for subgoal discovery and hierarchical planning (sim, comparison with published data). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650); [paper PDF](https://www.gwern.net/docs/rl/2017-stachenfeld.pdf)
- Gershman, Moore, Todd, Norman & Sederberg (2012), "The successor representation and temporal context", Neural Computation 24(6):1553-1568, relates the temporal context model's learning to estimation of the successor representation [BIB] (theory). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00282)

**One rule or two**
- Successor representation: one local rule (temporal-difference or asymmetric Hebbian) gives the predictive map; levels come from a separate spectral step or from a gradient of discount factors. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4650)
- Lynn et al.: one process (order-blurred transition counting) gives both transition knowledge and community abstraction, at one level only. — [arXiv 1805.12491](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12491)
- Clone-structured graphs: one rule for sequence learning; hierarchy by a separate community-detection pass; schema transfer by a separate rebinding step. — [PMC8062558](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8062558/)
- Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine: one objective, two memory systems on two time scales; scales are architectural. — [PMC7707106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707106)
- Schapiro et al. 2017: two pathways with different sparsity and learning rates. — [PMC5124075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5124075)

### Inferences
- "Predict each other" and "predict the same things" are separable. On an undirected clustered graph both hold for members of a community, which is why the Schapiro graph cannot tell them apart. Under directed transitions the predictive map is asymmetric (backward-skewed fields), and A predicting B does not make A and B similar. The Schapiro 2017 boundary-node result isolates the second statistic: nodes that never co-occurred became similar because they share neighbours.
- A mutual-prediction test for merging is, in these terms, a test on the symmetric part of the multi-step transition matrix. That is a legitimate and well-supported basis for grouping (communities, events), and it is exactly what the eigenvector and community-detection steps extract. It groups things that occur together, which gives events and contexts, not classes of interchangeable items.
- The Lynn et al. result is the strongest support in this section for a shared mechanism: no extra operation is needed for community-level abstraction beyond imprecise temporal integration of transitions. Its limit is that it produces one level; nothing in it recurses.
- Clone-structured graphs show the opposite operation is also needed: sequence context must split one percept into several latent states before any merging is meaningful. Abstraction built from sequences needs differentiation as well as compression, which is the Gluck-Myers pairing.
- In every model here the higher-order structure is read out of a learned transition structure by an operation different from the one that learned it.

- No source was found that tests a recursive application of the successor representation (a predictive map over the communities of a predictive map) with a single learning rule.

## 7. Chunking models (Miller; Soar; competitive chunking; CHREST; PARSER; Wu et al.; options and hierarchical reinforcement learning): is abstraction (a variable or category) a different operation from chunking (a concatenation)?

### Takeaway
In every model that states its operations explicitly, yes. Chunking joins units that follow each other more often than chance; abstraction groups units that occur in the same context and can replace each other. The models that learn both (ADIOS, the hierarchical variable model) implement them as two operations with two statistics and interleave them recursively; models with chunking alone build part-whole hierarchies but do not transfer to new items.

**One rule or two**
- Chunk-only learners (competitive chunking, PARSER, Sequitur, the 2022 hierarchical chunking model): one concatenation rule applied recursively gives sequence knowledge and a part-whole hierarchy; no categories or variables. — [arXiv 2410.21332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21332)
- ADIOS and the hierarchical variable model: two operations (concatenate by adjacency statistics; group by shared context), alternated recursively. — [PMC1187953](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1187953/); [arXiv 2410.21332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21332)
- Hierarchical reinforcement learning: the same error-driven rule learns at both levels once the levels exist; discovering the levels is a different computation. — [PMC2783353](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2783353/)

### Inferences
- The chunk and the class are different objects. A chunk is a conjunction in time (A then B, as one unit); a class is a disjunction in a position (A or B, in this slot). They are found by different statistics: dependence between neighbours for the chunk, sameness of neighbours for the class. Two items in one class typically do not predict each other at all, because they are alternatives.
- This bears directly on a rule that merges two units into an equivalence class because each predicts the other. Mutual prediction between adjacent units is the chunking statistic, and what it identifies is a composite, not a class. The class statistic is shared context. ADIOS and the hierarchical variable model both needed the second statistic, and the 2024/2025 comparison shows the chunk-only model does not transfer.
- The two operations are nonetheless mutually dependent and recursive in the working systems: classes are defined over the contexts that chunks provide, and new chunks are formed over classes. A single recursive loop with two alternating steps is the pattern that has actually been demonstrated.
- "Chunking" names at least three different operations in this literature: recoding in memory (Miller), concatenation of co-occurring units (PARSER, competitive chunking, Wu et al.), and compilation of a solved subgoal into a rule with variables (Soar). Only the third includes abstraction, and it is not driven by sequence statistics.
- In reinforcement learning the direction of dependence reported by Tomov et al. is from state abstraction to temporal abstraction: clusters of states determine how actions are chunked, not the reverse.

## 8. Evidence against one mechanism: complementary learning systems, and findings that categories and sequences rely on different statistics

### Takeaway
The strongest counter-evidence is architectural and statistical. Fast learning of specific sequences or episodes and slow extraction of shared structure trade off against each other and are assigned to systems with different sparsity and learning rates, even inside the hippocampus. Sequence structure rests on contiguity while category structure rests on substitutability, and human and model data dissociate them. The unification that survives is weaker: one predictive objective can produce both kinds of knowledge in a distributed system, but no model shows one rule that both learns sequences and recursively creates discrete levels of categories.

- What the hypothesis gets right: time is the dominant teaching signal for abstraction in nearly every family, and several models build higher units from temporal regularity at the level below with the same procedure repeated (HTM node, history compressor, chunking models, hierarchical SFA).
- What it gets wrong, on this evidence: no model obtains categories (classes of interchangeable units, variables) from the same statistic that gives sequences. Sequences and chunks come from dependence between successive units; categories come from sameness of surrounding context. Models that produce both either use two explicit operations or bury both in a distributed code with no discrete levels.
- Specific to the criterion "two units that each predict the other are merged into an equivalence class": mutual prediction identifies units that belong to one composite or one community (an assembly, a chunk, an event), which is a part-whole relation. Equivalence in the sense of a class requires that the two units predict, and are predicted by, the same third parties. The two criteria coincide only in undirected, community-structured transition graphs, which is the one case most experiments use.
- Different learning rates are likely unavoidable. Complementary-learning-systems theory, the within-hippocampus model, and the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine all separate a fast store of specific conjunctions or sequences from a slow extractor of shared structure. A medium-term memory that promotes recurring co-occurrences is functionally the slow pathway; if it shares weights and rates with the fast sequence store, the cited theory predicts interference.
- Recursion remains under-demonstrated. Where one rule is applied recursively and results are reported, the scale is small: a 22-symbol task, 29 words, 15-node graphs, motion capture with 239 poses, toy grammars. Large systems that work (language models) are not recursive level-builders.

- The search budget was exhausted before this question was searched. Henin et al., Peña et al., Endress & Bonatti, Dehaene et al., and the corpus-model papers are cited from verified bibliographic records, with content from prior knowledge; each should be re-read before being quoted in a report.
- The 1995 complementary-learning-systems paper was not opened; its claims are reported through the 2016 update by two of the same authors.
- No direct experimental test was found that pits mutual prediction against shared context as the driver of representational merging in the same subjects with directed transition graphs. The community-structure studies confound them.
- 2025-2026 literature was not swept. Possible relevant recent work on bidirectional or factorisation-agnostic training, on hierarchical predictive architectures, and on neural implementations of variable learning is therefore missing.
- Category-learning models that involve no temporal signal at all (exemplar and prototype models) were not reviewed; they would strengthen the case that category formation can proceed from similarity alone.
