## 1. Order theory: is "mutual relation resolved into an equivalence class one level up" exactly the preorder-to-poset quotient and the condensation of a digraph?

### Takeaway
Yes, provided the relation is reflexive and transitive (or is first closed under transitivity): the rule is the standard quotient of a preorder by its symmetric part, and for a directed graph it is the condensation by strongly connected components. It is one operation that returns two things at once (a partition and a partial order on the blocks), and it is idempotent, so by itself it is not a level-generating recursion. **Verdict: NEITHER** (one one-shot operation; it neither chunks nor recurses).

### Cited Findings
- [DEFINITION] A preorder (also "quasiorder") is a reflexive, transitive relation; it is a partial order without the antisymmetry requirement. — [nLab, preorder](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/preorder)
- [PROVED, elementary] For a preorder, define a ~ b iff a <= b and b <= a. This is an equivalence relation and the quotient set P/~ is a poset; the order on classes is [x] <= [y] iff x <= y. — [nLab, preorder](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/preorder); [Wikipedia, Preorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder)
- [PROVED] A preorder on X is the same data as an equivalence relation on X together with a partial order on the equivalence classes; the correspondence between preorders and (partition, partial order on blocks) pairs is one-to-one. — [Wikipedia, Preorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder)
- [PROVED] The strict part is a < b iff a <= b and not b <= a; and a <= b iff (a < b or a ~ b). So a preorder splits exactly into a strict (asymmetric) part and an equivalence (symmetric) part. — [Wikipedia, Preorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder)
- [PROVED] Category-theoretic form: a preordered set is a thin category (at most one arrow between two objects); every preordered set is equivalent, as a category, to a poset (a special case of the skeleton theorem); a preordered set is a poset exactly when it is skeletal. nLab refers to the result as the "poset reflection". — [nLab, preorder](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/preorder)
- [PROVED] Every preorder is the reachability relation of some directed graph; antisymmetry corresponds to having no cycles, so partial orders correspond to directed acyclic graphs. — [Wikipedia, Preorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder)
- [PROVED] In a directed graph, "each reachable from the other" is an equivalence relation; its classes are the strongly connected components (SCCs). Contracting each SCC to one vertex gives the condensation, which is a directed acyclic graph. A digraph is acyclic iff no SCC has more than one vertex. — [Wikipedia, Strongly connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_connected_component)
- [PROVED] Tarjan's algorithm finds all SCCs in one depth-first pass in time Theta(V+E). Reference: R. E. Tarjan (1972), "Depth-first search and linear graph algorithms", SIAM Journal on Computing 1(2):146-160. — [Wikipedia, Strongly connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_connected_component); [DOI 10.1137/0201010](https://doi.org/10.1137/0201010)
- [PROVED] The same construction is the standard "class structure" of a Markov chain: two states communicate iff each is reachable from the other; this is an equivalence relation whose classes are the communicating classes; a class is closed if it cannot be left; recurrence, transience and periodicity are properties of whole classes. — [Wikipedia, Markov chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain)
- [PROVED] The same construction appears in semigroup theory as Green's relations: a L b, a R b, a J b iff a and b generate the same principal left, right, two-sided ideal (mutual divisibility); each comes from a preorder (ideal inclusion); L- and R-classes are exactly the SCCs of the left and right Cayley graphs. Reference: J. A. Green (1951), "On the structure of semigroups", Annals of Mathematics 54(1):163-172. — [Wikipedia, Green's relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green%27s_relations)
- [PROVED] The same construction appears in topology: identifying topologically indistinguishable points yields a T0 space (the Kolmogorov quotient), and a space is T0 iff its specialization preorder is a partial order. Finite topologies correspond one-to-one to finite preorders. — [Wikipedia, Kolmogorov space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_space); [Wikipedia, Preorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preorder)

### Inferences
- The design rule is exactly this construction only when the base relation is transitive. The symmetric part of a non-transitive relation is symmetric but need not be transitive, so "A~B and B~C" would not put A and C in one class. SCC condensation works because it uses mutual reachability (the reflexive-transitive closure), not mutual direct links. A rule that fires only on direct mutual links finds 2-cycles, not components. (Clark and Eyraud, section 6 below, face the same issue and explicitly take the transitive closure of their substitution graph.)
- The operation is idempotent: the quotient of a poset by ~ is the poset itself, and the condensation of a DAG is the DAG itself (both follow from the cited facts "poset iff skeletal" and "acyclic iff no non-trivial SCC"). So applying rule (2) again at the next level does nothing unless a new relation is defined on the classes. Rule (2) alone therefore cannot generate an unbounded hierarchy of levels.
- One preorder yields both the classes and the order among classes. "Equivalence class formation" and "ordering of concepts" are thus two readouts of a single relation, not two mechanisms. But the construction only reorganises existing elements; it creates no new composite units, so it does not do what rule (1), recurrence/chunking, does.
- The meaning of the resulting class depends entirely on the base relation. Mutual succession or reachability ("A is followed by B and B by A") gives cycles/recurrent classes whose members are not interchangeable (Markov communicating classes). Mutual substitutability or mutual inclusion of contexts gives classes whose members are interchangeable (section 3 and 6). Mutual parthood gives coincidence (section 2). The same quotient applied to "followed by" and to "can replace" therefore produces opposite kinds of class (a loop versus a category).
- "One level up" is accurate in the set-theoretic sense: the elements of the quotient are sets of original elements. The order on them is inherited, not new.

## 2. Mereology: antisymmetry of parthood, mutual parthood, coincident objects, and the right resolution

### Takeaway
Classical mereology makes parthood a partial order by axiom, so mutual parthood is identity. Non-extensional mereologies drop antisymmetry, making parthood a preorder in which distinct things can be mutual parts; Cotnoir (2010) shows that making the resulting "mereological equivalence" explicit recovers classical structure, which is the section 1 quotient. The literature offers three resolutions of the statue-and-clay case: identity, coincidence without identity, and a further (formal) component. **Verdict: NEITHER** (confirms that rule (2) is the poset reflection; bears on parthood only, not on sequence learning).

### Cited Findings
- [DEFINITION] Core mereology takes parthood to be reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric, i.e. a partial order. Antisymmetry (P.3): if x is part of y and y is part of x then x = y. Because of P.3, identity can be introduced by definition as mutual parthood. — [SEP, Mereology (A. Varzi; rev. 27 June 2026), sections 2.1-2.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [DEFINITION] "Improper part": the entry treats identity as the limit, or improper, case of parthood; reflexivity amounts to counting each thing as an improper part of itself. — [SEP, Mereology, sections 1 and 2.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [DEFINITION] Two definitions of proper part: (20) PPxy := Pxy and not x = y; (20') PPxy := Pxy and not Pyx. They are equivalent only given antisymmetry. Without antisymmetry (20') is strictly stronger: under (20) distinct mutual parts are proper parts of each other, under (20') they are not, and (20') forces proper parthood to be asymmetric. If things may be proper parts of themselves, proper parthood needs another definition or must be primitive. — [SEP, Mereology, section 2.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROPOSAL, philosophical] J. J. Thomson (1998), "The Statue and the Clay", Nous 32:149-173, holds that a statue and the clay constituting it are parts of each other yet distinct. The SEP entry describes this as not a popular view. — [SEP, Mereology, section 2.1 and bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROVED, formal construction] A. J. Cotnoir (2010), "Anti-Symmetry and Non-Extensional Mereology", The Philosophical Quarterly 60(239):396-405. Abstract: Varzi's defence of extensionality depends on assuming antisymmetry; the anti-extensionalist can reject antisymmetry on independent grounds; the paper constructs a non-extensional mereology in which antisymmetry fails; when "mereological equivalence" is made explicit, the theory recaptures the structure of classical mereology. — [Oxford Academic, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9213.2009.649.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9213.2009.649.x)
- [PROPOSAL, formal] A. J. Cotnoir and A. Bacon (2012), "Non-Wellfounded Mereology", The Review of Symbolic Logic 5:187-204. The SEP entry reports: it is a systematic study of mereologies in which antisymmetry may fail; proper parthood is axiomatised as transitive but neither irreflexive nor asymmetric (parthood loops are allowed); the theory lacks P.3 and is not extensional even when it includes strong supplementation (P.5). — [SEP, Mereology, sections 2.1, 2.2, 3.2 and bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/); [DOI 10.1017/S1755020311000293](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1755020311000293)
- [PROVED] Supplementation (every proper part is supplemented by a disjoint part) entails antisymmetry given reflexivity and transitivity; it is typically rejected by those who reject antisymmetry; a two-element model with mutual parthood is the standard countermodel. The derivation of extensionality requires antisymmetry. — [SEP, Mereology, section 3.1-3.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROPOSAL, philosophical] The SEP entry's own treatment of material coincidence distinguishes three cases (mutual parthood, one-way parthood, no parthood) and argues that in the genuine case the coinciding things do not share all parts (the statue's nose versus the corresponding matter), so extensionality is not violated. — [SEP, Mereology, section 3.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROPOSAL, philosophical] Survey of responses to the statue and the clay: (i) coincident objects / constitution without identity (Wiggins 1968; Thomson 1998; Baker 1997, 2000; Fine 2000, 2003; Koslicki 2008; Lowe); (ii) identity via temporal parts and counterpart theory (Lewis 1976, 1986; Sider 2001); (iii) dominant kinds (Burke 1992, 1994); (iv) eliminativism (Unger 1979; van Inwagen 1990); (v) relative identity (Geach); (vi) deflationism; (vii) hylomorphic views on which coinciding objects share all material parts but differ in a non-material (formal) aspect (Rea 1998; Koslicki 2008). — [SEP, Material Constitution (R. Wasserman; rev. 14 Jan 2026)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/material-constitution/)
- [PROVED] Models of general extensional mereology: the parthood relation is in effect inclusion restricted to the non-empty subsets of a set, i.e. a complete Boolean algebra with the zero element removed (Tarski 1935; first proved in Grzegorczyk 1955). — [SEP, Mereology, section 4.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- Bibliographic details confirmed from the SEP bibliography: Thomson 1983, "Parthood and Identity Across Time", Journal of Philosophy 80:201-220; Varzi 2008, "The Extensionality of Parthood and Composition", Philosophical Quarterly 58:108-133; Simons 1987, Parts: A Study in Ontology, Clarendon; Koslicki 2008, The Structure of Objects, OUP; Wiggins 1968, Philosophical Review 77:90-95; Lewis 1991, Parts of Classes, Blackwell; Cotnoir and Varzi 2021, Mereology, OUP. — [SEP, Mereology, bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)

### Inferences
- Dropping antisymmetry turns parthood into a preorder; Cotnoir's "mereological equivalence" is its symmetric part; recovering classical mereology "modulo equivalence" is the poset reflection of section 1. So formal mereology already contains the design rule as a recognised option: mutual parts are not sorted, they are equivalent, and the quotient is classical.
- Terminology consequence: under definition (20'), distinct mutual parts are parts that are not proper parts, i.e. non-identical improper parts. Classical mereology has exactly one improper part per object (itself); non-antisymmetric mereology has a whole equivalence class of them.
- The three philosophical resolutions correspond to three design choices. Identity: merge the two nodes, no new level. Coincidence: keep both and record their equivalence; the class is a new item one level up. Further component (hylomorphism): deny the symmetry; the statue is the clay plus a form, so the statue sits strictly above the clay and the relation is ordinary asymmetric parthood with an extra structural part. The third option says that an apparent mutual relation can be a sign of a missing component rather than of equivalence.
- Layering presupposes well-foundedness. If genuine parthood loops are kept (Cotnoir and Bacon), proper parthood is no longer a strict order and cannot assign ranks or levels. Any architecture with numbered "orders" must first eliminate loops, which is what the quotient does.

- I could not open the full text of Cotnoir 2010 or of Cotnoir and Bacon 2012 (Cambridge returned 403), so their exact axioms, the formal statement of the "recapture" result, and Cotnoir and Bacon's model theory are reported only via the abstract and the SEP summary.
- Thomson's own formulation (recalled: constitution at a time defined partly by mutual parthood at that time) was not re-read; the SEP Material Constitution entry does not report it.
- Recalled, not verified: D. Lewis's main thesis in Parts of Classes (1991) that the parts of a class are exactly its subclasses, with the member-singleton relation as the one primitive mereology cannot supply. The SEP entry confirms only that Lewis 1991 locates the difference between mereology and set theory in the treatment of singletons (section 4.4).

## 3. Automata and formal languages: is a "state" or "category" an equivalence class under future prediction?

### Takeaway
For deterministic recognisers this is a theorem: the states of the minimal automaton are exactly the classes of prefixes with the same set of continuations (Myhill-Nerode), and the two-sided version (same set of contexts) gives the syntactic congruence and syntactic monoid. The classes are finite in number only for regular languages. Separately, Krohn-Rhodes theory shows that finite-state behaviour decomposes into two irreducible kinds of component. **Verdict: ONE for state formation (a single quotient, defined as a fixed point); TWO for hierarchical decomposition (Krohn-Rhodes).**

### Cited Findings
- [PROVED] Myhill-Nerode theorem. For a language L, strings x and y are equivalent iff no extension z puts exactly one of xz, yz in L. Then (1) L is regular iff this relation has finitely many classes; (2) that number equals the number of states of the minimal DFA; (3) the minimal DFA is unique up to isomorphism, with one state per class and transitions [x] -a-> [xa]. For a non-regular language the canonical automaton exists but is infinite. The theorem generalises to tree automata. References: A. Nerode (1958), "Linear automaton transformations", Proc. AMS 9(4):541-544; Nerode and Sauer, WADC technical report, 1957. — [Wikipedia, Myhill-Nerode theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myhill%E2%80%93Nerode_theorem)
- [DEFINITION / PROVED] The derivative (left quotient) of S by u is the set of all continuations v with uv in S. For a regular expression the set of distinct derivatives is finite and yields a DFA with that many states. Reference: J. A. Brzozowski (1964), "Derivatives of regular expressions", Journal of the ACM 11(4):481-494. — [Wikipedia, Brzozowski derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brzozowski_derivative)
- [DEFINITION / PROVED] Syntactic congruence (Myhill congruence): s and t are congruent iff for all x, y, xsy is in S exactly when xty is. The quotient of the free monoid by it is the syntactic monoid, which is the transition monoid of the minimal automaton and the smallest monoid recognising the language. One-sided versions (same right quotient; same left quotient) are also defined. — [Wikipedia, Syntactic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_monoid)
- [PROVED] Two strings are syntactically congruent iff they have the same set of contexts {(l, r) : l u r in L}; the syntactic monoid is finite iff L is regular. — [Clark and Eyraud 2007, JMLR 8, Definitions 2-3 (PDF)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/clark07a/clark07a.pdf)
- [PROVED] Schutzenberger: the star-free languages are exactly those with finite aperiodic syntactic monoids. M.-P. Schutzenberger (1965), "On finite monoids having only trivial subgroups", Information and Control 8(2):190-194. — [Wikipedia, Syntactic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_monoid)
- [PROVED] Bisimilarity is the largest bisimulation, is an equivalence relation, and is the greatest fixed point of a monotone operator on relations (two states are related iff every move of one can be matched by the other into related states). It has a coalgebraic formulation. For finite systems it is computed by partition refinement (coarsest partition problem). — [Wikipedia, Bisimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisimulation)
- [PROVED] Krohn-Rhodes theorem. Every finite semigroup divides a finite alternating wreath product of finite simple groups (each dividing the semigroup) and finite aperiodic semigroups. Automaton form: every finite automaton embeds, after expansion of the state set, in a feed-forward cascade of components that are finite simple groups and banks of flip-flops. The irreducible "primes" are the finite simple groups and the non-group irreducibles, which are subsemigroups of the flip-flop monoid (a two-state automaton with an identity input and two reset inputs). Group complexity is the least number of group layers needed. Reference: K. Krohn and J. Rhodes (1965), "Algebraic theory of machines. I. Prime decomposition theorem for finite semigroups and machines", Transactions of the AMS 116:450-464. — [Wikipedia, Krohn-Rhodes theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn%E2%80%93Rhodes_theory)
- [PROVED] The set of all formal languages over an alphabet is a residuated lattice: multiplication is concatenation, and the left and right residuals are quotient-like operations satisfying x.y <= z iff y <= x\z iff x <= z/y. — [Wikipedia, Residuated lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residuated_lattice)

### Inferences
- There are two different future-based equivalences on the same language. The Nerode relation is a one-sided equivalence on prefixes (pasts) and yields states. The syntactic congruence is a two-sided equivalence on substrings and yields categories in the grammatical sense. A "state" is a class of histories; a "category" is a class of interchangeable segments. They are related (the syntactic monoid is the transition monoid of the minimal automaton) but not the same object.
- Both are instances of the section 1 quotient applied to an inclusion preorder: put u below v when every continuation (or context) accepted after u is accepted after v. Mutual inclusion is equality of the continuation (context) sets, hence the Nerode (syntactic) equivalence. So design rule (2) matches these theorems exactly when the "relation" is "everything predicted from A is predicted from B", not when it is "A predicts B".
- Minimisation is recursive in its definition (classes are defined in terms of transitions into classes: a greatest fixed point) but its output is a single partition and the operation is idempotent (minimising a minimal automaton changes nothing).
- Krohn-Rhodes is a genuine two-kinds theorem for sequential machines: reversible components (groups, which permute states, so every state is mutually reachable) and irreversible reset/memory components (flip-flops), arranged in layers. Mapping this onto the designer's two rules is only an analogy (cycles/mutual reachability versus set-and-hold), not a theorem.

- The Wikipedia page for Brzozowski derivatives does not state the converse (regular iff finitely many derivatives); that is the Myhill-Nerode theorem.

## 4. Computational mechanics: causal states, the epsilon-machine, and hierarchical reconstruction

### Takeaway
Within one model class the result is a theorem: causal states (classes of histories with identical conditional distributions over futures) are the unique minimal sufficient statistic for prediction. Across model classes, Crutchfield's hierarchical reconstruction re-applies the same grouping-into-classes step to the states of lower-level models, treating a series of models as data; he states that this is largely a reapplication of the same operation but that each jump also needs an additional structural element whose discovery is not formalised. **Verdict: ONE (a reapplied quotient), as a proposal with worked examples rather than a theorem; the lowest step is of a different kind.**

### Cited Findings
- [DEFINITION] Causal states: two histories are equivalent iff they give the same conditional distribution over futures (of all lengths); the causal states are the equivalence classes; the epsilon-machine is the causal-state function together with the labelled transition matrices. — [Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001, Definitions 5, 8, 9 (arXiv cond-mat/9907176)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)
- [PROVED] Results in Shalizi and Crutchfield: past and future are independent given the causal state (Lemma 2); causal states are the largest sets of histories homogeneous for futures (Lemma 3); the epsilon-machine is deterministic in the automata sense, i.e. current causal state plus next symbol fix the next causal state (Lemma 5); causal states are maximally prescient, H[future | any partition R] >= H[future | causal states] (Theorem 1); they are sufficient statistics (Corollary 1); any equally predictive ("prescient") rival partition is a refinement of the causal states up to measure zero (Lemma 7); causal states have minimal statistical complexity among prescient rivals (Theorem 2) and are the minimal sufficient statistic; any prescient rival with the same complexity is the same partition up to measure zero (Theorem 3, uniqueness); the epsilon-machine has minimal next-state uncertainty among prescient rivals (Theorem 4). — [Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001 (arXiv cond-mat/9907176)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)
- [PROVED, scope] The stated assumptions: exact joint probabilities over blocks of all lengths are known; discrete values; discrete time; a pure time series; stationarity. — [Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001, section VII B](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)
- Bibliographic: C. R. Shalizi and J. P. Crutchfield, "Computational mechanics: pattern and prediction, structure and simplicity", Journal of Statistical Physics 104 (2001), listed on arXiv as pages 816-879 (commonly cited as 817-879), DOI 10.1023/A:1010388907793. Its reference list gives J. P. Crutchfield and K. Young, "Inferring statistical complexity", Physical Review Letters 63:105-108, 1989, and J. P. Crutchfield, "The calculi of emergence: computation, dynamics, and induction", Physica D 75:11-54, 1994. — [arXiv cond-mat/9907176](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176)
- [PROPOSAL] Crutchfield 1994, hierarchical epsilon-machine reconstruction, as an explicit procedure: (1) the data stream is the level-0 model; (2) build the level-l model from the level-(l-1) model by factoring out equivalence classes in its state-transition structure: groups of lower-level states that show regularity become the causal states of the new level and transitions between groups become its transitions; (3) estimate successively more accurate models; (4) if model size diverges as accuracy increases, go up a level and repeat; (5) stop at the first level that gives a finite model. An epsilon-machine is then defined as the minimal model at the least powerful level giving a finite description, i.e. a fixed point of hierarchical reconstruction viewed as an operator on representations. — [Crutchfield 1994, Part III sections 1-3 (PDF)](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf)
- [PROPOSAL] On whether the step is the same at each level, the paper says: the key step in "innovation" is discovering new equivalence relations; a large part of that is a reapplication of epsilon-machine reconstruction (grouping lower-level states into classes with the same future morph), with the series of increasingly accurate lower-level models serving as the data stream for the next level; but there is an additional element beyond grouping states (a resettable counter for certain hidden Markov models; string productions at the onset of chaos; domains, walls and particles in spatial systems), and because no exhaustive ordered list of such elements is known, innovation retains a component of undetermined discovery. — [Crutchfield 1994, Part III section 3 (PDF)](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf)
- [PROPOSAL] The paper's Table 1 lists levels and the relation factored out at each: level 0 data stream; level 1 trees, built by grouping sequential measurements into recurring subsequences (block independence); level 2 finite automata, built by grouping tree nodes (conditional independence); level 3 string-production machines, built by grouping automaton states and inferring production rules (a recursive form of conditional independence). — [Crutchfield 1994, Table 1 (PDF)](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf)
- [PROPOSAL, worked example] At the period-doubling onset of chaos the number of inferred finite-automaton states grows without bound; reconstruction crosses four levels (data, trees, finite automata, stack automata) before a finite model is found: a machine with two string registers updated by the productions A -> BB, B -> BA, equivalently a one-way nondeterministic nested stack automaton. A threshold ("innovation rate") measures the growth of model size with accuracy and signals when to move up. — [Crutchfield 1994, Part III sections 4-5 (PDF)](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf)

### Inferences
- The single-level theory is the stochastic counterpart of Myhill-Nerode: quotient histories by equality of predicted futures; the quotient is optimal, minimal and unique. It is one operation, and (like minimisation) it is idempotent: the causal states of the causal-state process are the same states.
- The hierarchy is recursive in the relevant sense only partly. What recurs is "factor out an equivalence from the previous level's states". What does not recur automatically is the choice of what counts as "the same" at the new level, and the extra register or production structure. Crutchfield presents this honestly as open. So the hierarchy supports "one operation, reapplied" as a research programme, not as a result.
- Table 1 shows two kinds of grouping even in this framework. The first step (data to tree) groups consecutive symbols into recurring subsequences, which is chunking. Every later step groups states into classes. The appearance of string productions at level 3 is again a concatenative element. So the worked hierarchy itself interleaves a sequence-building step and a class-forming step.
- A trigger for moving up a level is formally specified (divergence of model size), which is a usable design criterion: promote to a new level when the current level's class count does not converge.

### Gaps
- I verified Physica D 75:11-54 only through the reference list of Shalizi and Crutchfield, not from the journal page; the PDF header says only "Physica D (1994) special issue".
- Recalled, not verified: for processes generated by non-unifilar hidden Markov models the set of causal states is generically infinite even when the generating model is finite (e.g. Marzen and Crutchfield 2017, Physical Review E 95:051301), which is one reason equivalence classes are not always the most compact predictive state (see section 5 on predictive state representations).
- No theorem was found that the hierarchical procedure terminates, is unique, or is optimal across levels.

## 5. State abstraction in reinforcement learning: concept = class of states with the same predicted futures?

### Takeaway
Bisimulation makes this exact for Markov decision processes: states are equivalent iff they have the same rewards and the same transition probabilities into each equivalence class, and the coarsest such partition is found by iterative refinement. But the literature also shows that "the same future" is relative to what must be preserved (five nested abstraction criteria), and that predictive state need not be an equivalence class at all (it can be a vector of predictions). **Verdict: ONE (a fixed-point quotient) for state abstraction; silent on chunking.**

- The existence of five exact abstraction criteria, ordered by coarseness, shows that there is no single "the" equivalence under future prediction: which futures matter (the full model, the value of a fixed policy, optimal values, the optimal action) changes the partition. Equivalence classes are the special case where the predictions are compared for exact equality. ## 6. Distributional learning of categories from sequences, the syntactic concept lattice, and formal concept analysis

### Takeaway
Yes: in formal concept analysis and in Clark's syntactic concept lattice, the partial order of categories arises as the lattice of closed sets of a two-way (Galois) relation between items and their contexts, and equivalence classes fall out as items with identical closures. Sequence composition enters separately, as concatenation, and is tied to the categories by residuation (an adjunction). Learnability theorems show the class-forming step is sound only under a restriction such as substitutability. **Verdict: TWO operations (concatenation and closure/class formation) inside one algebraic structure; formal concept analysis alone is ONE closure operation but has no sequencing.**

### Cited Findings
- [DEFINITION / PROVED] Formal concept analysis. A formal context is (G, M, I) with objects G, attributes M and incidence I. For a set A of objects, A' is the set of attributes shared by all of A; dually for attribute sets. The two maps form a Galois connection and their composites are closure operators. A formal concept is a pair (A, B) with A' = B and B' = A (extent, intent). Concepts are ordered by (A1, B1) <= (A2, B2) iff A1 is a subset of A2, equivalently B1 a superset of B2. Basic theorem: the concepts form a complete lattice, and every complete lattice is isomorphic to the concept lattice of some context. References: R. Wille (1982), "Restructuring lattice theory: an approach based on hierarchies of concepts", in I. Rival (ed.), Ordered Sets, pp. 445-470; B. Ganter and R. Wille (1999), Formal Concept Analysis: Mathematical Foundations, Springer. — [Wikipedia, Formal concept analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_concept_analysis)
- [DEFINITION] Harris-style substitutability has two readings: two strings occur in all the same contexts (strong; this is the syntactic congruence), or in at least one common context (weak). Structure requires the strong reading; finite data can only ever show the weak one. Weak substitutability is in general neither transitive nor a congruence. Harris reference as cited: Z. Harris (1954), "Distributional structure", Word 10(2-3):146-162. — [Clark and Eyraud 2007, sections 1-2 and references (PDF)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/clark07a/clark07a.pdf)
- [PROVED] Clark and Eyraud. A language is substitutable iff weak substitutability implies syntactic congruence (if two strings share one context they share all). Theorem 9: the class of substitutable context-free languages is identifiable in the limit from positive data, with polynomial time and a polynomial characteristic set. The algorithm builds a graph on observed substrings with an edge when two share a context, takes connected components (transitive closure) as nonterminals, and adds a production [u] -> [v][w] whenever u = vw, i.e. it reads productions off multiplication in the syntactic monoid. A. Clark and R. Eyraud (2007), "Polynomial identification in the limit of substitutable context-free languages", JMLR 8:1725-1745. — [JMLR abstract page](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/clark07a.html); [PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/clark07a/clark07a.pdf)
- [PROVED] Limits stated in the same paper: context-free grammars as a whole are not learnable in this model because the class is superfinite; the finite language {a, aa} is not substitutable (the algorithm would output all powers of a); {a^n b^n} is not substitutable although {a^n c b^n} is; substitutability implies Angluin's reversibility but not conversely; for non-regular languages the number of syntactic congruence classes is infinite; on toy natural-language samples the learned grammars overgenerate; substitutable context-free languages are properly included in the congruential languages. — [Clark and Eyraud 2007, sections 6-8 (PDF)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/clark07a/clark07a.pdf)
- [PROVED] Clark, Eyraud and Habrard (2010), "Using contextual representations to efficiently learn context-free languages", JMLR 11:2707-2744: contextual binary feature grammars model the lattice structure of the distributions of substrings; learnable from positive data plus a membership oracle with polynomial update time; the class covers all regular languages and the context-free languages satisfying two constraints. — [JMLR](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/clark10a.html)
- [PROVED] A. Clark, "The syntactic concept lattice: another algebraic theory of the context-free languages?", Journal of Logic and Computation 25(5):1203-1229 (2015; online 2013). The syntactic concept lattice is a residuated lattice associated with a language; it generalises the syntactic monoid; it has equivalent definitions via Galois connections and via maximal factorisations; it is the terminal object among complete idempotent semirings recognising the language; the non-terminals of weakly minimal context-free grammars correspond to elements of the lattice. — [Oxford Academic, DOI 10.1093/logcom/ext037](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/ext037)
- [PROVED] Residuation in the algebra of languages: concatenation has left and right residuals with x.y <= z iff y <= x\z iff x <= z/y. — [Wikipedia, Residuated lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residuated_lattice)

### Inferences
- The lattice does the section 1 construction automatically. Order items by "has at least the attributes (contexts) of": this is a preorder; items with identical attribute sets have the same closure and collapse to one concept; the concept order is the induced partial order. So "classes" and "order among classes" come from one closure operator. But the underlying relation is heterogeneous (items against contexts), and the mutual relation that produces a class is mutual substitutability, not mutual succession.
- The base relation here is derived from sequences: a context is what precedes and follows. So conceptual order can be computed from sequential data, but through the relation "occurs in context", i.e. sequence statistics are the input to the closure, not the same operation as it.
- Residuation is the precise formal link between sequence and category. A category can be defined as a residual: "the things that, placed after X, give a Z". Concatenation and residual are adjoint, so each determines the other, but they are distinct operations with different algebraic roles (a product and its adjoint implication). This is the best-supported formal sense in which sequence learning and category formation are "two sides of one structure" rather than one operation.
- Equivalence classes are too narrow in general. Non-regular languages have infinitely many congruence classes, and the learnable class built on congruence classes (substitutable languages) excludes even {a^n b^n}. Clark's later work therefore represents categories as elements of the lattice (closed sets, which are unions of congruence classes ordered by inclusion) rather than as single classes; the abstract of the 2015 paper states that the non-terminals of weakly minimal grammars correspond to lattice elements. For a design this argues for "concept = closed set under the Galois connection" over "concept = equivalence class".
- The inference from one shared context to full equivalence is ampliative and can be wrong; it is provably safe only on the substitutable class. Any class-forming rule triggered by partial evidence needs an explicit bias or a way to retract.

## 7. Grammar induction by compression: one operation (digram replacement) versus two (chunk and class); can class formation be reduced to chunking?

### Takeaway
Pure chunking compressors (Sequitur, Re-Pair, byte-pair encoding) use one recursive operation and provably output a grammar that generates only the input, so they cannot generalise. Every system that generalises adds a second ingredient: merging/classes (Stolcke and Omohundro; ADIOS; Wolff's SNPR; the 2024 hierarchical variable model) or, equivalently, alignment that leaves a variable slot where two sequences differ (alignment-based learning; Wolff's SP theory). Minimum description length unifies the objective, not the operation. **Verdict: TWO alternating operations; reducible to ONE only by adding variables (abstraction), which is itself an additional construct.**

### Cited Findings
- [PROPOSAL, with complexity proof] Sequitur infers a hierarchy from a symbol sequence by replacing each repeated phrase with a rule that generates it, recursively; two constraints drive it; time and space are linear in the input. Witten (1997), "Identifying hierarchical structure in sequences: a linear-time algorithm", Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research 7:67-82. — [arXiv cs/9709102](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9709102)
- [PROVED, by construction] Re-Pair repeatedly replaces the most frequent adjacent pair by a new symbol until no pair occurs twice; the output is a straight-line program, a context-free grammar that generates only the original string. N. J. Larsson and A. Moffat (2000), "Off-line dictionary-based compression", Proceedings of the IEEE 88(11):1722-1732. — [Wikipedia, Re-Pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-Pair)
- [PROPOSAL] Byte-pair encoding is the same single operation (replace the most frequent adjacent pair by a new symbol), introduced by P. - [PROVED] The smallest grammar problem asks for the smallest context-free grammar generating exactly one given string (a straight-line grammar). - [PROVED] In Post's lattice of Boolean clones, the clone generated by AND contains only conjunctions (and a constant) and the clone generated by OR only disjunctions; they are distinct, and OR is not definable from AND alone. — [Wikipedia, Post's lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%27s_lattice)

- [PROVED] Tietze transformations: adding a new generator defined as a word in the existing generators, or adding a relation that already follows from the others, does not change the group presented; any two finite presentations of the same group are connected by such moves. H. Tietze (1908), Monatshefte fur Mathematik und Physik. — [Wikipedia, Tietze transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tietze_transformations)

### Inferences
- Chunking is conservative; class formation is ampliative. A chunk-only grammar has one rule per nonterminal and so generates exactly its input (cited for Re-Pair). Merging two nonterminals, or giving a nonterminal a second rule, enlarges the generated language (cited from Stolcke and Omohundro). These are different in kind: chunking is a definitional extension (it names a product and changes nothing that is generated, like adding a defined generator in a Tietze move), whereas merging is a quotient (it identifies two symbols and, unless the identification was already implied, changes what is generated).
- Counting argument for why classes compress what chunks cannot: if all m x n combinations a_i b_j occur, listing them costs on the order of m.n symbols and no adjacent pair repeats, so digram replacement finds nothing; with two classes the description costs on the order of m + n. Class formation exploits the product-of-sums structure; chunking exploits repetition of a fixed product.
- Each operation feeds the other, which is why working systems alternate them: merging symbols creates new repeated sequences to chunk, and chunking creates new units whose contexts can be compared and merged (the a^n b^n derivation in Stolcke and Omohundro is the minimal worked example; ADIOS iterates pattern then class in the same way).
- Can class formation be reduced to chunking? Not without something extra. In a variable-free, monotone setting the AND-like constructor cannot produce the OR-like one (Post's lattice). With variables it can be folded into one move: align two sequences, keep the common part, put a variable where they differ. The common part is a chunk and the range of the variable is a class (Wolff's schema-plus-correction; alignment-based learning; ADIOS's slot; the variable in the hierarchical variable model; and, in the functional setting, the Church/Scott encoding of sums). So the honest statement is: one operation suffices if that operation is "abstraction with a variable", but a variable-with-a-range is exactly a disjunction, so the two-ness has moved inside the operation rather than disappeared.
- Minimum description length gives one objective under which both operations are scored, and that is the sense in which Wolff and the Bayesian model-merging framework are "unified". One objective does not imply one operation.
- System-by-system: Sequitur, Re-Pair, byte-pair encoding: ONE (and no generalisation). Stolcke and Omohundro for HMMs: ONE (merge only; no hierarchy); for context-free grammars: TWO. ADIOS: TWO. SNPR: TWO. SP theory: claims ONE (pattern matching and unification), with the class appearing as a slot. Alignment-based learning: ONE alignment step yielding both constituents and their type. Hierarchical variable model: TWO.

## 8. AND-OR structures: is a concept hierarchy formally an alternation of two operations?

### Takeaway
Yes, in every formalism examined a hierarchy with both part-whole and kind-of links is a recursive structure built from exactly two constructors: a product (AND, composition, decomposition into parts) and a sum (OR, alternatives, mixture). The recursion scheme is one; the constructors are two. **Verdict: TWO alternating operations under ONE recursion.**

### Cited Findings
- [PROPOSAL] And-Or graphs: an Or-node represents alternatives, each pointing to a different sub-configuration; an And-node represents decomposition of an entity into components; an object category is the set of all valid configurations the grammar produces; parsing chooses at the Or-nodes to obtain a parse graph. S.-C. Zhu and D. Mumford, "A stochastic grammar of images", Foundations and Trends in Computer Graphics and Vision 2(4):259-362 (dated 2006 in the usual citation; the publisher page gives 2007). — [DOI 10.1561/0600000018](https://doi.org/10.1561/0600000018)
- [PROVED, in the paper] Sum-product networks are directed acyclic graphs with variables as leaves and sums and products as internal nodes; if complete and consistent they represent the partition function and all marginals of some graphical model; essentially all tractable graphical models can be cast in this form. H. Poon and P. Domingos (2011), "Sum-product networks: a new deep architecture", UAI 2011. — [arXiv 1202.3732](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3732)
- [DEFINITION] An algebraic data type is, in general form, a possibly recursive sum type of product types: a sum is a choice between variants, a product combines components; lists and trees are the standard recursive examples. — [Wikipedia, Algebraic data type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_data_type)
- [PROVED] Initial algebras give the semantics of such recursive types and a general account of induction and recursion: the natural numbers are the initial algebra of X -> 1 + X; lists over A are the initial algebra of X -> 1 + A x X. Final coalgebras play the dual role for coinduction (e.g. finite and infinite lists). — [Wikipedia, Initial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_algebra)
- [PROPOSAL, with model] Kemp and Tenenbaum: structural forms are defined by graph grammars; each of the first six forms in their figure is generated by a single context-free production that replaces a parent node with two child nodes, starting from a one-node seed; more complex forms (e.g. cylinders) are obtained by combining basic forms or using more complex productions. A structure is a graph over clusters of entities; the model selects form and structure by maximising a posterior that penalises many clusters and favours simple forms. C. Kemp and J. B. Tenenbaum (2008), "The discovery of structural form", PNAS 105(31):10687-10692. — [PMC2492756](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2492756/)
- [PROVED] AND and OR generate distinct clones; neither is definable from the other without negation. — [Wikipedia, Post's lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%27s_lattice)

### Inferences
- A concept hierarchy with parts and kinds is formally a least fixed point of a sum-of-products construction. Alternation of the two layers is a normal form rather than an extra assumption: two adjacent AND layers collapse into one, as do two adjacent OR layers, so any such structure can be written with strictly alternating layers.
- The two constructors carry the two classical relations. An AND-node relates a whole to its parts (partonomy; syntagmatic; product). An OR-node relates a category to its alternatives (taxonomy; paradigmatic; sum). Collapsing them into a single node type would lose the difference between "has all of these" and "is one of these".
- Kemp and Tenenbaum support "one recursive rule" at the level of a single form (each form is one production applied repeatedly), but different forms need different productions, and every structure in their model is a partition of entities into clusters plus a graph on the clusters. For their "order" form that is a partition plus an ordering of the blocks, i.e. the preorder decomposition of section 1. So even there a class-forming step (clustering) and a structure-building step (the production) coexist.

### Gaps

## 9. Program induction and library learning: is "abstraction = refactoring recurring structure" the same step for action programs and for concepts?

### Takeaway
In these systems it is the same step, because actions and concepts are both represented as programs and abstraction is compression of recurring program structure into a named, parameterised component. That operation subsumes chunking (the fixed body) and class formation (the parameter and its range). This is a modelling proposal supported by performance, not a theorem, and expressive universality makes "one operation suffices" true but weak. **Verdict: ONE (abstraction with variables), as a proposal.**

### Cited Findings
- [PROPOSAL, empirical] DreamCoder learns by writing programs, with a wake-sleep cycle that alternately extends its language with new symbolic abstractions and trains a neural search guide; learned concepts are built compositionally from earlier ones into multi-layered libraries, are interpretable and transfer across tasks. K. Ellis, C. Wong, M. Nye, M. Sable-Meyer, L. Cary, L. Morales, L. Hewitt, A. Solar-Lezama and J. B. Tenenbaum, arXiv 2006.08381 (2020); the conference version's DOI resolves to the ACM Digital Library (PLDI 2021). — [arXiv 2006.08381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381); [DOI 10.1145/3453483.3454080](https://doi.org/10.1145/3453483.3454080)
- [PROPOSAL, empirical] Library learning is the synthesis of library functions that capture common functionality across a corpus of programs, with quality measured by compression of the corpus; Stitch does this by top-down search and is three to four orders of magnitude faster than DreamCoder's library learner with comparable or better compression. M. Bowers, T. X. Olausson, L. Wong, G. Grand, J. B. Tenenbaum, K. Ellis and A. Solar-Lezama, "Top-down synthesis for library learning", Proc. ACM Program. Lang. 7 (POPL 2023). — [arXiv 2211.16605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16605)
- [PROPOSAL, empirical] Bayesian program learning represents concepts (handwritten characters) as simple probabilistic programs built compositionally from parts, sub-parts and relations, with a type level and a token level, and reaches human-level one-shot classification. B. M. Lake, R. Salakhutdinov and J. B. Tenenbaum (2015), "Human-level concept learning through probabilistic program induction", Science 350(6266):1332-1338. — [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rsalakhu/papers/LakeEtAl2015Science.pdf); [DOI 10.1126/science.aab3050](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab3050)
- [DEFINITION / PROVED] Anti-unification constructs a generalisation common to two expressions; in the first-order case the least general generalisation is a term from which both inputs are obtained by substituting for its variables, with differing subterms replaced by variables. G. Plotkin (1970), "A note on inductive generalization", Machine Intelligence 5. Applications listed include duplicate-code detection, code factoring and program synthesis. — [Wikipedia, Anti-unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-unification)
- [PROVED] The combinators S and K with application alone can express any lambda term, hence any computable function; a single combinator can serve as the whole basis. M. Schonfinkel (1924), Mathematische Annalen; H. Curry (1930). — [Wikipedia, Combinatory logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatory_logic)
- [EMPIRICAL] Human sequence memory is predicted by the length of the shortest program in a small language of thought with repetition, alternation and nesting. — [Planton et al. 2021, PLoS Computational Biology](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008598)

### Inferences
- The library-learning step is anti-unification at scale: find structure shared by several programs, keep the shared part as the body of a new function, and turn the parts that differ into parameters. A zero-parameter abstraction is a chunk. A parameter is a slot, and the set (or type) of things that fill it is a class. So one refactoring operation delivers both of the designer's rules, provided the representation has variables.
- The unification of "action programs" and "concepts" is achieved by the representation, not by a theorem: when a drawing routine, a list function and a physical law are all terms in one typed language, the same compression step applies to all. This is evidence that a common mechanism is workable, and that the price is a representation with binding (variables and application).
- Universality cuts both ways. Since a single combinator with application is computationally complete, any claim of the form "one recursive operation suffices to express both sequences and concept orders" is true and uninformative. The substantive question is which decomposition gives a learner the right inductive bias and tractable search, and on that the practical systems all use abstraction-with-parameters, which internally has a product part and a sum part (section 7).

## 10. Recursion itself: Merge, critiques, and the category-theoretic notions that add a precise point

### Takeaway
The "one recursive operation" thesis in linguistics (recursion/Merge as the sole uniquely human component) is a hypothesis about the combinatory side only, and it is contested; the categories that Merge combines are supplied separately. Three category-theoretic notions are exactly on point: equivalence of a preorder with its skeleton (rule 2), Galois connections/closure operators (concept lattices), and initial algebras of sum-product functors (recursive hierarchies). **Verdict: ONE for combination only (hypothesis); it does not cover class formation.**

### Cited Findings
- [PROPOSAL] Hauser, Chomsky and Fitch distinguish the faculty of language in the broad sense (sensory-motor system, conceptual-intentional system and the computational mechanisms for recursion) from the narrow sense, and hypothesise that the narrow faculty includes only recursion and is the only uniquely human component; they add that it may have evolved for reasons other than language (number, navigation, social relations). M. D. Hauser, N. Chomsky and W. T. Fitch (2002), "The faculty of language: what is it, who has it, and how did it evolve?", Science 298 (22 November 2002), starting at p. 1569. — [Author-hosted PDF](https://chomsky.info/wp-content/uploads/20021122.pdf)
- [DEFINITION] Merge takes two syntactic objects and forms a new one, the set {A, B}; because it applies to its own outputs it is recursive. External Merge combines separate objects; internal Merge applies when one object is already contained in the other. The result is labelled by features of one of the items (the head). Standing objections recorded on the page: strict binarity, the bottom-up direction, and the constituency (rather than dependency) basis. — [Wikipedia, Merge (linguistics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_(linguistics))
- [PROVED] Every preordered set is equivalent as a category to a poset (its skeleton); a preorder is a poset iff skeletal. — [nLab, preorder](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/preorder)
- [PROVED] The derivation operators of a formal context form a Galois connection whose composites are closure operators; concepts are the closed pairs. — [Wikipedia, Formal concept analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_concept_analysis)
- [PROVED] Initial algebras of endofunctors give recursion and induction; the standard examples are sum-of-product functors (1 + X; 1 + A x X). — [Wikipedia, Initial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_algebra)
- [PROVED] Concatenation and its residuals in the lattice of languages satisfy the adjunction law x.y <= z iff y <= x\z iff x <= z/y. — [Wikipedia, Residuated lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residuated_lattice)

### Inferences
- Merge is a single recursive product-like constructor (binary set formation). It does not create the categories it operates on: labels come from lexical features, and which items may merge is fixed by selection. So even the strongest "recursion only" position is a claim about one of the designer's two rules (composition), and leaves category formation to the lexicon and the interfaces.
- Skeleton: in a preorder viewed as a category, "A <= B and B <= A" says A and B are isomorphic; passing to the skeleton picks one representative per isomorphism class and loses nothing up to equivalence. Rule (2) is "replace the category by its skeleton". This also explains idempotence: a skeleton is its own skeleton.
- Galois connection: a concept is a fixed point of a round trip between two ordered sets (extents and intents). This is the precise sense in which a category can be said to arise from a "two-way" relation. The two directions are between two different kinds of thing (items and their properties or contexts), not between two items.
- Initial algebra: a recursive hierarchy is the least fixed point of a functor built from sums and products. "One recursive mechanism" is correct as a statement about the fixed-point scheme; the functor being iterated still has two kinds of constructor.
- Adjunction: residuation states exactly how sequencing and categorisation determine each other without being the same operation.

## 11. Results against unification: separations between chunking and class induction, and between partonomy and taxonomy

### Takeaway
There are real separations. Chunking is conservative and class induction is ampliative, and learnability theory shows the latter cannot be done safely in general from positive data. Algebraically the two constructors are distinct and not inter-definable without variables or negation. For part-whole versus kind-of, I found no impossibility theorem; both are partial orders and both can be housed in one formalism, but they are dual constructors with different inheritance behaviour, so collapsing them into one relation loses information. **Verdict: TWO.**

### Cited Findings
- [PROVED] Gold's theorem. A learner identifies a class in the limit if on every presentation of every language in the class it makes finitely many wrong guesses and then stays correct. From text (positive examples only), any family containing an infinite ascending chain of languages together with their union is not learnable; in particular a class containing all finite languages and one infinite language is not. The class of finite languages alone is learnable from text; with complete (positive and negative) presentation the regular, context-free, context-sensitive and primitive recursive classes are learnable. E. M. Gold (1967), "Language identification in the limit", Information and Control 10(5):447-474. — [Wikipedia, Language identification in the limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_identification_in_the_limit)
- [PROVED] Angluin's characterisation: an indexed class of recursive languages is learnable from positive data iff every language has a finite "tell-tale" subset (computably enumerable, for effective learners). D. Angluin (1980), "Inductive inference of formal languages from positive data", Information and Control 45(2):117-135. — [Wikipedia, Language identification in the limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_identification_in_the_limit)
- [PROVED] Positive counterpart: substitutable context-free languages are polynomially identifiable from positive data, and the class escapes Gold's theorem precisely because it is not superfinite ({a, aa} is excluded). — [Clark and Eyraud 2007, Theorem 9 and section 6 (PDF)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/clark07a/clark07a.pdf)
- [PROVED, by construction] A pair-replacement grammar generates only its input string. — [Wikipedia, Re-Pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-Pair)
- [PROPOSAL] In Bayesian model merging, merging is the operator that generalises and chunking is an abbreviation operator added because merging alone does not yield hierarchy. — [Stolcke and Omohundro 1994 (arXiv cmp-lg/9409010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cmp-lg/9409010)
- [PROVED] AND and OR generate different clones. — [Wikipedia, Post's lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%27s_lattice)
- [PROVED] Finite automata decompose into two irreducible kinds of component (simple groups; flip-flops). — [Wikipedia, Krohn-Rhodes theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn%E2%80%93Rhodes_theory)
- [DEFINITION] Meronymy relates a part to a whole and is distinct from the type-of relation (hypernymy): a leaf is part of a tree; an oak is a type of tree. — [Wikipedia, Meronymy and holonymy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meronymy_and_holonymy)
- [PROPOSAL, philosophical] Many restricted senses of "part" are not transitive (a handle is part of a door, the door part of a house, but the handle is not, in the relevant sense, part of the house); examples are attributed to Rescher 1955, Cruse 1979 and Winston, Chaffin and Herrmann 1987. The SEP diagnosis is that this shows the non-transitivity of restricted relations (functional or direct part), not of parthood in general. The entry also notes that group membership is regarded by some as fundamentally distinct from parthood, and that mereology differs from set theory in its treatment of singletons and in not being committed to abstract entities. — [SEP, Mereology, sections 1, 2.1, 4.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROVED] Classical mereology has the structure of a complete Boolean algebra without a zero element, i.e. inclusion among non-empty subsets. — [SEP, Mereology, section 4.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mereology/)
- [PROVED] In a concept lattice a more specific concept has a smaller extent and a larger intent. — [Wikipedia, Formal concept analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_concept_analysis)
- [PROPOSAL] Counter-evidence to a strict separation of representations: class-inclusion and part-whole hierarchies can be stored in one pattern format and integrated (SP theory); And-Or graphs and algebraic data types likewise hold both in one structure with two node types. — [Wolff 2013 (arXiv 1306.3888)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.3888); [Zhu and Mumford, DOI 10.1561/0600000018](https://doi.org/10.1561/0600000018); [Wikipedia, Algebraic data type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_data_type)

### Inferences
- Learnability separation. A learner that only chunks always conjectures a language equal to its sample, so it can identify finite languages and no infinite one. To identify any infinite language from positive data a learner must make generalising leaps, and Gold's theorem says no strategy for such leaps is safe across a class as rich as "all finite languages plus one infinite one". Hence class induction needs a restrictive bias (substitutability, reversibility, a tell-tale set), and chunking does not. The two operations differ in logical status: one is lossless re-description, the other is hypothesis.
- Algebraic separation. Chunking is a definitional extension (product; AND); class formation is a quotient or a disjunction (sum; OR). In the variable-free setting neither is definable from the other. With variables they can be packaged in a single abstraction step (sections 7 and 9), but the packaged step still contains both.
- Dynamical separation. Quotient and closure operations are idempotent (sections 1, 3, 6); chunking is not (each replacement creates new adjacent pairs). An unbounded hierarchy therefore cannot come from the class-forming operation alone, and a generalising hierarchy cannot come from chunking alone. Alternation is the minimal recipe that is both productive and generalising. This is the strongest argument in these notes against "one equivalent recursive mechanism" if "mechanism" means a single primitive operation, and it is fully compatible with "one recursion scheme and one objective applied to two primitives".
- Ambiguity of the mutual-relation rule. Applied to succession it yields cycles (recurrent classes); applied to substitutability it yields categories; applied to parthood it yields coincidence; applied to subsumption it yields synonymy (same extent). These are four different kinds of class. A single rule stated over an unspecified relation "is followed by, predicts, or is a part/kind of" conflates them.
- Partonomy versus taxonomy. No result was found that they are different order types: both are partial orders, both are Boolean-like in their classical forms (mereology is a Boolean algebra minus zero; subclass inclusion is inclusion among sets), and Lewis's thesis (recalled, see section 2 Gaps) identifies subclass with parthood among classes. The differences that are formally statable are: (a) duality of constructors: a whole is an AND over its parts, a kind is an OR over its sub-kinds, so in type-theoretic terms the arrows run in opposite directions (a product projects onto its components; a sum is injected into by its variants); (b) inheritance: specialisation adds attributes (larger intent, smaller extent), whereas a part does not thereby inherit the attributes of its whole nor the whole those of its parts; (c) restricted part relations fail transitivity while kind-of does not; (d) mereology has no null element and no counterpart of the member-singleton step. They can share one representational format, but as two edge types. Collapsing them into one relation is possible only by forgetting which nodes are conjunctive and which disjunctive.
- Overall reading for the design question. The formal results support: one fixed-point recursion and one compression objective; two primitive constructors (compose and class), provably distinct, linked by an adjunction (residuation) or packaged by variable abstraction; and rule (2) as a correct description of the preorder quotient for a transitive relation of the "can replace / predicts the same" type, which is idempotent and yields the order among concepts as a by-product. They do not support the claim that sequence learning and conceptual ordering are the same single operation.
