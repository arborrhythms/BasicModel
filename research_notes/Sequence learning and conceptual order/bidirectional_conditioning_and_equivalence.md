# Bidirectional association between a sign and its object, and the formation of equivalence classes (behavioral, developmental, comparative literature)

Conventions used in these notes. **[E]** = established / replicated and not seriously disputed. **[C]** = contested. **[S]** = taken from a search-result summary or abstract only (full text not read in this session). Items I know from background but could not source in this session are listed under *Gaps*, never under *Cited Findings*. Two corrections to the brief: the "words as invitations to form categories" paper is Waxman & **Markow** (1995), not Waxman & Markman; and the van Kerkoerle et al. paper appeared as a 2023 preprint and was published in eLife in 2025.

## 1. Sidman's stimulus equivalence: what is trained, what emerges, and the claim that equivalence underlies symbolic reference

### Takeaway
In verbally able humans, training a few arbitrary conditional discriminations (A→B, B→C) reliably yields untrained symmetry, transitivity and combined "equivalence" relations, and class members then share functions (fear, priming, N400). Whether equivalence is a primitive outcome of reinforcement (Sidman), a product of naming (Horne & Lowe) or a learned relational operant (RFT) is unresolved, and the "class" is not behaviorally homogeneous (nodal-distance effects).

### Cited Findings
- **[E]** Definition. Stimulus equivalence is defined by three emergent properties after conditional-discrimination (matching-to-sample, MTS) training: reflexivity (A=A), symmetry (A→B gives B→A) and transitivity (A→B, B→C gives A→C); symmetry is the property that has been hardest to find in nonhumans — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009, Learning & Behavior 37:188-203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[E]** Founding comparative result. Sidman, Rauzin, Lazar, Cunningham, Tailby & Carrigan (1982, JEAB 37:23-44): after A→B training, four of six children showed B→A symmetry; none of the rhesus monkeys and neither of the baboons did. The authors raised (but did not assert) that language or other human capacities may be needed — [Sidman et al. 1982 record](https://scilit.com/publications/a590ebcfc75d8a96c3da4118e5d14c12); pass rates as summarised in [Urcuioli 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5380386)
- **[E/S]** Sidman's theoretical claim on reference. Sidman argued that equivalence can account functionally for symbolic reference / semantic correspondence without a separate reinforcement history for each word-referent instance — [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/); [Athabasca overview quoting Sidman 1986](https://psych.athabascau.ca/open/sidman/intro.php)
- **[C]** Sidman (2000, JEAB 74:127-146): equivalence relations arise directly from the reinforcement contingency, which "produces at least two types of outcome: analytic units and equivalence relations"; all positive elements of the contingency, including class-specific responses and reinforcers, become class members — [Sidman 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284788)
- **[C/S]** Criticism of the contingency account: contingencies do not always produce equivalence, so the contingency cannot be sufficient — [2023 analysis, Perspectives on Behavior Science](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40614-023-00365-2). A JEAB test of Sidman's (2000) theory reported that 4 of 6 subjects showed reinforcer-based relations in all four classes, not only in classes with a class-specific reinforcer as the theory predicts (attribution to this exact paper not confirmed by full-text read) — [JEAB test of Sidman 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1459848)
- **[E]** Emergent property 1, transfer of function. Dougher, Augustson, Markham, Greenway & Wulfert (1994, JEAB 62:331-351), adult humans: after two four-member classes were formed, shock was paired with one member; skin-conductance conditioning transferred to the other members in 6 of 8 subjects. Extinguishing one member removed elicitation by the others, and reconditioning restored it — [Dougher et al. 1994](https://contextualscience.org/publications/transfer_respondent_eliciting_extinction_functions_through_stimulus_equivalence)
- **[E/S]** Emergent property 2, semantic-like priming. Barnes-Holmes et al. (2005) found priming among members of two trained 4-member nonsense-word classes; Haimson et al. (2009) found a larger N400 to cross-class than within-class pairs, like unrelated vs related real words — [review in Psychology & Neuroscience](https://scielo.br/j/pn/a/V9hRNwm9XxtwkwLXYmhXGVd/?lang=en); [Barnes-Holmes et al. 2005](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/10703/1/SC-Derived-2005.pdf)
- **[S]** Neural correlates. Dickins et al. (2001, fMRI, n=12): activation during trained, symmetry and transitivity trials resembled that of a verbal-fluency task — [Dickins abstract](https://u-picardie.fr/~LaboECCHAT/EMEAB4/Commun/Abstracts/Dickins.html). Schlund, Cataldo & Hoehn-Saric (2008): transitive and equivalence relations activated anterior hippocampus bilaterally; symmetry relations activated parahippocampus; a fronto-parietal network was common — [Schlund et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2276226/)
- **[C]** The class is not a uniform unit. Fields and colleagues: relatedness of class members is an inverse function of nodal distance (number of intervening trained links); one view holds members are fully substitutable (Fields & Verhave 1987), the other that they are differentially related (Fields & Moss 2007); whether the nodal effect is permanent or disappears once the class has formed is disputed — [Moss-Lourenco & Fields 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3088076)
- **[C]** Language dependence. Devany, Hayes & Nelson (1986, JEAB 46:243-257): with mental age matched, all typically developing and all language-able retarded children formed classes; no language-disabled child did — [Devany et al. 1986](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1348265). Against: Carr, Wilkinson, Blackman & McIlvane (2000, JEAB 74:101-114): 3 of 3 individuals with severe retardation and minimal verbal repertoires showed all emergent relations immediately in one lab, 1 of 2 in a second — [Carr et al. 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284786)

### Inferences
- In humans the equivalence class behaves like a functional unit in several independent measures (derived matching, autonomic transfer, extinction transfer, priming, N400). That is evidence for a real unit, but nodal-distance effects mean it is better described as a graded network than as a single node standing over its members.
- Sidman's procedure does **not** train both directions; symmetry is the untrained outcome. So the Sidman literature bears mainly on "does one direction yield the other" (yes in most verbal humans, rarely in other animals), not on "what does explicit two-way training build".
- The Dougher extinction result (change one member, all change) is the closest behavioral analogue to a shared representation; it is equally compatible with strong member-to-member links.

### Gaps
- I did not read Sidman (1994) *Equivalence Relations and Behavior* or Sidman & Tailby (1982) directly; statements about them come from secondary sources.
- Typical human pass rates across the adult equivalence literature (and the effect of training structure: linear vs one-to-many vs many-to-one, simultaneous vs delayed MTS) were not retrieved. Background knowledge says adult yields are high but vary strongly with training structure; unsourced here.
- The JEAB study contradicting Sidman (2000) is cited from a search summary; authorship (probably Minster and colleagues, 2006) not confirmed.

## 2. Relational Frame Theory: is bidirectionality (mutual entailment) learned or primitive?

### Takeaway
RFT holds that bidirectionality is a generalized operant acquired through multiple-exemplar training in both directions during early language interaction; the direct evidence is a handful of small-n child studies plus one sea lion, and it is opposed by chimpanzee data in which extensive two-way exemplar history did not produce symmetry. The construct itself is criticised as under-specified.

### Cited Findings
- **[E, as theory]** RFT defines mutual entailment as broader than symmetry: if "A more than B" is trained, "B less than A" is derived, which is bidirectional but not symmetric; hence the generic term. Combinatorial entailment and transformation of stimulus functions are the other defining properties — [Berens & Hayes 2007, JABA 40:45-71](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868810)
- **[C]** Core claim: arbitrarily applicable relational responding, with transformation of functions, is the key process in human verbal behavior, and it is an operant shaped by "a history of contacted consistencies in contingencies across multiple exemplars" — [Gross & Fox 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2779078); [Hayes & Barnes-Holmes 2004 reply](https://contextualscience.org/publications/hayes_barnesholmes_2004)
- **[S]** Best single developmental datum. Luciano, Gómez-Becerra & Rodríguez-Valverde (2007, JEAB 87:349-365), one infant: at 15 months 24 days she showed neither receptive symmetry nor naming; after one month of multiple-exemplar training (object→sound then sound→object, 10 objects) she showed generalized receptive symmetry with novel objects at a 3-hour delay; visual-visual equivalence emerged at 19 months, still without naming; naming appeared only at about 23 months — [Luciano et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868588)
- **[S]** Older children. Barnes-Holmes and colleagues (2001) used a multiple-baseline design: children who failed symmetry-based transformation tests passed after explicit symmetry exemplar training — [Barnes-Holmes et al. 2001](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/10675/1/BR-Exemplar-2001.pdf). Berens & Hayes (2007): four girls aged 4-5 acquired arbitrary more-than/less-than responding that generalised to novel stimuli after exemplar training, offered as evidence for a relational operant — [Berens & Hayes 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868810)
- **[S]** Naming literature parallel. Greer et al. (2007): bidirectional naming emerged in all four children given multiple-exemplar instruction across speaker and listener responses, but not under single-exemplar instruction — [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/)
- **[E]** Comparative support. The sea lion Rio (Schusterman & Kastak 1993) passed only after exemplar experience: first-trial symmetry on novel relations was at first poor (B→A 8/12), then C→B 11/12, transitivity A→C 11/12, and equivalence C→A 16/18 on 18 novel classes — [Schusterman & Kastak 1993, Psychological Record 43:823-839](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF03395915)
- **[E]** Comparative counter-evidence. Dugdale & Lowe (2000, JEAB): language-trained chimpanzees Sherman and Lana, who had extensive two-way lexigram exemplar history, failed standard symmetry tests even when correct test responses were reinforced; the authors conclude this does not support the exemplar-training hypothesis — [Dugdale & Lowe 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284759)
- **[C]** Conceptual criticism. Palmer (2004, JEAB 81:189-204, "Data in search of a principle"): the phenomena are important but the promised new principle is never specified; counterexamples suggest covert mediating verbal behavior, so verbal behavior may underlie relational performance rather than be defined by it — [Palmer 2004](https://contextualscience.org/publications/palmer_2004). Burgos (2003) called the theory "unintelligible, ambiguous, opaque, and contradictory" — [Gross & Fox 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2779078)
- **[E, as acknowledged limitation]** RFT authors concede that equivalence develops so early that testing its origin by exemplar training in infants is "technically challenging"; the longitudinal evidence (Lipkens, Hayes & Hayes 1993) is a single child — [Berens & Hayes 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868810)

### Inferences
- The literature shows exemplar training is **sufficient** to install or repair derived bidirectional responding in some children and one sea lion. It does not show it is the **origin** in typical development, and infant EEG claims (section 4) of reversal at 4-5 months, if they hold, would precede any plausible exemplar history for word-object relations.
- If bidirectionality is a learned operant, it is a property of a trained system applied to new pairs, not something that falls out of each individual two-way pairing. That is a different mechanism from "mutual prediction forces a merger".

### Gaps
- Hayes, Barnes-Holmes & Roche (2001) was not read directly.
- No meta-analytic estimate of how many exemplars are needed, or of failure rates after exemplar training, was found.
- Lipkens et al. (1993) details (ages at which symmetry and equivalence appeared) not retrieved.

## 3. Symmetry in nonhuman animals: does forward conditioning yield a backward association?

### Takeaway
After standard forward MTS training, nonhuman animals usually show no or weak symmetry; the reliable successes require specific procedures (successive go/no-go matching at one location with intermixed identity trials in pigeons; multiple-exemplar training in sea lions). The best-supported animal account (Urcuioli) explains symmetry through stimulus-plus-ordinal-position compounds and class formation by reinforcement, and the same account produces "antisymmetry" when baseline relations are changed.

### Cited Findings
- **[E]** Tally. Lionello-DeNolf (2009) reviewed 24 nonhuman symmetry studies (1982-2007; 229 subjects: 182 pigeons, 23 primates, 22 rats, 2 sea lions): 11 found no evidence, 10 mixed evidence, 3 strong evidence. Her Table 2 gives 95/229 (41%) subjects showing symmetry, though the text says "approximately 80 (41%)" (internal inconsistency in the source); by species pigeons 42%, primates 30%, rats 45% (rat figure inflated by group-level data) — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[E]** Procedural reasons for failure in two-alternative MTS: reversing sample and comparison roles changes spatial location (centre vs side keys) and temporal position (first vs second), so the test stimuli are functionally new; missing identity or successive/simultaneous discrimination prerequisites; mixed select/reject control — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[E]** Apparent successes that have simpler explanations: D'Amato et al. (1985), 2 of 6 monkeys on one test, attributed to stimulus generalization; Zentall et al., mediated generalization via pecking; reinforced test trials; rat studies (Bunsey & Eichenbaum 1996; Nakagawa 2001) open to reject-control and rapid-learning explanations — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[E]** Chimpanzees. Tomonaga et al. (1991): symmetry in 1 of 3 chimpanzees; Yamamoto & Asano (1995): 1 chimpanzee, not maintained with new stimuli — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068). Kyoto summary: the chimpanzee Ai showed no spontaneous symmetry between names and objects, only gradual improvement over repeated train-test cycles; chimpanzees "show no emergence of symmetry immediately after" conditional-discrimination training, with one 9-year-old female as an exception — [Tomonaga 2008](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcss/15/3/15_3_347/_article/-char/en). Sherman and Lana failed (see section 2) — [Dugdale & Lowe 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284759)
- **[E]** Pigeons, successive matching. Frank & Wasserman (2005): symmetry when arbitrary and identity go/no-go trials were intermixed at a single location. Urcuioli (2008, JEAB): Experiments 1A, 1B, 2 (two-alternative MTS; 4, 4 and 3 pigeons) gave no symmetry; Experiment 3 (successive matching, AB plus AA and BB identity) gave significant symmetry in 3 of 7 pigeons and transiently in a 4th; Experiment 4 (hue oddity substituted for hue identity) gave **antisymmetry** — more responding to reversals of the non-reinforced baseline relations — significant in 2 of 5 and present in all 5 over eight sessions — [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203)
- **[E]** Response as sample. Garcia & Benjumea (2006), pigeons: when the birds' own differential pecking behavior served as the sample, 9 of 10 first probe responses matched the symmetry prediction (classified by the reviewer as mixed evidence, not clear success) — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[E, as theory; C as general account]** Urcuioli's theory: the functional stimulus is the nominal stimulus plus its ordinal position (red-as-sample is not red-as-comparison); elements of reinforced sample-comparison combinations join one class, elements of non-reinforced combinations stay apart; classes sharing an element merge. Continual non-reinforcement on half the trials of successive matching is held to be essential. Two-alternative MTS fails because location changes the functional stimuli and accurate birds stop experiencing non-reinforcement — [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203)
- **[E/C]** Extensions in pigeons: transitivity in 2 pigeons trained AB, BC plus BB identity but not in 2 trained AB, BC only; "anti-transitivity" with BB oddity; reflexivity-like AA matching in 5 of 6 (later queried as generalized identity), and one pigeon with clear BB reflexivity without reinforced identity training. Problems for the theory: Frank (2007) found no symmetry after AB, CA, BD training or AB with CC, DD identity; 2 of 4 pigeons showed AA matching the theory forbids. Urcuioli concludes derived relations do not need language, but makes no direct mechanistic comparison with humans — [Urcuioli 2015, Conductual 3:4-25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5380386)
- **[E]** Sea lions: clear success after multiple-exemplar training with varied incorrect comparisons, and (Kastak, Schusterman & Kastak 2001) class-specific outcomes — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068); numbers in [Schusterman & Kastak 1993](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF03395915)
- **[E]** Baboons, partial transfer. Chartier & Fagot (2022, Learning & Behavior 51:166-178), 20 Guinea baboons, touchscreen MTS, A→B trained to 80%, then B→A tested as consistent (Sym) or inconsistent (NonSym) pairings: first test block 54.1% vs 45.6% (chance 50%); for the best-learned pairing 66% vs 32.5%; 17 of 20 correct on the first Sym test trial (p < .005); relearning about one-third shorter under Sym and about three-quarters longer under NonSym. Authors: transfer is real but partial; baboons hold two coexisting codes, a directional A→B relation and a non-directional co-occurrence relation, and species may differ in **access** to the non-directional code rather than in encoding — [Chartier & Fagot 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272242)
- **[S]** Medam, Marzouki, Montant & Fagot (2016): baboons learned categories but showed only a weak (significant) tendency toward symmetry — [Medam et al. 2016](https://lpc.univ-amu.fr/en/doc/hal-01432305/_1.html)
- **[S]** Two-way training without benefit. Navarro & Wasserman (2020, Neurobiology of Learning and Memory), Asratyan's alternating forward/backward technique in a two-alternative task: humans learned faster when forward and backward associations supported one another (bidirectional network) than when they did not; pigeons showed no evidence of bidirectional associations — [Navarro & Wasserman 2020](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Bidirectional-conditioning-Revisiting-Asratyans-alternating-training/9984070259702771)
- **[E/S]** Single-neuron level, macaque. Meyer & Olson (2011, PNAS): after passive exposure to image sequences, inferotemporal neurons responded more to unpredicted than predicted transitions (33 of 81 neurons individually significant) — [Meyer & Olson 2011 (abstract-level; link mapping not confirmed)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3228439); the effect did not reverse after about 816 exposures per pair, as summarised by [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/). By contrast, in an explicit pair-association memory task, anterior temporal "pair-coding" neurons responded selectively to both members of a learned pair — [Sakai & Miyashita 1991, Nature 354:152](https://www.nature.com/articles/354152a0.pdf)

### Inferences
- The comparative record says forward conditioning does **not** by itself yield a usable backward association in most species under most procedures. Where symmetry appears, it tracks procedural details that control what the functional stimulus is, which is evidence for a stimulus-control explanation rather than a general reversal operation.
- Antisymmetry is the sharpest result against a simple "pairing implies reverse link" view: identical A→B training yields the opposite of symmetry when the concurrent baseline (oddity instead of identity) reassigns class membership. In pigeons, class structure across the whole reinforcement matrix determines the reverse relation; the pair alone does not.
- Chartier & Fagot's partial transfer and "two codes" proposal, and Urcuioli's ordinal-position compound, both imply that temporal order is part of what is learned and competes with any order-free relation. That is evidence that sequence coding and class/co-occurrence coding are separable.
- The Navarro & Wasserman pigeon result is the most direct animal test of "train both directions": no measurable gain from mutual prediction in that species and task.

### Gaps
- Fagot, Malassis & Medam (2018), reported to show that positional processing in a two-item sequence limits symmetry in baboons but not humans, was confirmed only as a citation; data not retrieved.
- Chartier & Fagot's (2022) Trends in Cognitive Sciences commentary on whether associative symmetry divides humans from nonhumans was not read.
- Whether the Sakai & Miyashita task cued with either pair member (i.e. trained both directions) is my recollection, not verified here; it matters for reading pair-coding neurons as the product of two-way training.
- Navarro & Wasserman (2020) effect sizes and a 2025 JEAB paper titled "Humans exhibit associative symmetry in the absence of backward training and stimulus overlap" (fetch blocked) were not read.
- Imai, Murai, Miyazaki, Okada & Tomonaga (2021, Cognition 214:104755; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33667974/)) compared 8-month-old infants and adult chimpanzees with a habituation-switch design; I could not retrieve the results (cited by van Kerkoerle et al. as animals failing and humans reversing).

## 4. Dehaene-group and related work on spontaneous reversal as a possibly human-specific basis of symbols

### Takeaway
Three EEG/fMRI reports claim that human infants (4-5 months) and adults reverse a one-direction arbitrary pairing without training, while macaques show direction-specific effects confined to sensory cortex. The human adult fMRI evidence is rated solid; the infant reversal effects are small and methodologically disputed, and the macaque evidence rests on four animals and was rated incomplete.

### Cited Findings
- **[C]** Kabdebon & Dehaene-Lambertz (2019, PNAS 116:5805-5810), 5-month-olds, high-density EEG, about 150 infants over three experiments (Methods give 47, 57, 44 recruited; 32-39 analysed per test). Trisyllabic pseudo-words with abstract structure (AAB, ABA, ABB; syllables varied so only structure predicts) were followed about 1 s later by a label (fish or lion picture; in Exp 2 also an auditory label). After 36 familiarisation trials, incongruent labels evoked a late violation response (Exp 1 cluster Pcorr = 0.036; Exps 1-2 combined Pcorr = 0.0024, n = 66). Bidirectionality rests on Experiment 3 only: blocks with the order reversed (label first, then a novel word) gave a congruency effect about 1.7 s after word onset, Pcorr = 0.044, n = 34, with a supporting one-tailed t(33) = 1.73, P = 0.046 — [Kabdebon & Dehaene-Lambertz 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6431210)
- **[C]** Critique. Chartier & Rey (2020, Learning & Behavior 48:279-280, "Is symmetry inference an essential component of language?") argue the evidence is insufficient and that the supposed absence of symmetry in animals should be reassessed — [Chartier & Rey 2020](https://www.pubmed.gov/31942690/); [abstract](https://lpc.univ-amu.fr/en/doc/hal-02943216/_1.html). As summarised from the article text in search results **[S]**: the flaw is present in all three experiments; familiarisation did not exclude simple contiguity (a label is soon followed by the next trial's word), and a longer inter-trial interval with fully randomised pair presentation would be needed — [Chartier & Rey 2020, publisher PDF](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13420-019-00405-5.pdf)
- **[S, conference abstract only]** Ekramnia & Dehaene(-Lambertz) (2019, Proc. Cognitive Science Society 41). Study 1: two groups of 25 infants aged 4.5 months learned two word-category pairs (bird, vehicle) with a 1 s SOA, one group word→image, the other image→word; in test, 70% trained-direction trials and 30% incongruent or reversed trials. Congruency clusters (mainly right temporal) appeared in both trained and reversed directions, stronger for the word→image group. Study 2: 54 infants trained at home over three days by video on four pairs (two in each direction), then tested; early and late surprise effects in both directions. No statistics are given in the abstract. Authors: infants, unlike nonhuman animals, learn associations bidirectionally — [Ekramnia & Dehaene 2019](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1129w54p)
- **[E for humans; C for the species claim]** van Kerkoerle, Pape, Ekramnia, Feng, Tasserie, Dupont, Li, Jarraya, Vanduffel, Dehaene & Dehaene-Lambertz (2025, eLife 12:RP87380). Design: 55 human adults (Exp 1 auditory pseudo-word/visual object, n = 31; Exp 2 visual shape/visual object, n = 24) and 4 macaques (2 in Exp 1, 3 in Exp 2). Each set had four pairs, two presented label→object and two object→label; passive exposure for 3 days (24 trials/day); monkeys in Exp 1 had about 960 extra trials per pair. fMRI test: 70% congruent canonical, 10% incongruent canonical, 10% congruent reversed, 10% incongruent reversed — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[E]** Human result: incongruity effects in both directions across a bilateral network (inferior and middle frontal gyri, anterior insula, intraparietal sulcus, dorsal anterior cingulate, STS, precuneus, caudate, cerebellum), with no congruity × direction interaction in any region in the pooled n = 55 analysis. A behavioural reversibility index correlated with the reversed-trial congruity effect in left dorsal area 44 (r = 0.64, p = 0.002). The network extends beyond classical language areas — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[E, but note]** Humans did not lose order information entirely: congruent reversed pairs were rated less familiar than congruent canonical pairs (t(20) = 2.8, p = 0.01), though far more familiar than incongruent or unseen pairs (t(20) > 7) — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[C]** Monkey result: congruity effects only in the trained direction, in V1/V2/V4 and, in Exp 2, area 45A/46v, area 6/STS and TPO; significant congruity × direction interactions (e.g. left V1/V2 t = 4.82). Frontal effects appeared only for high-reward pairs — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[E]** Authors' stated limits: four monkeys vs 55 humans; one nonhuman species; adult monkeys only; uncontrolled differences in lifetime experience, education and attention; they propose, as hypothesis only, bidirectional hippocampal replay as a mechanism, and they describe reversal as necessary but not sufficient for symbols — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[E]** Peer review. eLife assessment: human evidence "solid", macaque evidence "necessarily incomplete". Reviewer 3: monkeys received far more training and testing than humans; defining symbols by reversal and then claiming human uniqueness risks circularity; "singularity" and "mechanisms" overreach; this reviewer remained unconvinced after revision. Reviewer 1 accepted the congruity × direction interaction in monkeys as a positive result rather than a mere null — [eLife reviews, appended to the article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **[E]** The eLife paper itself cites the partial nonhuman positives: Medam et al. 2016 (possibly 1 of 20 baboons), Chartier & Fagot (54% on reversed pairs), sea lions and a beluga — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)

### Inferences
- The secure finding is narrow: adult humans, after brief passive one-direction exposure, show expectation violations for the reversed order in a domain-general frontoparietal/insular network. That is spontaneous reversal of a link. It is not a demonstration that a new class-level representation was built; no transitivity, transfer-of-function or representational-similarity test was run.
- The infant claim would, if secure, rule out explicit exemplar training and overt naming as prerequisites. It currently depends on one experiment with a corrected p near 0.04 and a contested design, plus a conference abstract.
- The species contrast and the baboon behavioural data are not strictly in conflict: partial, weak transfer in baboon behaviour and absent reversed fMRI effects in four macaques can both be true. The disagreement is over "absent" vs "weak and hard to access".
- Residual order sensitivity in humans (familiarity ratings) means the two directions are not fully equivalent even in the species that reverses.

### Gaps
- No full journal article for the Ekramnia infant studies was found; only the 2019 abstract.
- I could not open the Chartier & Rey full text; the "two-fold flaw" is from a search-engine summary of it.
- No independent replication of the infant reversal effect was found. No chimpanzee imaging data exist (authors say they are attempting it).
- Dehaene and colleagues' theoretical papers on symbols and mental programs (2022) were not consulted.

## 5. Associative symmetry in human paired-associate memory; backward conditioning in Pavlovian learning

### Takeaway
In human episodic memory an A-B pair studied once in one order is recalled about equally well from either end, and forward and backward recall of the same pair are very highly correlated, which favours a single holistic pair representation; this breaks down for serial lists and for pairs with asymmetric prior knowledge. In animal Pavlovian conditioning, US→CS (backward) pairing gives weak excitation after few trials and inhibition after many, the opposite of a symmetric link.

### Cited Findings
- **[E, as history]** Asch & Ebenholtz (1962, Proc. Am. Philosophical Society 106:135-163) proposed the principle of associative symmetry: an association is a unitary entity with no direction imposed by encoding order; either member can recover the whole — [Kahana 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03195769)
- **[E]** Kahana (2002, Memory & Cognition 30:823-840) contrasts the associative symmetry hypothesis (the association is a holistic conjunction blending both items into a new representation) with the independent associations hypothesis (separate, separately stored forward and backward links). Equal mean forward and backward recall cannot decide between them; the decisive test is the correlation, pair by pair, between forward and backward recall on successive tests. Symmetric storage predicts a high correlation, independent storage a correlation near zero — [Kahana 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03195769)
- **[E/S]** Result: forward and backward recall of a pair are correlated nearly as highly as two tests in the same direction (Rizzuto & Kahana 2001; Kahana 2002); later work has mostly supported the symmetry account for pairs — [Popov et al. 2019, Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13421-019-00950-4.pdf)
- **[E/S]** Boundary 1, sequences. For 19-word lists learned to perfect serial recall, the forward-backward probe correlation took a lower, mid-range value (Caplan and colleagues) — [Caplan et al. 2006](https://www.psych.ualberta.ca/~cml/papers/Caplan_et_al_2006a.pdf)
- **[S]** Boundary 2, prior knowledge. Whether a new episodic association is stored symmetrically depends on pre-existing semantic relations between the words (the "macaroni and cheese" case) — [Popov et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13421-019-00950-4.pdf); [Psychonomic Society summary](https://featuredcontent.psychonomic.org/why-we-dont-serve-cheese-and-macaroni-investigating-directionality-of-relationships-between-words/)
- **[E/S]** Backward conditioning. Number of US→CS pairings largely decides the outcome: few pairings give excitation, many give inhibition (Heth 1976); the alternative reading is that the early excitatory association persists while the animal additionally learns the backward temporal relation. A 1999 conditioned-suppression study in rats compared 4, 16 and 96 backward trials — [Cole & Miller 1999 record](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/conditioned-excitation-and-conditioned-inhibition-acquired-throug/); number of backward pairings as a neglected variable — [Chang, Blaisdell & Miller 2003](https://pigeonrat.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/144/2019/09/Chang-Blaisdell-Miller-2003-JEPABP.pdf)
- **[E/S]** Temporal coding account. Miller and colleagues: animals encode the temporal relation in simultaneous and backward pairings, and that temporal map can be integrated with later learning to produce excitatory responding (Arcediano, Escobar & Miller 2003; Molet, Miguez, Cham & Miller 2012) — [Molet & Miller review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3823740)
- **[S]** D'Amato, Salmon, Loukas & Tomie (1985, JEAB): monkeys and pigeons show at best weak, transient backward associations, unlike children (from a search-result summary; link-to-paper mapping not confirmed by full-text read) — [D'Amato et al. 1985](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1348159)
- **[S]** Modern Pavlovian models differ on whether associations are intrinsically bidirectional: HeiDI assumes reciprocal CS-US associations; other models do not — discussed in [Chartier & Fagot 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272242)

### Inferences
- Human pair memory is the strongest evidence in any literature for a single order-free representation over two items created by one ordered exposure. It needs neither two-way training nor reinforcement.
- The same human literature gives a direct dissociation between pair learning and sequence learning: pairs behave holistically, well-learned serial chains do not. That is evidence against a single mechanism for "A is followed by B" and "A and B belong together", at least in episodic recall.
- Pavlovian backward conditioning shows that in a prediction-driven system the reverse temporal arrangement tends, with training, to signal the **absence** of the outcome. A design that presents B→A after A→B in an error-driven learner should expect interference or inhibition effects, not automatic reinforcement of one link. The temporal-coding view softens this: the order is stored and can be used flexibly.

### Gaps
- Exact statistics (Yule's Q for forward-backward vs same-direction test-retest) could not be extracted; the publisher and lab PDFs were inaccessible. Background recollection is that both are very high and close to each other; treat as unverified.
- Arcediano, Escobar & Miller (2005), "Bidirectional associations in humans and rats", was not retrieved.
- Thresholds for inhibitory backward conditioning (reported as 65, 125 and 495 pairings in different rabbit nictitating-membrane studies) came from a search excerpt whose exact source article I could not pin down.
- Asch & Ebenholtz's "availability" explanation of observed asymmetries (response items more available than stimulus items) is from background knowledge, not sourced here.

## 6. Acquired equivalence, mediated learning and medial temporal involvement: do cues with a shared associate come to share a representation?

### Takeaway
Across rats and humans, cues that share an outcome or antecedent come to be treated alike and generalise new learning to each other, and events can be learned about through their associatively evoked representations. Theories split between mediation by the evoked associate (Hall, Holland; no new unit) and a shared configural/hidden unit (Honey), and medial temporal data show both integration and its opposite, differentiation.

### Cited Findings
- **[E]** Honey & Hall (1989, JEP: Animal Behavior Processes 15:338-346), rats: cues given the same training history generalise more to each other than cues given different histories (acquired equivalence and distinctiveness), challenging purely physical-similarity accounts of generalization — [Honey & Hall 1989](https://doi.org/10.1037/0097-7403.15.4.338)
- **[E, as theory]** Hall (1996, Animal Learning & Behavior 24:233-255): a representation can be activated by an associative link as well as by its stimulus, and a representation activated that way can enter new associations; this is offered as a common mechanism for sensory preconditioning, second-order conditioning, acquired equivalence and distinctiveness, equivalence-class formation and perceptual learning. On this view, if A and B were each paired with X, either one evokes X, and X mediates generalization between them — [Hall 1996 record](https://scilit.com/publications/6d1616ac9522b3dc5bccdb3ba2b01c56)
- **[E]** Holland's representation-mediated learning (rats): a food aversion formed when an associatively evoked representation of food (via a tone) was paired with illness, and an existing aversion extinguished faster when the evoked representation was presented without illness (Holland 1981); Holland (1983) adapted Wagner's SOP so that learning between directly and associatively activated states can be excitatory or inhibitory — [mini-review, Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.845616/full)
- **[E]** Sensory preconditioning (S2→S1, then S1→US) and second-order conditioning (S1→US, then S2→S1) differ only in phase order; representation-mediated accounts are set against the simpler directional associative-chain account (S2→S1→US); simultaneous S1-S2 presentation gives stronger sensory preconditioning than serial presentation — [Honey & Dwyer, higher-order conditioning review](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/147851/3/Honey.%20Higher-order%20conditioning%20DOI%20%281%29.pdf); [Frontiers mini-review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.845616/full)
- **[E/S]** Rodent lesions. Coutureau et al. (2002, JEP:ABP 28:388-396): rats with hippocampal lesions still showed the acquired equivalence/distinctiveness effect; rats with entorhinal cortex lesions did not, although both learned the conditional discrimination. The companion paper (Honey & Ward-Robinson 2002) develops a neural-network account — [Coutureau et al. 2002](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/3328)
- **[E/S]** Human neuropsychology. Myers, Shohamy, Gluck and colleagues (2003, J. Cognitive Neuroscience): on one acquired-equivalence task, mild Parkinson's patients were slow to learn but transferred normally, while non-demented elderly with hippocampal atrophy learned normally but failed to transfer — a double dissociation — [Myers et al. 2003](https://brainhealth.rutgers.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Myers2003.JOCN_.Fish_.pdf)
- **[E]** Shohamy & Wagner (2008, Neuron 60:378-389), human fMRI, acquired-equivalence task: successful later generalization was predicted by coupled increases in hippocampal and midbrain (VTA/substantia nigra) activity **during learning**; hippocampal activity at the generalization test did not track performance, which the authors read as integration at encoding rather than inference at test — [Shohamy & Wagner 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2628634); [Alzforum commentary](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/general-knowledge-dopamine-linked-hippocampal-memory)
- **[E]** Zeithamova, Dominick & Preston (2012, Neuron): in associative inference (AB, BC, test AC), reactivation of the earlier memory during overlapping learning and coupled hippocampal-ventromedial prefrontal activity tracked both integrated-memory formation and inference success — [Zeithamova et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3398403). vmPFC damage disproportionately impairs associative inference (Spalding et al. 2018) — [Spalding et al. 2018](https://preston.clm.utexas.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/spalding_jneuro_2018.pdf)
- **[E]** Not only integration. Schlichting, Mumford & Preston (2015, Nature Communications): learning-related representational change shows dissociable integration and separation signatures in hippocampus and medial prefrontal cortex; as later summarised, anterior hippocampus tends to integrate related episodes and posterior hippocampus to differentiate them — [Schlichting et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/ncomms9151); [Schlichting & Preston 2015 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4346341). Both differentiation and integration of competing memories are treated as outcomes to be explained in current models — [eLife reviewed preprint 88608](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/88608/reviews)

### Inferences
- "Shared prediction leads to shared treatment" is well established behaviorally. "Shared prediction leads to a shared **representation**" is supported in part (integration signatures in anterior hippocampus/mPFC; entorhinal dependence in rats; Honey's hidden-unit account) and contradicted in part (differentiation signatures; Hall's mediation account needs no new unit).
- Hall's and Holland's mechanism is the closest classical precedent for "an evoked representation can stand in for the event itself": it makes a sign's evoked object-representation a substrate for new learning about the object. It predicts function transfer without positing a class node.
- These effects arise from a **common associate** (many-to-one or one-to-many structure), not from mutual prediction between the two cues. They show a route to class-like behavior that does not involve bidirectional A↔B association at all.
- Integration is variable across individuals and conditions (it predicted generalization only in those who showed the encoding-time signal), so it is not an obligatory consequence of overlap.

### Gaps
- Brogden (1939) was not consulted directly.
- Honey & Ward-Robinson (2002) hidden-unit model details not retrieved.
- Favila, Chanales & Kuhl (2016) on hippocampal differentiation of overlapping associations could not be verified directly.
- Wimmer & Shohamy (2012) on hippocampal sensory preconditioning in humans and orbitofrontal work in rats are known from background only; not sourced here.

## 7. Naming and bidirectional naming: does naming cause equivalence or the reverse?

### Takeaway
Horne & Lowe hold that naming (speaker and listener relations fused into one bidirectional unit) is what produces equivalence and categorization; Sidman holds the reverse dependency; RFT treats both as instances of a learned relational operant. The child data are mostly correlational (children who name, categorize), and there are clear cases of equivalence without naming.

### Cited Findings
- **[E, as theory]** Horne & Lowe (1996, JEAB, "On the origins of naming and other symbolic behavior", with 26 commentaries): naming is the basic unit of verbal behavior, a higher-order bidirectional relation joining speaker and listener behavior in one individual; success on equivalence tests is attributed largely to naming, which would explain nonhuman failures — [Horne & Lowe 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16812780/); [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/)
- **[E]** Miguel (2016, The Analysis of Verbal Behavior): proposes "bidirectional naming" (BiN). Common BiN: a shared tact and listener response relate stimuli into a class. Intraverbal BiN: verbal statements linking names ("milk comes from the cow") relate stimuli. Naming is "tacting with understanding"; a tact alone is unidirectional — [Miguel 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381345)
- **[E]** Asymmetry between the two directions of training in young children. Speaker (tact) training reliably yielded untrained listener behavior in all participants (Lowe et al. 2002, 2005; Sprinkle & Miguel 2012); listener training yielded tacting in only 2 of 9 (Horne et al. 2004) and 10 of 14 (Horne et al. 2006) — [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/)
- **[E]** Naming and categorization co-occur. Lee, Miguel, Darcey & Jennings (2015): of four children with autism aged 3-5, the two who could tact category names after listener training categorized, the two who could not, failed. Across such studies participants sorted physically dissimilar pictures only when they responded correctly as both speaker and listener; additional tact or listener training repaired failures (Miguel et al. 2008) — [Miguel 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381345)
- **[E]** Intraverbal route in adults. Ma et al. (2016): 14 of 14 adults passed symmetry, transitivity and MTS equivalence tests after intraverbal training alone; results with young children are inconsistent (Petursdottir et al. 2015) — [Miguel 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381345)
- **[E]** The systematic review (46 studies) characterises the naming-equivalence evidence as correlational: 13 studies used naming to explain equivalence or categorization, 33 studied how naming itself is established; only six studies (44 children) involved children under 3 — [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/)
- **[E]** Equivalence without naming. Luciano et al. (2007): generalized receptive symmetry after one month of exemplar training begun at 15 months 24 days, and visual-visual equivalence at 19 months, in an infant who showed no naming until about 23 months — [Luciano et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868588). Carr et al. (2000): equivalence in minimally verbal individuals — [Carr et al. 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284786). Sea lions and pigeons show symmetry or equivalence without language — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068)
- **[C]** Language-linked failure. Devany et al. (1986): no language-disabled child formed classes — [Devany et al. 1986](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1348265)
- **[C]** Conceptual objections: Lowenkron attributes the effects to joint control rather than naming; Michael (1996) sees naming as an interaction of separate repertoires; Saunders & Spradlin (1996) note the critical behavior is covert and unobservable — [Miguel 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381345). Harnad (1996): an experimental analysis of naming behavior cannot explain naming capacity — [Harnad 1996](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/252902/1/harnad96.naming.html)
- **[C]** Methodological point: with simultaneous name-object presentation, apparent derived naming may reflect direct training; non-simultaneous presentation is the stricter test — [Sivaraman & Barnes-Holmes 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733260/)

### Inferences
- Naming is neither shown to be necessary (counter-cases exist) nor shown to be merely a by-product; the defensible summary is that in young children the joining of the two directions (hear name→select object; see object→say name) co-occurs with, and can be used to repair, categorization of dissimilar items.
- The production direction matters. Training the output (speaker) direction gives the listener direction for free far more reliably than the reverse. That is a consistent, replicated asymmetry and bears on the "output/response requirement" question: an overt, stimulus-specific response may be what supplies the reverse link (the pigeon results with the bird's own behavior as sample, and response-mediated generalization, in section 3 point the same way).
- Naming is a clear case in which a common response to several stimuli does define a class (common BiN). The class is carried by a shared response/label, which is a many-to-one structure, again not pure A↔B mutual prediction.

### Gaps
- Horne & Lowe (1996) and its commentaries were not read in full; specific numbers for Lowe et al. (2002) and Horne et al. (2004) categorization tests (ages 1-4) were not retrieved.
- No study was found that randomly assigns children to acquire or not acquire bidirectional naming and then tests equivalence with adequate n; causal direction therefore remains open.

## 8. Do labels create or sharpen categories? Is there a new, more abstract representation over word and object?

### Takeaway
Words reliably promote category formation and object individuation in infants where tones do not, and labels speed category learning and bias perception in adults. The leading mechanistic accounts describe this as top-down modulation or as one strongly interconnected word-referent circuit; neither posits a separate higher node, though Pulvermüller's account does claim the label enlarges the unique, non-overlapping part of a concept's circuit.

### Cited Findings
- **[E]** Waxman & Markow (1995, Cognitive Psychology): 12-13-month-olds familiarised with objects formed a category when a novel word accompanied them and not in a no-word control — "words as invitations to form categories" — [Waxman lab review](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/waxman/VLCognition.pdf)
- **[E]** Specificity to words. Balaban & Waxman (1997): words but not tone sequences aided 9-month-olds' categorization; Fulkerson & Waxman (2007): words but not tones at 6 and 12 months — [Fulkerson & Waxman 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2099297)
- **[E/S]** Xu (2002, Cognition 85:223-250): for 9-month-olds, two distinct labels supported individuating two objects; words are proposed to act as "essence placeholders" for object kinds — [Xu 2002](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~babylab/Xu(2002).pdf)
- **[E]** Lupyan, Rakison & McClelland (2007, Psychological Science): adults learned novel ("alien") categories faster when redundant nonsense labels accompanied feedback, although the labels added no information — [Lupyan, Rakison & McClelland 2007](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/infant-cognition-lab/publications/Lupyan,%20Rakison,%20&%20McClelland%20(2007).pdf)
- **[E, as theory]** Lupyan's label-feedback hypothesis (2012, Frontiers in Psychology): an active label feeds back on perceptual representations and boosts category-diagnostic features, making representations more categorical and typical exemplars more prominent, at a cost to memory for individual exemplars. The effect is on-line and transient: verbal interference removes cross-linguistic colour effects; hearing "five" speeds attention to 5s, with the benefit fading after about 1600 ms. The author calls the account "merely a sketch" with neural underpinnings "almost completely unexplored", and acknowledges lasting perceptual-learning warping as a live alternative — [Lupyan 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3297074)
- **[E, as theory]** Pulvermüller, Garagnani & Wennekers (2014, Biological Cybernetics 108:573-593): word form and referent/semantic information that are co-active become bound by Hebbian correlation learning into a distributed cell assembly (action-perception circuit) with reciprocal connections; partial input ignites the whole circuit, which then reverberates; the most strongly interlinked "core" neurons lie in multimodal prefrontal and anterior temporal hubs. No extra symbolic node is posited: the higher-order assembly is the representation of the meaningful word. Linking similar concepts to distinct word forms is claimed to enlarge each circuit's unique part and reduce overlap (the authors flag this as speculative) — [Pulvermüller et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4228116)
- **[S]** A spiking model with brain-like connectivity from the same group: Hebbian learning of the correlation structure of symbol, perception and action information produced distributed assemblies extending into motor or visual areas according to word meaning — [Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2018.00088/pdf)
- **[E]** Convergent animal single-unit datum: after pair-association learning, some anterior temporal neurons respond to both members of a pair (pair-coding) — [Sakai & Miyashita 1991](https://www.nature.com/articles/354152a0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Labels have two opposite, both well-documented effects: a shared label pulls items together (Waxman), distinct labels push items apart (Xu; Pulvermüller's overlap reduction). A word-object link therefore changes the representation of the objects, which is more than a link, but the change is describable as re-weighting of existing features plus recruitment of convergence-zone neurons.
- In Pulvermüller's account the multimodal "core" of the assembly, located in hub cortex, is the nearest thing in this literature to a representation "one level up" over word and referent. It arises from Hebbian co-activation, which is symmetric by construction, and does not depend on mutual **prediction** in sequence.
- Lupyan's transience finding argues against a permanent merged unit: remove the label's activity and the categorical sharpening recedes.
- The infant word-vs-tone specificity implies that which sign is used matters; arbitrary co-occurring signals are not equivalent in their class-forming power.

### Gaps
- Replication concerns about redundant-label facilitation (Brojde and colleagues 2011 was named in a search summary) could not be traced to a specific accessible source.
- The competing "labels as features / auditory overshadowing" view (Sloutsky and colleagues), which holds that labels are just additional features and that unfamiliar sounds disrupt visual processing, is known from background; not sourced in this session.
- Effect sizes for Lupyan et al. (2007) and for the infant studies were not extracted.
- No study was found that directly tests, with representational-similarity methods, whether learning a word-object pair creates a new pattern distinct from both constituents.

## 9. Evidence against: where bidirectional association does not form a class, where verbal mediation seems required, and where simpler processes explain symmetry

### Takeaway
The negative evidence is substantial: most animals do not reverse forward-trained relations; explicit two-way training did not help pigeons or two language-trained chimpanzees; the same forward training can yield antisymmetry; Pavlovian backward pairing turns inhibitory; human classes are graded and integration is neither obligatory nor the only outcome; and several headline "symmetry" results have contiguity, location or generalization explanations. On the four objective questions: (1) one-direction training yields the reverse relation readily in humans and rarely elsewhere; (2) two-way training yields pair-coding and faster learning in humans but is not shown to create a higher-order unit; (3) the equivalence class has real emergent properties in humans but is graded; (4) the conditions that matter are differential reinforcement structure, a distinctive response or name, stimulus-control details, and multiple-exemplar history.

### Cited Findings
- **Bidirectional training without class formation.** Chimpanzees with long two-way lexigram histories failed symmetry tests, even with reinforced test trials — [Dugdale & Lowe 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284759). Pigeons given interleaved forward and backward training showed no evidence of bidirectional associations, whereas humans benefited — [Navarro & Wasserman 2020](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Bidirectional-conditioning-Revisiting-Asratyans-alternating-training/9984070259702771). The chimpanzee Ai improved only gradually over repeated train-test cycles — [Tomonaga 2008](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcss/15/3/15_3_347/_article/-char/en)
- **Forward training can produce the opposite of symmetry.** Antisymmetry in pigeons when oddity replaces identity in the baseline; anti-transitivity likewise — [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203); [Urcuioli 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5380386)
- **Extended backward pairing is inhibitory in Pavlovian preparations** — [Cole & Miller 1999 record](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/conditioned-excitation-and-conditioned-inhibition-acquired-throug/); [Chang, Blaisdell & Miller 2003](https://pigeonrat.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/144/2019/09/Chang-Blaisdell-Miller-2003-JEPABP.pdf)
- **Order is retained alongside any order-free relation.** Baboons: partial transfer, interpreted as coexisting directional and non-directional codes — [Chartier & Fagot 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272242). Pigeons: ordinal position is part of the functional stimulus — [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203). Humans: reversed congruent pairs rated less familiar than canonical ones — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/). Human serial lists: forward and backward probes only moderately correlated, unlike pairs — [Caplan et al. 2006](https://www.psych.ualberta.ca/~cml/papers/Caplan_et_al_2006a.pdf). Macaque IT: sequence predictions are direction-specific — [Meyer & Olson 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3228439)
- **Verbal mediation apparently required.** No language-disabled child formed classes — [Devany et al. 1986](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1348265). Categorization tracked the presence of both speaker and listener relations — [Miguel 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381345). Palmer: covert verbal mediation may explain derived relations — [Palmer 2004](https://contextualscience.org/publications/palmer_2004). Counter-cases in section 7 — [Carr et al. 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284786); [Luciano et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868588)
- **Simpler explanations of apparent symmetry.** Stimulus generalization, response-mediated generalization, reinforced test trials, select/reject control, location and temporal-position confounds — [Lionello-DeNolf 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2685068). Stimulus-plus-position compounds and reinforcement-defined classes — [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203). Contiguity during familiarisation in the infant EEG design — [Chartier & Rey 2020](https://www.pubmed.gov/31942690/). Mediation by an associatively evoked common associate, with no class unit — [Hall 1996 record](https://scilit.com/publications/6d1616ac9522b3dc5bccdb3ba2b01c56)
- **The class is not a single homogeneous unit.** Nodal-distance gradients in relatedness — [Moss-Lourenco & Fields 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3088076). Overlap can drive representations apart as well as together — [Schlichting et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/ncomms9151). Label effects are transient modulation — [Lupyan 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3297074). Cell-assembly theory posits no extra node — [Pulvermüller et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4228116)
- **Reversal is not sufficient for symbols, by its proponents' own statement**, and the species-uniqueness framing was judged circular by a reviewer — [van Kerkoerle et al. 2025 and appended reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820117/)
- **Evidence on the other side (for completeness).** Symmetry and holistic pair storage after a single ordered exposure in humans — [Kahana 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03195769). Transfer and co-extinction of functions across class members — [Dougher et al. 1994](https://contextualscience.org/publications/transfer_respondent_eliciting_extinction_functions_through_stimulus_equivalence). Pair-coding neurons — [Sakai & Miyashita 1991](https://www.nature.com/articles/354152a0.pdf). Encoding-time hippocampal integration predicting generalization — [Shohamy & Wagner 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2628634). Class merger through shared elements in Sidman's and Urcuioli's theories — [Sidman 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1284788); [Urcuioli 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582203)

### Inferences
- **On the specific hypothesis "A predicts B and B predicts A, therefore the two must be merged one level up."** No study found tests this directly. The nearest evidence divides. For: human pair memory behaves as one holistic unit; pair-coding neurons exist; in Sidman's and Urcuioli's theories, classes merge through shared members. Against: in pigeons and two chimpanzees, explicit two-way training did not produce class behavior; where classes do form they are explained by reinforcement structure across a whole set of relations, by a common response/name, or by a common associate, none of which is mutual prediction between the two items; and the literature's own mechanistic theories (Hall, Lupyan, Pulvermüller) do without a superordinate node.
- **Symmetry in humans does not wait for two-way training.** In adults (and, contestedly, infants) the reverse relation is present after one-direction exposure. So in the one species that clearly forms sign-object equivalence, the equivalence is not the resolution of a conflict created by training both directions; it appears to be a default of how the pair is encoded. A design in which the class is produced by the second direction of training would be modelling something the human data do not require.
- **Conditions that matter (objective 4).** (a) Reinforcement: differential reinforcement and, in Urcuioli's account, continued non-reinforcement of non-class combinations define class membership; class-specific outcomes help (sea lions; Sidman 2000). Human pair symmetry and the eLife result need no reinforcement at all (passive exposure). (b) Response/output: a distinctive overt response to each stimulus supplies the reverse link far more reliably than listener-only training (naming studies); responses can themselves be class members (Sidman 2000). (c) Stimulus control: constant location, each stimulus experienced in both temporal positions, single-stimulus successive presentation. (d) History: multiple-exemplar training in both directions is sufficient to induce generalized symmetry in some children and a sea lion, not in two chimpanzees. (e) Sign type and relevance: words, not tones, drive infant categorization; reward relevance gated frontal effects in macaques.
- **On a shared mechanism for sequence learning and conceptual order.** Evidence for commonality: the same hippocampal-medial prefrontal system supports temporal overlap integration and inferential/class-like generalization; Hall's mechanism covers sensory preconditioning, second-order conditioning and equivalence with one process; temporal-coding theory lets stored order be read out flexibly. Evidence against: every comparative and memory dataset above that separates a directional/ordinal code from a non-directional/co-occurrence code (baboons, pigeons, macaque IT, human serial lists vs pairs), and the eLife proposal that human symbolic reference involves precisely **discarding** temporal order. None of these studies addresses "is a kind of / part of" relations; the equivalence literature concerns sameness classes, and RFT explicitly treats hierarchical and comparative frames as different relational operants from coordination (sameness).
- The status of the human-uniqueness claim is unsettled: defensible as "humans show strong, immediate, order-free reversal; other species show none to weak, procedure-bound transfer", not as a categorical absence.

### Gaps
- No experiment was found that compares one-direction, two-direction and common-associate training head to head on class-level outcomes (transitivity, transfer of function, representational similarity) in any species. That is the experiment the hypothesis needs.
- No source was found on equivalence classes formed between stimuli of different **kinds** in the sense of the brief (a symbolic token and a graded magnitude). The number-cognition literature on mapping number words or digits to magnitudes would be the place to look and was out of scope here.
- RFT work on hierarchical and comparative relational frames (as possible analogues of "kind of / part of" order) was only touched via Berens & Hayes (2007); no evidence was gathered on whether hierarchical framing depends on, or shares a mechanism with, sequence learning.
- Hippocampal transitive-inference and ordinal-sequence literatures (which bear on the shared-mechanism question) were not searched.
- Several full texts were inaccessible (Kahana 2002; Chartier & Rey 2020; Navarro & Wasserman 2020 and 2025; Imai et al. 2021; Fagot et al. 2018); claims from them are marked [S] or listed as gaps above.
