# Thought as internalized (covert, simulated) behavior, and what symbols add

Conventions used in these notes. Status tags: **[Established]** = replicated or uncontroversial as a description of the source or finding; **[Contested]** = live dispute in the literature; **[Unverified]** = I could not confirm against a primary or authoritative source in this session. Sources are paraphrased, not quoted; short phrases in quotation marks are the authors' terms of art. Hull (1930), Hesslow (2002), Piaget (1964) and McClelland (2021) were read page by page from scans; most other items were checked against abstracts, PMC full text, or repository records. The session's web-search allowance ran out about two thirds of the way through, so some citations were confirmed only by direct fetch of a known page, and a few are parked under Gaps as recollection.

Each section ends its Inferences with a plain verdict on the question the brief asks of every tradition: is the mechanism that internalizes behavior the SAME mechanism that builds higher-order concepts?

## 1. Learning-theory mediators: Hull, Osgood, Mowrer, Pavlov, Watson, Skinner

### Takeaway
The neobehaviorists had a precise mechanism for turning an act into a representation of an act: a response that has lost its instrumental function survives only as a producer of internal stimuli that cue the next response, and can then shrink, speed up and drop segments. Hull (1930) explicitly called this the organic basis of symbolism. The accounts fell because the mediators were unobservable copies of overt S-R links with no independent evidence and no account of structure (syntax, reference, serial order), but the functional idea survives as outcome expectancy, ideomotor/common coding, forward models and model-based reinforcement learning.

### Cited Findings

**Hull 1930, "Knowledge and purpose as habit mechanisms"**
- Citation confirmed from Hull's own 1934 reference list: Psychological Review 37, 511-525 (1930); the companion is "Goal attraction and directing ideas conceived as habit phenomena," Psychological Review 38, 487-506 (1931). [Established] — [Hull 1934, Classics in the History of Psychology](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Hull/Hierarchy/part1.htm)
- Step 1, knowledge as an internal copy of a sequence. A world sequence S1...S5 evokes responses R1...R5 in the organism; each response produces proprioceptive stimuli (s1...s5); by "redintegration" each s becomes conditioned to the next R. After a few repetitions the response chain R1 -> s1 -> R2 -> s2 ... can run off by itself if the world sequence is interrupted after its first member. Hull says the world has thereby stamped the pattern of its action on the organism, which now carries a functional replica of that world segment, and that this is a naturalistic sense of "knowing". [Established as a description of the text] — [Hull 1930, scan, pp. 511-514](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Step 2, foresight. The internal chain is not tied to the tempo of the world sequence and tends to run faster, so its last member can occur before the world event it originally answered. Foresight is defined as reacting to an impending event that has not yet occurred, achieved through an internal substitute stimulus. [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, pp. 514-515](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Step 3, "pure stimulus acts". Hull defines these as acts whose sole function is to serve as stimuli for other acts: the intermediate links of the anticipatory chain have no instrumental value yet are indispensable because they supply the cue for the final, useful act. His homely demonstration is that a person asked how they button a coat lets the hand run through the buttoning sequence in the air to find out. He states that the pure stimulus act emerges as an organic, strictly internal and individual symbolism, that commonplace instrumental acts are transformed by a natural reduction process into a rudimentary kind of thought, and that this lets the organism react to the not-here and the not-now. In his summary: "The concept of the pure stimulus act appears to be the organic basis of symbolism" (p. 524). [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, pp. 515-517, 524](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Hull separates this individual symbolism from two things often conflated with it: the stimulus acts of social communication, and Watson's subvocal speech, which he treats as a derivative of social communication by a reduction process. He declines to analyze those "latter forms of symbolism" as too complex for the paper. [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, footnote 3, p. 517](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Step 4, reduction. Because pure stimulus acts no longer do instrumental work they can be reduced in amplitude to almost any degree compatible with still delivering their stimulus (energy economy), which Hull offers as an explanation of the subjectivity of imagery. A footnote allows that they might shrink to an actual zero leaving only a neural vestige, and says the hypothesis fits either case. [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, pp. 517-518 and footnote 4](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Step 5, short-circuiting. A persisting stimulus S_p present through the whole sequence (the paper's "purpose mechanism", e.g. hunger) becomes conditioned to every act of the series, most strongly to the goal act. The resulting "intraserial competition" can let a late response win early, so intermediate segments drop out ("serial segment elimination"). Hull's example: a person multiplies 49 x 67 step by step, and asked again soon after answers 3283 directly; the whole sequence of pure stimulus acts has dropped out. [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, pp. 519-525](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)
- Hull adds an explicit engineering conjecture: a "psychic" machine designed to evolve pure stimulus acts could reach a degree of freedom and spontaneity unknown to rigid machines. [Established as description] — [Hull 1930, p. 517](https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/classes/psy5300/Documents/Hull1930.pdf)

**Hull 1931/1934, the fractional anticipatory goal response (r_G - s_G)**
- A later historical account says Hull arrived at fractional anticipatory goal responses in 1930 while trying to explain the Gestalt notion of goal attraction, and groups the 1930 and 1931 papers as his articles on purpose. [Established] — [Revista de Historia de la Psicologia article](https://journals.copmadrid.org/historia/archivos/fichero_salida20210910142241469000.pdf)
- In the 1934 habit-family paper Hull calls the fractional anticipatory goal reaction the major mechanism integrating the habit-family hierarchy; it carries its own proprioceptive stimulus s_G, is present throughout all members of a family once integrated, is linked to "ideomotor action", and the same paper speaks of "pure-stimulus or symbolic act sequences" as subject to short-circuiting. [Established as description] — [Hull 1934](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Hull/Hierarchy/part1.htm)
- Textbook gloss: a fraction of the reinforcing event at the end of a chain comes to act at its beginning and guides overt behavior toward the goal as if purposefully, e.g. explaining a rat's learned turn in a T-maze. [Established as secondary summary] — [UTEP learning theorists page](https://www.utep.edu/liberalarts/psychology/research/the-learning-theorists/hull.html)

**Osgood's representational mediation theory**
- Osgood's theory posits a "representational mediation process": a stimulus evokes an internal response whose self-stimulation mediates overt responding; the internal process represents what the sign signifies rather than the sign itself. Wikipedia dates a two-stage mediation learning theory to 1954 and ties it to the semantic differential (Evaluation, Potency, Activity). [Established as secondary summary; primary texts not read] — [student guide to mediational theory](https://www.afirstlook.com/docs/mediational.pdf); [Wikipedia, Charles E. Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_E._Osgood)
- Fodor attacked it in "Could meaning be an r_m?" (Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior 4, 1965) and Osgood replied in "Meaning cannot be r_m?" (same journal 5, 402-407, 1966). Rozeboom (1979, Philosophy of Science) judged Osgood's the only carefully developed behavioral account of reference and still found that it had not faced the difficulty of analyzing reference by effects shared between a symbol and its significate. [Established that the exchange occurred; page range for Fodor 1965 Unverified, see Gaps] — [Rozeboom 1979](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/on-behavioral-theories-of-reference/E4AC2A76A5689C74B5ECE270FF7B8DA0)

**Mowrer 1960**
- "Learning Theory and the Symbolic Processes" (Wiley, New York, 1960, 473 pp.) is the companion volume to "Learning Theory and Behavior" (1960). [Established] — [Wellcome Collection record](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/ab4mnhjw)
- Mowrer's two-factor theory first separated conditioning ("sign learning") from habit formation ("solution learning") (1947) and was revised in the 1950s to one kind of learning with two kinds of reinforcement; fear is a conditioned, anticipatory response. [Established as secondary summary] — [Wikipedia, Orval Hobart Mowrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orval_Hobart_Mowrer)
- In his 1954 APA presidential address Mowrer proposed that the sentence is a conditioning device (the predicate's meaning is conditioned to the subject). [Established as secondary summary] — [Revista de Historia de la Psicologia article](https://journals.copmadrid.org/historia/archivos/fichero_salida20220923111222296000.pdf)

**Pavlov's second signal system**
- Pavlov's "second signal system" is dated to about 1932. The word is described as a "signal of signals": the second system arises on the basis of the first (direct sensory conditioning), words in spoken or written form become conditioned stimuli, and its work is the analysis and synthesis of generalized speech signals. [Weakly sourced: popular Russian physiology site and a scholarly mailing list; no primary Pavlov text read] — [orlova-center.ru summary](https://orlova-center.ru/en/the-history-of-man/fiziologicheskie-osobennosti-rechi-rech-e-funkcii-i-fiziologicheskie/); [XMCA list discussion](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/MCA/Mail/Archives/2016-October/008684.html)
- Luria (1959) uses Pavlov's term when describing the directive (regulating) function of speech; a Vygotsky scholar on the same list notes that Vygotsky's own references to the second signal system appear only around 1933-34 and that Luria was politically obliged to use the idiom. [Established as secondary] — [Luria 1959, "The directive function of speech"](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luria/works/1959/directive-funtion-speech.pdf); [XMCA list](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/MCA/Mail/Archives/2016-October/008684.html)

**Watson and Skinner: covert behavior**
- Watson, "Is thinking merely the action of language mechanisms?" British Journal of Psychology 11, 87-104 (1920). He opens by denying he ever held that thinking is *merely* language mechanisms; thinking covers all implicit language activity plus other activity substitutable for it; there is no principled reason it must sit in the larynx (practically, large muscles are busy with manual work). [Established] — [Watson 1920, Classics in the History of Psychology](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Watson/thinking.htm)
- Alderson-Day and Fernyhough characterize Watson's (1913) view as a mechanical process in which speech becomes quieter until it is silent thought. [Established as secondary] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- Skinner: verbal behavior includes covert talk and so covers much of what is called thinking, but the index to Verbal Behavior (1957) lists covert behavior as "an unsatisfactory equivalent for thinking" (pp. 434-438), i.e. he did not identify thought with subvocal speech. Skinner did not deny inner events; he held they are behavior describable with the same concepts and are not explanatory in a functional analysis. The SEP notes he gave little detail on how this works. [Established] — [B. F. Skinner Foundation index](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/2014_05_P_003.pdf); [SAGE Encyclopedia entry](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/the-sage-encyclopedia-of-theory-in-psychology/chpt/verbal-behavior); [SEP, Behaviorism (Graham, rev. 2023)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)

**Why the accounts fell out of favor**
- The peripheral version was taken as refuted when subjects fully paralyzed by curare could still think: Smith et al. (1947), "The lack of cerebral effects of d-tubocurarine," Anesthesiology 8, 1-14. Hesslow argues the idea was rejected prematurely and that a modified, central version (motor structures active, execution suppressed) lived on. [Established that the experiment was read this way; "premature" is Hesslow's opinion] — [Hesslow 2002, p. 242 and ref. 7](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Chomsky's 1959 review of Verbal Behavior charged that reinforcement histories are too impoverished to determine linguistic competence; the SEP names internal representation as the deepest objection, and dates the maturing of cognitive science to roughly 1960-1985. [Established] — [SEP, Behaviorism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)
- Mandler: very little of Hullian theory applied to complex human behavior; Dollard and Miller and Osgood tried to stretch mediation-based associationism over human action. A course history states that information-processing theory gradually replaced mediational theory as the language of cognitive psychology in the 1960s and 70s. Kihlstrom cites Lashley's 1951 "The problem of serial order in behavior" in this context. [Established as historiography] — [Mandler, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt22s8x969/qt22s8x969_noSplash_440a51bfddabec049430f5b70eb8ee14.pdf); [Kihlstrom, cognitive revolution notes](https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/MemoryWeb/cognitive/cognitive_supp.htm)

**What survives**
- The SEP: robust elements of behaviorism survive in behavior therapy and laboratory animal learning theory. [Established] — [SEP, Behaviorism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)
- Ideomotor and common-coding theory (Lotze 1852; James 1890; Prinz 1984, 1997; Hommel, Musseler, Aschersleben & Prinz 2001, Theory of Event Coding): actions are coded by their anticipated perceivable effects, and the action-effect association runs in reverse to select actions. [Established as a research program] — [Wikipedia, Common coding theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_coding_theory)
- Hesslow re-reads Tolman and Gleitman (1949) and reinforcer-devaluation results (Colwill & Rescorla 1985) as a rat initiating a response, having that initiation evoke the learned sensory consequence, and that consequence suppressing completion: functionally Hull's anticipatory mechanism, relocated centrally. [Established as Hesslow's interpretation] — [Hesslow 2002, Box 1](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Daw, Niv & Dayan (2005, Nature Neuroscience 8(12), from p. 1704) treat devaluation sensitivity as the signature of a model-based (prefrontal) controller competing with a model-free (dorsolateral striatal) one, arbitrated by relative uncertainty. [Established as an influential model] — [Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005](https://www.princeton.edu/~yael/NN2005.htm)

### Inferences
- Hull's 1930 mechanism has five separable parts that map onto modern components: (a) a learned next-step predictor over the organism's own response-produced states (the R -> s -> R chain is a sequence model run in closed loop); (b) decoupling from world tempo (faster-than-real-time rollout); (c) outputs retained only as cues ("pure stimulus acts": tokens whose only job is to condition the next step); (d) amplitude reduction toward a purely central trace; (e) goal-conditioned short-circuiting (a persistent goal/drive input lets late responses win early, i.e. caching or amortization of a computed result).
- "Symbol" in Hull means something narrower than in linguistics: any act-derived internal event that functions only as a stimulus. It has no arbitrariness, no system of contrasts and no compositionality. Hull says outright that social and verbal symbolism is a further development he is not explaining.
- Hull's short-circuiting runs in the opposite direction to level-building: it deletes the intermediate sequence and keeps the answer. It explains automatization (thought replacing behavior, then habit replacing thought), not the creation of a higher-order unit that can itself be operated on.
- The r_G - s_G construct is functionally an outcome expectancy: a learned, anticipatory, goal-specific internal state that conditions earlier choices. Its modern counterparts (devaluation-sensitive goal-directed control, action-effect codes, successor-style predictive codes) kept the function and dropped the commitment to a peripheral fractional response.
- Same mechanism? For Hull, Osgood, Mowrer, Watson and Skinner: yes by assertion. One conditioning process is claimed to produce covert chains, meaning, purpose and symbolism, and no separate concept-building process is proposed. This is exactly the point at which the tradition failed: it had no account of hierarchy, reference or recursion. Pavlov is the partial exception: the same conditioning laws, but a distinct, human-only second level of signalling.

## 2. Tolman: vicarious trial and error, cognitive maps, hippocampal sweeps and replay, model-based RL and learned world models

### Takeaway
This is the line with the strongest evidence that offline simulation of behavior exists and guides choice, and it needs no symbols: rats do it. Hippocampal sequences sweep ahead of the animal down candidate paths at choice points, and learned-world-model agents (Dyna, World Models, MuZero, Dreamer) are literal engineering versions of "thought replaces behavior". What is contested is whether awake replay is planning or memory maintenance, and none of this work shows that simulation by itself builds higher-order concepts.

### Cited Findings

**Tolman**
- Tolman, "Cognitive maps in rats and men," Psychological Review 55(4), 189-208 (1948). In learning, something like a field map of the environment is established; the brain is more like a map control room than a telephone exchange; narrow "strip maps" transfer worse than broad comprehensive maps and are induced by brain damage, impoverished cues, over-repetition and excessive motivation or frustration. Latent learning (Blodgett; Tolman & Honzik; Spence & Lippitt) shows learning without reward. [Established] — [Tolman 1948](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Tolman/Maps/maps.htm)
- VTE is the hesitating, looking-back-and-forth behavior at a choice point; Tolman credits the term to Muenzinger. In discrimination tasks more VTE went with better learning; in mazes harder units produced more VTE. [Established] — [Tolman 1948](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Tolman/Maps/maps.htm)
- Guthrie's 1935 jibe was that Tolman's theory leaves the rat buried in thought, i.e. it had no account of how deliberation ends in action. Redish notes that search-based accounts lost to situation-action chains in the 1940s partly for lack of mechanism and measurement tools. [Established as historiography] — [Redish 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029271)

**Redish 2016 and the neural evidence**
- Redish, "Vicarious trial and error," Nature Reviews Neuroscience 17(3), 147-159 (2016). VTE occurs when animals face difficult, changing choices in tasks whose structure they already understand: early learning, after contingency changes, at hard choice points; it is absent in initial exploration and disappears with automation. Deliberation is defined as searching through possibilities using a hypothesized model of the world, and linked to episodic future thinking. [Established as review] — [Redish 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029271)
- Johnson & Redish, "Neural ensembles in CA3 transiently encode paths forward of the animal at a decision point," Journal of Neuroscience 27(45), 12176-12189 (2007): decoded CA3 position swept ahead of the rat, first down one arm then the other, during pauses at the choice point; representations were coherent, forward-biased, occurred under theta/gamma rather than sharp waves, and changed with experience. [Established] — [Johnson & Redish 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6673267)
- Within a theta cycle each sweep follows a single path to a single goal, serially; sweeps go both ways early, one way later, then vanish as behavior automates. Ventral striatal covert reward signals precede turning around; orbitofrontal signals appear after commitment; dorsolateral striatal task-bracketing is negatively correlated with VTE lap by lap. [Established as review] — [Redish 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029271)
- Redish's own caveats: the action-selection process that ends deliberation is unknown; ventral striatum and OFC did not show integration-to-threshold; sweeps correlate with but do not track head orientation one-to-one; how candidate futures are chosen and evaluated is open. The body text frames the contrast as deliberative versus procedural rather than model-based versus model-free. [Established] — [Redish 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029271)
- Pfeiffer & Foster, "Hippocampal place-cell sequences depict future paths to remembered goals," Nature 497, 74-79 (2013): in a two-dimensional arena with up to about 250 simultaneously recorded cells, pre-movement sequences predicted the immediately following path to the goal, including for novel start-goal combinations. [Established] — [Pfeiffer & Foster 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3990408)
- Kay et al., "Constant sub-second cycling between representations of possible futures in the hippocampus," Cell 180(3), 552-567 (2020): hippocampal activity alternated between two upcoming paths at about 8 Hz (one hypothetical per roughly 125 ms cycle), for location and heading, including while running. [Established] — [Kay et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7126188)
- Against the planning reading: Gillespie et al., "Hippocampal replay reflects specific past experiences rather than a plan for subsequent choice," Neuron 109(19) (2021). In an eight-arm win-stay/lose-switch task, awake replay content was decoupled from the next choice and enriched for previously rewarded and not-recently-visited arms, supporting a storage/maintenance role. The authors cite earlier weak trial-by-trial coupling (Carey et al. 2019; Igata et al. 2021) and leave open that replay serves storage, planning or both. [Contested] — [Gillespie et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8497431)
- Human evidence for abstraction in replay: Liu, Dolan, Kurth-Nelson & Behrens, "Human replay spontaneously reorganizes experience," Cell 178(3), 640-652 (2019). After learning an ordering rule, participants saw new objects in scrambled order; during rest, MEG-decoded replay followed the rule-implied order, not the experienced order, was time-compressed and reversed after reward. An abstract code factorized into sequence position and sequence identity preceded the sensory code by about 50 ms. [Established as a single influential study] — [Liu et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6657653)
- Pezzulo, van der Meer, Lansink & Pennartz, "Internally generated sequences in learning and executing goal-directed behavior," Trends in Cognitive Sciences 18(12), 647-657 (2014): sequences not tied to current location support consolidation, augmentation of representations, internal simulation and recombination, and can be modeled as a sampling-based inference engine serving choice, control and learning. [Established as review/proposal] — [Pezzulo et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25156191/)

**Computational theory and engineering versions**
- Mattar & Daw, "Prioritized memory access explains planning and hippocampal replay," Nature Neuroscience 21(11), 1609-1617 (2018). Built on Sutton's Dyna (cited as Sutton 1990, "Integrated architectures for learning, planning, and reacting based on approximating dynamic programming," Proc. 7th ICML, 216-224), which views planning as learning values from remembered experience. The utility of replaying a memory is gain x need (how much the backup improves policy x how soon and often the state will be visited, from the successor representation). This yields forward replay before a run (need-driven), reverse replay at reward (gain-driven) and remote replay in rest and sleep, and frames planning and learning as the same backup operation in different orders, with consolidation as active recomputation. [Established as a leading normative model] — [Mattar & Daw 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6203620)
- Ha & Schmidhuber, "World Models," arXiv:1803.10122 (2018): a generative model learns a compressed spatial and temporal representation unsupervised; a compact policy is trained on its features, and can be trained entirely inside the model's own hallucinated "dream" and transferred back to the real environment. [Established] — [Ha & Schmidhuber 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122)
- Schrittwieser et al., "Mastering Atari, Go, chess and shogi by planning with a learned model" (MuZero), arXiv:1911.08265, Nature DOI 10.1038/s41586-020-03051-4 (2020): tree search over a learned latent model that predicts only reward, policy and value, with no knowledge of the rules; matches AlphaZero on board games. [Established] — [Schrittwieser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265)
- Hafner, Pasukonis, Ba & Lillicrap, "Mastering diverse domains through world models" (DreamerV3), arXiv:2301.04104 (2023, rev. 2024): learns a world model and improves behavior by imagining future scenarios; one configuration across more than 150 tasks; first to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data. World model, critic and actor are trained concurrently from replayed experience. [Established; the 2025 Nature version could not be opened, see Gaps] — [Hafner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)
- Language-model analogues: Wei et al., "Chain-of-thought prompting elicits reasoning in large language models," arXiv:2201.11903 (2022): emitting intermediate reasoning steps improves multi-step arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic reasoning. Hao et al., "Training large language models to reason in a continuous latent space" (Coconut), arXiv:2412.06769 (2024): feeding the last hidden state back as the next input ("continuous thought") lets one step encode several alternatives, giving a breadth-first-search-like pattern that beats chain-of-thought on search-heavy logical tasks; the authors argue most word tokens serve fluency, not reasoning. [Established as reported results] — [Wei et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903); [Hao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769)

### Inferences
- The minimal mechanism this tradition implies is: a learned transition model, a way to run it decoupled from sensory input and motor output, serial sampling of candidate futures, and an evaluator. Every element is present in rats, so the tradition is direct evidence that replacing overt trial and error with covert trial and error does not require symbols in any linguistic sense.
- Three behavioral signatures are worth copying as tests for any architecture claiming this mechanism: simulation should appear when the world model exists but the policy is uncertain (not during first exposure); it should fade as the policy is compiled; and its content should be prioritized (gain x need), not uniform.
- Hull's short-circuiting, Redish's fading of VTE, and the model-based to model-free handover in Daw et al. are the same phenomenon at three levels of description: internal simulation is a transitional regime between ignorance and habit.
- Liu et al. is the most relevant single result for a "one mechanism for sequence memory and conceptual order" hypothesis: replay ran over an abstract, factorized position-and-sequence code and imposed learned structure on new content. That shows replay *uses* an abstract ordering schema; it does not show replay *builds* it.
- Chain-of-thought tokens are functionally Hull's pure stimulus acts (outputs kept only as inputs to later steps), and Coconut is Hull's footnote 4 (the act reduced to a purely central trace). The Coconut result suggests discrete symbols are not needed for the stepwise computation itself; what discreteness buys is commitment, inspectability and communicability.
- Same mechanism? Partly. In Dyna and Mattar-Daw, learning from real experience and planning from simulated experience are one operation, so internalized behavior and value learning share a mechanism. But model learning, planning and abstraction are distinct computations, and state or temporal abstraction is a separate, unsolved problem in this literature. Tolman himself gave no concept-formation mechanism.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session (search allowance exhausted): Tolman 1939 "Prediction of vicarious trial and error by means of the schematic sowbug" (Psychological Review 46); Muenzinger 1938; Sutton's 1991 SIGART Dyna paper; the Nature volume and pages for MuZero (I recall 588, 604-609) and the 2025 Nature version of DreamerV3 (the publisher page redirected to a login).
- Relevant but unverified here: Widloski & Foster 2022 (replay rerouting around barriers); Jensen, Hennequin & Mattar 2024 (a recurrent meta-RL agent that learns when to do policy rollouts, as a model of replay and human thinking time); Kurth-Nelson et al. 2016 (fast non-spatial sequences in humans); Behrens et al. 2018 and Whittington et al. 2020 on cognitive maps as general relational structure.
- No source found that tests causally whether disrupting theta sweeps (as opposed to sharp-wave ripples) impairs deliberative choice specifically.

## 3. Craik's internal model; Dennett's Popperian and Gregorian creatures

### Takeaway
Craik gives the canonical statement that thought is the running of an internal model so that alternatives can be tried without acting, and unlike the animal literature he makes symbols constitutive: thought is translation into symbols, inference over them, and retranslation into action. Dennett splits the idea into two tiers, internal pre-selection of actions (Popperian) and imported cultural mind tools such as words (Gregorian), which is a claim that symbols are a second, different mechanism layered on simulation.

### Cited Findings
- Craik, The Nature of Explanation (1943), is credited as the origin of the "mental model" idea: an organism carrying a "small-scale model" of external reality and of its own possible actions can try out alternatives and react to situations before they arise. [Established] — [Farnam Street excerpt of Craik](https://fs.blog/the-nature-of-explanation/); [Wikipedia, Mental model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_model)
- Craik's three processes of reasoning: translation of an external process into an internal representation in words, numbers or other symbols; derivation of further symbols by an inferential process; retranslation of those symbols into actions or a recognition of the correspondence between symbols and external events. He asks whether brains use mechanisms comparable to calculating machines to the same end. [Established as excerpted; no page numbers given by the source] — [Farnam Street excerpt of Craik](https://fs.blog/the-nature-of-explanation/)
- Dennett's "tower of generate and test": Darwinian creatures (selection only), Skinnerian (learn by trial and reinforcement), Popperian (pre-test candidate acts against an inner model), Gregorian (use culturally transmitted tools, above all language). The Popperian level is named for Popper's point that this design lets hypotheses die in our stead. Gregorian creatures import "mind tools" such as words, which improve both the generator and the tester. [Established as secondary summaries; primary texts Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995) and Kinds of Minds (1996) not read] — [PhilPapers record summarizing the tower](https://api.philpapers.org/rec/ROCSDW); [Godfrey-Smith, "Towers and Trees"](https://petergodfreysmith.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Towers-and-Trees-PGS-corrected-typo.pdf); [blog summary of Dennett's creatures](https://billkerr2.blogspot.com/2006/07/dennetts-creatures.html)
- Roboticists have taken the Popperian level literally as an internal "consequence engine". [Established as secondary] — [Robohub, on internal models and Popperian creatures](https://robohub.org/on-internal-models-consequence-engines-and-popperian-creatures/)

### Inferences
- Craik's "symbols" are states of any physical mechanism whose relations parallel those of the modeled process; the claim is about structure-preserving internal states, not specifically words. Read that way Craik is compatible with rat hippocampal sweeps and with latent world models, and the word "symbol" is doing less work than it seems.
- Dennett's two tiers predict a dissociation that the evidence in sections 2 and 9 supports: offline simulation without language (rats, apes, aphasic patients) and a further gain from public symbols that is cultural and cumulative rather than a precondition of simulation.
- Same mechanism? Craik: one modeling process, with no learning account. Dennett: explicitly different. Simulation is one mechanism; word-borne mind tools are another, acquired from outside.

### Gaps
- I did not read Craik or Dennett in the original; exact wording and page numbers should be checked before quoting. Godfrey-Smith's paper is a critical reassessment of the tower and was only seen in search-result summary.

## 4. Simulation theories: Hesslow, Jeannerod, Grush, Barsalou, Pezzulo

### Takeaway
Simulation theory restates covert-behavior theory in central terms: motor structures are activated with execution suppressed, sensory cortex is activated from within, and learned action-consequence associations link the two so chains can run. Evidence that imagery and covert action recruit motor and sensory systems is strong; evidence that this activity constitutes thought or meaning, rather than accompanying it, is contested. Hesslow claims no symbols and no evolutionary leap are needed; Barsalou is the one author who claims the same simulators also are the concepts.

### Cited Findings

**Hesslow**
- Hesslow, "Conscious thought as simulation of behaviour and perception," Trends in Cognitive Sciences 6(6), 242-247 (2002). Three assumptions: (1) simulation of actions: motor structures can be activated as in a normal action without overt movement; (2) simulation of perception: imagining is perceptual activity generated by the brain itself; (3) anticipation: associative mechanisms let behavioral and perceptual activity elicit other perceptual activity, in particular the activity that would have followed the action had it been performed. [Established] — [Hesslow 2002](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Lineage he claims: British empiricists and associationists; Alexander Bain (1868), for whom thinking is restrained speaking or acting; Watson and Skinner; the curare result; a modified version surviving. [Established] — [Hesslow 2002](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Chaining: once anticipation exists, the percept generated by a simulated action can serve as the stimulus for the next response preparation, giving long simulated sequences so whole courses of action can be evaluated before being risked. No central agent runs the simulation and no separate evaluator is needed: simulated sensory events evoke their learned emotional consequences, which reinforce or punish the simulated action. [Established as the theory's claim] — [Hesslow 2002, pp. 244-245](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Evidence cited: imagined and executed movement durations match (Decety et al. 1989); premotor and supplementary motor activation during imagined movement with weaker or absent primary motor activation (Ingvar & Philipsson 1977 onward); mental rotation times scale with angle (Shepard & Metzler 1971); visual cortex and fusiform activation in imagery; imagery loss after visual cortex damage and representational neglect (Bisiach & Luzzatti 1978); premotor, parietal and occipital activation in Tower of London; cerebellar and striatal activation in imagined movement and planning; prism adaptation from merely imagined pointing errors. [Established as cited evidence] — [Hesslow 2002](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Language: preparing a verbal response can feed auditory cortex or Wernicke's area directly, so we "hear" it before it is spoken and can respond to it; inner speech is internal simulation of conversation, built from components that can each run unconsciously. [Established as the theory's claim] — [Hesslow 2002, Fig. 3](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Hesslow's own assessment: evidence for assumptions 1 and 2 is strong; assumption 3 is less well supported by specific evidence. He claims as virtues that the theory needs no images or representations as entities and no evolutionary leap, since the mechanisms are shared by all mammals; a robot navigating on predicted stimuli alone (Jirenhed, Hesslow & Ziemke 2001) is offered as an existence proof. [Established] — [Hesslow 2002, pp. 246-247](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Hesslow, "The current status of the simulation theory of cognition," Brain Research 1428, 71-79 (2012; online June 2011): restates the three assumptions (the third as eliciting the most probable consequences), calls the theory ontologically parsimonious and independent of internal models or representations, says the support is mainly neuroimaging, and extends it to memory and cognitive maps. [Established from abstract] — [Lund University record](https://portal.research.lu.se/en/publications/the-current-status-of-the-simulation-theory-of-cognition/)

**Jeannerod**
- Jeannerod, "The representing brain: neural correlates of motor intention and imagery," Behavioral and Brain Sciences 17(2), 187-245 (1994); "Neural simulation of action: a unifying mechanism for motor cognition," NeuroImage 14(1), S103-S109 (2001). The 2001 abstract, as returned in search, says paradigms from cognitive psychology have illuminated covert stages of action and develops the hypothesis that the motor system is part of a simulation network engaged whether an action is self-intended or observed in others. Later reviews summarize motor simulation theory as the claim that motor imagery shares representations and mechanisms with execution. [Established; abstract seen only via search excerpt] — [PubMed record, Jeannerod 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11373140/)
- A critical review asks whether motor simulation theory actually explains the cognitive mechanisms of motor imagery, as opposed to documenting shared activation. [Contested; abstract not retrievable, title only] — [PubMed record, 2017 critical review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28261079/)

**Grush**
- Grush, "The emulation theory of representation: motor control, imagery, and perception," Behavioral and Brain Sciences 27(3), 377 ff. (2004). Using control theory (forward models) and signal processing, the brain builds circuits that model the body and environment; run offline these emulators yield imagery, and the framework is claimed to synthesize motor control, imagery and perception. Commentators questioned normativity, level of detail and implementation. [Established as a theory; end page reported inconsistently, 422 vs 442 with commentary] — [Grush 2004, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt15t2595z/qt15t2595z.pdf); [Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/emulation-theory-of-representation-motor-control-imagery-and-perception/20AF7D2B406EE14EDFFD3CB47C69A5E7)

**Barsalou**
- Barsalou, "Perceptual symbol systems," Behavioral and Brain Sciences 22(4), 577-660 (1999). Memories of a perceptual component are organized around a frame to form a "simulator" that can produce indefinitely many simulations; simulators combine combinatorially and recursively to give productivity; they support propositions and abstract concepts (the latter via simulations of combined physical and introspective events). [Established as a theory] — [PubMed record, Barsalou 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11301525/)
- Commentaries: what simulators are, as opposed to what they do, is not defined; activation of perceptual areas does not show it constitutes concepts; simulators or frames appear in the limit to be amodal; the account of abstract terms is very limited. [Contested] — [BBS commentary on simulators and amodality](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/simulations-simulators-amodality-and-abstract-terms/CFD391CB1794708CA30E1018099B183F)

**Neural evidence on covert speech and mirror systems**
- Inner speech engages left inferior frontal gyrus (ventral pars opercularis) as overt articulation does; generating inner speech attenuates perception of external sounds and biases perception of ambiguous sounds, as a forward-model account predicts (Scott 2013; Scott et al. 2013); Tian & Poeppel (2010) found distinct imagined-speech effects. But Oppenheim & Dell's "flexible abstraction" view, which the reviewers adopt, holds that inner speech is at core an abstract phonological code whose articulatory detail varies. [Established findings; interpretation Contested] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- Hickok, "Eight problems for the mirror neuron theory of action understanding in monkeys and humans," Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 21(7), 1229-1243 (2009): no lesion evidence in monkeys that disrupting F5 impairs action perception; production and recognition doubly dissociate in patients; Broca's aphasics with almost no speech output comprehend words normally; a signer with severe production deficits kept comprehension. He concludes the human evidence makes a strong case against motor simulation as the basis of understanding. [Contested, but the dissociations are established] — [Hickok 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773693)

**Critiques of embodied simulation**
- Mahon & Caramazza, "A critical look at the embodied cognition hypothesis and a new proposal for grounding conceptual content," Journal of Physiology-Paris 102, 59-70 (2008): strong embodiment conflicts with neuropsychology; sensorimotor activation may be spreading activation that follows amodal conceptual processing; some concepts (e.g. beautiful) have no consistent sensorimotor content. Their alternative, "grounding by interaction", keeps concepts abstract and symbolic while letting sensorimotor information instantiate them online. [Contested] — [Mahon & Caramazza 2008](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/caoslab/pdf/Mahon,%20Caramazza_Journal%20of%20Physiology%20Paris_2008.pdf)
- SEP, "Embodied Cognition" (first published 2021, substantive revision November 2026): behavioral evidence for grounded concepts is far from conclusive; the action-sentence compatibility effect has been reported as not reproducible; activation does not establish causal relevance; abstract concepts raise generalization, disembodiment and flexibility problems; TMS and single-unit results in turn threaten the claim that sensorimotor activity is always epiphenomenal; neuroscience has moved to a middle ground of weak embodiment with convergence zones or hubs. [Established as a current authoritative summary of a Contested field] — [SEP, Embodied Cognition](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition/)

### Inferences
- Hesslow's mechanism is Hull's 1930 mechanism with two substitutions: proprioceptive feedback from a reduced overt act is replaced by a direct cortical route from response preparation to sensory areas, and conditioning of s to the next R is replaced by simulated percepts cueing the next response preparation. The engineering content is identical: closed-loop rollout of a learned sequence model with the motor output gated off.
- Three requirements follow for an implementation: an output gate that suppresses execution while leaving preparation intact; a learned mapping from prepared action to expected observation; and re-entry of the predicted observation at the same input site real observations use. Evaluation can be the ordinary value or affect pathway applied to predicted observations, with no separate critic.
- Hesslow's "no symbols, no leap" position is a direct denial of the hypothesis that symbols are what let thought replace behavior. On his account words are just one more behavior that can be simulated.
- The weakest link, by Hesslow's own account, is anticipation, which is exactly the learned world model. The well-supported parts (motor and sensory areas active in imagery) are the least informative about mechanism.
- Same mechanism? Hesslow, Jeannerod, Grush: one mechanism for covert action and imagery, with concept formation either unaddressed or waved at. Barsalou: yes, explicitly: simulators are both the re-enactment mechanism and the conceptual units, with recursion from combining simulators. That is the part of his theory critics find least defined.

### Gaps
- Jeannerod's "S-states" (my recollection: his label for the family of covert action states, intended, imagined, observed, dreamed) could not be confirmed because PubMed pages would not load. Body text of Hesslow 2012, Grush 2004 and Barsalou 1999 was not read.
- Unverified: Whitford et al. 2017 (eLife) on N1 suppression by inner speech as efference-copy evidence; EMG studies of subvocalization (McGuigan and others).
- I found no direct test of whether suppressing the putative anticipation pathway abolishes covert chaining while sparing single imagined acts.

## 5. Cerebellum and basal ganglia: Ito, Llinas, Graybiel

### Takeaway
The claim that thought is controlled like movement is meant literally by Ito (the cerebellum forms internal models of cortical mental representations as it does of limbs) and as an evolutionary thesis by Llinas (thinking is internalized movement), and as a circuit analogy by Graybiel (basal ganglia modulate cognitive pattern generators as they do motor ones). Anatomy and imaging support cerebellar and basal ganglia loops with prefrontal cortex; the computational claim remains, in the field's own consensus statement, highly inferential.

### Cited Findings
- Ito, "Control of mental activities by internal models in the cerebellum," Nature Reviews Neuroscience 9(4), 304-313 (2008): cerebellar circuitry encodes internal models reproducing the dynamics of body parts; the hypothesis is that it may also encode internal models reproducing the essential properties of mental representations in cerebral cortex; the article reviews the conceptual basis and evidence for that hypothesis. Later reviews cite Ito (2005, 2008) for the idea of the cerebellum as a generalized forward controller for cognition as well as sensorimotor control (attribution seen in search-result excerpts of citing reviews, not re-checked in full text). [Established as hypothesis] — [Scilit record, Ito 2008](https://scilit.com/publications/1f1bdba2b8a7403a603ba330e040f962); [Frontiers in Neural Circuits 2012 review citing Ito](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncir.2012.00116)
- Koziol et al., "Consensus paper: the cerebellum's role in movement and cognition," Cerebellum 13(1), 151-177 (2014), with sections by Ito, Schmahmann, Pezzulo, Ramnani, Manto and others. All contributors accept a cognitive role, but the abstract calls the conclusion highly inferential and the summary says the study of cerebellar cognition is in its infancy. Ito cautions that the role in cognition remains a psychological conceptual model not yet demonstrated computationally, while not doubting a contribution to thinking. [Established] — [Koziol et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4089997)
- Same paper, supporting evidence: prefrontal area 46 connects with cerebellar Crus I and Crus II, not with the cerebellar zones linked to primary motor cortex (Kelly & Strick, per Ramnani); cerebellar activations persist when motor components are controlled (Parker); cerebellar cognitive affective syndrome and "dysmetria of thought" after lesions of the cognitive loops (Schmahmann). Schmahmann's "universal cerebellar transform" (one computation applied to motor, cognitive and limbic inputs) is presented as hypothesis. [Established anatomy and clinical syndrome; transform Contested] — [Koziol et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4089997)
- Same paper, cautions: Manto says cognitive deficits in ataxias cannot yet be attributed to cerebellar pathology because frontal and basal ganglia involvement can produce them; Pezzulo notes that once cortical representations are shaped they may run without sustained cerebellar activity, which confounds imaging; the general statement that the cerebellum assists working memory, perhaps by copying neocortical content, is offered as shared opinion; Yamazaki reports the proposal that internal models first acquired sensorimotorically later support executive function. [Established as stated cautions] — [Koziol et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4089997)
- Llinas, I of the Vortex: From Neurons to Self (MIT Press, 2001): the nervous system evolved for movement and its core function is prediction of the outcome of movement; reviewers quote the thesis that what we call thinking is the evolutionary internalization of movement; the opening chapters are titled on motor primacy and thinking as internalized movement. [Established as the book's thesis; not empirical evidence] — [Project MUSE review](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/26141/pdf); [chapter excerpt](https://www.cse.iitk.ac.in/users/se367/14/Readings/llinas-Rodolfo-2002_i-of-the-vortex-ch-1-2.pdf); [Times Higher Education review](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/86600)
- Graybiel, "The basal ganglia and cognitive pattern generators," Schizophrenia Bulletin 23(3), 459-469 (1997): by analogy with motor pattern generators, basal ganglia circuits help control cognitive pattern generators; dysfunction could underlie positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia. [Established as hypothesis] — [Scilit record, Graybiel 1997](https://scilit.com/publications/54f4c43ac9e17928a3d5dea773eb224c)
- Hesslow reads cerebellar and striatal activation in imagined movement, mental rotation and Tower of London, and planning deficits in Parkinson's disease, as what a simulation theory predicts, and notes that prism adaptation can be driven by imagined pointing errors. [Established as cited evidence] — [Hesslow 2002, p. 246](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)

### Inferences
- How literal? Ito's claim is the most literal and the most useful for an engineer: a slow, flexible controller (prefrontal cortex) manipulates a controlled object (a cortical representation); a fast learner trained on error signals copies the input-output behavior of that loop; thereafter the fast copy runs the transformation without the slow controller. That is distillation or amortization of a deliberate process, and it is the cerebellar version of Hull's short-circuiting. It explains how thought becomes automatic, not how thought arises.
- Llinas supplies an evolutionary rationale (prediction for movement comes first, and cognition is that predictive machinery run without overt output) but no testable mechanism beyond that.
- Graybiel's proposal amounts to: the same selection-and-sequencing circuit operates on cognitive as on motor patterns. Combined with chunking (see Gaps) it would give one mechanism that both automates sequences and creates higher-order units.
- Same mechanism? Ito: no. Cortex builds the mental models; cerebellum copies and automates them. Internalization-as-automation and concept construction are assigned to different structures. Graybiel: the same circuit motif for motor and cognitive sequences. Llinas: one predictive function, unspecified for concepts.

### Gaps
- Ito's own detailed section in the consensus paper (forward versus inverse models of mental models; implicit thought and intuition) fell in a part of the page I could not retrieve; my summary of the controller/controlled-object scheme beyond the 2008 abstract is from background knowledge.
- Unverified: Graybiel 1998, "The basal ganglia and chunking of action repertoires" (Neurobiology of Learning and Memory 70), which is the natural source for chunking as a shared motor-cognitive mechanism.
- I found no decisive experiment separating "cerebellum computes a forward model of thought" from "cerebellum contributes timing or error correction to cortical processes generally".

## 6. Developmental internalization: Vygotsky, Luria, inner speech; Piaget; Bruner; Werner and Kaplan; Karmiloff-Smith

### Takeaway
Two rival internalization theories matter here. Vygotsky: social speech becomes private speech becomes inner speech, transformed (not merely muted) on the way, and sign mediation is what creates higher mental functions; yet thought is not identical to inner speech. Piaget: operations are interiorized, reversible actions organized in structures, and each new level is built by "reflecting abstraction", projecting the coordinations of one's own actions to a higher plane and reorganizing them there; language is neither the source of logic nor sufficient for it. Piaget and Karmiloff-Smith are the clearest sources for the claim that level-building and recursion come from re-representing one's own action sequences, but Karmiloff-Smith treats redescription as a process distinct from the initial procedural learning.

### Cited Findings

**Vygotsky and Luria**
- Vygotsky's Thinking and Speech (Myshlenie i rech, 1934; partial English translation 1962, complete 1986/1987) grounds the best-known private speech work in a disagreement with Piaget: self-directed speech is self-guiding, not a sign of communicative immaturity, and develops toward inner speech rather than fading through socialization. [Established] — [EBSCO research starter on Thought and Language](https://ebsco.com/research-starters/history/thought-and-language-lev-vygotsky); [Cambridge UP excerpt on private speech](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/66071/excerpt/9780521866071_excerpt.htm)
- From chapter 7: inner speech is speech for oneself, originating in the differentiation of an initially social function; egocentric speech is fated to become inner speech. Its syntax tends to pure predication (subject dropped, predicate kept); sense predominates over meaning; words agglutinate. Vygotsky rejects the "speech minus sound" definition and Watson's view that inner speech is external speech left incomplete: the difference is of kind, not degree. [Established as description of the text] — [Vygotsky, Thinking and Speech, ch. 7](https://www.marxists.org/archive/vygotsky/works/words/ch07.htm)
- Thought is not identical to inner speech. Thought is completed, not merely expressed, in the word and is restructured in becoming speech; thought has its own structure and does not consist of separate words (the image of a cloud that pours out a rain of words); behind thought stands an affective-volitional tendency. The path runs from motive to thought to inner word to word meanings to external words. Inner speech is described as, to a significant extent, thinking in pure meanings. [Established as description of the text] — [Vygotsky, Thinking and Speech, ch. 7](https://www.marxists.org/archive/vygotsky/works/words/ch07.htm)
- Luria (1959) describes speech acquiring a directive function over the child's own behavior, using Pavlov's second-signal vocabulary. [Established] — [Luria 1959](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luria/works/1959/directive-funtion-speech.pdf)

**Alderson-Day and Fernyhough 2015**
- "Inner speech: development, cognitive functions, phenomenology, and neurobiology," Psychological Bulletin 141(5), 931-965 (2015). Private speech is near-universal, emerges at about 2-3 years, peaks near 5 (Kohlberg, Yaeger & Hjertholm 1968), declines as inner speech grows (Damianova et al. 2012), rises with task difficulty (Fernyhough & Fradley 2005), and persists into adulthood (Duncan & Cheyne 2001). Only 5-year-olds, not older children, did better with more overt self-talk (Winsler & Naglieri 2003). Fernyhough (2004) distinguishes condensed (default) from expanded inner speech. [Established] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- Dual-task evidence is selective. Articulatory suppression raises task-switch costs (Emerson & Miyake 2003), an effect reduced by explicit cues and present only with opaque cues (Miyake et al. 2004). In Tower of London, suppression impaired children only when told to plan ahead (Lidstone, Meins & Fernyhough 2010); adult results are mixed (Williams et al. 2012 impairment; Phillips et al. 1999 and Law et al. 2013 none). Matrix reasoning was unaffected (Rao & Baddeley 2013). The authors' summary is that inner speech is a domain-general tool that is selectively deployed and does not appear necessary for logical reasoning. [Established] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- Aphasia: in 27 post-stroke patients inner and overt speech were correlated but dissociated in some individuals (Geva et al. 2011); language impairment went with poorer problem-solving, and suppression hurt intact adults on the same task (Baldo et al. 2005, 2010). Deaf signers show signed soliloquy (Zimmermann & Brugger 2013). Some autistic adults report little inner speech and are unaffected by suppression on planning tasks (Hurlburt, Happe & Frith 1994; Williams et al. 2012). [Established as individual findings] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- The authors' conclusion: it would be premature to call the involvement of language in cognition constitutive; inner speech may be an exaptation, a motor-based linguistic tool that happens to have created an inner life. Laboratory work mostly assumes a Watsonian picture (overt speech without articulation) while developmental theory follows Vygotsky and Luria. [Established as the review's position] — [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)

**Piaget**
- Piaget, "Development and learning," Journal of Research in Science Teaching 2(3), 176-186 (1964; reprinted 2003, vol. 40 supplement, S8-S18). To know an object is to act on it and transform it. An operation is an interiorized action that modifies the object of knowledge; it is reversible (joining/separating, adding/subtracting); and it is never isolated but always part of a total structure. A class exists only within a classification; an asymmetric relation only within a seriation, which he calls the natural, basic operational structure; a number only within the number series. [Established, read from the reprint] — [Piaget 1964](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/611/Piaget.pdf)
- Interiorization is not direct translation: at the representational level everything built at the sensorimotor level must be reconstructed, and through the pre-operational period there are symbols and language but no operations (no conservation, no reversibility). [Established] — [Piaget 1964, p. S9](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/611/Piaget.pdf)
- Logico-mathematical experience draws knowledge not from objects but from the subject's actions on them. A child counting pebbles in a row, then backwards, then in a circle, discovers that the sum is independent of order: a property of the actions of ordering and joining, not of pebbles. Later deduction consists of interiorizing these actions and combining them without the pebbles; the mathematician can combine operations simply with symbols. Once operations are attained, coordinations of actions proceed by themselves as deduction. [Established] — [Piaget 1964, pp. S11-S12](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/611/Piaget.pdf)
- Piaget states that logic is not a derivative of language; its source is the total coordination of actions. Linguistic transmission is insufficient: children heard class-inclusion constructions daily for years without understanding them until they had built the corresponding logical structure. Equilibration (self-regulation with feedback and feedforward, in the cybernetic sense) is the principal factor and yields reversibility; levels come in a necessary sequence, each the most probable once the previous is reached. [Established] — [Piaget 1964, pp. S12-S13](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/611/Piaget.pdf)
- Reflecting abstraction (abstraction reflechissante; Recherches sur l'abstraction reflechissante, 1977; English edition Studies in Reflecting Abstraction, ed. R. L. Campbell, Psychology Press, 2001). Empirical abstraction bears on observables; reflecting abstraction bears on the coordinations of the subject's actions. It has two inseparable aspects: projection of what is drawn from a lower level onto a higher one, and a more or less conscious reconstruction or reorganization there. Its conscious product is "reflected" abstraction, thought taking its own operations as objects. "Pseudo-empirical" abstraction reads off objects properties the subject's own activity put there. [Established as Piaget's theory] — [Piaget 1977, Fondation Jean Piaget](https://unige.ch/piaget/piaget1977RARB_16); [von Glasersfeld 1991](https://dbis-digivis.uibk.ac.at/mediawiki/index.php?oldid=3239); [Routledge page for Studies in Reflecting Abstraction](https://www.routledge.com/Studies-in-Reflecting-Abstraction/Piaget/p/book/9781315800509)
- Level-building is iterated: what one level produces becomes content for coordination at the next, a spiral; action is the source of conceptualized knowledge, with awareness lagging. A symbol can point to an operation without the operation being carried out, so forms can be manipulated independently of content, provided the user could re-run the operation if needed. Reflective abstraction may be triggered by cognitive conflict. [Established as secondary exposition of Piaget] — [von Glasersfeld 1991](https://dbis-digivis.uibk.ac.at/mediawiki/index.php?oldid=3239); [Lancaster glossary, reflective abstraction](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/reflective_abstraction/)
- Standard caveat: later research finds development considerably more continuous than Piaget's stage model claims. [Established as a textbook commonplace; seen in a search excerpt from one of several open-textbook summaries, and the specific page below was not re-checked] — [open-textbook summary of Piaget's concrete and formal stages](https://content.one.lumenlearning.com/introductiontopsychology/chapter/9-2-3-learn-it-concrete-operational-stage/)

**Bruner; Werner and Kaplan**
- Bruner, "The course of cognitive growth" (1964): enactive, iconic and symbolic representation appear in that order, each depending on the previous, all persisting through life; the modes translate into one another; the symbolic mode is called the most mysterious. [Established; journal details Unverified, see Gaps] — [Bruner 1964, course copy](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/544/Bruner_1964_CoCG.pdf); [Simply Psychology summary](https://www.simplypsychology.org/bruner.html)
- Werner & Kaplan, Symbol Formation: An Organismic-Developmental Approach to Language and the Expression of Thought (Wiley, 1963): symbol formation is grounded in bodily, organismic activity and proceeds by "distancing"; the book's notions of distancing and physiognomic metaphor are still being re-examined. [Established at this level of description] — [Routledge page](https://www.routledge.com/Symbol-Formation/Werner-Kaplan/p/book/9780898593709); [Wikipedia, Heinz Werner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Werner_%28psychologist%29); [Culture & Psychology special issue abstract](https://vbn.aau.dk/da/publications/symbol-formation-reconsidered-moving-forward-by-looking-back)

**Karmiloff-Smith's representational redescription**
- Beyond Modularity (MIT Press, 1992; BBS precis 1994) defines representational redescription (RR) as the process by which information in a cognitive system becomes progressively explicit knowledge to that system, moving from implicit procedural format (I) through explicit levels E1 to E3, with E3 close enough to natural language to be stated. Critics note that key terms (behavioral success, implicit information, endogenous metaprocesses, the levels themselves) elude definition, and that E2 and E3 are hard to distinguish. [Established as theory; definitions Contested] — [BBS precis record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/precis-of-beyond-modularity-a-developmental-perspective-on-cognitive-science/D6B5A485C8B44CA6158CC558A494EF60); [BBS commentary "How far beyond modularity?"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/how-far-beyond-modularity/483B5D32B161F577BF34E089AC6C966F)
- McClelland's account of the original statement (Karmiloff-Smith 1986, Cognition 23(2), 95-147): phase 1, implicit representations support correct performance; phase 2, implicit comparisons among procedures, sometimes producing new errors or over-marking; phase 3, qualitatively different representations, potentially conscious and usable planfully. Two stressed assumptions: the cycle is not age-bound but recurs for each aspect of language or cognition, and it is driven by success, not failure; only after mastery can the child reflect on what was learned. [Established, read from the chapter] — [McClelland 2021](https://stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/McClelland21AppreciationOfAKS.pdf)
- Clark & Karmiloff-Smith (1993, Mind & Language 8(4), 487-519): the implicit representations that are the endpoint of learning in a connectionist network are, in humans, the starting point for redescription; networks model initial learning well but not the subsequent representational change. McClelland agrees that standard networks do not explicitly represent or manipulate their own knowledge, points to Lampinen & McClelland (2020, PNAS) where an activation pattern describing a task network's weights is itself transformed by another network, and concedes this is still imposed by the modeler rather than generated by the learner. [Established as assessment by a leading connectionist] — [McClelland 2021](https://stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/McClelland21AppreciationOfAKS.pdf)
- Other responses: Shultz argued some computational models already do a form of RR; a thesis proposing "Recursive Re-Representation" rejects mastery-as-trigger; a Sussex thesis (Brook) tests RR with connectionist models including one on the effect of redescription on sequence learning. [Established that these exist; contents not read] — [Shultz abstract](https://www.psych.mcgill.ca/perpg/fac/shultz/personal/Recent_Publications_files/redescribe94_abs.htm); [Sussex CSRP 491](https://www.sussex.ac.uk/informatics/cogslib/reports/csrp/csrp491.pdf)

### Inferences
- Vygotsky's and Piaget's internalizations are different operations and should not be merged. Vygotsky's is the transfer of a social, sign-using control loop to the inside, with compression (predication, sense over meaning). Piaget's is the detachment of an action scheme from its objects plus its closure into a reversible, composable system. An architecture could implement either without the other.
- Piaget gives the most explicit version of the brief's hypothesis, with three constraints an engineer should note. First, the unit that gets internalized is not the action sequence but the *coordination* among actions (order, inclusion, correspondence), i.e. relational invariants of the subject's own activity. Second, the mark that internalization has succeeded is reversibility and composition within a closed structure, not mere offline replay; pre-operational children replay and imagine without having operations. Third, each level must be reconstructed, not copied: projection plus reorganization.
- On Piaget's account classification and seriation, hence conceptual order, are derived from the actions of grouping and ordering, and number from their synthesis. This is a direct claim that the mechanism producing sequence-in-action and the mechanism producing conceptual order are one, operating at successive levels.
- Symbols for Piaget are enablers, not sources: the semiotic function lets an operation be pointed to without being run (a token standing for a whole procedure), which is what lets operations be composed "simply with symbols". This is close to Hull's pure stimulus act with one decisive addition: the token stands for a reversible, composable operation rather than a link in a fixed chain.
- Karmiloff-Smith's level I is, in effect, a compiled sequence that runs as a whole and whose parts are not addressable; redescription makes components addressable and recombinable. If that reading is right, RR is the missing step between sequence learning and conceptual flexibility, and it is success-triggered consolidation rather than error-driven learning. McClelland's verdict is the honest state of the art: nobody has a network that does this endogenously.
- The inner-speech evidence fits a tool view: verbal self-cueing helps when the task set must be retrieved or held without external support and is dispensable otherwise. That is what one expects if words function as retrieval cues for control states, not as the medium of inference.
- Same mechanism? Vygotsky: yes for higher mental functions, which are formed by internalized sign mediation, but thought has a separate pre-verbal root and is not inner speech. Piaget: yes, most explicitly; interiorization of action and reflecting abstraction are one constructive process iterated across levels. Bruner: partly; a distinct symbolic system reorganizes earlier modes. Karmiloff-Smith: no; implicit procedural learning and redescription are distinct processes, though redescription re-applies recursively to its own outputs.

### Gaps
- Karmiloff-Smith's drawing studies (as I recall, 1990, Cognition: younger children asked to draw a nonexistent man could alter only the end of the drawing procedure, older children could insert or reorder mid-sequence, the "sequential constraint") are the most pertinent evidence that level-I knowledge is a rigid sequence relaxed by redescription. I could not verify this in the session; it should be checked before use. The BBS precis page returned an access error, so its volume and pages (I recall 17(4), 693-707) are unverified.
- Campbell's introduction "Reflecting abstraction in context" was not read; whether Piaget anywhere uses the word recursion for the iteration of reflecting abstraction is unconfirmed. The iterated, spiral character is confirmed via von Glasersfeld.
- Bruner 1964 journal details (I believe American Psychologist 19(1), 1-15) were not confirmed. Werner and Kaplan's four-component scheme (addressor, addressee, symbolic vehicle, referent) and their "primordial sharing situation" are from background knowledge only.
- Luria's 1961 book on verbal regulation of behavior and the mixed replication record of his bulb-press studies were not sourced.
- The private-speech literature is observational; I found no intervention study showing that preventing private speech in early childhood alters later inner speech or executive function.

## 7. Hebb's phase sequence and its descendants: Pulvermuller, Buzsaki

### Takeaway
Hebb supplied the first neural mechanism for thought as activity running without sensory input: cell assemblies that outlast their stimulus, chained into a "phase sequence" that he identified with the thought process. Buzsaki's modern version makes the strong claim that cognition is the action-perception loop disengaged from the body, running the same self-organized sequence generators used for navigation. Both are single-mechanism theories in which words or symbols are not required for internal sequences to run.

### Cited Findings
- Hebb, The Organization of Behavior (1949): neurons that fire together form a cell assembly whose activity can persist after the triggering event and represent it; a series of such events is a "phase sequence", which Hebb equates with the thought process; each assembly may be aroused by a preceding assembly, by a sensory event, or normally by both. Later accounts locate the assembly at pp. 69-74 and the phase sequence as the basis for a train of thought at pp. 79-106, and note that a persisting assembly could be the structural basis of imagery. [Established] — [Brown 2020, "Donald O. Hebb and the Organization of Behavior: 17 years in the writing," Molecular Brain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7137474); [Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 2021 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.732195/pdf)
- Research on the phase sequence lagged behind research on the synapse and assembly; multi-electrode recording has only recently allowed study of how assemblies integrate into sequences. [Established; statement seen in a search excerpt of a 2020 review, most likely the Brown article above, not re-checked in full text] — [Brown 2020, publisher PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13041-020-00567-8.pdf)
- Buzsaki, Peyrache & Kubie, "Emergence of cognition from action," Cold Spring Harbor Symposia on Quantitative Biology 79, 41-50 (2014). Thesis: cognition arises from the brain's disengagement from immediate overt action as a result of experience; cognition is defined as input/output disengagement with continued computation of potential responses, so the consequences of alternative actions can be anticipated without testing them; the authors call this vicarious action a cognitive process. Changing cell-assembly sequences can be generated in the hippocampus without external cues. Memory and planning evolved from navigation, and the algorithms for navigating real and mental space are fundamentally the same; semantic knowledge arises from repeated episodic encounters until items lose their spatiotemporal context. Meaning is acquired by matching pre-existing neuronal patterns to action-perception. [Established as the authors' theory] — [Buzsaki, Peyrache & Kubie 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895837)
- Buzsaki, The Brain from Inside Out (Oxford University Press, 2019): the brain is an explorer that tests hypotheses through action, with preconfigured, self-organized dynamics; its main job is predicting the consequences of its actions; self-generated sequences are called the foundation of cognitive ability. [Established as the book's thesis] — [APS summary of Buzsaki's Kavli keynote](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/2020-kavli-keynote-cognition)
- Pulvermuller and colleagues: "action perception circuits" are distributed cell assemblies binding motor and sensory information, proposed as the substrate of word meaning (Pulvermuller, Moseley, Egorova, Shebani & Boulenger, Neuropsychologia, 2013/2014). In spiking models with realistic connectivity, Hebbian learning of co-activated frontal, temporal and occipital populations produces word assemblies with category-specific extensions into motor or visual areas (Tomasello, Garagnani, Wennekers & Pulvermuller 2018, Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience); assemblies also form for concrete and abstract conceptual patterns under unsupervised learning (Henningsen-Schomers & Pulvermuller). [Established as models; relation to meaning Contested, see section 4] — [Pulvermuller et al., FU Berlin repository](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/fub188/15766/1/1-s2.0-S0028393213004247-main.pdf); [Tomasello et al. 2018](https://journal.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fncom.2018.00088); [Henningsen-Schomers & Pulvermuller](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9674741)

### Inferences
- Hebb's innovation over Hull is that the chain is central from the start: assembly-to-assembly excitation replaces response-produced stimulation. The mechanism that makes thought possible is the same Hebbian plasticity that makes perception possible, plus reverberation.
- Buzsaki supplies a specific answer to "where do concepts come from in a sequence system": semantic structure is what remains when many episodic sequences through the same items are superimposed and their particular contexts cancel. That is a single-mechanism account of sequence memory and conceptual knowledge and is testable in a model (items become context-free nodes when visited on many trajectories).
- His phrase about matching pre-existing patterns to experience implies sequence generators exist before learning and are bound to content, rather than sequences being learned from scratch. An architecture taking this seriously would have a pretrained or intrinsic sequence generator and learn only the mapping to content.
- Same mechanism? Hebb: yes (assemblies and their sequencing underlie percept, concept and thought). Buzsaki: yes (one navigation-derived sequence system for path integration, episodic memory, semantic memory and planning). Pulvermuller: yes for word and concept circuits. None of the three assigns symbols a special causal role in running internal sequences.

### Gaps
- Hebb's treatment of higher-order assemblies (a superordinate assembly for a triangle built from assemblies for its corners) is from background knowledge; I did not read the 1949 text.
- The 2014 paper does not use the terms corollary discharge or dead reckoning, although Buzsaki's book develops both; I could not source the book's detailed argument.
- I found no direct evidence in this session for the episodic-to-semantic "context stripping" claim beyond the authors' statement of it.

## 8. What symbols add: Deacon, Harnad, Hofstadter, Clark, Penn-Holyoak-Povinelli, Corballis, Suddendorf and Corballis

### Takeaway
Authors who think symbols matter mostly do not say symbols are what let thought replace behavior; they say symbols change what offline thought can be about and how it can be structured: reference sustained by relations among tokens rather than by correlation with the world (Deacon), stable objects for second-order thought (Clark), higher-order relational structure (Penn et al.), self-reference across levels (Hofstadter). Corballis and Suddendorf reverse the dependency: recursive, generative simulation (mental time travel, theory of mind) came first and language evolved to share it.

### Cited Findings
- Deacon, The Symbolic Species (Norton, 1997), as summarized by Port: a hierarchy in which an index is built from icons linked by correlation and a symbol from relations among indices; symbols represent each other as well as things; an index needs ongoing correlation to be maintained whereas symbolic reference survives changes in correlation. The chimpanzees Sherman and Austin acquired symbolic use only under training deliberately structured by humans; Kanzi crossed the threshold more easily, attributed to his immaturity. [Established as secondary summary; primary text not read] — [Port's notes on Deacon](https://legacy.cs.indiana.edu/~port/teach/500/deacon.signs.html); [XMCA list summary](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/MCA/Mail/xmcamail.2005_01.dir/0170.html)
- Peirce scholars most often criticize Deacon for applying the icon-index-symbol trichotomy to broad evolutionary phases. [Contested] — [Sign Systems Studies article](https://ojs.utlib.ee/index.php/sss/article/download/SSS.2015.43.4.05/10856/16179)
- Harnad, "The symbol grounding problem," Physica D 42, 335-346 (1990): symbols manipulated by shape alone cannot get meaning from other symbols (like learning Chinese from a Chinese-Chinese dictionary). Proposed hybrid: iconic representations (analogs of sensory projections), categorical representations (learned and innate feature detectors, plausibly connectionist) and, grounded in these, symbolic representations as strings stating category membership relations. [Established] — [Harnad 1990](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~harnad/Papers/Harnad/harnad90.sgproblem.html)
- Hofstadter, Godel, Escher, Bach (1979) and I Am a Strange Loop (2007): a strange loop is a cyclic structure passing through several levels of a hierarchy so that moving up or down returns one to the start (a tangled hierarchy with no fixed top or bottom); the complexity of active symbols in the brain yields Godel-like self-reference, from which the "I" emerges. [Established as the books' thesis, via tertiary source] — [Wikipedia, Strange loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_loop); [Wikipedia, I Am a Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_a_Strange_Loop)
- Clark, "Magic words: how language augments human computation," in Carruthers & Boucher (eds.), Language and Thought: Interdisciplinary Themes (Cambridge University Press, 1998), pp. 162-183: language transforms and simplifies the computational problems the biological brain faces. A lecture summary lists six roles: memory augmentation, environmental simplification, coordination and reduced online deliberation, taming path-dependent learning, attention and resource allocation, and data manipulation and representation. Words act as fixed points that make thoughts into objects for further attention, producing second-order thought. [Established; six-item list from a secondary source] — [Cambridge chapter page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/language-and-thought/magic-words-how-language-augments-human-computation/F2F6830F0F0DBD13F4D4CAAB637F6E62); [lecture slides on the chapter](https://unstable.nl/andreas/ai/phil/pres.pdf)
- Penn, Holyoak & Povinelli, "Darwin's mistake: explaining the discontinuity between human and nonhuman minds," Behavioral and Brain Sciences (2008): biological continuity masks a functional discontinuity in the ability to approximate the higher-order, systematic, relational capabilities of a physical symbol system; the gap runs through nearly every domain and is deeper than language or culture alone can explain ("relational reinterpretation"). Lupyan's commentary argues language itself may produce the difference and cites dolphin relational competence against a sharp discontinuity; the authors replied in "Darwin's triumph". [Contested] — [Penn, Holyoak & Povinelli 2008](https://sites.lifesci.ucla.edu/psych-reasoninglab/wp-content/uploads/sites/273/2021/04/Penn-Holyoak-Povinelli.2008.pdf); [Lupyan commentary](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~lupyan/lupyan_commentary_PHP.pdf)
- Corballis, The Recursive Mind (Princeton University Press, 2011; updated 2014): recursion, embedding thoughts within thoughts, is the distinctive human capacity; it arose before and outside language, in mental time travel and theory of mind; language evolved to share these. DeWitt's review objects that neither mental time travel nor theory of mind is well established in other primates, so there is at present no evidence for the prior evolution of recursion in non-linguistic domains. [Contested] — [Princeton UP page](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691160948/the-recursive-mind); [Corballis essay, "Language evolution and recursive thought"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3812905/); [DeWitt 2013 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00812)
- Suddendorf & Corballis, "The evolution of foresight: what is mental time travel, and is it unique to humans?" Behavioral and Brain Sciences 30, 299-351 (2007): episodic memory is part of a general faculty of mental time travel composed of several subsidiary mechanisms (a theater metaphor); no convincing evidence of it in other animals. Osvath & Gardenfors' commentary challenges the explanation of human uniqueness. [Contested] — [Suddendorf & Corballis 2007](https://core.ac.uk/download/15037936.pdf)
- Suddendorf, Addis & Corballis, "Mental time travel and the shaping of the human mind," Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 364, 1317-1324 (2009): generativity is what most distinguishes human mental time travel; the generativity of language reflects that of the underlying thought; language may have evolved first for sharing past and planned events. [Established as the authors' position] — [Suddendorf, Addis & Corballis 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2666704)

### Inferences
- Sorting the proposals by what a symbol is supposed to do yields four distinct functions, each implying a different mechanism:
  1. Cue: an internally producible event that triggers a simulation in the absence of its usual stimulus (Hull's pure stimulus act, Hesslow's inner speech, the inner-speech task-set evidence). Requires only that some outputs be re-enterable as inputs.
  2. Handle: a compact token standing for a whole procedure or chunk so it can be an argument of further operations (Piaget's symbol pointing to an operation, Karmiloff-Smith's explicit formats, Clark's second-order thought). Requires a re-representation step and a workspace where tokens can be operands.
  3. System: reference fixed by token-to-token relations so it survives loss of correlation with the world (Deacon; Harnad's grounded symbol strings; Penn et al.'s higher-order relations). Requires learning relations among tokens and a shift in what supports reference, which Deacon's ape evidence suggests is hard and not a by-product of associative learning.
  4. Loop: tokens that can refer to the system's own processing across levels (Hofstadter; Piaget's reflected abstraction). Requires function 2 applied to the system's own operations, iterated.
- Only function 1 is about thought replacing behavior, and the animal and machine evidence says it does not need symbols in senses 2 to 4. Functions 2 to 4 are about building higher-order structure. So the phrase in the brief, that the use of symbols allows thought to replace behavior, conflates two things the literature keeps apart: simulation (old, shared, non-symbolic) and re-representation (possibly human-specific, symbol-dependent).
- Corballis's position is the mirror image of the brief's hypothesis and is equally coherent: recursive structure originates in simulation of episodes and minds, and symbols externalize it. The data do not currently decide between "symbols enable recursion" and "recursion enables symbols".
- Same mechanism? Deacon: no; symbolic reference needs a restructuring beyond associative learning even though it is assembled from indices. Harnad: no; grounding is nonsymbolic, composition symbolic. Clark and Dennett: no; language is an external tool acquired by an otherwise pattern-completing brain. Penn et al.: no; a distinct relational capacity. Hofstadter: one substrate (active symbols), with self-reference as an emergent consequence of its richness. Corballis: recursion is a single domain-general capacity that precedes language.

### Gaps
- I did not read Deacon, Hofstadter, Clark or Penn et al. in full. Deacon's account of what the apes had to unlearn (dropping token-object associations in favor of token-token relations), his prefrontal hypothesis and the co-evolution argument are from background knowledge. Port's page was reachable by search but not by direct fetch.
- Penn et al. 2008 volume and pages (I recall 31(2), 109-130) and the page range of Clark 1998 (one source gives 162-183, another 177-198) are not confirmed.
- I found no experimental work testing whether acquiring an arbitrary token for a learned action sequence changes how that sequence can be recombined, which is the experiment the hypothesis most needs.

## 9. Evidence against: thought without inner speech or imagery, critiques of embodied simulation, and planning without symbols

### Takeaway
The strong claims fail. Thought is not covert speech (curare; severe aphasia with intact reasoning; the language network silent during logic; inner speech present in only about a quarter of sampled moments), not dependent on conscious imagery (aphantasia), and not constituted by motor simulation (production-comprehension dissociations). And offline planning does not need symbols (rats, and arguably apes and corvids). What survives is a weaker thesis: thought reuses predictive machinery that evolved for acting, and symbols are a later, separable amplifier whose main contributions are cueing, chunking, relational structure and sharing.

### Cited Findings

**Thought without language**
- Fedorenko & Varley, "Language and thought are not the same thing: evidence from neuroimaging and neurological patients," Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences 1369, 132-153 (2016): the language system is distinct from regions supporting many higher cognitive functions, and people with global aphasia show that many aspects of thought do not depend on language. [Established] — [UCL Discovery record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/cgi/export/eprint/1489434/HTML/discovery-eprint-1489434.html)
- Fedorenko, Piantadosi & Gibson, "Language is primarily a tool for communication rather than thought," Nature 630, 575-586 (2024; Perspective): language and thought run in distinct brain networks; language is optimized for communication; severely aphasic patients can still do arithmetic, play chess and plan; language is not a prerequisite for complex, including symbolic, thought and reflects rather than creates human cognitive sophistication. [Established evidence; the overall thesis is a Perspective and Contested by language-of-thought and linguistic-relativity camps] — [MIT News summary](https://news.mit.edu/2024/what-is-language-for-0703); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v630y2024i8017d10.1038_s41586-024-07522-w.html)
- 2026 update: Kean, Fedorenko and colleagues (with Varley's group), PNAS, reported by MIT News on 8 July 2026. Two patients with severe aphasia inferred hidden rules relating number lists and solved pattern-matrix problems as well as controls, conveying rules by gesture and sketch; in fMRI of neurotypical adults the language network was not engaged by inductive or deductive reasoning; the multiple-demand network supported induction but not deduction. The authors say this overturns the view that symbolic rule induction requires linguistic capacity. [Established as a new result; small patient sample; deduction tested only in the imaging group] — [MIT News, July 2026](https://news.mit.edu/2026/separating-logic-and-language-0708)
- Curare: full neuromuscular paralysis left thinking intact (Smith et al. 1947), against any peripheral covert-behavior theory. [Established] — [Hesslow 2002](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/u/ahyvarin/teaching/niseminar4/Hesslow_Simulation.pdf)
- Experience sampling: inner speech appears in roughly a quarter of randomly sampled moments (Heavey & Hurlburt 2008: 28%; later estimates 20-25%), with large individual differences and some people showing none; "unsymbolized thinking", a thought present without words or images, is one of five common forms of inner experience. Alderson-Day & Fernyhough contest the low frequency estimates. [Contested on frequency; the existence of wide individual variation is Established. The 28% figure was seen in a search excerpt of a later experience-sampling paper reporting the 2008 result, not in the 2008 paper itself] — [Heavey & Hurlburt 2008, Consciousness and Cognition (hosted copy)](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/inner-voice/2008-heavey.pdf); [Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013](https://www.hurlburt.faculty.unlv.edu/hurlburt-heavey-kelsey-2013-inner-speaking.pdf); [Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4538954)
- Nedergaard & Lupyan, "Not everybody has an inner voice: behavioral consequences of anendophasia," Psychological Science (2024): people reporting little inner speech (n = 46) versus much (n = 47) did worse on verbal working memory, with no difference on some other comparisons, and may compensate with other strategies. A 2025 commentary in the same journal argues there is no compelling evidence that anyone wholly lacks inner speech and that press coverage overstated the result. [Contested] — [Nedergaard & Lupyan 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/09567976241243004); [2025 commentary](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/09567976251335583); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/silent-inner-world-anendophasia)

**Thought without imagery**
- Zeman, "Aphantasia and hyperphantasia: exploring imagery vividness extremes," Trends in Cognitive Sciences 28(5) (2024): self-reported extremes of imagery have distinctive behavioral, physiological and neural correlates. People with aphantasia complete mental rotation, in one study more accurately but more slowly than controls (Kay, Keogh & Pearson 2024, Consciousness and Cognition), attributed to different strategies. Michel, Morales, Block & Lau reply that the slower times are compatible with unconscious imagery in some aphantasics. A 2024 systematic review covers 65 papers. [Established that task performance is largely preserved; whether imagery is absent or merely unconscious is Contested] — [Zeman 2024](https://medicine-vet-medicine.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-09/Zeman%202024%20%28Trends%20in%20Cognitive%20Sciences%29.pdf); [Psychology Today summary](https://psychologytoday.com/us/blog/consciousness-and-beyond/202406/aphantasia-what-imageless-minds-tell-us-about-consciousness); [Michel et al. reply](https://philarchive.org/archive/MICAAI-5); [systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39330760/)

**Against simulation as constitutive**
- Motor and speech production can be destroyed with comprehension spared and vice versa, so motor simulation is neither necessary nor sufficient for understanding. [Established dissociations] — [Hickok 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773693)
- Sensorimotor activation during conceptual tasks may be downstream spreading activation; some concepts have no stable sensorimotor content; the action-sentence compatibility effect has failed to replicate; activation does not establish causal relevance. [Contested field, see section 4] — [Mahon & Caramazza 2008](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/caoslab/pdf/Mahon,%20Caramazza_Journal%20of%20Physiology%20Paris_2008.pdf); [SEP, Embodied Cognition](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition/)

**Planning without symbols**
- Rats generate serial forward sweeps over alternative paths at choice points and pre-play paths to goals, including novel ones. [Established] — [Redish 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5029271); [Pfeiffer & Foster 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3990408)
- Great apes selected and kept tools for later use (Mulcahy & Call 2006) and chose a tool over an immediate food reward when the tool gave access to a larger later reward (Osvath & Osvath 2008); scrub-jays cache in ways sensitive to what, where and when. Suddendorf and colleagues argue the jay results do not show anticipation of future need (Correia et al. 2007), call the ape results the most promising challenge to the Bischof-Kohler hypothesis (that only humans act for needs they do not currently feel) while noting methodological concerns, and judge animal examples limited to instinct-laden situations without human flexibility. [Contested] — [Suddendorf, Addis & Corballis 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2666704)
- Machine existence proofs: agents plan by rollout in learned latent models with no symbolic representation supplied (MuZero, Dreamer), and continuous latent "thoughts" can outperform verbalized chains on search-heavy reasoning. [Established] — [Schrittwieser et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265); [Hafner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104); [Hao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769)
- Even the planning interpretation of replay is disputed at the level of trial-by-trial choice. [Contested] — [Gillespie et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8497431)

### Inferences

**What is left standing**
- Refuted: thought = subvocal speech (Watson as usually read); thought requires natural language; thought requires conscious imagery; understanding = motor simulation.
- Well supported: brains and machines can substitute internal rollouts of a learned model for overt trial and error; these rollouts reuse sensory, motor and hippocampal machinery; they appear when a model exists but the policy is unsettled and fade with automation.
- Open: whether rollouts build abstractions or merely use them; whether any symbol-like token is needed for level-building; whether human recursion depends on language or precedes it.
- A caution on the aphasia evidence: it shows that adult reasoning survives loss of language. It does not show that the reasoning capacity could have developed without language (Vygotsky's and Clark's claims are developmental). And the 2026 result that symbolic rule induction survives aphasia implies that if "symbols" matter, they are not natural-language words: an internal token system distinct from the language network would be needed.

**Cross-tradition summary: is internalization the same mechanism as concept-building?**

**What mechanism the hypothesis would imply, and how to tell if it is wrong**
- The literature supports three mechanisms that are usually run together. An architecture could have any subset.
  - M1, anticipatory closed-loop rollout (Hull, Hesslow, Tolman, Dyna): the sequence learner that predicts the next state from current state and action is run with motor output gated off and its own prediction fed back as input. Needs an output gate, an action-to-outcome predictor, re-entry at the input, and prioritization. No symbols. Strongly supported.
  - M2, compilation (Hull's short-circuiting, VTE fading, cerebellar copying, model-based to model-free): results of rollouts are cached so the sequence drops out. This explains why thought stops, and it is the reverse of abstraction. Strongly supported.
  - M3, re-representation (Piaget, Karmiloff-Smith, Clark, Hofstadter): a mastered sequence or the coordination among several is given a token that the same sequence machinery can then take as an element, so operations apply to operations. This is the only place where "the same recursive mechanism" for sequence learning and conceptual order is actually claimed. Supported by developmental observation; no agreed computational implementation; McClelland's judgment is that networks do not yet do it endogenously.
- For the designer's hypothesis to hold, M3 must be M1 applied to tokens produced by M2-like chunking: one sequence learner, whose outputs at level n become inputs at level n+1. The traditions that say "same mechanism" (Piaget, Hebb, Buzsaki, Barsalou, Graybiel) are all compatible with that; the traditions that say "different" (Karmiloff-Smith, Deacon, Ito, Dennett, Clark) say an extra step is required to turn a compiled procedure into an addressable object.
- Discriminating predictions an engineer can test. (a) If one mechanism suffices, higher-order units should emerge from repeated rollouts alone (Buzsaki's context-stripping), without a separate redescription process. (b) If Karmiloff-Smith is right, units will stay sequence-bound (modifiable only at their ends) until a distinct success-triggered consolidation makes their parts addressable. (c) If Piaget is right, the test of a genuine higher-order unit is reversibility and closure under composition, not replay. (d) If Hull's and Hesslow's view of symbols is right, re-entrant tokens need only be cues and can be continuous; if Deacon's is right, the system must learn token-to-token relations that hold when token-to-world correlations are removed.
- Since simulation without symbols is common and level-building without something symbol-like has not been demonstrated, the safest reading of the evidence is that symbols are not what allow thought to replace behavior; they are a candidate for what allows thought to operate on thought.
